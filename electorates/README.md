# 2026 electorates — voter guide

New Zealand votes on **Saturday 7 November 2026**. You get two votes: a **party vote** (decides how many seats each party gets) and an **electorate vote** for your local MP. There are **71 electorates** in 2026 — 64 general (48 North Island, 16 South Island) and 7 Māori — one fewer than in 2023 after the 2025 boundary review.

Find your electorate: [Electoral Commission electorate maps](https://elections.nz/democracy-in-nz/historical-events/boundary-review-2025/electorate-maps) · [interactive map](https://elections.nz/maps/boundary-review-2025). Data: [`data/electorates/2026.json`](../data/electorates/2026.json).

## What changed in the 2025 boundary review

The Representation Commission finalised boundaries on 8 August 2025: 19 electorates were unchanged and the boundaries of 49 general and three Māori electorates were adjusted, mainly in Auckland, Bay of Plenty, Christchurch and the lower North Island, where one North Island general seat was removed ([Electoral Commission](https://elections.nz/media-and-news/2025/electorate-boundaries-finalised); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_New_Zealand_general_election)).

**Abolished or renamed 2023 seats:** Bay of Plenty (renamed Mt Maunganui); East Coast (renamed East Cape); Kelston (split into Glendene and Waitākere); New Lynn (split into Waitākere (and a small part to Glendene)); Te Atatū (split into Henderson and Glendene); Panmure-Ōtāhuhu (became Ōtāhuhu (Panmure moved out)); Mana (split between Kenepuru and Kapiti); Ōtaki (replaced by Kapiti); Ōhāriu (split mainly between Kenepuru and Wellington North); Wellington Central (renamed Wellington North (Mount Cook/Brooklyn to Wellington Bays)); Rongotai (renamed Wellington Bays).

## About the data

- **Descriptions** are quoted verbatim from policy.nz with attribution. Several contain numerical errors (often the winner’s vote total is described as the “margin”); each electorate page lists fact-check notes against official results.
- **2023 results** are the official counts as reproduced in each Wikipedia electorate article (which cite the Electoral Commission; electionresults.govt.nz itself is blocked from this environment). Where `data/elections/2023.json` (built from the Wikipedia summary table, which uses some preliminary-count figures) differs, the page says so.
- **Candidates** are the final list published by policy.nz; list ranks from policy.nz or Wikipedia. Each links to the candidate’s folder under `parties/`.
- Compiled 2026-10-23 by `tools/electorates_build.py` (inputs fetched by `tools/electorates_wiki_fetch.py`, parsed by `tools/electorates_parse_wiki.py`).
