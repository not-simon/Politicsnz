Digital Future
Protect people. Harness innovation. Ensure ownership.

New technology can help New Zealand build a more productive economy and a better quality
of life. Artificial intelligence offers opportunities to develop new products, improve public
services and grow businesses that compete around the world. Realising those opportunities
means protecting the people, information and natural resources on which our prosperity
depends.

Opportunity believes we can both embrace technology and manage its risks. Clear rules can
give businesses the confidence to invest and people the confidence to use new tools. Our
ambition is a country where researchers and entrepreneurs can develop technology here,
businesses can adopt it with confidence, and New Zealanders share in the benefits rather
than carry the costs of change alone.

That requires a durable national direction. We will work across political lines to build a
technology economy that rewards innovation, protects personal information and intellectual
property, and strengthens our ability to make decisions in New Zealand’s interests. We
should be a country that creates technology as well as uses it, connected to the best the
world can offer without becoming dependent on any single provider.

1. Protect our people, data and environment
The benefits of innovation should not depend on workers bearing the costs of disruption,
communities subsidising resource use, or people surrendering control of their information.
Practical protections can build the trust that allows useful technology to flourish. We will
back adoption while making responsibilities clear and supporting people through change.

---

Support workers through change
Technology should help create better work and higher living standards, not make people
disposable. Businesses should be able to improve how they operate, while workers have the
financial security and opportunities to build new skills that make change manageable.

Opportunity will:

- fund the R&D experience and R&D career grants - which enable interns and graduates
to work in firms undertaking R&D - permanently and expand the number of available
grants
- use Opportunity’s Citizens’ Income to provide financial security through technological
change. See our Tax Reset policy for more detail.
- pursue technology adoption as part of our broader commitment to higher wages and
better-quality jobs
- require government agencies undertaking restructures based on technology-driven
efficiencies to share the business-case with affected staff and unions then publish it
in advance of implementation and evaluate outcomes.

Build digital infrastructure within environmental limits
AI infrastructure can support new industries and services, but its growth must be matched
by responsible use of electricity and water. Clear requirements will give investors certainty
while ensuring that expansion supports, rather than undermines, a resilient low-emissions
economy.

Opportunity will harness the growing consensus around permitting of AI data centres with
guidance through the National Policy Direction and National Standards under the new
Planning and Natural Environment Acts that requires them to:

- underwrite their own new clean energy generation, pay the full cost of grid
connections, meet efficiency mandates and curtail power use during peak grid
events.
- report publicly on power usage effectiveness and water usage effectiveness, and
impose limits on potable water use.

Strengthen cyber security and accountability
Confidence in technology depends on being able to use it safely and respond effectively
when something goes wrong. Stronger technical capability and clear reporting
responsibilities can protect essential services, help businesses manage risks and give
regulators evidence for proportionate action.

Opportunity will:

- increase funding for the National Cyber Security Centre, so it can extend its cyber and
technical support to government agencies and the private sector
- bring the Electoral Commission's systems within the proposed critical infrastructure
cyber security regime and increase its funding
- require mandatory reporting of serious AI incidents and their harms and remedial
actions
- strengthen the Privacy Commission with infringement notices for lower-level
breaches and court-imposed civil pecuniary penalties for serious or repeated failures
to protect personal information.
- broaden the Law Commission’s current review of automated decision-making to
include private sector decisionmaking and key human rights and other protections in
light of AI-use, including the potential for algorithmic bias and discrimination,
securing rights to human review and transparency in AI decision-making, personal or
cultural likeness rights, Māori data sovereignty and copyright.

2. Back Kiwi innovation and become a leader in
technology exports
Our goal is not simply to use more AI and technology for the sake of it. It is to help New
Zealanders create more value: better products, stronger exports, more rewarding work and
public services that meet people’s needs. Support for technology should make room for
experimentation while ensuring public investment delivers benefits and risks are managed.

Help businesses adopt and develop technology
Innovation includes putting proven tools to work, not just inventing something new. We want
smaller businesses to have access to that opportunity, with support that is straightforward
to use and tied to real economic outcomes.

Businesses also need a practical way to safely test new technology and products, and the
public needs confidence that experimentation will not come at its expense. Supervised trials
can help innovators and regulators learn together, identifying benefits and risks before new
products are used more widely.

Opportunity will:

- introduce a technology tax credit to support businesses adopting AI technology to
increase productivity. See our Breakthrough Economy policy for more.
- set a lower qualifying threshold and simplify applications for Research and
Development tax credits so firms without in-house tax or research and development
teams can participate. See our Breakthrough Economy policy for more.

Give innovation a stable national direction
Long-term investment needs more than enthusiasm for the latest technology. It needs clear
expectations about data, intellectual property and accountability, alongside the
infrastructure and incentives that help good ideas succeed. Public participation and
cross-party cooperation should make those settings both trusted and durable.

Opportunity will:

- develop a cross-party national AI framework to cover intellectual property protection,
data rights, investment incentives and the infrastructure needed for adoption and
give clear responsibility for implementing it to the Department of Prime Minister and
Cabinet
- convene a committee within the first year of government to oversee the framework’s
development.
- support the framework with participative democratic processes on AI’s wider impacts
on society and employment, building public legitimacy for major governance
decisions. See our Citizens’ Voice policy for more.

3. Own and benefit from our digital future
New Zealand should benefit from global advances in technology while building the skills and
capability to develop solutions of our own. Public purchasing can help create that
opportunity, connecting local innovation with public needs. The aim is better services and
long-term value, with security and accountability built in.

Use government purchasing to grow local innovation
A promising local product should have a fair opportunity to prove itself. Smaller contracts and
simpler processes can open doors for New Zealand businesses, while shared learning across
government can help successful trials grow without repeating mistakes.

Opportunity will:

- build on Government Procurement Rule 8 by increasing the minimum
economic-benefit weighting for AI procurement, splitting large AI contracts into
smaller lots where practical, and actively prioritising New Zealand suppliers for pilot
contracts under $100,000 — while complying with New Zealand's international trade
and procurement obligations.
- prioritise open-weight AI models suitable for local fine-tuning in public-sector
deployments where feasible.

- recognise Te Mana Raraunga's Māori Data Sovereignty Principles in public-sector AI
procurement.
- maintain a central register of public sector technology and their outcomes across
agencies, with clear responsibility for deciding which should be scaled.
- encourage and prioritise open-source solutions within government technology
procurement where the business case and operational overhead make sense.
- build on programmes in agriculture, health and social services, exporting small and
medium-sized businesses, and public services, each with a dedicated transformation
office and named targets.

Combine global access with trusted local capability
Building capability at home does not mean shutting out international innovation. We should
use our relationships overseas to access leading technology while ensuring that systems
used here meet clear expectations for governance and security. That balance supports both
economic opportunity and the ability to act in New Zealand’s interests.

Opportunity will:

- use New Zealand’s trade, security and science relationships, alongside our own
science investment, to secure reliable access to leading AI models for productivity
and national security.
- phase in requirements to comply with international AI governance standards (e.g. ISO
42001).
- protect New Zealand data sovereignty by requiring sensitive and restricted
government data to be hosted and processed in New Zealand where suitable
capability exists, and strengthen Privacy Act protection against foreign access to
New Zealanders’ personal information.
