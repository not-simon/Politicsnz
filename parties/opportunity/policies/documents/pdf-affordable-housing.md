Affordable Housing
A home is security and stability — the place we build good,
productive lives from.

But our housing market is broken, with houses unaffordable
for many, unhealthy for some, and home ownership out of
reach for generations of Kiwis. It’s time to make houses places
to live again, not just investment vehicles.

In addition to costing more than 7 times the median income, some of our homes are causing
a serious public health problem. New Zealand has some of the worst rates of respiratory
disease and breathing difficulties in the OECD, caused by decades of underinvestment and
weak building standards. One in five Kiwis are trapped in cold, damp, and mouldy homes,
directly fuelling thousands of preventable childhood hospital admissions and contributing
to an estimated 1,600 winter deaths every single year. That is a cost we can not afford.

We need to permanently narrow the gap between wages and house prices by stabilising the
market while we lift incomes and we have to build better homes, a lot faster. Opportunity’s
Affordable Housing policy presents a plan to do just that: Faster, Better, Cheaper.

1. Faster (Planning, Consenting & Systemic Pre‑Approval)
With over 1,000 disparate regional zones, developers face an administrative minefield.
Innovative pre-fabricated and off-site manufacturing methods are stalled by slow council
consent, localised design reviews, and endless Requests for Information. To build more and
better houses, Opportunity will:

- Build on the Planning Bill with a simplified set of only 10 national consistent zones.
These will cut red tape that holds back the higher density development we need, while
affording automatic protections for highly productive farmland, civic assets, heritage
buildings and nature reserves.
- Expand existing frameworks to ensure a truly centralised pre-approval system for
proven building products, off-site manufacturing (OSM), and modular pre-fabricated
systems. Once a system is certified at the national level, local territorial authorities
will be prohibited from issuing Requests for Information (RFIs) or requiring redundant
design reviews. This will reduce the need for building inspections, reducing costs and
delays.
- Modify the Unit Titles Act and other legislation to make it faster and easier to build a
more diverse mix of homes, including everything from home offices and shop fronts to
co-housing, housing co-ops, Papakāinga, and multi-generational homes.

2. Better (Quality, Climate & Social Incentives)
Our cold, damp, and mouldy housing stock triggers severe, chronic respiratory illnesses
like asthma, bronchiolitis, and rheumatic fever. This gives New Zealand some of the highest
hospital admission rates for lung disease in the developed world and places an immense,
preventable burden on our public healthcare system. To improve the quality of our housing
stock as well as expand it, Opportunity will:

- Expand the “Warmer Kiwi Homes” insulation and heating retrofit programme already
run very successfully by EECA to cover private rentals that meet other eligibility criteria.
Available retrofits will also expand to include, double glazing, solar and appliance
electrification. See our Abundant Energy policy for more.
- Refund 100% of the GST collected during construction directly back to the local
council for any new build that achieves strong environmental and build standards
(such as Homestar 6+ or Passive House frameworks with future ready EV or Solar
infrastructure). This revenue is ring-fenced to fund local infrastructure and expand
community housing. This has the added bonus of reducing costs at the local council
level, reducing pressure on rates.
- Raise the minimum standards in the existing Healthy Homes Standards for rental
properties over time.
- Transition the Building Code away from rigid compliance checklists and toward
performance-based energy modelling. This shifts the focus from minimum
compliance to actual performance, guaranteeing that all new builds remain healthy,
energy-efficient, and naturally self-cooling year-round without relying on expensive
mechanical air conditioning.

3. Cheaper (Finance and De-risking)
Residential construction is plagued by financial volatility, and has fallen well below demand.
When lending conditions tighten, private developers go under, skilled tradespeople flee to
Australia, and New Zealand is left with a chronic housing shortage.

Funding for community-led and affordable housing has traditionally been locked behind
a messy patchwork of disconnected government grants and loans. This lack of certainty
prevents Community Housing Providers (CHPs) from building multi-year development
pipelines, while centralised quotas fail to adapt to regional demands.

To expand the supply of affordable homes Opportunity will:

- Task Kāinga Ora with delivering a diverse mix of housing types tailored to regional
needs so that smaller towns can clear their housing waitlists entirely and ensure
everyone has a safe place to live.
- Take the ‘bust’ out of the residential construction sector with a ‘Crown Build
Guarantee’ to back reputable residential developments by providing pre-sale and
completion guarantees.
- Make the Housing Flexible Fund a permanent capital pipeline and upgrade it so that it
uses localised waitlist data to back projects that address specific regional deficits and
so that a dedicated share of the fund is reserved for affordable rentals, building the
crucial “missing middle” between state housing and private ownership.
- Turn local councils, Community Housing Providers and developers into active partners
in ending the housing shortage by expanding the Community Housing lending facility
to give councils, CHPs, iwi and reputable private builders direct access to low‑cost
Crown development finance when they are delivering affordable or social housing that
is clearly tailored to local communities’ needs
- Defer land value tax liabilities for land under development up to a limit of two years
and subject to interest payment. See our Tax Reset for more information on the Land
Value Tax.

How do national pre-approvals stop local council delays?
Currently, every builder has to prove the safety and compliance of their building systems
over and over again to every single local council across the country for each and every build,
leading to endless, repetitive Requests for Information (RFIs) and design reviews.
By scaling the use of systems such as the MultiProof framework, we take core design and
component certification out of the hands of local councils and handle it once at a national
level. This delivers two massive upgrades to our building system:

- Quality: By elevating complex technical sign-offs to a centralised national standard,
we ensure that innovative, high-performance building systems are evaluated by
the country’s top technical experts, not overstretched local council staff. Precision
manufacturing in a controlled factory environment inherently delivers superior quality,
tighter structural seals, and better thermal performance than building standard homes
on a muddy site out in the elements.
- Certainty: Currently a design approved in one district can be arbitrarily delayed
or rejected by a council inspector in another region. This unpredictability stops
companies from scaling up. National pre-approval gives off-site fabricators, banks, and
homebuyers certainty. If a system is certified once, it is legally valid everywhere. Banks
can lend with confidence, manufacturers can safely invest in advanced machinery, and
families get guaranteed, predictable move-in dates.

By restricting local councils to checking local site foundations and utility hookups, they
are barred from re-opening structural design files. This avoids months of administrative
delay, guarantees a higher-quality build, and provides the exact certainty the sector
needs to grow.

How are local councils incentivised to support housing under this plan?
Under the current system, local councils are penalised for growth; they have to fund
the expensive pipes, roads, and infrastructure for new housing, but central government
pockets all the GST and income tax revenue generated by that growth.

First, by refunding 100% of construction GST back to the local council for any build that
hits high environmental baselines, we provide local authorities with an immediate revenue
stream. This turns housing development from a financial liability into a powerful revenue
generator for local communities, allowing councils to comfortably invest in high-quality
social housing of their own.

Second, by giving councils low-cost development loans, we dramatically reduce their
financial risk.

How do the Citizens Income and Tax Reset change rent subsidies?
Under Opportunity’s Tax Reset, housing support is modernised to complement the financial
floor provided by the Citizens Income. Because renters benefit the most from the broader
tax reset, the structural need for emergency interventions will naturally decrease. However,
to ensure targeted housing stability, the Accommodation Supplement will be replaced by
the Housing Support Income (HSI) built on two fundamental pillars:

- Decoupled, Instant Support: HSI is available to everyone who rents, subject to a
raised income maximum that captures more middle-to-low-income Kiwis. To stop
landlords from swallowing the subsidy, renters only enter their address—not their rent
amount. Rates are set to regional maximums, breaking the direct link between state
support and rent inflation.
- Rental Bond Insurance: To eliminate the stressful cash deficits that occur when
moving, traditional cash bonds will be replaced by a low-cost, annual rental bond
insurance paid by the renter. Tenants will no longer have to wait to recover a cash bond
from a previous landlord just to fund a new one—a practice that forces many low-
income families into deep debt. This single shift drastically improves labour mobility
and reduces financial anxiety.

The Income-Related Rent Subsidy (IRRS) and Temporary Accommodation Support (TAS)
will be retained as essential, targeted backups. However, by removing the waiting lists and
approval friction through HSI, these systems will serve as a clean, efficient safety net rather
than a default crutch for a broken rental market.

Won’t lower house prices hurt everyday Kiwi homeowners?
Our goal isn’t to crash the market or penalise families who worked hard to buy a home,
especially those who bought at the peak of the market. Our goal is to restore balance.

For decades, runaway speculation has driven a massive, unsustainable wedge between
what everyday Kiwis earn and what a home costs. We need to remember what housing is
actually for: homes for people to live in, not an investment vehicle for people to retire on.

To fix this responsibly, we need to narrow that gap from both sides:

- Lifting Incomes and slowing growth: We want to see New Zealanders’ wages rise
faster while gently slowing down house price rises. House prices will still rise over time,
but by keeping that growth low and stable for several years, we allow real salaries to
catch up. This narrows the affordability gap without destabilising the economy.
- Rewarding work over speculation: Our broader Tax Reset is specifically designed
to cushion this transition. By introducing a Land Value Tax (LVT) to shift speculative
capital out of empty land, we are simultaneously delivering significant income tax cuts
along with our universal Citizens Income. This means the average working family will
keep substantially more of their hard-earned pay packet every single week, boosting
household financial security.
- Lowering the cost to build: Alongside our Next Economy policies, our focus is on
stripping out the unnecessary bureaucratic costs of delivering new housing. By

What is the Housing Flexible Fund?
The Housing Flexible Fund is currently a year to year “trial” contestable fund designed to
support delivery of social housing and affordable rentals.

We are upgrading this fund to provide long-term, predictable financing for affordable
rentals, urban equity-share co-operatives, and whānau-led papakāinga. Turning this into
a permanent fixture allows Community Housing Providers (CHPs) and iwi to plan multi-
year build pipelines with absolute financial certainty, rather than surviving year to year on
disconnected government grants.

What is a Crown Build Guarantee and how does it work?
Residential construction in New Zealand is trapped in a boom-bust cycle. When the
economy slows down, banks tighten lending, private buyers pull back, developers go under,
and our skilled tradespeople flee to Australia. When the market recovers, we are left with a
severe housing shortage and no workforce left to build.

The Crown Build Guarantee permanently takes the “bust” out of the sector by using the
government’s balance sheet to underwrite reputable residential developments. The
Crown provides pre-sale and completion guarantees. By ensuring that these homes will
be finished and purchased, banks can safely lend to developers even during an economic
downturn. This keeps hammers swinging, maintains a steady supply of houses, and
protects our construction workforce from downsizing.

If the government steps in during a market bust, what is the mechanism for
stepping back during a boom?
The Crown Build Guarantee is designed to act as an automatic economic stabiliser, it scales
down naturally when the private market is healthy.

The guarantee is only triggered when private market pre-sales drop below the threshold
required for a project to secure commercial bank financing. During a housing boom, private
buyers are plentiful, developments hit their pre-sale targets easily on the open market, and
the Crown’s underwriting framework remains completely untouched. The government isn’t
replacing the private sector; we are simply setting a structural floor to ensure construction
never grinds to a halt.

How do national pre-approvals and off-site assembly actually lower costs and
drive productivity?
The New Zealand construction sector has a productivity problem because we treat almost
every house like a bespoke project, built outside in the rain, the same way we have for
hundreds of years.

By combining national pre-approvals with Off-Site Manufacturing (OSM), we unlock true
industrial scale:

- Time is money: Standardized pre-approvals mean that once a factory-built design or
modular component is certified nationally, local councils are barred from reopening
design files or triggering endless Requests for Information (RFIs). This slashes months
off the consenting timeline.
- Factory precision: Building in a controlled environment reduces material waste,
avoids weather delays, and guarantees tighter structural seals and superior thermal
performance.
- Sector certainty: When manufacturers know a design is legally valid anywhere in
New Zealand, they can confidently invest in advanced automation and machinery,
permanently driving down the per-square-meter cost of building.
