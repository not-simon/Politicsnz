Future-Fit Education
Every child deserves a world-class education: safe, stable,
coherent and designed to prepare them not just for work but
for life.

New Zealand’s education system is capable of excellence. But students, families and
educators have been repeatedly subjected to political churn. Governments rewrite
curricula, restructure qualifications and reorganise institutions before previous
changes have been properly implemented or evaluated. School operational budgets
haven’t kept up with inflation, creating further strain for teachers and principals.

Education is a compounding investment in our collective future. Uncertainty and
destabilisation not only increases workload, it detracts focus from what matters:
excellent teaching and learning.

A plan for kids’ futures, not an election cycle

New Zealand’s education system has been treated as a political football for too long.
Politicians are not the right people to be making operational decisions that should
be left to the profession, and the three-yearly election cycle is too frequent to make
sweeping changes.

A ten-year, cross-party plan for education
Opportunity will work across Parliament to secure a ten-year, cross-party plan for
education that provides stable direction across early learning, primary and secondary
education.

It will:

- be developed with educators, tangata whenua, learners, families and
communities,
- support the rights of learners and follow the best available evidence,
- set clear national goals, an agreed programme of work, realistic implementation
timeframes, workforce and funding requirements, and regular independent
evaluation,
- be grounded in Te Tiriti o Waitangi, meaning genuine partnership with iwi and
Māori communities in the development of the plan with a focus on equitable
access, participation and achievement, and

An Independent Curriculum and Assessment Council
To support the implementation of the ten-year plan, Opportunity supports the
establishment of an Independent Curriculum and Assessment Council to steward
the long-term curriculum and assessment direction for early childhood, primary and
secondary education. The Council will maintain consistency across electoral cycles
and will be given explicit structural protections to preserve professional and technical
independence.

A strong and trusted teaching profession
A supported profession is the foundation of a strong education system. Teacher
shortages are not simply a recruitment problem, but a retention one due to workload
pressures, under-preparation, and inadequate support. Research from NZPF, NZEI
and the OECD shows that New Zealand teachers feel undervalued and politicised
leading to lower job satisfaction and greater intent to leave their roles.

Opportunity will advocate for the long-term plan to:

- Allocate clear responsibility to deliver the 10 year plan in terms of workforce
planning.
- Strengthen initial teacher education by setting higher entry requirements;
establishing a rigorous exit practicum assessment; and funding structured, high-
calibre placements in schools and early childhood centres that follow a staged
progression, integrate theory and practice, and are supported by trained and paid
mentors.
- Fund ongoing professional development, including in-school induction and
peer-to-peer mentoring, leadership development, trauma-informed and
neurodiversity-affirming practice, behaviour and complex‑needs capability,
cultural and reo Māori competency and Te Tiriti-informed practice, and curriculum
and assessment literacy.
- Extend Te Whāriki, the early childhood curriculum, as a complement to the school
curriculum and encourage the inclusion of early childhood-trained teachers in
primary schools to ease the transition for children.
- Restore an Independent Teaching Council with democratically-elected
practitioner representation.
- Strengthen regional and rural networks by tailoring support to each region’s
needs, including clustering where it makes sense, deploying teacher and
specialist capability equitably across schools, establishing regional relief pools
for rural and small schools, and providing administrative support.
- Ensure recent investments in specialist support are delivered well - including
learning support co-ordinators, RTLBs, speech-language therapists,
psychologists and mental health services available to every school.

Achievement for the 21st Century
Education is more than workforce preparation; it shapes the societies we build in an
increasingly complex world. Achievement today requires learners to develop strong
knowledge, apply it confidently and build the capability to act with agency, to think
critically and creatively, to act ethically and responsibly, to navigate complexity, and
to be active citizens in their community. Our schools and learners need a coherent
framework that gives teachers clarity and flexibility to use effective, evidence‑based
practice, while creating the conditions for deep understanding, growing learner
agency and achieving excellence.

Opportunity will advocate for the long-term plan to:

- Empower the Curriculum and Assessment Council to review and decide on the
government’s replacement of NCEA with NZCE/NZACE.
- Promote purposeful technology use in schools, providing guidance for parents
and teachers for healthy screen use, online safety, critical evaluation of digital
information and AI, and the ethical decision-making skills to use it responsibly,
while reducing passive screen-based learning.
- Expand the availability of te reo Māori learning by rewarding and retaining
specialist teachers and enabling more flexible delivery across multiple schools,
within communities of learning and using distance-learning platforms.
- Build a resilient tertiary sector through a 20-year Skills and Learning Accord,
seamless pathways between vocational and academic learning, restored Adult
and Community Education, stable and sustainable funding, coherent national
strategies, and investment in research capability for universities, wānanga, and
polytechs - so institutions can plan, not just survive.
- Provide regional polytechnic stability — long‑term funding and mission clarity
for regional polytechnics and wānanga, ensuring learners outside major centres
have access to high‑quality vocational and academic pathways. See our
Breakthrough Economy policy for more detail.

Early learning that prioritises children
Every child has a right to the strongest possible start in life and parents deserve
early childhood education and care options they can trust to keep their children safe,
happy and thriving.

New Zealand’s ECCE system has drifted away from what children actually need.
Teachers report high burnout and turnover, impacting their relationships with
children. Ratios in many centres remain stretched. In a 2023 sector survey, close to
half of the early childhood teachers who responded said ratio requirements had been
breached at their centre in the past year, and one in eight said it happened often or
constantly.

Opportunity will advocate for the long-term plan to:

- Lift quality and safety standards by:
- Requiring minimum ratios of 1:3 for under-twos, 1:5 for 24-36 months and 1:8
for children aged three and over.
- Improving space per child indoors and outdoors to match Australian
standards.
- Setting maximum indoor group sizes.
- Requiring 80% minimum qualifications for teacher-led services.
- Expanding the NZCER Wellbeing@School survey to ECCE teachers.
- Move away from the current licensing-only model to quality-based contracting
to ensure public money delivers the best possible outcomes for children and
families.
- Support community-owned early learning to provide more choice to parents, by:
- Prioritising increased core funding for community-owned services such as
Playcentre, Kōhanga Reo, Kindergartens, Pacific language nests, Playgroups
and Nature Based Services.
- Enabling more flexibility for ECCE centres to co-locate with retirement
villages and support mana whenua partnerships.
- Supporting home-based care to implement the 2:8 model (two educators,
including at least one qualified teacher, for up to eight children),
complementing the existing 1:4 model.

Future-Fit Education — Frequently Asked
Questions
How do you make a 10-year education plan stick?
Opportunity will use our position as a party that can work across the aisle to bring
other parties together. We will be open with the public about who comes to the table
and who doesn’t. We believe there is enough public support for this approach that
parties will be incentivised to take part, and approaches like this have worked in other
countries like Denmark, Estonia and Scotland.

How much will this policy cost?
A large part of this policy is governance and regulatory change that imposes no direct
cost. For the elements of this policy that do require additional funding Opportunity
will advocate for that within the context of the ten-year plan.

Do you support the Foundational Award, NZCE and NZACE being implemented
from 2028?
That’s not our call to make, and it shouldn’t be any politician’s. Politicians have
been in the weeds of curriculum design for far too long. The decision on the new
qualifications should sit with an independent Curriculum and Assessment Council.

Will you fix teacher pay?
We will advocate for including teacher workforce planning inside the ten-year plan, so
training intakes, supervision, career pathways and fair remuneration are worked out
as part of a ten-year pathway instead of bid for one Budget at a time.
