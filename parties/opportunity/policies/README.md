## Community & inclusion

### Support the online safety bill on a potential under-16 social media ban to select committee (POL54783)

**Why** (*policy.nz*): This party says that it is concerned about the effects of social media on those under 16, and supports a discussion on whether to impose a ban. However, it adds that an age restriction is not a substitute for stronger obligations on social media companies to address digital harm.

**How** (*policy.nz*): This party would support National’s online safety bill at first reading so it can be properly tested at select committee. It will wait for the select committee to examine the evidence before taking a position on the effectiveness of a social media ban for young people.

Full text: ['Opportunity to support online safety bill to select committee', 25 August 2026 (accessed 28 August 2026)](documents/web-opportunity-to-support-online-safety-bill-to-select-committee.md) ([original](https://www.opportunity.org.nz/opportunity_to_support_online_safety_bill_to_select_committee))

### Bring in tougher rules around tobacco, vaping, alcohol, and unhealthy food (POL54885)

**Why** (*policy.nz*): This party says that tobacco, alcohol and unhealthy food account for roughly a quarter of all the healthy years New Zealanders lose to death and disability. Prevention receives a small fraction of health spending relative to the share of health loss these risks cause.

**How** (*policy.nz*): This party would work to reduce the harm from tobacco, as well as alcohol and unhealthy food, as part of a 10-year health plan. This would include a renewed Smoke-free New Zealand programme, with fewer places selling tobacco, and mandated de-nicotinisation of smoked tobacco. 
Quit programmes and regulated alternatives for adult smokers would be better funded, and there would be stronger protections against youth access to vapes.

Full text: ['Healthy People ', undated (accessed 19 August 2026)](documents/web-healthy-people.md) ([original](https://www.opportunity.org.nz/healthy_people))

### Restore pay equity deals for care and support workers (POL55265)

**Why** (*policy.nz*): This party says that recent law changes cancelled many active pay equity claims. This included disability care and support workers, and experts warn this will make it harder to hire and keep workers, cause staff shortages, and lower the quality of care.

**How** (*policy.nz*): This party would re-instate rules from the Equal Pay Amendment Act 2020 to ensure care and support workers get fairer pay and recognise their skills, helping to create a stable, long-lasting care workforce.

### Improved respite and carer support for the disabled (POL55267)

**Why** (*policy.nz*): This party says accessibility and care is a growing issue. One in six Kiwis have a disability and if ageing-related access issues are factored in, 1 in 4 Kiwis will have accessibility needs in their lifetimes. Everyone should be able to participate in society with dignity and make the most of opportunities. However, the current Disability Support Services Bill did not properly consult with those it affects, and fails to give paid and unpaid carers the support they deserve.

**How** (*policy.nz*): This party would look to improve disability policy, including good care plans for disabled adults as their family carers age. It would back pay equity for care and support workers. It would also seek more support for unpaid carers, with improved respite care and more flexibility to balance their employment and unpaid caring roles.

### Oppose disabled people being paid less than minimum wage (POL55269)

**Why** (*policy.nz*): This party says that many disabled people lose benefit income if they work and some are even legally paid less than the minimum wage. One in six Kiwis are disabled and when ageing-related access issues are factored in, one in four Kiwis will face access issues in their lifetimes. So access to care and income are key issues for those with disabilities.

**How** (*policy.nz*): This party would work to end rules that allow disabled people to be paid less than the minimum wage. It would improve support for employers to recruit and retain disabled workers. This party argues its Citizens’ Income and Disability Allowance would ensure disabled people would not lose income support for doing paid work.

### Ensure better connections between government systems and those with a disability (POL55271)

**Why** (*policy.nz*): This party says people with long-term disabilities often find themselves endlessly repeating their story or having to prove their disability to authorities. Or they may fall through the cracks if their health, education, ACC and disability support systems are not strongly connected.

**How** (*policy.nz*): This party would back a "Tell Us Once" system, which would mean those with long-term disability support needs would not have to repeat their story or have tests over and over. Privacy would be protected and they would have control over how their information was shared. They would be supported to navigate the various government agencies that are connected to their case.

## Economy

### Increase innovation by lifting science funding to 2 percent of GDP (POL54779)

**Why** (*policy.nz*): This party says that economic growth is slowing and new ideas are needed in the areas of science and technology.

**How** (*policy.nz*): This party would increase innovation by lifting science funding to 2 percent of GDP. It would also turn polytechs into regional innovation hubs, offer incentives to invest in technology and beef up proven accelerator programmes. The wider innovation plan would cost an additional $1.33bn but create $4bn revenue annually.

### Enforce and fund a 30-year infrastructure plan (POL54785)

**Why** (*policy.nz*): This party says that short-term political cycles have led to wastage and a lack of planning for long-term infrastructure.  It notes New Zealand compares badly against most OECD countries for maintaining its existing assets. Te Waihanga, the Infrastructure Commission, could be given a greater role to ensure that major projects continue between governments.

**How** (*policy.nz*): This party would lock into law the 30-year National Infrastructure Plan published by Te Waihanga, which would also be put in charge of the funding and delivery of projects. Major projects, where the government is investing over $250m, would require a bill through Parliament to cancel. This policy would cost around $27 million annually for the enhanced infrastructure commission and ties in with a $150m free public transport policy and a $60bn infrastructure capital fund.

Full text: ['Intergenerational Infrastructure' (PDF), undated (accessed 11 August 2026)](https://cdn.opportunity.org.nz/documents/policy/intergenerational-infrastructure.pdf)

### Give councils more tools to invest in infrastructure (POL54811)

**Why** (*policy.nz*): This party says local authorities have been forced to rely almost entirely on property rates to service ageing infrastructure under growing demand. New tools are needed to help them maintain these assets and build new ones.

**How** (*policy.nz*): This party would ensure councils had access to more tools such as congestion charges, value capture and accommodation levies and user-pays charges to help fund infrastructure. Funds would also be available through housing funds, climate resilience funds and council-specific loans.  A 30 percent cap on the Uniform Annual General Charge would also be repealed. 
Council spending would be overseen by an expanded Infrastructure Commission.
Other sources of funding could include a Local Government Regeneration Fund, a new NZ Infrastructure Fund and a full-scale Ratepayer Assistance Scheme. All three funds would be covered by a $60bn infrastructure capital fund.

Full text: ['Intergenerational Infrastructure' (PDF), undated (accessed 11 August 2026)](https://cdn.opportunity.org.nz/documents/policy/intergenerational-infrastructure.pdf)

### Give the Commerce Commission greater powers and more market support to small businesses (POL54824)

**Why** (*policy.nz*): This party says consumers are paying too much because of a lack of market competition in sectors such as the supermarkets and banking. It says small business also need more business support to be competitive and keep up with technology.

**How** (*policy.nz*): This party would give the Commerce Commission greater powers to investigate, intervene and force structural separation of anti-competitive businesses across the economy. It would also boost R&D tax credits for small businesses and offer budgeting help. It would also ensure fairness for small businesses in the ACC and retail payment systems. This is part of a wider innovation plan which would cost an additional $1.33bn but create $4 billion revenue annually.

### Invest in sustainable ocean industries beyond fishing (POL54826)

**Why** (*policy.nz*): This party says that our seas are hitting their ecological and economic limits. Fish stocks are depleted, critical habitats are unprotected and just 0.5 percent of NZ’s extensive ocean territory is protected as marine reserve. A strong "blue economy" requires investment and different management.

**How** (*policy.nz*): This party would invest in sustainable aquaculture, marine research and tourism initiatives, with the aim of creating $500m per year in additional “blue economy” revenue. The party's wider ocean policy also addresses fisheries management, expanding NZ's marine reserves and protecting coastal habitats. The short-term cost of these policies is expected to be no more than $100m a year, some of which would be recovered from the commercial fishing industry.

**Why** (*policy.nz*): This party says that the seas are hitting their ecological and economic limits. Fish species are being depleted under the current quota management system, and critical habitats are unprotected. Just 0.5 percent of NZ’s extensive ocean territory is protected as a marine reserve.

### Rewrite gene technology regulations (POL54842)

**Why** (*policy.nz*): This party says that there is broad agreement across science, industry, and government that the current gene technology framework is no longer fit for purpose. However, the current bill was developed without proper public consultation, leaving critical questions around trade, liability and environmental risk. The bill includes no clear statement obligating the regulator to protect people and the environment and no full regulatory impact statement. Left as it is, the party says the bill could create one of the most liberal gene technology regimes in the world.

**How** (*policy.nz*): This party would not support the Gene Technology Bill in its current form, but believes it is possible to get the regulation right. It favours full industry and public consultation and backs a modern framework, based on independent expert assessment, case-by-case evaluation and accountability. It calls for a national conversation on gene technology.

Full text: ['Gene Technology Policy', undated (accessed 19 August 2026)](documents/web-gene-technology-policy.md) ([original](https://www.opportunity.org.nz/gene_technology_policy))

### Put 60 percent of future infrastructure spend on maintenance and renewal (POL54874)

**Why** (*policy.nz*): This party says that says New Zealand runs 50-year assets on three-year political cycles, resulting in billions of dollars of wasted money. It also says the number of government agencies that cannot list the assets they own is concerning.

**How** (*policy.nz*): This party would put the Infrastructure Commission's 30-year National Infrastructure Plan into law, with a heavy focus on maintenance and infrastructure finance. Sixty percent of the future infrastructure spend would go towards maintenance and renewal. There would also be  greater accountability, with a register of public assets, improvements to procurement and project planning skills across government departments, and scorecards which would show the progress towards infrastructure at 100 days, 12 month and three year intervals. 
The Infrastructure Commission would have a much greater role, costing another $27m annually.

### Raise up to $60b to finance infrastructure (POL54875)

**Why** (*policy.nz*): This party says that NZ's government debt is around 50 percent, about half of the average of the other OECD countries. It believes NZ has room to borrow carefully for assets that repay us over lifetimes. It adds that deferred maintenance is borrowing from our children.

**How** (*policy.nz*): This party would raise up to $60b over ten years through a new Infrastructure Fund, with interest costs under 1 percent of GDP. It would borrow at Crown rates instead of costly private finance, and issue bonds that KiwiSaver funds can invest in. It will also back councils with grants, not just loans, plus new funding tools so those who benefit contribute and ratepayers don't carry the load.

## Education

### Forgive student loan interest for returning Kiwis (POL54852)

**Why** (*policy.nz*): This party says our economy needs new ideas and science, technology and innovation are traditional sources for fresh thinking. The country needs young talented Kiwis who are currently offshore to bring their experience and skills back to help drive the economy.

**How** (*policy.nz*): This party would roll back the student loan interest of returning Kiwis on up to 3 years spent overseas. This would apply to both newly graduates and those currently overseas. The rollback would be applied at the end of their loan repayment, and conditional on being resident in New Zealand when the loan is repaid. This policy would cost an estimated $95m.

### Create a 10-year education plan (POL54860)

**Why** (*policy.nz*): This party says that New Zealand's education system is capable of excellence, but it has since become politicised and student results have fallen. Schools need more funding, and teachers face high workloads, poor pay and workforce planning issues. A plan is needed where curriculum and assessment design is not set by politicians, and students are better prepared to learn in new ways.

**How** (*policy.nz*): This party would create a 10-year cross-party education plan. It would include an independent council to oversee curriculum and assessment design, including a review of the government’s replacement of NCEA with NZCE/NZACE. It would also promote the purposeful use of technology and expand access to te reo Māori learning. The costs would be spread over 10 years, and many parts of the plan would not require extra funding.

## Environment

### Phase out inshore bottom-trawling by 2030 and make fishing more sustainable (POL54776)

**Why** (*policy.nz*): This party says that our seas are hitting their ecological and economic limits. Fish species are being depleted under the current quota management system, and critical habitats are unprotected. Just 0.5% of NZ’s extensive ocean territory is protected as a marine reserve.

**How** (*policy.nz*): This party would change the fisheries management system and make it more transparent. This includes the phasing out bottom trawling in all inshore areas by 2030 and installing cameras on commercial fishing boats. It would also move away from single-species quotas towards an ecosystem-based approach, and raise the baseline for sustainable fish stocks. 
In the short-term, the party says its wider oceans policy would cost no more than $100m a year, with some costs recovered from the commercial fishing industry.

### Retain the Climate Change Commission (POL54782)

**Why** (*policy.nz*): This party says New Zealand has solar, wind, hydro and geothermal resources but continues to rely too heavily on fossil fuels. It says increasing renewable energy and promoting the electrification household and industry electrification would have many benefits such as increased GDP, lower carbon emissions, smaller power bills and 5,000 new jobs over the next 30 years.

**How** (*policy.nz*): This party would incentivise the electrification of industries and housing. Low-interest loans and funding would be made available for households, community energy projects and public transport.  This would include an expansion of EECA’s Warmer Kiwi Homes subsidies ($80m annually) for household solar and electricity initiatives, and low-interest electrification loans via the national Ratepayer Assistance Scheme.

### Increase the energy grid up to 30GW by 2050 (POL54807)

**Why** (*policy.nz*): This party says New Zealand has solar, wind, hydro and geothermal resources but continues to rely too heavily on fossil fuels. Increasing renewable energy and promoting the electrification of households and industries would have many benefits such as increased GDP, lower carbon emissions, smaller power bills and 5,000 new jobs over the next 30 years.

**How** (*policy.nz*): This party would push for cross-party agreement to boost renewable energy by adding 19GW of new renewable generation capacity to the grid by 2050, bringing it up to 30GW. It would also launch a capacity investment scheme like Australia's to unlock capital and encourage industries to electrify their processes. The party's full energy policy would be funded through ringfenced government dividends from electricity generators.

### Offer incentives to businesses to buy electric vehicles (POL54876)

**Why** (*policy.nz*): This party says encouraging businesses to buy electric vehicles will reduce their ongoing fuel costs and make them more resilient to volatile prices. As businesses are big buyers of new vehicles, this would also increase the pool of EVs in the secondhand market.

**How** (*policy.nz*): This party would allow accelerated depreciation and fringe benefit tax exemptions for business electric vehicles worth up to $50,000 (or $75,000 for utes and vans).
The fringe benefit tax exemption is expected to cost $31m a year, growing to $155m as uptake increases. 
The impact of accelerated depreciation will start at about $95m a year, but much of it will be recovered as depreciation timing unwinds. Both will be funded from ringfenced dividends from state gentailers.

### Help industries decarbonise with a new fund (POL54900)

**Why** (*policy.nz*): This party says that electrification of industry is being blocked by the cost of plant capital, grid connection and network upgrades. Industry has been hampered by a fall in gas reserves and the scrapping of GIDI, a government industry decarbonisation fund. A replacement loan guarantee scheme has fallen short because it cannot overcome the upfront cost, debt constraints and investment risks. This party says money collected from selling carbon units should be used to fight climate pollution, not disappear into the government's consolidated fund.

**How** (*policy.nz*): This party would establish a ring-fenced Industry Energy Resilience Fund (IERF) which would cover capital and upgrade costs. The fund will be capitalised by a $1b green bond raised against forecast ETS auction revenues over the next decade. 
IERF will offer a greater mix of instruments than GIDI , with grants reserved for smaller targeted uses. 
Money collected from selling carbon units would be used to fight climate change, putting it into IERF by law so businesses could plan ahead.

### Use the money received from stakes in gentailers to fund renewable energy (POL55049)

**Why** (*policy.nz*): This party says that New Zealand has solar, wind, hydro and geothermal resources but continues to rely too heavily on fossil fuels. It says increasing renewable energy and promoting the electrification household and industry electrification would have many benefits. The current electricity system is unfair; renewable energy generation has been underfunded and created expensive power bills for individuals and businesses. The government has a majority stake in several major power companies and is incentivised to keep prices high.

**How** (*policy.nz*): This party would add 30 gigawatts of renewable energy to the grid by 2050. This ambitious target will require building just over 556 megawatts of electricity generation to the country every year. The government currently receives approximately $500m from stakes in the electricity gentailers each year. This money would be ringfenced for investment in generation so that there is no incentive to maintain high prices. Electricity distributors would be consolidated and all electricity regulation would fall under one regulator. This party estimates that this policy would cost 500 m.
