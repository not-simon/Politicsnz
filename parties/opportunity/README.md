## History

**Gareth Morgan (2016–17).** Economist and philanthropist Gareth Morgan launched TOP in November 2016 as a party of
"evidence-based policy" in the political centre, intended to be able to work with either side. Chief of staff Geoff Simmons came third (4.56%) in the February 2017 Mount Albert by-election. In
the 2017 campaign TOP unsuccessfully sued TVNZ to join the debates, and Morgan drew criticism for calling Jacinda
Ardern "lipstick on a pig" and for remarks about the death of her cat; when candidate Jenny Condie criticised the
party culture, Morgan told her to resign. TOP won **2.44%** (63,261 votes) — the best result of any party outside
Parliament that year. Morgan stood down in December 2017, and in July 2018 briefly announced the party would deregister
before reversing course. [1][2]

**Geoff Simmons (2018–20).** Simmons was elected leader in December 2018 and relaunched the brand in October 2019
(UBI, environment, housing, "break the Labour/National duopoly"). TOP targeted Ōhāriu (Jessica Hammond Doube, third with
4,443 votes); Simmons came fourth in Rongotai. Result **1.51%** (43,449). Simmons resigned in November 2020. [1][3]

**Raf Manji (2022–23).** Former Christchurch city councillor Raf Manji — who had run second as an independent in Ilam in
2017 — became leader on 27 January 2022. TOP won its first elected office (Featherston Community Board) in October
2022. Its 2023 strategy was to win **Ilam**: Manji came second with 10,863 votes to National's Hamish Campbell (18,693).
Party vote **2.22%** (63,344). Manji resigned in December 2023; in August 2025 the party advertised the leader's job on
SEEK. [1][4][5]

**Qiulae Wong and the "Opportunity" rebrand (2025–26).** In November 2025 the party chose Qiulae "Q" Wong — formerly a
sustainable-business adviser at KPMG and NZ director of B Lab — as leader and rebranded as the Opportunity Party (the
Electoral Commission notified the name change in January 2026). [1][6]

## 2026 campaign summary

- **Pitch:** a means-tested **citizen's income** (about $370/week for almost all adults, not available above
  $350,000 income) replacing most benefits *and* NZ Superannuation, funded by a **land value tax**; compulsory
  KiwiSaver; **citizens' assemblies** ("Citizen's Voice"); a ban on **bottom trawling**; competition reforms and a
  stronger Commerce Commission; forgiving student-loan interest to draw emigrants home; lobbying regulation (register,
  one-year cooling-off) and a **$30,000 per-donor cap**; a methane emissions trading scheme and $5 billion for climate
  adaptation. On 30 September Wong named five "non-negotiables": a ten-year health plan, lobbying reform, a bottom-trawling
  ban, attacking monopolies/duopolies, and stronger climate commitments. [1][7][8]
- **Coalition stance:** open to either side, negotiating first with the largest party; Wong later said TOP would not
  support a government with Winston Peters as foreign minister. On **27 July 2026 National ruled out working with
  Opportunity** ("We wouldn't be in business with people who want to increase taxes" — Christopher Luxon). [1][9]
- **Polling:** TOP polled above 5% for the first time in 2026, peaking at 9.5% (Roy Morgan, August). October 2026
  polls: 7% and 8% (1 News–Verian), 5.4% (Freshwater Strategy; Anacta), 4.8% (Taxpayers' Union–Curia). It was the only
  extra-parliamentary party to qualify for TVNZ's multi-party debate. A Talbot Mills analysis (Jul–Aug 2026) found
  roughly a third of its supporters came from the left, a third from the right and a quarter had voted TOP in 2023. [1][10]
- **Money:** $671,061 in declared donations 2025–Oct 2026 (incl. $180,000 from Alan Wilderland, $100,000 from Brian
  Cartmell, $50,000 from Phillip Mills); broadcasting allocation $114,179 (amended to $118,755). [11]
- **List (top 10 of 40):** 1 Qiulae Wong (Mount Albert), 2 Daniel Eb (Kaipara ki Mahurangi), 3 Jessica Hammond
  (Wellington North), 4 Kayla Kingdon-Bebb (Wellington Bays), 5 Eden Skipper (Christchurch Central), 6 Jodie Kuntzsch
  (Nelson), 7 Sarah Lucas (New Plymouth), 8 David Bainbridge-Zafar (Dunedin), 9 Holly Knill (North Shore), 10 Pāpā
  Wharewera (Waiariki). Announced 31 July 2026. [12] People: [Qiulae Wong](people/qiulae-wong/),
  [Jessica Hammond](people/jessica-hammond/), [Daniel Eb](people/daniel-eb/).

## Notable events / controversies

- Morgan's 2017 "lipstick on a pig" tweet and the Condie episode; a splinter ("Next Big Thing") formed by two
  candidates in December 2017. [1][2]
- 2020: Auckland Central candidate Joshua Love left after a dispute over his conduct and Electoral Act compliance. [1]
- Critics have called the $370/week citizen's income a "poverty-level payment". [1]
