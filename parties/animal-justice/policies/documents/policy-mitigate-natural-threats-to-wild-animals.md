# Mitigate natural threats to wild animals

### Web page text

AJP recognises that wild animals often suffer from causes of natural origin. In many cases, intervention can be appropriate to protect wild animals from threats that they are not able to cope with.

There are many examples today of people and conservation agencies intervening to protect particularly endangered or charismatic species. For example, stranded whales or dolphins are often kept wet and efforts are made to refloat them; lost penguins are transported back to Antarctica; underweight yellow-eyed penguin/hoihoi chicks have been hand-reared until they can survive at sea; and following cyclone Gabrielle, wildlife stranded in mud and floodwaters were rescued. However, non-threatened and less charismatic species often suffer from similar threats. AJP wishes to see these existing efforts increased and expanded to other species. Research is also needed to understand the welfare of wild animals and how their flourishing can be ensured and legally protected.

**Key Objectives:**

1. Promote wild animal welfare science to better understand the lives of wild animals and how their flourishing and protection can be promoted.

2. Redefine the functions of the Ministry of the Environment to include the promotion of wild animal welfare.

3. Encourage other government and community agencies and local councils to consider the impacts of their policies on wild animal welfare where appropriate.

**Interim Measures:**

4. Establish protocols in local councils and environmental bodies for interventions to protect wild animals in the event of natural disasters.

5. Support volunteer groups that protect wild animals from natural threats.

6. Increase interventions to protect endangered species from natural threats.

7. Establishment of a national research institute for the study of wild animal welfare and the design and implementation of impact assessments and protection strategies.

Wild animals also suffer frequently from less cataclysmic events. Many suffer from
starvation, disease, harsh weather, or threats from other species or members of their
own species.9 While these threats are more challenging to address without disrupting
ecosystems, the development of wild animal welfare science10 will help us to understand
these problems and may lead to better ways to promote the flourishing, and protection,
of wild animals in the future.
AJP is committed to developing a comprehensive approach to protecting all wild
animals from natural threats. We will encourage interventions during natural disasters,
promote scientific research into wild animal welfare, and support volunteer rescue
groups. Our policy aims to expand protection beyond endangered species to include all
animals affected by natural threats, while establishing a national research institute
dedicated to understanding and improving the lives of wild animals throughout
Aotearoa.

Key Objectives:

1. Promote wild animal welfare science to better understand the lives of wild

promotion of wild animal welfare.

consider the impacts of their policies on wild animal welfare where appropriate.
