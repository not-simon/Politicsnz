### Web page text

AJP supports greater protection and restoration of natural habitats and ecosystems. We recognise that the destruction or degradation of habitats and ecosystems causes direct harm to wild animals, and that both humans and wild animals are sustained by natural habitats and ecosystems.

63% of Aotearoa’s land area has now been converted for human use, with animal agriculture being a primary historical cause of habitat destruction. By 1930, 39% of New Zealand’s land area had been cleared and transformed to make way for pastoral farming. This transformation of land involved destroying forests, draining wetlands and disrupting waterways, causing significant harm to natural ecosystems and free living animals, whose habitats were obliterated or disrupted. As of 2021, this remains largely unaddressed, with 42% of land converted for farming and 8% converted for forestry. In order to reverse this process, AJP would enable greater protection of native ecosystems along with the restoration of ecosystems, especially in areas currently used for animal agriculture. Pastoral farming is a highly inefficient use of land for food production, using far more land compared to other crops, but producing a fraction of the calories. Moving towards a plant-based food system and away from animal agriculture would allow us to free up significant areas of land to re-wild.

**Key Objectives:**

1. **Restrict** further habitat destruction, of both terrestrial and aquatic ecosystems.

2. **Ban** bottom trawling

3. **Restrict** seabed mining

4. **Redirect** funds away from animal agriculture, through taxes and regulations, and towards plant-based agriculture and habitat restoration projects and practices, as outlined in our ‘*Introduce tighter standards and enforcement for agricultural pollution’ and ‘Provide financial incentives for good environmental practices’ sub-policies under Objective 2 in our ‘Just Transition to a Plant-Based Economy’ policy*. This redirection of funds would enable: \
a. Reserves: Stronger protection of, and creation of more, land and marine reserves (including more Highly Protected Marine Areas) \
b. Restoration: Roll out restoration projects to restore habitats, including mangroves, peat bogs, forests, marine habitats and other natural ecosystems. Subsidise farmers and landowners actively restoring the land and associated ecosystems. \
c. Subsidise green community projects \
d. Create wildlife corridors (link to Objective 3: *Protect Wildlife From Anthropogenic Harms*)

5. **Implement** educational programmes in schools, universities, and communities to promote awareness of the importance of living within our ecological limits, for the purpose of avoiding, remedying and mitigating the destruction of natural environments in which animals live. *See Compassionate Education policy.*

Protect and restore natural habitats
AJP supports greater protection and restoration of natural habitats and ecosystems. We
recognise that the destruction or degradation of habitats and ecosystems causes direct
harm to wild animals, and that both humans and wild animals are sustained by natural
habitats and ecosystems.
63% of Aotearoa’s land area has now been converted for human use, with animal
agriculture being a primary historical cause of habitat destruction.1 By 1930, 39% of
New Zealand’s land area had been cleared and transformed to make way for pastoral
farming.2 This transformation of land involved destroying forests, draining wetlands and
disrupting waterways, causing significant harm to natural ecosystems and free living
animals, whose habitats were obliterated or disrupted. As of 2021, this remains largely
unaddressed, with 42% of land converted for farming and 8% converted for forestry.3 In
order to reverse this process, AJP would enable greater protection of native ecosystems
along with the restoration of ecosystems, especially in areas currently used for animal
agriculture. Pastoral farming is a highly inefficient use of land for food production,4 using
far more land compared to other crops, but producing a fraction of the calories. Moving
towards a plant-based food system and away from animal agriculture would allow us to
free up significant areas of land to re-wild.
Ocean and freshwater ecosystems must be protected against the destructive impacts of
fishing and pollution. Industrial fishing kills vast numbers of fishes and other marine
animals, disrupts marine ecosystems and destroys seafloor habitats.5 Mining further
