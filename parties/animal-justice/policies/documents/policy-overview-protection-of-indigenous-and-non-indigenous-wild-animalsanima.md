The Animal Justice Party (AJP) recognises all wild animals (indigenous and non-indigenous) as individuals with unique needs and value. We believe that within Aotearoa New Zealand both native and introduced animals have a right to live their lives unimpeded and unharmed by human activities.

AJP recognises there can be conflicts between endemic and introduced animals, while emphasising the need for solutions that prioritise compassion and protection. We reject divisive language, hunting and the glorification of trapping and killing animals for conservation purposes. Fundamentally, wild animals deserve the same rights to liberty, health, and freedom from harm as domesticated animals, and this should be reflected in law by specifically including them under the protection of the Animal Welfare Act, rather than considering them “pests” for eradication.

**Key Objectives:**

1. **Use compassionate approaches to manage introduced species**. Implement non-lethal, humane and species-specific methods to address challenges posed by introduced animals while recognising their right to live without unnecessary harm.

2. **Protect and restore natural habitats.** Preserve and enhance the environments where wild animals thrive, using whole-ecosystem approaches that benefit both native and introduced species.

3. **Shield wild animals from human-caused harms**. Mandate practices that minimise the negative impacts of human activities, development and pollution on wild animals and their habitats.

AJP Policy Overview

Protection of indigenous and
non-indigenous wild animals
The Animal Justice Party (AJP) recognises all wild animals (indigenous and
non-indigenous) as individuals with unique needs and value. We believe that within
Aotearoa New Zealand both native and introduced animals have a right to live their lives
unimpeded and unharmed by human activities. For the purpose of this document, ‘wild
animal’ refers to free-living animals who live outside captivity and without direct human
control, including feral animals.

AJP recognises there can be conflicts between endemic and introduced animals, while
emphasising the need for solutions that prioritise compassion and protection. We reject
divisive language, hunting and the glorification of trapping and killing animals for
conservation purposes. Fundamentally, wild animals deserve the same rights to liberty,
health, and freedom from harm as domesticated animals, and this should be reflected in
law by specifically including them under the protection of the Animal Welfare Act, rather
than considering them “pests” for eradication.

The health and integrity of these ecosystems are of value to New Zealanders and
necessary to uphold the Crown’s obligations under Te Tiriti o Waitangi, and are also vital
for the flourishing of wild animals. For these reasons, AJP supports compassionate and
just efforts to mitigate the threats that our ecosystems face.

transition to a plant-based economy. This is important for three key reasons. Firstly,

agricultural interests from disease vectors, particularly targeting possums and stoats

Four overarching objectives
Our policy for wild animals is focused on protecting and managing wildlife through
humane and sustainable approaches that respect all animals as individuals with
inherent value. It is centered around four objectives, each supported by specific
proposals:

Key Objectives:

1. Use compassionate approaches to manage introduced species. Implement
non-lethal, humane and species-specific methods to address challenges posed
by introduced animals while recognising their right to live without unnecessary
harm.

2. Protect and restore natural habitats. Preserve and enhance the environments
where wild animals thrive, using whole-ecosystem approaches that benefit both
native and introduced species.

3. Shield wild animals from human-caused harms. Mandate practices that
minimise the negative impacts of human activities, development and pollution on
wild animals and their habitats.
