# Election 2026 Manifesto

## A message from our co-leaders

Most of us slow down for a duck crossing the road. We feel uneasy watching a truck of animals go past on the motorway. We love our dogs like family.

And yet, somehow, that care rarely makes it into our politics.

That’s the gap we exist to fill.

Every year in this country, around 27 million sheep, cows, and pigs are killed for food. 120 million chickens. Billions of fish[1](#2d6d1b75-98ba-4f54-a148-93ad32806472). Many of them never see daylight, never feel grass, never get to do what comes naturally to them. And almost no one in parliament is talking about it.

We are.

We also know life is tough right now. Food costs too much. People are worried about their health, their jobs, the state of our rivers. Farmers are under real pressure. These things matter to us too, because the way we treat animals is tangled up with all of it.

Sheep and cattle alone account for nearly half of New Zealand’s greenhouse gas emissions[2](#b4bad5a9-4236-48a1-a1a6-ef4bd90f0a28). Ninety-five per cent of our waterways in farming areas are exceeding safe limits for contamination[3](#ca8d9314-5142-4115-9b64-1d30a6f06d76). The health costs of our current food system run into the billions. These aren’t abstract problems. They’re showing up in people’s lives.

Here’s what we’re working to change.

---

#### A politics you can trust

New Zealanders are tired of politics that serves donors and sidesteps hard questions. We’re committed to getting money out of politics, protecting whistleblowers, and supporting the kind of open, peaceful democratic participation that keeps power honest. We say what we mean.

---

#### Ending factory farming

Most New Zealanders, if they could see inside an intensive farming operation, wouldn’t be comfortable with what they saw. We want to ban the most harmful farming and fishing practices, and we want to support farmers to make that transition properly, with real funding and time to adjust. Change that leaves rural communities behind isn’t good change.

---

#### Pound reform

In Auckland, 60%[4](#562720e4-ed4e-420e-a778-cd2e649ec235) of dogs impounded last year were killed rather than rehomed.

That is not because these dogs were beyond help. It is because the system around them is broken: short timeframes to claim a dog, upfront costs many families cannot pay in one go, and shelters running well past capacity.

We want rehoming and no-kill to be the default, nationally, not the exception. That means fixing the system so fewer dogs end up unclaimed in the first place, and giving every dog who does the best possible chance of finding a home.

---

#### Vet costs and the bonds that matter

For many people, their animal companion is their closest relationship. Vet bills have become genuinely unaffordable for a lot of families. We’ll push for fairer access to veterinary care, and we support bereavement leave when someone loses an animal they love, because that loss is real.

---

#### Giving animals legal standing

Right now, animals are property under New Zealand law. That makes it very hard to protect them in any meaningful way. We want the law to catch up with what most people already feel: that animals are individuals who can suffer, and that matters.

---

#### An independent voice for animals

Animal welfare is currently overseen by the same ministry that promotes the farming industry. That’s a conflict of interest, and it shows. We want an Independent Commissioner for Animals with real powers, answerable to the public.

---

#### Smarter conservation, not a war on cats (or other animals deemed ‘pests’)

New Zealand’s indigenous species deserve real protection, and we’re fully behind that. But mass killing of cats is not the answer, especially when there’s no reliable way to tell a ‘feral’ cat from someone’s companion or a community cat that neighbours have cared for for years.

Cats didn’t put themselves in the wild. Humans did. So that’s where the solution has to start: nationwide desexing programmes, Trap-Neuter-Return, habitat restoration, and predator-proof fencing. Conservation that actually works, and that we can be proud of.

---

#### Healthier food, lower costs

More plant-based food in schools, hospitals, and public institutions would cut costs and improve nutrition. Estimates suggest a shift toward plant-rich diets could save New Zealand’s health system up to $22 billion in prevented chronic disease.[5](#77b9673a-ba40-41d8-8739-1c0c9c3d17a9) We’ll also push for clearer food labelling so people can make informed choices. Our circular and sharing economy policies reduce waste and help keep money in communities. None of this is radical. It’s just good policy.

---

#### Compassionate education

We want empathy, critical thinking, and environmental awareness to be genuinely central to how we raise children in this country, not an add-on, but a foundation. Kids who grow up understanding their connection to the natural world and to other living beings are better equipped for the future we’re all heading into.

We’re here because the animals don’t have a vote, and someone needs to speak up for them honestly and without apology.

If that sounds like your kind of politics, we’d love your support.

Danette Wereta and Rob McNeil\
Co-Leaders, Animal Justice Party\
