# Pet Refuge Support for Domestic Violence Survivors

> Archive note: Web text is the policy post body from animaljustice.org.nz/wp-json/wp/v2/policies; the PDF version linked from the page (if any) follows, extracted with pdftotext -layout.

## Full text (verbatim)

### Web page text

The AJP is committed to eliminating barriers that prevent people from escaping domestic violence, recognising that the safety of beloved companion animals is often the deciding factor that keeps victims trapped in dangerous situations. We acknowledge that New Zealand has the highest rate of domestic violence among OECD countries, combined with one of the highest rates of pet ownership globally, yet we have only one pet refuge service operating in Auckland. We propose establishing a nationwide network of pet refuge facilities to ensure no family stays in danger because they cannot safely care for their animals.

**Key Objectives:**

1. Advocacy for stronger legal penalties for those who threaten or harm animals as tools of domestic control.
2. Establish a nationwide network of pet refuge facilities ensuring every region has accessible, safe temporary housing for companion animals affected by domestic violence
3. Provide comprehensive support services including veterinary care, behavioural rehabilitation, and trauma recovery for animals who have experienced abuse
4. Create integrated referral systems connecting domestic violence services with pet refuge facilities to streamline support for families
5. Fund ongoing operations to ensure pet refuge services remain free for families escaping violence, removing financial barriers to safety
6. Establish a 24/7 emergency hotline for immediate pet refuge placement needs and rapid response protocols for situations where pets are in immediate danger
7. Develop and implement a comprehensive animal trauma rehabilitation programme that includes specialized behavioral therapy for abused animals, staff training in trauma recognition, and integrated therapeutic interventions that support the healing of both human survivors and their companion animals.
