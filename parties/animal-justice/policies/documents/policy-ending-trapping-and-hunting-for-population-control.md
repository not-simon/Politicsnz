# Ending trapping and hunting for population control

> Archive note: Web text is the policy post body from animaljustice.org.nz/wp-json/wp/v2/policies; the PDF version linked from the page (if any) follows, extracted with pdftotext -layout.

## Full text (verbatim)

### Web page text

AJP opposes the trapping and hunting of wild animals as a part of so-called ‘pest control’ activities. For AJP’s policy on hunting for entertainment, see: *Hunting and Fishing policies*.

A lack of enforcement for existing codes of conduct means that inhumane practices persist with minimal accountability. This regulatory gap allows for the continuation of trapping and hunting methods that cause unnecessary suffering to animals.

**Key Objectives:**

1. Amend the Animal Welfare Act to prohibit the unnecessary killing and suffering of all animals, requiring that population control be carried out with humane and non-lethal methods, and ensuring that greater accountability and stricter enforcement methods are upheld.

**Interim Objectives:**

While working toward long-term legislative change, AJP supports introducing more stringent regulations and safeguards to minimise harm to wild animals. These include:

1. **Mandatory live cameras on traps** to ensure compliance with animal welfare regulations and to facilitate immediate response once an animal is captured. Current legal requirements for trap checks every 12 hours are inadequate, and real-time monitoring would enable faster intervention to reduce suffering.

2. **Strict codes of conduct for hunting and trapping**, making ethical guidelines enforceable in legislation, rather than voluntary. AJP will advocate for uniform and legally binding standards across all hunting and trapping activities. (*aligns to Hunting and Fishing policies*)

3. **Age restrictions** to prohibit individuals under 18 from participating in trapping, hunting or any wild animal control activities that involve direct harm to animals.

4. **Stronger regulation of live trapping**, ensuring that any continued use of live traps adheres to high welfare standards, with a focus on transitioning toward non-lethal population management solutions.

AJP Policy Document

Ending trapping and hunting for
population control
AJP opposes the trapping and hunting of wild animals as a part of so-called ‘pest
control’ activities. For AJP’s policy on hunting for entertainment, see: Hunting and
Fishing policies.
Common trapping practices include:
- Leg-Hold Traps: These devices are designed to capture animals by the limb,
restraining them until they are dispatched by trappers. The use of leg-hold traps
can result in severe injuries, including fractures, dislocations, and lacerations.
Animals caught in these traps can experience prolonged periods of distress and
pain before being euthanised.1,2
- Kill Traps: Intended to provide a swift death, even the most humane traps can kill
non-target species or cause non-lethal injury.3
- Live-Capture Cage Traps: These traps aim to capture animals without immediate
harm, allowing for relocation or euthanasia. Animals may experience stress,
dehydration and exposure to harsh environmental conditions while confined for
up to 24 hours, according to regulations. However, these regulations are not
actively enforced.4
A lack of enforcement for existing codes of conduct means that inhumane practices
persist with minimal accountability. This regulatory gap allows for the continuation of
trapping and hunting methods that cause unnecessary suffering to animals.

Children and young people in New Zealand are sometimes involved in trapping
activities without sufficient age restrictions.5 This exposure raises ethical concerns
regarding the normalisation of violence towards animals and potential desensitisation to
animal suffering. It also poses safety risks, as handling traps and captured animals can
be hazardous without proper training and supervision.
Current trapping practices, coupled with inadequate regulation and enforcement, often
result in significant, and unnecessary, animal suffering. Addressing these issues
requires comprehensive policy and legislative reform, including the development and
implementation of more humane control methods, stricter regulations and educational
initiatives to promote ethical practices in wildlife management.

Key Objectives:

1. Amend the Animal Welfare Act to prohibit the unnecessary killing and suffering of
all animals, requiring that population control be carried out with humane and
non-lethal methods, and ensuring that greater accountability and stricter
enforcement methods are upheld.

regulations and to facilitate immediate response once an animal is captured.
Current legal requirements for trap checks every 12 hours are inadequate, and

2. Strict codes of conduct for hunting and trapping, making ethical guidelines
enforceable in legislation, rather than voluntary. AJP will advocate for uniform
