## History

### Colin Craig era (2011–2015)
Businessman Colin Craig, who had organised a 2009 protest march and polled third (8.7%) in the 2010 Auckland mayoralty,
launched the party on 3 August 2011. It absorbed members of the Kiwi Party (Larry Baldock) and New Citizen Party,
spent NZ$1.88 million in 2011 — the second-highest of any party, mostly Craig's own money — and campaigned on raising
the drinking age to 21, parental notification for abortion, repealing the "anti-smacking" law and opposing asset
sales. It won **2.65%**; Craig came second in Rodney. [1]

In 2014 there was speculation that National would stand aside in a North Shore seat for Craig, but on 28 July 2014 John
Key said Murray McCully would not step aside in East Coast Bays. Craig won a High Court injunction to be included in a
minor-party TV debate. Days before the election press secretary Rachel MacGregor resigned, citing Craig's behaviour. The
party won **3.97%** (95,598 votes), short of the 5% threshold — the largest single-party "wasted" vote at any election from 2011
to 2023 (NZ First's 75,020 in 2020 and TOP's 63,344 in 2023 were the next largest). [1][2][5]

Craig resigned on 19 June 2015 amid the MacGregor dispute; the board collapsed in recriminations. In 2016 a jury found
Craig had defamed Taxpayers' Union director Jordan Williams (awarding $1.27m, later set aside by the judge as excessive).
A police investigation into 2014 campaign spending cleared Craig. He left the party in October 2016. [1][3]

### Leighton Baker era (2017–2020)
Under Leighton Baker the party fell to **0.24%** in 2017, renamed itself New Conservative in November 2017, and rebuilt
around free speech, opposition to abortion and euthanasia law reform, opposition to the UN migration compact and to the
post-Christchurch gun reforms. It rose to **1.48%** (42,613 votes) in 2020 after unsuccessfully suing TVNZ to join a
minor-party debate. The board replaced Baker with Elliot Ikilei in November 2020; Ikilei quit on 31 December 2020. [1]
Baker later founded the short-lived [Leighton Baker Party](../leighton-baker/README.md).

### Houghton era (2021– )
Christchurch teacher Helen Houghton and lawyer Ted Johnston became interim co-leaders in October 2021, saying they
wanted to appeal to centrist voters; Johnston said the party no longer sought to abolish the Māori seats, ease gun laws
or introduce forced prison labour. Johnston left in September 2023 to form the Unity Party (allied with the Democratic
Alliance). The 2023 "Family Builder" policy ($9.1 billion in year one: first $20,000 of income tax-free, child tax
credits, income splitting, funding parents rather than ECE centres) was questioned by researcher Max Rashbrooke. The
party won **0.16%**, its worst result. In 2026 it reverted to the Conservative name. [1][4]

## MPs
None. Local office: two members (Christine Rankin, Callum Blair) on Auckland's Upper Harbour Local Board 2013–16. [1]

## 2026 campaign

- **List (8):** 1 Helen Houghton (Christchurch East), 2 Karl Thomas (Rangitata), 3 Mel Libre, 4 Doug Lyell (Northland),
  5 Marie Drury (Northcote), 6 Paul Deacon, 7 Jonathan Marshall, 8 Amy Turner (Banks Peninsula). Released via Scoop on
  30 September 2026. [6]
