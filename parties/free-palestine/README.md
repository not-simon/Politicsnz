## History

The party was set up in May 2026 to put Gaza and the Palestinian cause at the centre of the 2026 election. On 7 May the
Electoral Commission allocated it $78,887 of broadcasting funding (amended to $82,011), contingent on registration; it
applied in June and was registered on 5 August 2026, but its proposed logo was declined. [1][2]

Its stated objectives support a one-state solution and seek to "unite ... supporters of Palestine from all traditions".
Wikipedia reports that Hopkinson — who burnt a New Zealand flag in 2004, is described as an affiliate of the Popular
Front for the Liberation of Palestine, and is a former member of the Workers Party — has said the 7 October 2023
attacks were overall positive for Palestinians; that claim is sourced there to a Facebook video. Critics (e.g. *The
Spectator Australia*, *Jerusalem Post*) have attacked the party as hostile to Israel. [1]
