## History

Kim Dotcom — the Megaupload founder whose Auckland home was raided in January 2012 at the request of US authorities —
announced the Internet Party in January 2014. It signed up members through a phone app and was registered on 13 May
2014. Dotcom gave it NZ$3.5 million, described as the largest personal contribution to a New Zealand party on record.
Former Alliance MP and minister Laila Harré was chosen as leader. [1]

**Internet MANA (2014).** In May 2014 the party agreed an alliance with Hone Harawira's [Mana Movement](../mana/README.md):
the combined "Internet Party and MANA Movement" (registered 24 July 2014) contested the party vote, with Mana taking list
places 1, 3 and 4 and the Internet Party 2, 5 and 6. The strategy depended on Harawira holding Te Tai Tokerau, which would
have let Internet MANA bring in list MPs below 5%. On election night Harawira lost to Labour's Kelvin Davis (8,969 to
9,712 on election-night figures) and Internet MANA's 1.42% won no seats. Dotcom said: "the brand Kim Dotcom was poison
for what we were trying to achieve." The alliance was dissolved on 13 December 2014 and Harré resigned. [1][2][3]

**2017.** Activist Suzie Dawson, then living in Russia, became leader in February 2017. The party ran eight list
candidates and won 499 party votes (0.02%), the lowest of any registered party. Dawson stepped down in April 2018 (no
longer eligible, having lived abroad three years) and the party was deregistered on 12 June 2018. It obtained a
conditional 2020 broadcasting allocation ($41,457) but did not re-register or stand. [1]

## Policy (2014 launch agenda)

Unlimited high-speed internet 50% cheaper; a second submarine cable; high-tech jobs; restricting government
surveillance; reviewing the TPPA; copyright reform; clean energy; reducing inequality. [1]

## Notable events

- Days before the 2014 vote Dotcom staged the "Moment of Truth" event; media criticised him for "failing to deliver"
  his long-promised proof that John Key had lied about his case. Multiple polls had suggested Internet MANA could win
  more than 2%. [1][5]
- Dotcom, not a NZ citizen, could not stand. In 2024 his extradition to the United States was ordered (see Wikipedia,
  "Kim Dotcom"). [5]
