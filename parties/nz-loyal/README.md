## History

**2023.** NZ Loyal pledged to pull New Zealand out of the UN, WEF and WHO, "establish" a written constitution, impose
an immigration moratorium, oppose "race-based policies", and investigate the Reserve Bank, the "Green Agenda" and the
media. Gunn's health statements repeated false claims about vaccines (she called the COVID-19 response a
"mini-holocaust") and promoted alternative therapies. [1]

The party won **1.21%** (34,478 votes) and none of its 33 electorate candidates won. Because it missed the nomination
deadline for most of its intended 15-person list, its official list held only three names (one of whom left the party);
the High Court refused to let it add names after the deadline ("If you leave it to the last minute and something goes
wrong, the responsibility falls squarely on the party"). Had it passed 5%, most of its seats would have been left
empty. [1]

**Deregistration and split (2024–25).** The party's registration was cancelled at its own request in July 2024; Gunn
then applied to trademark the name personally. Kelvyn Alp of the far-right platform Counterspin Media accused her of
shutting the party down unconstitutionally; a new board elected Alp leader in November 2024. Alp polled 26 votes (last)
in the 2025 Tāmaki Makaurau by-election. Gunn calls Alp's party a "fake NZ Loyal". [1]

**2026.** The party applied to re-register in June 2026 and was registered in August (its logo was refused). [1][2]
policy.nz notes Alp's "background in conspiracy media, an armed militia that has targeted bankers, and ... legal battles
regarding the distribution of videos from the Christchurch Mosque attacks". [3]

- **List (17):** 1 Kelvyn Alp (Māngere), 2 Paul Nooroa Teio (Takanini), 3 Brenton Faithfull (Whangaparāoa), 4 Alexander
  Hislop (West Coast-Tasman), 5 Kelly Blomfield, 6 Greer Young (Tauranga), 7 Julian Crawford (Nelson), 8 Leslie Sales

- policy.nz describes the programme as revolving around "opposition to 'globalist' institutions such as the United
