## Current status

Former MP. Elected for the Greens in 2023, she resigned from the party on 8 July 2024 and sat as an independent until Speaker Gerry Brownlee declared her seat vacant on 22 October 2024 under the "waka-jumping" law, following a Green Party vote. Replaced by Benjamin Doyle. Not a 2026 candidate.

## Biography

Darleen Sheree Tana Hoff-Nielsen was born in Northland and is of Ngāpuhi, Ngāti Porou, Ngā Rauru, Te Āti Awa and Ngāti Kahungunu ki Wairarapa descent. Educated at Bay of Islands College and Whangārei Girls' High School; Bachelor of Chemical Technology (Massey) and, on a Rotary scholarship, an MBA from Solvay Business School in Brussels, where she worked in telecommunications talent management. Returned to New Zealand in 2013 and with her husband Christian Hoff-Nielsen founded e-bike business Bikes and Beyond (Waiheke Island, later E Cycles NZ). Speaks Māori, English, Danish, Flemish and French.

## Political career

- Green candidate for Northland in 2020 (fourth) and Tāmaki Makaurau in 2023 (third); elected at list #13 in 2023.
- Suspended by the party and stood down from her small business portfolio on 15 March 2024.
- Resigned from the Greens on 8 July 2024 after an independent investigation; returned to Parliament as an independent on 23 July 2024.
- Her High Court challenge to the party's process failed on 20 September 2024; on 17 October 2024 Green delegates voted to invoke the Electoral (Integrity) Amendment Act and she was removed on 22 October 2024.

## Record & notable actions

- Supported the Greens' Hoki Whenua Mai (land return) policy.

## Controversies

- **Migrant exploitation allegations (2024):** two former workers at her husband's bike business, including an Argentine man on a seasonal visa, alleged unpaid wages and being told they could work outside their visa conditions. Barrister Rachel Burt's independent report (summary released 17 July 2024) found Tana played a major role in running the business and had been aware of the allegations before standing as a candidate. Tana denied exploiting workers and said natural justice had not been followed. E Cycles NZ went into liquidation owing creditors including IRD over $400,000.
- **Refusal to resign and waka-jumping:** she refused party and opposition calls to resign, arguing use of the waka-jumping law would chill dissent; the Greens had historically opposed that law. Some Pasifika members resigned from the party in protest at her treatment, while former MPs Sue Bradford and Eugenie Sage backed the party.
- The Electoral Commission referred her (and a magazine publisher) to police in June 2024 over a 2023 advertisement allegedly lacking a promoter statement.
