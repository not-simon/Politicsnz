# TTP Pātaka Policy (Our Policies)

## Full text (verbatim)

# TTP Pātaka Policy

A values-led kaupapa grounded in lived practice, not just principle. Policies that centre whānau, uplift rangatahi, and prioritise intergenerational wellbeing.

## Our Tikanga & Values

#### Tika · Pono · Aroha · Manaaki · Kotahitanga · Transparency

These values must not be symbolic. They must be actively embodied in behaviour, decision-making, and organisational culture.

## Pillar One — Whānau

##### Policies that put mokopuna, hauora and whānau wellbeing at the centre of government.

### Te Ture Mokopuna — The Mokopuna Act

Flagship Policy

Te Tai Tokerau Party is committed to introducing **Te Ture Mokopuna (The Mokopuna Act)** — legislation that would require the Government and all departments to take into consideration the wellbeing of all mokopuna, both those living today and those yet to be born, in law, budgets and major decisions.

Te Ture Mokopuna takes as its starting point the Well-being of Future Generations Act 2015 exercised in Wales, drawing on the indigenous wisdom of the Seventh Generation Principle and models such as the Wampís people of Peru. It grounds these learnings in mātauranga Māori, whakapapa, and the obligations of Te Tiriti o Waitangi. Every mokopuna, regardless of heritage, is protected equitably — all stand to benefit from a system of government legally required to act as a dependable tīpuna.

#### The Mokopuna Principle

All persons exercising powers under any Act of Parliament, and all public bodies making significant policy, spending or regulatory decisions, would be accountable to the Mokopuna Principle — acting *“in a manner which seeks to ensure that current and future generations can meet their own needs”* — namely:

- Promoting the physical, emotional, cultural, and spiritual wellbeing of mokopuna.
- Ensuring mokopuna are protected from harm, abuse, and neglect.
- Recognising the importance of whakapapa, whānau, hapū, and iwi in the lives of mokopuna.
- Supporting mokopuna to thrive within their communities and across generations.
- Upholding obligations under Te Tiriti o Waitangi and international instruments relating to children’s rights, including the UN Convention on the Rights of the Child.

#### Mokopuna Wellbeing Goals

Every public body would show how its decisions contribute to seven wellbeing goals:

- **Thriving Whānau** — mokopuna are safe, healthy in body and mind, and free from harm, abuse, and neglect.
- **Resilient Whenua** — a resilient, biodiverse, healthy natural environment is protected and preserved for those to come.
- **Strong Whakapapa** — mokopuna have strong, secure connections to culture and language.
- **Oranga ki Aotearoa** — gaps in wealth, health, housing, and education are closed across generations and communities.
- **Oranga ki te Ao** — any action takes into account whether it makes a positive contribution to global wellbeing.

#### How It Works

- **Duty on Government and the public sector** — public bodies set out annually how they will improve mokopuna wellbeing and report publicly on progress.
- **Mokopuna Impact Assessments (MIA)** — significant legislation, Budget decisions, and major infrastructure and regulatory decisions require an assessment of likely effects on mokopuna wellbeing, short and long term, and how adverse effects will be mitigated.
- **Independent Commissioner for Mokopuna** — monitors and reports on the Mokopuna Principle, advises and supports public bodies, and advocates on behalf of mokopuna today and in the future. The appointment process requires agreement of a panel including iwi leadership and rangatahi.
- **National Mokopuna Wellbeing Indicators** — co-designed with iwi, hapū and communities, published and tracked annually, with a full National Mokopuna Wellbeing Report tabled in Parliament at least once every parliamentary term.

#### Building on What Exists

Te Ture Mokopuna is not designed to replace what already exists, but to connect, strengthen, and give lasting force to it — building on Treasury’s Living Standards Framework, the Local Government Act 2002, the Children’s Commissioner and Children’s Monitor, and iwi-led planning models such as Tokona Te Raki’s Mō Āpōpō Toolkit and the five-hundred-year Te Tauihu Intergenerational Strategy.

### Mahinga Whānau — Wealth Fund Policy

Instead of the state simply “creating jobs” for Māori, the state should act as a **co-investor in iwi and hapū-owned commercial assets** — ensuring whānau are the owners of the value chain, not just the labour force.

- **Policy:** Establish a Crown–Iwi Investment Fund specifically for “Transition Infrastructure” (renewable energy, green hydrogen, and digital infrastructure).
- **Core component:** Any major regional infrastructure project (e.g. wind farms or high-speed rail) must offer a minimum **25% equity stake** to local mana whenua, funded through low-interest government loans.
- **Whānau impact:** Moves whānau from low-wage seasonal labour into becoming shareholders in the national economy, providing intergenerational dividends that fund health and education independently of the state.

### Ōranga Whānau — "Clean Slate" & Universal Whānau Allowance

We cannot ask whānau to innovate or train for new careers while they are being penalised by the Ministry of Social Development (MSD).

- **Policy:** An immediate **Amnesty on Whānau Debt** — wiping all $2 billion of debt owed to MSD and IRD by low-income whānau — and replacing the complex, punitive benefit system with a simplified, non-taxable **Universal Whānau Allowance** for those in training or caregiving.
- **Core component:** Remove all “relationship status” policing. The state should not be in the bedrooms of our whānau, determining their worth based on who they live with.
- **Whānau impact:** Removes the “poverty trap” where earning an extra dollar results in a loss of state support, giving whānau the breathing room to transition into vocational training without fear of financial collapse.

### Te Hokinga Mai — Prison Policy (Fiscal Reallocation & Grassroots Rehabilitation)

The current centralised corrections system is a financial drain that perpetuates multi-generational trauma. Keeping a single individual incarcerated costs taxpayers over **$150,000 annually**, yet reoffending rates remain critically high — while Wellington funds corporate prison expansions and deprives rural communities of preventative resources.

Te Tai Tokerau Party will implement a **Prison Sinking Lid Policy** — shifting non-violent offenders, specifically those experiencing severe addiction and mental health crises, out of punitive cells and into intensive, localised rehabilitation. Savings are captured and redeployed to fund local community care.

#### Core Operational Pillars

- **The Statutory Funding Diversion:** a legally binding fiscal mechanism — for every bed safely vacated through health-focused diversion, the operational saving is stripped from the Department of Corrections budget and transferred directly into local hapū-led rehabilitation trusts.
- **Hapū-Managed Healing Sanctuaries:** highly secure, alcohol- and drug-free residential sanctuaries managed locally under custom and tikanga — replacing clinical prison blocks with land-based rehabilitation, trauma counselling, and practical trade integration.
- **Mandatory Restorative Work Pipelines:** diversion is not an escape from accountability — offenders enter mandatory, structured work collectives contributing to regional community assets, land remediation, and construction projects.

### Hauora Policy — Te Aka Motuhake (Independent Māori Commissioning Authority)

- **Te Aka Motuhake:** reject the mainstreaming or dissolution of independent indigenous health structures by establishing an entirely autonomous, state-funded but fully iwi-governed health authority — full indigenous autonomy over clinical design, regional funding allocations, and provider licensing, removing Crown management from Māori health.
- **Iwi Scholarship & Bonding Scheme:** eliminate the severe indigenous health workforce shortage with an uncapped, culturally bonded educational pathway. Graduates are bonded to work for a Māori, iwi, or rural provider for a minimum of four years, systematically flooding our clinics and marae with highly qualified indigenous clinicians.
- **Universal Service Delivery:** de-centralise healthcare by moving clinical intervention out of Western hospital settings and directly into tribal hubs — drastically reducing transport costs, removing institutional alienation barriers, and anchoring diagnostic, preventative, and dental public health services within the community.

## Pillar Two — Whare

##### Kai sovereignty in every backyard, dignity for our kaumātua, and warm dry homes built by our own people.

### Mana Māra Policy — Grassroots Food Autonomy & Cost-of-Living Security

The cost of basic kai has become a crippling burden for whānau across Te Tai Tokerau, while corporate global supermarkets exploit our isolation and extract millions from our local economy. True self-reliance begins at home — political and economic independence is impossible if a community cannot feed itself. Te Tai Tokerau Party will treat home food production not as a leisure hobby, but as **vital regional infrastructure** — a clear pathway to a high-yield vegetable garden, a water-catchment setup, and an active chicken coop in every backyard capable of sustaining one.

#### Core Operational Pillars

- **The Backyard Sovereignty Kit:** a funded, standardised flat-pack kit — heavy-duty raised garden beds, organic nutrient-dense soil, seasonal heritage seed packs, a predator-proof chicken coop, initial feed, and a starter flock of heritage laying hens.
- **Sovereign Water Catchment:** integrated roof-guttering feeding rain barrels with gravity-fed irrigation — gardens and poultry stay watered through dry summers without water rates.
- **Mobile Gardening Specialists:** a funded network of mentors travelling regional communities to help families with assembly, soil health, flock welfare, and seasonal planting rotations.
- **Te Tauhokohoko — Exchange Network:** complete deregulation of hyper-local domestic food trade — families can barter, trade or sell directly to neighbours, free of council licensing, corporate regulation, or state transaction taxes.

### Mana Kaumātua Policy — Honour, Material Dignity & Intergenerational Integration

A society is judged by how it treats its elders. Too many of our kaumātua and kuia live in severe energy poverty, cut off from family and tribal duties. The Mana Kaumātua Policy re-establishes our elders at the absolute centre of community life — direct, non-bureaucratic material support, and funded pathways that use their vast repositories of life experience, language, and cultural history to guide younger generations.

#### Core Operational Pillars

- **Sovereign Energy and Warmth:** every home where an elder is the primary occupant prioritised for fully funded solar panel installations and battery storage, integrated into community-managed grids guaranteeing zero-cost baseline electricity for heating, cooling, and medical devices.
- **Direct Kai Support:** a whole-food prescription delivery service — regional health networks issue monthly vouchers for fresh produce, whole grains, and protein, sourced directly from local agricultural networks and delivered to elders’ homes.
- **Paid Mentorship Roles:** kaumātua and kuia formally employed as paid cultural guardians and advisors within Local Business Ruruhau and community education programmes.
- **The Ringa Manaaki Dividend:** funded, trained, paid full-time in-home caregivers drawn from local whānau — keeping grandparents independent on their own land, surrounded by loved ones.

### Mana Taumaru — Local Build Policy (Eliminating Housing Deprivation)

Generations of our families are trapped in overcrowded, uninsulated, damp houses — leading to some of the highest rates of preventable rheumatic fever and respiratory illness in the developed world. The current building model relies on expensive imported materials and corporate supply chains. The Mana Taumaru Local Build Policy uses agricultural products from our industrial hemp industry to train local rangatahi to manufacture certified building panels and construct **highly insulated, fireproof, mould-free homes** for our people.

#### Core Operational Pillars

- **The Rohe Construction Academy:** a fully funded, practical trade academy in Te Tai Tokerau employing young locals — particularly those transitioning off government assistance — at a living wage while they learn certified skills in masonry, eco-engineering, and advanced construction.
- **Local Material Manufacturing:** regional processing facilities mixing agricultural hemp hurd with local lime binders to create high-spec, carbon-negative, Building Code compliant panels.
- **The Papakāinga and Social Rollout:** homes built on ancestral land and community papakāinga blocks, allocated first to families in overcrowded conditions or substandard rentals — bypassing commercial developers.

## Pillar Three — Whenua

##### Kaitiaki authority, land back for climate resilience, marae as civil defence hubs, and healthy moana.

### Mana Kaitiaki Policy — Kaitiaki Statutory Authority

We must move past Māori being “consulted” on environmental matters and instead empower them as the legal **“eyes and ears” of the land and sea** with real enforcement powers.

- **Policy:** Establish a national Kaitiaki Guardians Network funded by a **$150M annual Crown appropriation**.
- **Core component:** iwi and hapū-led guardian teams granted statutory authority to monitor, manage, and enforce environmental regulations within their rohe — including the power to issue “protection notices” for urupā, marae, and biodiversity hotspots.
- **Whānau/Whenua impact:** thousands of high-value “green” jobs for whānau in their own regions, with climate monitoring and disaster resilience grounded in mātauranga Māori.

### Hoki Whenua Mai Policy — "Land Back" for Climate Resilience

Climate adaptation requires space. To protect against sea-level rise and food insecurity, iwi must regain control of the ecosystems that act as natural buffers.

- **Policy:** Create a Whenua Climate Restoration Fund to facilitate a compulsory first right of refusal for iwi to buy back ecologically significant low-lying lands and coastal “buffer zones.”
- **Core component:** A Māori Biodiversity Credit System — the Crown pays whānau and iwi for the carbon sequestration and ecosystem services provided by restored native forests and wetlands.
- **Whānau/Whenua impact:** food security as global supply chains become more volatile, emulating Hawaiʻi’s model of restoring traditional food systems such as Loko Iʻa (fishponds) and agroforestry.

### Oranga Marae Policy — Frontline Climate & Civil Defence Infrastructure

When extreme weather strikes Te Tai Tokerau, roads slip and communication networks collapse — and our marae instinctively open their doors long before state agencies arrive. Te Tai Tokerau Party will formally recognise, institutionalise, and resource this reality, transforming participating marae into **self-reliant, off-grid civil defence hubs** legally integrated into the national emergency management grid.

#### Core Operational Pillars

- **Energy and Water Independence:** funded high-capacity solar arrays and battery storage banks, paired with commercial rainwater collection and advanced UV filtration, at all designated resilience marae.
- **Localised Emergency Stockpiles:** climate-controlled repositories with industrial first aid kits, emergency bedding, cooking gas reserves, water purifiers, satellite communication terminals (e.g. Starlink), and land-clearance tools.
- **Statutory Recognition and Training:** marae legally designated as co-equal Civil Defence Hubs under the NEMA architecture, with funded accredited training in emergency coordination, search and rescue, and advanced first aid for local whānau.

### Oranga Moana Policy — The Healthy Oceans Act

Current marine management is fragmented and ignores the customary rights and holistic views of mana whenua. We need a clear Te Tiriti-led framework for our oceans.

- **Policy:** Repeal and replace current marine legislation with a **Moana Ora Act** that mandates Indigenous Protected and Conserved Areas (IPCAs).
- **Core component:** A binding target to protect **30% of Aotearoa’s territorial waters** — “no-take” or “restricted” zones co-designed and managed by iwi/hapū, including the power to implement seasonal rāhui based on indigenous lunar observations.
- **Whānau/Whenua impact:** ends destructive bottom-trawling while securing customary fishing rights — keeping the moana a sustainable *pātaka kai* (storehouse of food) for future generations.

## Pillar Four — Tino Rangatiratanga

##### Constitutional protection for He Whakaputanga and Te Tiriti o Waitangi — the founding documents born here in Te Tai Tokerau.

### Entrenched Protection of He Whakaputanga & Te Tiriti o Waitangi

Te Tai Tokerau is where both Te Tiriti and He Whakaputanga were born. Our kaupapa is to constitutionally protect against any legislative harm toward both He Whakaputanga and Te Tiriti o Waitangi — platforming the voices of our communities on the ground who hold the kōrero, to lead constitutional changes anchored in the words of both kawenata tapu.

- Advance a **Tiriti-centred constitution**.
- As a matter of priority, vote to **repeal both the Regulatory Standards Act 2025 and the Treaty Clauses Review**.

### Waitangi Tribunal Funding & Powers

Te Tai Tokerau Party will increase and entrench funding to the Waitangi Tribunal, and extend its binding jurisdiction on the Crown beyond specific land types. The specific funding amount will be determined through kōrero with actors in the Tribunal and by assessing the needs of whānau on the ground who rely on the Tribunal as a forum for justice.

### Te Tiriti Re-education

Commitment to increased funding for Te Tiriti re-education, led by tangata whenua — particularly whānau on the ground who have maintained the kōrero about tino rangatiratanga for generations — with the education embedded in the NZ Curriculum and in public service professional development, including for all sitting MPs.

### "Hoki Whenua Mai" Statutory Trust Framework

- **The concept:** overhaul the land return process by replacing lengthy negotiations with automated, revenue-backed acquisition mechanisms.
- **Action:** combine Public Works Act offer-back reforms with Hawaiʻi’s Public Land Trust revenue model — legislate a mandatory **20% redirection of all Crown revenues** derived from conservation and public lands into a centralised, Māori-governed Land Acquisition Fund. Strip LINZ of its disposal authority and mandate the automatic return of unused public works land to its original ancestral owners.
- **Outcome:** an independent financial engine to re-purchase private lands under a Right of First Refusal framework, systematically re-establishing the tribal land base.

### "Te Whare Tohu Tātai" — Independent Māori Data & Compliance Commission

- **The concept:** transition from state-led monitoring toward an independent, well-resourced indigenous watchdog with enforcement capabilities.
- **Action:** establish an autonomous, statutory Commission tasked with monitoring Crown compliance across all historical settlements, holding absolute ownership and control over all state data regarding Māori whānau.
- **Outcome:** removes data management from central ministries, giving iwi the empirical tools and legal authority to systematically enforce Crown settlement obligations.

### Māori Justice & Autonomous Policing Act

- **The concept:** dismantle institutional inequities by replacing state-controlled correction networks with fully autonomous rohe systems.
- **Action:** legislate the direct transfer of regional youth justice votes into iwi-governed, marae-anchored authorities, and fund professional, iwi-administered community safety services operating under tribal jurisdiction.
- **Outcome:** replaces Western punitive policing and incarceration with culturally immersive, self-governing restorative justice networks with full legal standing.

## Pillar Five — Ōhanga Ā-Rohe — Regional Economy

##### Retaining regional talent, keeping state money in our regions, and breaking the supermarket duopoly.

### Local Business Ruruhau Policy — Retaining Regional Talent & High-Value Employment

Te Tai Tokerau suffers persistent economic extraction and youth depopulation — our most driven young people are forced to leave because the North lacks high-specification commercial infrastructure. Te Tai Tokerau Party will establish a network of high-specification **Local Business Incubators** across key regional hubs (including Whangārei, Kaikohe, and Kaitaia) — regional innovation launchpads providing the technological, legal, financial, and strategic infrastructure to turn local ideas into globally competitive enterprises.

#### Core Operational Pillars

- **High-Specification Co-Working Infrastructure:** fully funded premium workspaces with commercial-grade fibre, digital fabrication labs (3D printing and prototyping), digital media production studios, and professional meeting rooms.
- **The 5-Year Innovator Tax Holiday:** businesses founded, registered, and operating out of the incubators receive a **100% exemption from regional corporate income tax for their first five years**, provided local payroll and staffing targets are met.
- **Commercialisation and Mentorship Pipelines:** startups matched with elite business mentors, IP lawyers, and financial strategists, with seed-capital grants and direct connections to domestic and international venture capital.

### Kai Sovereignty Policy — "Community-First" Kai Procurement

Money spent by the state in our regions should stay in our regions.

- **Policy:** A “Social & Indigenous Procurement Mandate” requiring all government agencies (schools, hospitals, construction) to **apply a target of 10% government procurement increasing to 15% by 2030 of goods and services from Māori-owned businesses and local providers**.
- **Core component:** A “Local-Hire” requirement – any regional development project must demonstrate that a 35% minimum local Māori employment target and 50% Youth and Apprenticeship target of the workforce is sourced and trained from the local community.
- **Whānau impact:** a guaranteed market for Māori farmers, foresters, and builders — and by mandating “Farm to School”, our tamariki eat Māori-grown food in schools, lowering the cost of living and supporting our own ōhanga.

### Kai Sovereignty Policy — Dismantling the Supermarket Duopoly

The supply of food in Te Tai Tokerau is controlled by an anti-competitive corporate supermarket duopoly. Produce grown right here in the North is trucked to Auckland distribution centres and trucked back with massive markups — our growers are squeezed while our families are priced out of fresh nutrition. Te Tai Tokerau Party will support a decentralised, community-owned network of **Kai Sovereignty Food Hubs** connecting local farmers, fishermen, and growers directly with our families.

#### Core Operational Pillars

- **The Food Hub Infrastructure:** funded regional processing hubs with commercial kitchens, community cold-storage, clean butchery units, and wholesale packing areas.
- **Direct Consumer Distribution Pathways:** support for hapū and small businesses to run direct retail networks, mobile market fleets, and box-delivery schemes — bypassing supermarkets entirely.
- **Supermarket Anti-Gouging Enforcement:** any corporate supermarket operating in Te Tai Tokerau must allocate a minimum of **30% of shelf space to locally grown, unprocessed food** purchased directly from regional suppliers at fair, locally set rates.
