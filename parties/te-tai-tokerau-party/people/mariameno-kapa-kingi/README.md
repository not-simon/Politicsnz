## Current status

MP for **Te Tai Tokerau** since 2023. Elected for Te Pāti Māori, she was expelled in November 2025, reinstated by the High Court (which ruled the expulsion unlawful on 10 March 2026), and on 11 May 2026 launched her own **Te Tai Tokerau Party**, registered in August 2026. She is standing again in Te Tai Tokerau and is **#2** on her party's list (behind Kyla Campbell-Kamariera), and has urged Māori to vote strategically to defeat the National-led government.

## Biography

Born at Te Kao (Te Aupōuri; Ngāti Kahu ki Whangaroa) and raised in Onerahi, Whangārei. Worked for more than 30 years in iwi health and social services — helping develop Te Kohao Health in Hamilton, suicide-prevention and homelessness services — and was chief executive of Te Rūnanga Nui o Te Aupōuri Trust and a project specialist for Te Rūnanga o Whaingaroa; involved in the Far North iwi COVID-19 response. Married to Korotangi Kapa-Kingi; triplet sons and a daughter, including activist Eru Kapa-Kingi.

## Political career

- TPM candidate in Te Tai Tokerau in 2020 (second to Labour's Kelvin Davis).
- Won the seat in 2023 by 517 votes on special votes, after trailing Davis by 487 on election night.
- TPM whip from December 2023 until replaced by Debbie Ngarewa-Packer in September 2025.

## Record & notable actions

- Strong advocate on children and Oranga Tamariki; spokesperson for housing, children, infrastructure, transport and regional development.

## Controversies

- **"Mission to exterminate Māori" speech (May 2024):** said the government's repeal of section 7AA showed a mission to exterminate Māori and called Oranga Tamariki "matenga tamariki" (killing children); criticised by Luxon, Hipkins, Peters ("ignorant and offensive") and Seymour, and by Labour's Willie Jackson and the Greens' Hūhana Lyndon; defended by TPM's co-leaders.
- **2025 dispute with Te Pāti Māori:** the party circulated allegations that her office overspent its budget by $133,000 and failed to pay staff (she said the budget had been adjusted to support Takutai Tarsh Kemp and was never overspent); president John Tamihere accused her and Tākuta Ferris of "greed, avarice, and entitlement" and plotting against the co-leaders, which they denied. She was suspended and then expelled on 9 November 2025; the High Court reinstated her membership on 5 December 2025 and ruled the process unlawful in March 2026.
- In 2024 Karen Chhour complained after Kapa-Kingi called her an ACT "puppet".
