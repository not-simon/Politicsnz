## Background

Kapa-Kingi won Te Tai Tokerau for [Te Pāti Māori](../te-pati-maori/README.md) in 2023 (by 517 votes over Labour's Kelvin
Davis). After tensions with the party leadership she was suspended in October 2025 and then expelled by its National
Council. policy.nz records that the suspension followed "accusations of mismanaging funds". On 10 March 2026 the High
Court (Justice Paul Radich) ruled the suspension and expulsion unlawful because the process did not follow the party's
kawa and tikanga as its constitution required. Te Pāti Māori accepted the ruling, but in May 2026 Stuff reported that the
two were not working together. [1][2][3]

## Foundation and 2026 campaign

- On 11 May 2026 Kapa-Kingi announced the new party, saying she would return to Te Pāti Māori only if president John
  Tamihere stood down, Te Tai Tonga MP Tākuta Ferris was returned to the party and the leadership apologised publicly.
  The split was widely reported as a break from Te Pāti Māori, which said it would contest all seven Māori seats
  (Aperahama Edwards is its Te Tai Tokerau candidate). [1][4]
- Labour's Chris Hipkins and National's Chris Bishop suggested the electorate-based name might breach electoral law; the
  Electoral Commission registered it on 5 August 2026, judging confusion not likely enough to refuse. [1][5]
- **Candidates:** list 1 **Kyla Campbell-Kamariera** (list only; former Te Pāti Māori Te Tai Tokerau electorate
  co-chair, previously no. 23 on TPM's list), list 2 **Mariameno Kapa-Kingi** (Te Tai Tokerau). Kapa-Kingi is seeking
  both votes; a big enough party vote could elect both. [1][6]
- She said she would consider working with National if Christopher Luxon sought her support, and would not rule out
  working with any party. [1]
- Donations: $50,000 from Richard Allen via the Stanley Howard Fund. [7]
- People: [Mariameno Kapa-Kingi](people/mariameno-kapa-kingi/). Policies: [policies/README.md](policies/README.md).
