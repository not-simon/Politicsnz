## Current status

Retired. Peter Dunne left Parliament at the 2017 election after 33 years and is now a political commentator. CNZM (2018). United Future won 0.1% without him in 2017.

## Biography

Born in Christchurch on 17 March 1954; St Bede's College; MA in political science (Canterbury) and business administration studies (Massey). Worked at the Department of Trade and Industry and the Alcoholic Liquor Advisory Council (deputy chief executive 1980–84). Married Jennifer Mackrell in 1976; two sons. His brother John Dunne is a broadcaster.

## Political career

- Labour MP for Ohariu from 1984; minister in 1990 (Regional Development).
- Left Labour in October 1994; founded Future New Zealand, then co-founded United New Zealand (1995), entering Cabinet in National's 1996 coalition as Internal Affairs and Revenue Minister.
- From 1996 usually United's/United Future's only MP (except 2002–2008, when the party won up to 8 seats after his strong 2002 debate performances).
- Supported Labour-led governments (2002–2008; Minister of Revenue outside Cabinet from 2005) and National-led governments (2008–2017; Revenue, then Internal Affairs, plus associate health and conservation).
- In the 50th–51st Parliaments (2011–2017) he often held the balance of power on more than ten bills, including the partial asset-sales legislation.
- Announced his retirement in August 2017.

## Record & notable actions

- **Psychoactive Substances Act 2013**: created a regulated market for legal highs (later tightened); led the 2015 National Drug Policy and moves to widen access to cannabis-based medicines (2015–17).
- As Internal Affairs Minister extended passport validity from 5 to 10 years, created Fire and Emergency New Zealand (merging urban and rural fire services), and oversaw the He Tohu exhibition rehousing the Treaty of Waitangi, 1835 Declaration of Independence and 1893 suffrage petition.
- Income-sharing tax credit bill for families (2010; not passed); secured a statutory Game Animal Council (2011).
- Supported homosexual law reform, liberal alcohol laws, a republic and renaming Waitangi Day "New Zealand Day".

## Controversies

- **GCSB report leak (2013):** resigned his ministerial posts on 7 June 2013 after refusing to give the Henry inquiry all 86 emails with journalist Andrea Vance; the Privileges Committee later found he was entitled to refuse and criticised the inquiry, and the Ombudsman ruled the emails were not official information. He was reinstated as a minister in January 2014.
- United Future was briefly deregistered (May–August 2013) for lacking 500 members, costing him party-leader funding until it was re-registered.
