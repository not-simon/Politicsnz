# United Future — track record (2008–2017 support agreements)

United Future never had more than one MP after 2008, so its "promises" that could actually be delivered were the
policy items it negotiated into its confidence-and-supply agreements with National. This page compares those written
commitments with what happened. Structured data: [`data/promises/united-future.json`](../../data/promises/united-future.json).

**How to read this:** only items with a source showing the outcome are given a status. Items we could not verify are
listed as *not verified* rather than guessed. The agreements themselves are primary sources (copies hosted by the
McGuinness Institute; local copies in `raw/minor-parties/agreements/`). [A1][A2][A3]

## What United Future gave

- Confidence and supply to National-led governments for three full terms (2008–2017), plus support on procedural
  motions. There is no record in our sources of Dunne withdrawing confidence. [A1][A2][A3]
- In the 2011–14 term the government's majority was thin and Dunne "held the balance of power for more than ten pieces
  of legislation", including the asset-sales (mixed-ownership) programme, which he supported subject to his 49% cap. [S1]

## 2008 agreement (Dated 14 November 2008) [A1]

| Commitment | Outcome | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Dunne Minister of Revenue and Associate Minister of Health, outside Cabinet | Appointed and held both until his June 2013 resignation [S1] | kept |
| Support United Future's income-splitting legislation to first reading | Taxation (Income-sharing Tax Credit) Bill passed first reading 64–54 in September 2010 [S2][S1] | kept |
| Establish a Big Game Hunting Council, with a view to a statutory body | Government announced a Game Animal Council in 2011; the Game Animal Council Bill was before Parliament in 2012 [S1][S3] (see 2011 item) | kept |
| Support PPPs for major roads such as Transmission Gully | Transmission Gully built as a public-private partnership; construction began 8 Sept 2014 [S4] | kept |
| Keep the Families Commission's policy/research/advocacy role | Commission retained, then restructured (see 2011) [S5] | partially-kept |
| Reduce elective-surgery waiting lists using private capacity; progress Medicines NZ strategy | not verified | — |

## 2011 agreement [A2]

| Commitment | Outcome | Status |
|---|---|---|
| No sale of any part of Kiwibank or Radio New Zealand | Both remained wholly state-owned; Kiwibank is still state-owned in 2026 [S6][S7] | kept |
| Statutory cap: no more than 49% of shares in public assets sold to private interests | The mixed-ownership model sold down Genesis, Meridian and Mighty River Power from 100% to 51% and Air NZ from 74% to 51%; Solid Energy's sale was dropped [S8] | kept |
| Pass the Game Animal Council legislation to make the Council a statutory body | The Council became a statutory body (Game Animal Council Act 2013 — Act title from general knowledge; legislation.govt.nz could not be fetched) [S3] | kept |
| Reinstate the Income Sharing Bill | Reinstated, but it "never had a second reading" [S1] | partially-kept |
| Government discussion document on "Flexi-Super" (choose NZ Super start age 60–70) | Discussion document released by Finance Minister Bill English in August 2013; Treasury later advised against proceeding and the policy was not adopted [S9] | kept (document) / not adopted (policy) |
| Restructure the Families Commission (single commissioner, new monitoring/evaluation role, Families Status Report, $4m for parenting programmes) | Families Commission Amendment Act 2014 (third reading 24 March 2014, 64–56) created Superu with a stronger research function [S5] | kept |
| PPPs for major roading, "in particular the Transmission Gully highway" | PPP contract; construction from 2014, opened 2022 (late and over budget) [S4] | kept |
| Maintain at least current Budget funding tracks for TVNZ and RNZ | not verified in this pass (RNZ's funding level over 2011–14 not sourced) | — |
| Investigate a free annual health check for over-65s "when fiscal circumstances allow" | No evidence it was implemented | not verified |
| End guided helicopter hunting/herding on the conservation estate; youth one-stop shops; pre-release AOD assessments for prisoners; free public access to rivers/lakes/coast | not verified | — |
| Dunne Minister of Revenue, Associate Health, Associate Conservation | Appointed; resigned 7 June 2013 over the GCSB-report email inquiry, reinstated (as Internal Affairs, Associate Health, Associate Conservation) 28 Jan 2014 [S1] | kept |

## 2014 agreement (Dated 29 September 2014) [A3]

The 2014 agreement was much thinner: United Future's "priorities to progress" rather than firm commitments.

| Priority | Outcome | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Dunne Minister of Internal Affairs, Associate Health, Associate Conservation (outside Cabinet) | Appointed and served to 2017. As Internal Affairs minister he extended passports to 10 years and led creation of Fire and Emergency New Zealand [S1] | kept |
| Next iteration of the Medicines NZ strategy; enhanced role for pharmacists | not verified | — |
| Improve water quality in lakes, rivers and streams | not verified (no measurable commitment) | — |
| Recreational fishers — National's recreational fishing parks policy | not verified | — |
| Re-affirm PPPs for major roading where appropriate | Transmission Gully PPP proceeded [S4] | kept |
| Flexible superannuation (sought by UF) | Not included in the 2014 deal [S10] | — |

## Other actions in office (not in the agreements)

- **Psychoactive Substances Act 2013:** Dunne, as Associate Health Minister responsible for drugs, introduced the
  regulated market for "legal highs"; in 2015–17 he moved to make cannabis-based medicines more available. [S1][S11]
- **2013 resignation:** Dunne refused to hand over emails with a journalist to the inquiry into the leaked GCSB report
  and resigned; the Privileges Committee later found he was within his rights. [S1]

## Assessment

On the items that can be checked, United Future's record of getting its negotiated items implemented is strong:
the "no sale" of Kiwibank/RNZ, the 49% cap, the Game Animal Council, the Families Commission changes and the
Transmission Gully PPP all happened. Its two signature tax/retirement policies — income splitting and flexi-super —
got only procedural wins (a first reading; a discussion document) and were never enacted. Critics argued its
influence depended on National's tolerance in Ōhāriu rather than voter support (0.2–0.6% of the party vote). [S1][A2]
