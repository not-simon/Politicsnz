## History

United Future was formed for the 2002 election by merging Peter Dunne's liberal United New Zealand (founded by
moderate Labour and National MPs in 1995) with Future New Zealand, a "secularised" successor to the Christian
Democrats. Helped by a strong televised-debate performance by Dunne, it won 6.7% and eight seats in 2002, but fell to
2.7% and three seats in 2005 and, after its socially conservative wing (Gordon Copeland, Larry Baldock and others)
left over the 2007 "anti-smacking" law to form what became the Kiwi Party, to a single seat — Dunne's — from 2008.
Dunne then rebranded the party as a moderate centrist party. [1]

After 2005 Dunne gave confidence and supply to Helen Clark's Labour-led government and became Minister of Revenue
outside Cabinet. From 2008 to 2017 he supported John Key's and Bill English's National-led governments under three
written agreements (2008, 2011, 2014) — see the [track record](track-record.md). [1][2]

### The 2011–2017 period covered by this archive

- **2011:** United Future won 0.60% of the party vote. Dunne held Ōhāriu by 1,392 votes over Labour's Charles
  Chauvel, who called National's soft-pedalling in the seat an "unprincipled political deal". [1][3][4]
- **2013 deregistration episode:** On 31 May 2013 the Electoral Commission cancelled the party's registration at its
  own request because it could not show 500 financial members. The Speaker ruled Dunne would sit as an independent
  and lose party-leader funding until it re-registered; the Commission re-registered the party on 13 August 2013. [1][5][6]
- **GCSB-report leak (June 2013):** Dunne resigned his ministerial portfolios on 7 June 2013 after declining to give
  the Henry inquiry all his emails with a Fairfax journalist about the leak of the Kitteridge report on the GCSB. In
  December 2013 Parliament's Privileges Committee found he had been entitled to refuse, and he was reinstated as a
  minister on 28 January 2014. [7][8]
- **2014:** 0.22% of the party vote; Dunne held Ōhāriu by 710 votes over Labour's Ginny Andersen. [3][9]
- **2017:** On 21 August 2017, citing polling and a "mood for change" in Ōhāriu, Dunne announced his retirement.
  Damian Light became leader on 23 August. The party won 0.07% (1,782 votes) and lost its only seat; Labour's Greg
  O'Connor won Ōhāriu. [1][10]
- **Dissolution:** The party's AGM voted to disband in November 2017; Light and Dunne confirmed it on 14 November and
  the registration was cancelled on 28 February 2018. [1][11]

## Ideology and policy

Its 2007 mission statement described "a modern centre party" promoting "strong families and vibrant communities" and
"a competitive economy ... through market policies where possible, and government where necessary". Signature
policies in this period were **income splitting/sharing** for families, **flexible ("flexi") superannuation**, health
and medicines policy (Medicines New Zealand, a bigger role for pharmacists), recreational hunting/fishing interests
(a Game Animal Council) and, in 2011, limits on state-asset sales. [1][2]

## MPs (2011–2017)

- **Peter Dunne** — MP for Ōhāriu (and its predecessors) 1984–2017; Minister of Revenue (2005–2013), Associate
  Minister of Health (2008–2013, 2014–2017), Associate Minister of Conservation (2011–2013, 2014–2017), Minister of
  Internal Affairs (2014–2017). No person folder exists for him in this archive. [7][12]

## Notable events and controversies

- Reliance on a single electorate seat, aided by National's low-key campaigns in Ōhāriu, drew criticism as a
  "deal" (2011). [4]
- 2013 membership/registration failure and loss of party funding. [5][6]
- 2013 resignation over the GCSB-report email inquiry, later vindicated by the Privileges Committee. [7]
- As Associate Health Minister Dunne steered the Psychoactive Substances Act 2013 (a regulated market for "legal
  highs") and later eased access to cannabis-based medicines. [7]

## Current status

Dissolved; it did not contest 2020, 2023 or 2026. Damian Light went into Auckland local politics and in 2022 was
elected to (and became chair of) the Howick Local Board. Peter Dunne has appeared as a political commentator since
retiring. [7][13]
