## Current status

Co-leader of Te Pāti Māori and MP for **Te Tai Hauāuru**. Standing again in Te Tai Hauāuru in 2026 (Te Pāti Māori is seeking electorate votes only). She stepped back from the campaign in late September 2026 to care for her mother, who died on 21 October 2026.

## Biography

Grew up in Pātea and attended New Plymouth Girls' High School; affiliates to Ngāti Ruanui, Ngāti Ruahine, Ngāruahine and Ngā Rauru, with Irish ancestry through her mother. Chief executive of Te Rūnanga o Ngāti Ruanui, which runs health services in Hāwera and Pātea; led Ngāti Ruanui's long legal fight against Trans-Tasman Resources' seabed iron-sand mining off Taranaki (consents ultimately quashed by the Supreme Court). South Taranaki deputy mayor 2007–2010. Married to Neil Packer.

## Political career

- Elected co-leader with John Tamihere in April 2020 (replaced by Rawiri Waititi as male co-leader after the election).
- Entered Parliament on the list in 2020 (1.2% party vote, after Waititi won Waiariki); lost Te Tai Hauāuru to Labour's Adrian Rurawhe.
- Won **Te Tai Hauāuru** in 2023 by 9,162 votes over Labour's Soraya Peke-Mason, as TPM took six of seven Māori seats.

## Record & notable actions

- Prohibition on Seabed Mining Legislation Amendment Bill — defeated 106–13 at first reading in May 2023.
- Led Māori-run "yaks and vax" COVID-19 vaccination outreach in South Taranaki (2021) and criticised the Auckland reopening plan as treating Māori as "expendable".
- Called for a joint taskforce on anti-Māori hate speech (2021) after a rise in threats; campaigns for Aotearoa as the country's official name and restoring Māori place names.
- With Waititi proposed a Parliamentary Commissioner for Te Tiriti with veto powers (2025).

## Controversies

- **Treaty Principles Bill haka (November 2024):** named by the Privileges Committee for advancing towards ACT's seats and making a "finger gun" gesture; boycotted the committee, was found in contempt and suspended for 21 days (June 2025).
- **MACA Act video (October 2025):** filmed burning a copy of the Marine and Coastal Area amendment law on Parliament's grounds with Tākuta Ferris; rebuked by the Speaker.
- **Undeclared properties (October 2025):** the *Herald* reported she had not declared two properties on the register of pecuniary interests; she said she would update it but accused the media of targeting her because she is Māori.
- Her December 2024 claim that a capital gains tax could raise $200 billion over six years was called "absolutely daft" by economist Brad Olsen.
- Central figure, as co-leader, in the 2025–26 party split that saw Mariameno Kapa-Kingi and Tākuta Ferris expelled.
