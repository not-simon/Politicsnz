## Current status

Co-leader of Te Pāti Māori (with Debbie Ngarewa-Packer) and MP for **Waiariki** since 2020. Standing again in Waiariki in 2026; Te Pāti Māori is campaigning for electorate votes only (its 2026 list contains only general secretary Lance Norman) and urging supporters to party-vote Labour or Green. In 2025–26 the party went through a bitter internal split that saw MPs Mariameno Kapa-Kingi and Tākuta Ferris expelled (Kapa-Kingi was reinstated by the High Court before forming her own party).

## Biography

Rawiri Wikuki Waititi was born in Ōpōtiki on 17 October (c. 1980) and raised at Whangaparāoa near Cape Runaway in Te Whānau-ā-Apanui, attending kōhanga reo and kura kaupapa before moving to West Auckland at 13 (Rutherford High School). Affiliations include Te Whānau-ā-Apanui, Ngāi Tai, Te Whakatōhea, Ngāi Tūhoe, Ngāti Awa, Te Arawa and Ngāti Porou. A fluent te reo speaker, iwi leader, Ringatū minister and kapa haka exponent; grand-nephew of Hoani Waititi. Married to Kiri Tamihere-Waititi (daughter of party president John Tamihere); five children.

## Political career

- Labour candidate for Waiariki in 2014 (lost to Te Ururoa Flavell); switched to the Māori Party in 2016.
- Won **Waiariki** in 2020 by 836 votes over Labour's Tāmati Coffey, returning the party to Parliament (and bringing in Ngarewa-Packer on the list); confirmed male co-leader on 28 October 2020, replacing John Tamihere.
- Re-elected in 2023 with an outright majority (21,500 of 28,958 votes).

## Record & notable actions

- Helped negotiate the end of the 2020–21 Waikeria Prison rooftop protest at the inmates' request.
- His 2021 refusal to wear a tie ("a colonial noose") led to his ejection and then to Parliament dropping the tie requirement.
- Member's bill to remove GST from food (defeated at first reading March 2024, supported only by TPM).
- With Ngarewa-Packer proposed a Parliamentary Commissioner for Te Tiriti with a "Tiriti veto" (February 2025); advocates a Māori Parliament, a wealth tax and foreign-policy neutrality ("Switzerland of the South Pacific").
- Voted for the conversion-practices ban (2022) and abortion safe-areas law (2022); against the one-off Queen Elizabeth II Memorial Day holiday (2022).

## Controversies

- **Treaty Principles Bill haka (14 November 2024):** with Maipi-Clarke, Ngarewa-Packer and Labour's Peeni Henare performed a protest haka that disrupted the bill's first-reading vote. The three TPM MPs boycotted the Privileges Committee ("kangaroo court"); the committee found them in contempt, and on 5 June 2025 Parliament suspended Waititi for 21 days — the harshest such penalty in its history. An RNZ-Reid Research poll found 54% thought the punishment about right or too lenient, 36% too harsh.
- **Suppression breach (August 2023):** suspended for 24 hours for referring to suppressed court proceedings under privilege; the Privileges Committee upheld the action.
- Joked at a July 2023 party conference about putting karaka berries in David Seymour's water; Seymour demanded an apology.
- Defended the party's 2023 sports-policy wording about Māori "genetic makeup being stronger", later removed from its website, against accusations of racism.
- Ejected from the House several times (2021 haka during an argument with Judith Collins; 2023 haka welcoming Meka Whaitiri).
- **2025–26 internal conflict:** as co-leader he was at the centre of the dispute in which president John Tamihere accused Kapa-Kingi and Ferris of plotting to replace the leaders; the national council expelled both in November 2025, which Ferris called "plainly unconstitutional". At Waitangi in February 2026 a confrontation with Eru Kapa-Kingi's supporters ended hopes of reconciliation.
