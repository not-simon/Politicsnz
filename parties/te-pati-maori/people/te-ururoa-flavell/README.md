## Current status

Former MP and Māori Party co-leader. Lost Waiariki to Labour's Tāmati Coffey in 2017 and retired from politics. Not a 2026 candidate. Honorary doctorate from the University of Waikato (2024).

## Biography

Born in Tokoroa on 7 December 1955 (Ngāpuhi, Ngāti Rangiwewehi, Te Arawa); raised by his mother at Ngongotahā. Head boy at St Stephen's School; studied Māori studies and anthropology at Waikato (MA thesis in te reo Māori on Ngāti Rangiwewehi traditions, 1986) and trained as a teacher. Taught PE and Māori studies, headed Māori studies at Taranaki Polytechnic, set up kōhanga reo and kura kaupapa with his wife Erana Hond (five children), and was principal of St Stephen's in the 1990s.

## Political career

- Interim co-leader during the Māori Party's formation (2004); won **Waiariki** in 2005, defeating Labour's Mita Ririnui; held it in 2008, 2011 (against Mana's Annette Sykes) and 2014.
- Co-leader from July 2013 (with Tariana Turia, then Marama Fox).
- Minister for Māori Development and Whānau Ora outside Cabinet (2014–2017).

## Record & notable actions

- Gambling (Gambling Harm Reduction) Amendment Act 2013 (his member's bill).
- Public Works (Offer Back of and Compensation for Acquired Land) Amendment Bill (defeated 2010); Local Electoral (Māori Representation) Amendment Bill (defeated 2010).
- Marama Fox credited him with lobbying for national recognition of the New Zealand Wars, the pardon of Rua Kenana, and ratification of UNDRIP.

## Controversies

- In 2006, speaking in te reo at the first reading of the Ngāti Mutunga settlement bill, he called the Crown "thieves" and invited the iwi to return for more despite the "full and final" settlement.
- As St Stephen's principal he reportedly tried and failed to change a school culture later described as "maintained by violence"; bullying reports persisted after he left and the school closed in 2000.
