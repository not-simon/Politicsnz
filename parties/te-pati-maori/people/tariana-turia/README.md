## Current status

Deceased. Dame Tariana Turia died at Whangaehu Marae on 3 January 2025 after a stroke, drawing cross-party tributes.

## Biography

Born Tariana Woon on 8 April 1944 (Ngāti Apa, Ngā Rauru, Ngāti Tūwharetoa, Whanganui) and raised at Pūtiki on the Whanganui River; trained as a nurse. Married George Turia in 1962 (died 2019). Worked with Te Puni Kōkiri and Māori health providers, including as chief executive of Te Oranganui Iwi Health Authority, and was a leader of the 79-day 1995 occupation of Pākaitore (Moutoa Gardens). DNZM (2015); Blake Medal (2018).

## Political career

- Labour list MP from 1996 (swore allegiance to the Treaty of Waitangi); won Te Tai Hauāuru in 2002. Minister outside Cabinet 1999–2004 (associate health, housing, social services, Māori affairs; Community and Voluntary Sector).
- Resigned from Labour and Parliament in 2004 over the foreshore and seabed legislation; won the July 2004 by-election (92.7%) for the new Māori Party, which she co-led with Pita Sharples.
- Held Te Tai Hauāuru until 2014. Under the 2008–2014 confidence-and-supply agreements with John Key's National government she was a minister outside Cabinet.
- Retired at the 2014 election; succeeded as co-leader by Marama Fox.

## Record & notable actions

- **Whānau Ora** (2010): created and led the whānau-centred social services approach as Minister for Whānau Ora.
- Led the **Smokefree 2025** goal (2011), including plain packaging and tobacco excise increases.
- Secured the review and replacement of the Foreshore and Seabed Act with the Marine and Coastal Area (Takutai Moana) Act 2011 — supported by the Māori Party, which prompted Hone Harawira's departure.
- Launched a ten-year Māori housing strategy (2014); later sat on Whanganui DHB and represented Te Awa Tupua.

## Controversies

- In 2000 she apologised in Parliament after being warned by Helen Clark over comments describing a colonial "holocaust" against Māori; in 2001–02 she was criticised for intervening on behalf of individual prisoners as Associate Corrections Minister.
- After politics she opposed the End of Life Choice Act (2019) and COVID-19 vaccine and mask mandates; in February 2022 she falsely claimed on RNZ that Jacinda Ardern had been filmed doing "almost a Heil Hitler salute". In 2023 she backed National's Harete Hipango over Te Pāti Māori's candidate and supported abolishing the Māori Health Authority.
