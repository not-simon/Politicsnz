## Current status

Former MP and co-founder of the Māori Party. Retired in 2014. Not a 2026 candidate.

## Biography

Sir Pita Russell Sharples was born Peter Russell Sharples in Waipawa on 20 July 1941; his mother was Ngāti Kahungunu and his father a shearer of Bolton, England, descent. Head boy of Te Aute College; studied at the University of Auckland (education; MA first-class and PhD in anthropology and linguistics) and taught there. A leading kapa haka exponent (led the haka at the 1984 *Te Maori* exhibition opening at the Metropolitan Museum, New York; Te Matatini national male leader five times). CBE (1990), KNZM (2015), Arts Foundation Icon (2024).

## Political career

- Co-founded the Māori Party with Tariana Turia in July 2004 after the foreshore and seabed controversy; co-leader until July 2013 (succeeded by Te Ururoa Flavell).
- Won **Tāmaki Makaurau** in 2005, defeating Labour's John Tamihere; re-elected 2008 and 2011.
- Minister of Māori Affairs outside Cabinet (2008–2014) under the Māori Party's confidence-and-supply agreements with National.

## Record & notable actions

- As minister, part of the government that replaced the Foreshore and Seabed Act with the Marine and Coastal Area (Takutai Moana) Act 2011; Wikipedia's infobox photo shows him at the UN in New York in April 2010, when New Zealand endorsed the UN Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples (detail not covered in the article text).
- Earlier activism included opposing the Auckland Thermal No. 1 power station at Te Atatū (abandoned 1973).
