## Current status

Former MP (2013–2023). Elected for Labour, she defected to Te Pāti Māori on 3 May 2023 and lost Ikaroa-Rāwhiti to Labour's Cushla Tangaere-Manuel by 2,843 votes in October 2023. She does not appear among the 2026 candidates. (A Labour-folder profile may also exist; this one is filed under her most recent party.)

## Biography

Melissa Heni Mekameka Whaitiri was born at Manutuke near Gisborne on 11 January 1965 (Rongowhakaata, Ngāti Kahungunu) and raised in Whakatu, Hastings; head girl of Karamu High School. Her mother was the model for Napier's Pania of the Reef statue. She worked at a freezing works, gained a master's in education from Victoria University, played softball and netball at national level (a Silver Ferns non-travelling reserve), and worked for Parekura Horomia, the Māori Women's Welfare League and the Department of Labour (deputy secretary) before leading Ngāti Kahungunu Iwi Inc (2009–2013). Two sons.

## Political career

- Won the 2013 Ikaroa-Rāwhiti by-election for Labour after Horomia's death (majority 1,659); re-elected 2014, 2017 and 2020.
- Minister of Customs outside Cabinet 2017–2018; removed in August 2018 after a staff member alleged assault.
- Reappointed Minister of Customs in 2020, plus Veterans, Food Safety (2022) and Hawke's Bay cyclone recovery lead (2023).
- Resigned from Labour to join Te Pāti Māori on 3 May 2023 and was stripped of her portfolios; the Speaker treated her as an independent for parliamentary purposes without invoking the waka-jumping law.

## Record & notable actions

- Voted with TPM and the Greens for Debbie Ngarewa-Packer's seabed-mining ban bill (2023).

## Controversies

- **2018 staff assault allegation:** an inquiry found it "probable" that she had bruised a staffer; she was not restored to her ministerial roles that term.
- **2023 defection:** criticised by Labour (Willie Jackson suggested disappointment at not being promoted to Cabinet) and by National and ACT MPs over the Speaker's decision not to apply the waka-jumping law.
