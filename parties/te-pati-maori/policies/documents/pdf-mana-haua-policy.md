Mana Hauā Policy
Executive Summary
Mana Hauā relates to the life force and the inherent value of Māori disabled.
Whānau Hauā and Māori disabled are terms used in relation to whānau with
physical, mental, intellectual, or sensory impairments.
Te Pāti Māori dreams and aspirations are to achieve tino rangatiratanga for
whānau, hapū and iwi. This policy is a strength based kaupapa that recognises
Mana Hauā and seeks to assert the rights and wellbeing of Whānau Hauā within
our whānau, hapū and iwi.
Te Pāti Māori will:
Establish a Mana Hauā Authority
Reallocate 25% of all Disability funding to the Māna Hauā Authority
Establish Kaupapa Māori disability organisations and rehabilitation
facilities.
Abolish ACC and establish a Māori ACC Authority.
Ensure that Māori disability organisations are formally included in the
development of all disability-related policy and legislation
Reform building standards to require that all new housing builds are
fully accessible
Remove all barriers for Tangata Hauā to access health services
Ensure that antenatal screening programmes are not biased towards
termination of pregnancies if a disability is diagnosed
Recognise Te Reo Rotarota as an official language in Aotearoa
Require te reo Māori proficiency to be assessed in NZSL qualifications
Provide pathways for te reo Māori speakers to learn Te Reo Rotarota
and NZSL
Abolish abatement rates on benefits for Tangata Hauā and their care
workers
Immediately abolish the minimum wage exemption for Tangata Hauā

Mana Hauā Policy
Context
Māori directly or indirectly experience disability at a higher rate than any other
population group in Aotearoa. Despite one in three Māori having some form of
disability, Māori have less access to support and health and disability services.
The pandemic and natural disasters have highlighted major racism and inequity
that impacts on wellbeing of whānau hauā. Statistics show the inequity of
services for Māori disabled.
The current environment sees an increase in costs for housing, transport, food,
and other requirements. An environment that makes the lives of Whānau Hauā
worse than they were must not be tolerated. There are growing concerns that
Tangata Hauā are being treated as economic burdens - mistreated, abused, and
systematically targeted by government organisations and Crown agencies.
The evidence for this across the whole of government is best identified by our
access to employment, education, housing, cultural access, transport,
healthcare, government funding and whānau relationships plus the inaccessible
attitudes within government and organisational structures.
Based on Te Pae Mahutonga – Mauri ora (Cultural identity and access to the
Māori world), Waiora (Environmental Protection), Toiora (Healthy Lifestyles), Ngā
Manukura (Leadership) me te Mana Whakahaere (Autonomy), Te Pāti Māori
assert the rights of Whānau Hauā and have put forward a range of solutions to
improve outcomes for Tangata Hauā in Aotearoa.
Solutions

Te Pāti Māori assert the inherent value of Mokopuna Hauā and their role in Te Ao
Marama as creators, innovators, and models of change.

improved, the outcomes for everyone improves substantially. Both Māori and
non-Māori disabled children face discrimination from birth.

from whānau, hapū and iwi to access their environment whether it is schools,

Hauora
Te Pāti Māori assert that all barriers must be removed for Tangata Hauā to
access timely, accessible, and affordable health services.
Whānau Hauā have the right to access cultural avenues of hauora in addition to
the medical and or the psychological model.
The American Medical Association model of practice, which is deeply embedded
into ACC, is economically driven rather than being people-focussed. Kaupapa
Māori practices place whānau hauā at the centre along with their whānau, hapū
and iwi. Te Pāti Māori will ensure equity of service provision between ACC and
Ministry of Health, as these systems currently discriminate against people born
with disabilities compared to those with accident-related injuries. This will
ensure fair application and enforcement of the Act and Regulations, as we would
create policies based on an Aotearoa New Zealand model of practice that is
people- and community-focussed.

Mahi
Whānau Hauā have high levels of unemployment due to inaccessible
workplaces, economic situations, mistreatment by government agencies and
employers’ definitions of work. They are often seen as economic burdens.
Te Pāti Māori assert the rights of Whānau Hauā to participate in an accessible
workforce without fear of discrimination or prejudice.
We will immediately abolish the minimum wage exemption for Tangata Hauā,
rather than delaying this until 2025 like the current government is doing.
More employment roles need to be developed based on whānau hauā skills and
expertise. Many Tangata Hauā would prefer to work, but the lack of flexibility
amongst employers can create barriers.
Te Pāti Māori also acknowledge that for many Tangata Hauā, paid employment
will remain inaccessible. We will therefore ensure that the welfare system
upholds their mana.
The institutionalised racism and prejudice and ongoing mistreatment by
management and staff within WINZ towards Whānau Hauā needs to be
addressed.
We will abolish abatement rates on benefits for Tangata Hauā and their care
workers. They create unnecessary financial stress for Whānau Hauā, their
partners, and their carers. Tangata Hauā having their financial supports
removed because they seek a relationship with a non-disabled partner or
spouse is unfair, unjust, and cruel.

Mana Hauā Policy
Te Pāti Māori assert the rights of Whānau Hauā to be housed in safe, accessible,
and affordable housing in the community of their choice.
We will reform building standards to require that all new housing builds are fully
accessible. We will also retrofit existing public housing to meet universal design
standards and provide funding to papakāinga who wish to apply universal
design standards to their whare.
Te Reo Rotarota
Te Pāti Māori assert the rights of Mana Turi to have all the support needed to
fully participate in society.
Statistics New Zealand says hearing impairment affects nine percent of the New
Zealand's total population, 54,000 being Māori.
However, qualified signers, who can translate in to and from Māori, number
around a dozen.
We will recognise Te Reo Rotarota as an official language in Aotearoa, provide
scholarships for te reo Māori speakers to learn Te Reo Rotarota and NZSL, and
require te reo Māori proficiency to be assessed in NZSL qualifications.
Ahurea
Te Pāti Māori assert the rights of Whānau Hauā as valued members of their
whānau, hapū and iwi.
Inclusion of whānau hauā in tikanga Māori improves hauora and enriches our
culture as Tangata Whenua.
Te Pāti Māori assert rights and accessibility for wheelchair users and all Tangata
Hauā in our cultural spaces, including marae. This includes that that female
NZSL and Te Reo Rotarota interpreters should be able to speak on behalf of
male Mana Turi as they are kaiāwhina for Māori Deaf to communicate with.
All national Māori events such as Te Matatini and Ngā Manu Kōrero should be
accessible for Whānau Hauā.
Hākinakina
