Tax
A tax system that serves people, whenua,
and mokopuna.
For decades, the tax system in Aotearoa has
privileged wealth over work, property over
people, and extraction over care. Māori have
borne the cost of this imbalance through
low wages, insecure housing, underfunded
services, and intergenerational poverty, while
those with the greatest means contribute
the least.
The tax system reflects whose lives are protected,
whose labour is valued, and whose wealth is allowed
to accumulate unchecked. A fair tax system must
uphold mana, strengthen whānau wellbeing, and
restore balance through the just redistribution of
resources.
Te Pāti Māori will redesign our tax system to serve
people, whenua, and mokopuna. This document sets
out what we will do, who it helps, and what it raises.

Summary

Our plan at a glance Every component of our Tax Policy has
been modelled using the best available
Government data and, where appropriate,
benchmarked against Treasury and
Parliamentary Service analysis.
Our transformational Tax Policy will reduce
income tax for 97% of people in Aotearoa,
delivering significant relief for those
who work for a living, while rebalancing
the tax system so that those who have
accumulated the greatest wealth contribute
their fair share.
Together, these reforms create a fairer and
more progressive tax system – one that
puts more money back into the pockets of
whānau, reduces inequality, and ensures
everyone contributes fairly to the future
of Aotearoa.

Tax
Aotearoa has one of the most unequal tax
systems in the OECD. Income from wages
is taxed more heavily than income from
wealth, while property speculation remains
largely untaxed.
Māori are over-represented among low-
income earners and under-represented
among asset owners, meaning we pay
proportionately more tax while receiving
fewer benefits from the system. At the same
time, under-investment in public services
has placed pressure on whānau to absorb
costs that should be collectively carried,
from healthcare and housing to education,
transport, and care.
The current system rewards accumulation
without contribution, allowing extreme
wealth to grow detached from social
responsibility. This undermines social
cohesion and breaches the Crown’s
obligations to equity and fairness.
Tax reform is essential to restoring balance
and enabling the Crown to meet its
responsibilities to Tangata Whenua, and all
people who call this whenua home.

Te Pāti Māori will...
1. Transform the Income Tax System
• Remove tax for the first $30,000.
• Less tax for 97% of people (4.5 million
people).
• 90% of Aotearoa (4.2 million people) will
receive, on average, an additional $4,000
per year.
• Introduce additional higher tax brackets for
high-income earners to ensure those with
the greatest means contribute fairly.
2. Remove GST Costs from Kai
• For people earning less than $60,000, we
provide an Income Tax Kai Credit worth up
to eight weeks of free kai each year.
• Benefits 64% of Aotearoa (3 million people).
3. Implement a Targeted Wealth Tax
• Implement a net wealth tax on the
wealthiest individuals to address extreme
inequality and fund essential public
services.
• Excludes all people with less than $2 million
in net assets.
• Targets high-wealth individuals (only 3%
of people) with an average net wealth of
$6m each.
4. Ensure Corporate Tax Fairness
• Return the company tax from 28% back
to 33%.
• Introduce an International Profit Transfer
Tax with a rate of 5% on the value of profits
transferred offshore.
• Eliminate aggressive tax avoidance
practices through a $1 billion investment
into the Inland Revenue Department, the
Serious Fraud Office, the Financial Markets
Authority and the NZ Financial Intelligence
Unit.
5. End Property Speculation
• Implement a Land Banking Tax of 33%
discourage land banking and support the
productive use of land.
• Implement a Vacant House Tax of 2% to
free up the 111,700 empty homes across
Aotearoa.
• Implement a Stamp Duty of 5% on
Residential Sales, excluding first home
buyers for homes under $1 million.
6. • Guarantee Environmental and Climate
Alignment
• Align tax policy with climate responsibilities
by incentivising low-emissions activity and
discouraging environmental harm.
• Ensure any environmental taxes are
designed to protect low-income households
and do not transfer disproportionate costs
onto whānau.
Our Commitment
Te Pāti Māori is committed to a tax system
that restores fairness, funds public good, and
supports whānau to live with dignity.
Revenue raised through progressive taxation
will be directed toward housing, health,
education, climate resilience, and Kaupapa
Māori solutions that strengthen collective
wellbeing.
A fair tax system is not only the foundation of
a fair society; it is the price of civil society. Our
commitment is to ensure that the economy
serves people, not the other way around.
