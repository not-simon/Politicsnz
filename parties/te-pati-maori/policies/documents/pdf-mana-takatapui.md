Te Pāti Māori Policy Manifesto 2026
53
Takatāpui
Takatāpui have always existed within
te ao Māori. Our bodies, identities, and
expressions sit within whakapapa, tikanga,
and whanaungatanga.
Yet colonial systems (health, education, justice,
housing, religion) have failed to recognise, protect,
or serve Takatāpui with dignity. The result has been
preventable harm: poorer health outcomes, higher
suicide risk, exclusion from care, and exposure to
violence and discrimination.
Te Pāti Māori affirms that Takatāpui wellbeing is a
core measure of Māori wellbeing. When Takatāpui
are safe, affirmed, and supported, whānau are
stronger and communities are healthier.
This policy ensures gender-affirming, mana tīnana,
kaupapa Māori support across health, education,
justice, housing, cultural development and
community services, led by Takatāpui, delivered
locally, and grounded in mana.

Summary

Takatāpui
54
54
Our bodies, identities, relationships, and
expressions are held within whakapapa,
tikanga, and whanaungatanga. The
historical meaning of Takatāpui as intimate
companionship, discovered and reclaimed by
Emeritus Professor Ngahuia Te Awekotuku
and Lee Smith, has evolved to now include
Māori with diverse genders, including
whakawāhine, tangata ira tāne, irawhiti,
irarere, non-binary, and trans identities;
diverse sexualities, including those who
identify as lesbian, gay, bisexual, or queer;
and diverse sex characteristics, including
intersex Māori. Gender identity and sexuality
are distinct, and this policy does not treat
one as a stand-in for the other. No Māori is
required to use this word to belong; everyone
holds the right to name their own identity.
Before colonisation, takatāpui Māori
existed openly within whānau, hapū,
and iwi structures. Diversity of identity,
expression, and role was understood
through whakapapa, tikanga, and relational
responsibility.
Colonial systems imposed rigid gender
norms, criminalised difference, and
dismantled Māori systems of care. Health,
education, justice, and social services were
designed without Māori, and often against
us. For takatāpui Māori, this compounded
harm: racism layered with exclusion based on
gender and sexuality.
Today, takatāpui Māori experience:
• Higher rates of mental distress and suicide
risk
• Barriers to gender-affirming and culturally
safe healthcare
• Discrimination and violence within public
systems
• Exclusion from education, housing, and
employment pathways.
At the same time, takatāpui Māori continue
to lead, sustaining whānau, creating culture,
caring for community, and holding space for
future generations. Where kaupapa Māori,
body-positive, and affirming services exist,
wellbeing improves and harm reduces.

Takatāpui

Te Pāti Māori will...
1. • Establish a Takatāpui Commission to
hold agencies accountable for delivering
this policy
• Takatāpui-governed, with real decision-
making authority over how Takatāpui-
specific funding is allocated to community-
led organisations
• Guaranteed, ring-fenced funding
transferred by Te Puni Kōkiri each year, with
no ministerial discretion over the amount,
timing, or whether it occurs
• Subject to public annual reporting and
independent audit oversight.
2. • Protect Takatāpui from Violence
and Hate
• Recognise and respond to hate-motivated
harm against Takatāpui including political,
legislative, and religious attacks
• Prioritise prevention, community safety,
and restorative justice
• Ensure Takatāpui are protected without
expanding carceral systems
• Update the Human Rights Act to identify
specific provisions for people with diverse
genders, sexualities and innate variations of
sex characteristics
• Name and respond to the specific needs
of Takatāpui rangatahi currently in Oranga
Tamariki care and youth justice settings
• Name and respond to the speci�c needs of
Takatāpui whaikaha/whānau hauā currently
experiencing residential care.
3. Grow Takatāpui Cultural Leadership
Support Takatāpui pathways to te reo Māori
and tikanga fluency
Support whānau, hapū, iwi and Māori
community led wānanga
Fund Takatāpui-led creative and cultural
expression as an investment in Māori
cultural continuity
Ensure whaikaha/whānau hauā lead
accessibility at all stages and levels of
cultural development.
4. Guarantee Gender-Affirming, Kaupapa
Māori Healthcare
Fund and deliver gender-affirming
healthcare within the public system,
including:
• Hormone therapy
• Primary and specialist care
• Pathways to gender-affirming surgery
where clinically indicated
• Protection for intersex Takatāpui
from non-consensual or medically
unnecessary intervention, with bodily
autonomy, informed consent, and
access to medical records upheld as
rights distinct from, not folded into,
gender-affirming care
• For intersex tamariki specifically,
prevent gender normalising surgery
• Ensure services are mana-tīnana, culturally
safe, and Takatāpui-led
• Ensure appropriate services for whaikaha/
whānau hauā are designed and led by them
• Invest in growing the kaupapa Māori and
Takatāpui health workforce, so Takatāpui
and Māori-led services come with the
clinicians and kaimahi to deliver them.
