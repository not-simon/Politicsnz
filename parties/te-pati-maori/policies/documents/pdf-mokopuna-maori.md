Mokopuna
Māori
Mokopuna are tāonga.
For generations, however, the Crown has
used state care as a tool of colonisation,
severing mokopuna from their whakapapa,
eroding whānau, and undermining Māori
sovereignty.
State care institutions have not protected our
mokopuna. They have harmed them.
Residential facilities became sites of abuse,
criminalisation, and gang recruitment. Foster care
placements frequently pushed mokopuna into
survival behaviours.
One in three children placed in residential care later
entered the prison system.
Under Te Tiriti o Waitangi, the Crown has no mandate
to remove mokopuna Māori from their whānau,
hapū, and iwi and place them into systems that
repeatedly breach their collective mana; both the
child and the whānau, hapū and iwi they belong to.
The care, protection, and nurturing of mokopuna
Māori must be reclaimed by whānau Māori.

Mokopuna Māori
36
In 2024, the Crown formally apologised to
the more than 200,000 survivors of abuse in
state and faith-based care from 1950 to 2019.
Mokopuna Māori are disproportionately
represented among survivors. Recent data
shows that between 65-70% of children
abused in state care are Māori.
Today, nearly 70% of children in state care
are Māori.
Multiple reports and inquiries have confirmed
that institutional and structural racism
across government agencies has driven the
historical and ongoing over-representation of
mokopuna Māori in state care.
In 2021, the Waitangi Tribunal found that
the Crown’s removal of mokopuna Māori
into state care constitutes a profound and
ongoing breach of Te Tiriti o Waitangi.
The Tribunal was unequivocal: reforming
Oranga Tamariki from within will fail another
generation.
Care and protection are whakapapa
responsibilities. Whānau, hapū, and iwi are
best placed to care for their own mokopuna,
grounded in whanaungatanga, tikanga, and
collective responsibility.
The continued removal of mokopuna Māori
into state care is a violation of Te Tiriti,
a breach of mana, and a denial of tino
rangatiratanga.
The solution does not lie in fixing a broken
system. It lies in replacing it.
Context
Our Position

Te Pāti Māori will...
1.End State Care of Mokopuna Māori
• Commit to the end of state care for
mokopuna Māori
• Replace state care with Māori-led,
whakapapa-based care systems
• Restore whānau authority over the care and
protection of mokopuna.
2. • Establish an Independent Mokopuna
Māori Authority
• Introduce enabling legislation to partially
repeal the Oranga Tamariki Act.
• Create a Mokopuna Māori Act establishing
an independent Mokopuna Māori Authority.
• Give effect to the Waitangi Tribunal’s
recommendation for a Māori Transition
Authority.
Functions of the Authority will include:
• Asserting and protecting rights guaranteed
under He Whakaputanga and Te Tiriti o
Waitangi.
• Acting as the primary national agency
for the care and protection of mokopuna
Māori.
• Prioritising whakapapa, whanaungatanga,
tikanga, reo, and kawa Māori.
• Designing care pathways that sit outside
punitive care-and-protection frameworks.
• The Authority will not be constrained by
Oranga Tamariki policy settings and will
be free to support whānau in ways that
prevent harm before state intervention
occurs.
3. • Implement a Transition Plan Away from
Oranga Tamariki
• Develop a three-year transition plan to shift
power, funding, and workforce from Oranga
Tamariki to the Mokopuna Māori Authority.
• Oversight provided by the inaugural
Authority board and the responsible
Minister.
Immediate reforms during transition will
include:
• Reinstate Section 7AA into the Oranga
Tamariki Act.
• Ending the practice of uplifting pēpi and
mokopuna without whānau, hapū, and iwi
consent.
• Closing youth care and protection
residences.
• Reviewing and reforming the Child
Protection Protocol.
• Mandatory de-escalation and use-of-force
training.
4. Reallocate Funding to Māori Control
• Reallocate 70% of Oranga Tamariki’s annual
budget; approximately $1 billion per
annum; to the Mokopuna Māori Authority.
• Align funding with responsibility, authority,
and outcomes.
• Ensure resourcing reflects the proportion of
mokopuna Māori currently in state care.
5. • Establish a National Mokopuna Māori
Partnership Network
• Require the Authority to build a partnership
network across hapū, iwi, and Māori
organisations.
• Ensure mokopuna remain connected to
their whakapapa, whenua, and people.
• Partner with urban Māori organisations and
survivors of state care.
• Support whakapapa-appropriate care and
protection arrangements.
• This network recognises that mokopuna
Māori experience the sharpest end of
disconnection; and that reconnection is
essential to wellbeing.

6. • Implement all Whanaketia
recommendations
• We will implement all 138
recommendations from the Royal
Commission of Inquiry into Abuse in Care.
7. Empowering the Rights of Mokopuna
• No decision made by government
undermines the safety of our mokopuna,
and their rights to access their language
and culture, the best standard of health,
education, to have their needs met, and to
have a voice in policies that affect them.
• We will ensure that all legislation and policy
decisions uphold the mana and special
rights of mokopuna to live their best lives
free from harm.
• These rights are derived from whakapapa
and enshrined He Whakaputanga, Te Tiriti o
Waitangi, and the United Nations Children’s
Convention.
Our Commitment
Te Pāti Māori is committed to a future where
mokopuna Māori are raised with love, safety,
identity, and belonging. State care has failed
our mokopuna. Māori solutions will not.
