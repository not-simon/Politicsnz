```text
Justice
The justice system is not fit for purpose in a
Te Tiriti centric Aotearoa.
It was designed without Māori, imposed upon
us, and continues to function as a tool of
control rather than justice.
For generations, the Crown has fractured whakapapa,
punished poverty, and criminalised survival.
The system has failed to keep communities safe,
reduce harm, and honour Te Tiriti o Waitangi. Māori
remain over-policed, over-charged, over-convicted,
and over-incarcerated. There is nothing more unjust
than the mass incarceration of indigenous peoples on
their own land.
For Māori, justice is not defined by punishment but
by balance. True justice is grounded in accountability,
restoration, healing, and the return of authority to
whānau, hapū and iwi.
Te Pāti Māori is committed to dismantling a justice
system that harms Māori and replacing it with one
that restores mana, reduces harm, and protects
our people.
Executive
Summary
```

```text
Justice
28
Māori are more likely to be stopped,
searched, arrested, denied bail, convicted,
and imprisoned than anyone else in
Aotearoa.
Rangatahi Māori are criminalised early and
often, beginning a pipeline from state care to
youth justice to prison.
Poverty, trauma, addiction, disability, and
mental distress are met with punishment
instead of support.
Multiple inquiries have found that the
Crown’s justice system breaches Te Tiriti o
Waitangi and perpetuates systemic racism.
It has been almost 40 years since the
publication of Moana Jackson’s ground-
breaking report, He Whaipaanga Hou, and
no progress has been made to address the
overincarceration of tangata whenua. Māori
men still make up over half of the male prison
population, and the rate of incarceration for
wāhine Māori is now one of the highest in
the world.
Incremental reform has failed. Structural
transformation is required.
Justice must be grounded in whakapapa,
tikanga, and collective responsibility.
True justice requires Māori authority over
Māori outcomes. It requires shifting power,
resources, and decision-making out of Crown
institutions and into Māori hands.

Our Position
```

### Page 3

```text
Te Pāti Māori will...
1. • Establish an Independent Māori Justice
Authority
• The Authority will:
• Operate independently of existing
Crown justice agencies
• Hold real decision-making power
• Be tikanga-led and whakapapa-
centred
• Be accountable to Māori communities.
• Funding will be reallocated from existing
justice budgets, including:
• 50% of Vote Corrections
• 50% of Vote Police
• 50% of Vote Courts.
• The Authority will enable Māori-led justice
visions, including He Whaipaanga Hou and
Ināia Tonu Nei, to be fully realised.
2. Invest in Kaupapa Māori Justice Services
• Te Pāti Māori will invest directly in Māori-
designed and Māori-delivered justice
services, including:
• An Independent Māori Legal Aid
Service
• Kaupapa Māori Community Law
Centres
• Māori legal units within existing
community law centres.
• Expanded support for Māori Wardens
• Justice services delivered through Whānau
Ora providers
• Justice services will be devolved to iwi, hapū,
and Māori organisations closest to whānau.
3. • Implement Waitangi Tribunal
Recommendations
• Te Pāti Māori will implement all relevant
Waitangi Tribunal recommendations
relating to justice, including WAI 3060 –
Te Rau o Te Tika (Justice inquiry)
• Tribunal findings will guide justice sector
transformation.
4. • Overhaul Discriminatory Laws and
Practices
• Repeal Three Strikes Legislation
• Repeal Gangs Legislation
• Repeal Sentencing (Reform) Amendment
Act
• Repeal the Bail Amendment Act
• Raise the age of criminal responsibility from
10 to 16
• Amend the Clean Slate Act to apply to
custodial sentences
• End benefit attachment orders
• Introduce a judgement-proof debtor policy
• Require all Police officers to wear body
cameras
• Replace the ‘Independent’ Police Conduct
Authority with a genuinely independent,
Māori-grounded oversight body.
• Reinstate Funding for Section 27 Sentencing
Reports (Cultural Reports).
5. Decriminalise Drug Use
• Overhaul Misuse of Drugs Act so that drug
use is treated as a health issue.
• Wipe criminal convictions for drug use and
possession.
```

```text
Justice

Te Pāti Māori will...
6.• Uphold Human Rights for People in
Prison
• End the use of solitary confinement as
punishment.
• Expand rehabilitation, healing, and recovery
services.
• Ensure adequate access to whānau and
cultural support.
• Guarantee safe accommodation, food,
healthcare, and humane living conditions.
• Improve reintegration support.
• Increase the Steps to Freedom Grant to
$1,000.
7. • Invest in Mental Health and Addiction
Services
• Expand Mental Health Co-response Teams.
• Increase funding for Whānau Ora.
• Double investment in prevention, harm
reduction, and treatment for alcohol and
drug harm.
• Shift responses from enforcement to care.
• All services will be Māori-designed and
whānau-centred.
8. Achieve Electoral Justice
• Restore voting rights for all people in
Corrections facilities.
• Lower the voting age to 16.
• Allow people to register to vote at any time.
• Allow Māori to switch rolls at any time.
• Ensure boundary reviews are conducted
before every election.
9. • Close Military Style Bootcamps and
Youth Justice Residences
• Military Style Bootcamps will be closed
immediately.
• Youth justice residences will be closed by
2030.
• Rangatahi will be supported through:
• Whānau-based care.
• Tikanga-grounded rehabilitation
programmes.
• Education, training, and healing
pathways.
• Trauma-informed, community-based
responses.
10. Abolish Prisons by 2040
• Te Pāti Māori commits to the abolition
of prisons by 2040, replacing them with
community-based, restorative, and tikanga-
led alternatives.
• This transition will:
• Reduce harm and reoffending.
• Address the root causes of offending.
• Restore accountability through
community-led processes.
• Protect public safety through
prevention, not punishment.
Our Commitment
Te Pāti Māori is committed to ending a justice
system that perpetuates cycles of harm and
avoids the root-causes of crime by building
one that restores balance, protects whānau,
and honours Te Tiriti o Waitangi.
```
