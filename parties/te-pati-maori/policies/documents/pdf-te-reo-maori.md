```text
Te Pāti Māori Policy Manifesto 2026
45
Te Reo
Māori
Te reo Māori is the heartbeat of this whenua
and continues to bespeak this land we all call
home. It carries whakapapa, memory, tikanga,
and the authority of our tīpuna. When te reo
is weakened, so too is our collective identity
and authority over the land.
For generations, te reo Māori was deliberately
suppressed; punished in schools, excluded from
public life, and framed as optional or expendable.
Despite this, whānau, hapū, and iwi never let it die.
Te Pāti Māori will ensure te reo Māori is protected,
normalised, and passed confidently to future
generations, not as a cultural courtesy, but as a
foundational language of Aotearoa.

Summary
```

```text
Te Reo Māori
46
Although te reo Māori is recognised as an
official language, its daily use remains fragile.
Access to quality reo education is uneven.
Public services often treat bilingualism
as piecemeal and optional. Too much
responsibility is still placed on whānau to
carry revitalisation alone.
Language loss is structural and reflects
political choices.
Revitalisation also requires political will.
Te reo Māori must be upheld as a living
language, used naturally in homes, schools,
workplaces, and public institutions. Policy
settings must reflect this choice to revitalise
the language.
Te reo Māori is a tāonga guaranteed
protection under Te Tiriti o Waitangi. It
belongs to this land and must be visible,
audible, respected and integrated across all
domains of public life.
Revitalisation is not achieved through
symbolism alone. It requires structural
commitment, sustained investment, and
accountability across all institutions of
government.
Normalising Te Reo Māori
Te reo Māori must be part of modern life in
Aotearoa, and not something people have to
ask for.
Context
Our Position
```

### Page 3

```text
Te Pāti Māori will...
• Make te reo Māori compulsory within the
education system, with age-appropriate
progression.
• End “opt-in” and discretionary approaches
to reo provision.
• Ensure te reo Māori is treated as a normal
language of communication in Aotearoa.
1. Te Reo in Public Institutions
The Crown must model the behaviour it
expects from society.
• Require public institutions and agencies to
operate bilingually.
• Embed te reo Māori across legislation,
policy, and service delivery.
• Ensure public servants are resourced and
supported to learn and use te reo Māori
confidently.
2. Whānau- and Marae-led Revitalisation
Language lives where people live and thrives
within environments that enable it to do so.
• Fund whānau-led, marae-based, hapū and
iwi reo initiatives.
• Support iwi language strategies and long-
term revitalisation planning.
• Protect regional dialects and tikanga-
specific reo traditions.
3. Media, Broadcasting, and Digital Reo
Modern language survival requires modern
platforms.
• Strengthen Māori media and broadcasting
in te reo Māori.
• Support digital platforms, content creation,
and innovation that grows te reo usage.
4. Accountability and Protection
Language gains must not be held hostage by
political change.
• Establish protections to prevent rollback of
te reo Māori commitments.
• Ensure long-term, stable investment in reo
revitalisation, including for Te Reo Māori
broadcasting.
• Hold the Crown accountable for upholding
its Tiriti obligations to te reo Māori.
Our Commitment
Te reo Māori will not survive on goodwill
alone, it will survive through active protection,
normalisation, and transmission.
```
