```text
Freshwater Policy
Freshwater
Policy
Freshwater has mauri, and is held within
whakapapa that sustains whenua, people,
and all forms of life.
Wai is not merely a resource to be owned,
traded, or exhausted. It is a tāonga tuku iho;
inherited, relational, and essential to our survival.
When waterways are polluted or controlled
without Māori authority, it is not only the
environment that suffers, but whānau health,
food systems, and cultural continuity.
Te Pāti Māori asserts that freshwater governance
must be grounded in tikanga Māori, Te Tiriti o
Waitangi, and the restoration of mana whenua
authority.

Summary
```

```text
Across Aotearoa, freshwater systems are
under severe stress. Industrial agriculture,
extractive land use, and weak regulation have
degraded rivers, lakes, and aquifers. Māori
communities bear the greatest burden of this
degradation through unsafe drinking water,
loss of customary practices, and declining
ecosystems.
Despite Te Tiriti o Waitangi guaranteeing tino
rangatiratanga over wai, Māori have been
systematically excluded from meaningful
decision-making. Current freshwater
frameworks continue to prioritise commercial
use over ecological and cultural wellbeing.
Te Pāti Māori affirms that:
• Wai is a tāonga with its own mauri
• Māori never ceded authority over
freshwater; nor extinguished by the Crown
• Healthy waterways are essential to human
and environmental wellbeing
• Governance must begin with mana whenua.
Freshwater policy must move beyond simply
managing harm toward restoring and
protecting the integrity of our waterways.
Māori authority must be embedded
structurally, expressed in practice, and
recognised as both necessary and long
overdue.
```

```text
Freshwater Policy
18
Te Pāti Māori will...
1. • Recognise and uphold mana whenua
authority over freshwater
• Ensure Māori decision-making power in all
freshwater governance structures.
• End Crown-only control of water allocation
and management.
• Uphold Te Tiriti guarantees of tino
rangatiratanga.
2. • Protect and restore the mauri of
waterways
• Prioritise ecological health over commercial
extraction, particularly by overseas foreign
investors.
• Set enforceable limits that restore rivers,
lakes, and aquifers.
• Support tikanga-based restoration
practices.
3. • Guarantee safe, affordable drinking
water for all whānau
• Address long-standing water quality
and water services failures in Māori
communities.
• Invest in infrastructure as critical life-line
services that protects both human health
and ecosystem health.
• End inequities in access to clean water
supply and services.
4. End the commodification of freshwater
• Reject privatisation and trading of water
rights.
• Ensure water is governed as a taonga, not
as a market asset only.
• Protect future generations from water
scarcity, hoarding and profiteering.
5. Resource Māori-led freshwater
solutions
• Fund iwi, hapū, and marae-based water
initiatives at a sub-catchment level.
• Support mātauranga Māori alongside
scientific approaches to research
development and decision-making.
• Build long-term capacity for kaitiakitanga
with clear statutory tools and roles in water
management, alongside local authorities.
Our Commitment
Te Pāti Māori is committed to restoring the
balance between people and water.
We will protect wai as a living tāonga, restore
Māori authority, and ensure that mokopuna
inherit waterways that are clean, accessible,
healthy, and life-giving.
```
