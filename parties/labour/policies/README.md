# Labour Party: 2026 election policies

Every 2026 election policy of the New Zealand Labour Party that this archive could find, with summaries and links to the **full verbatim text** of every source page.

- **126 policies** in total: **76** listed by [policy.nz](https://policy.nz) (summaries by policy.nz, marked *policy.nz*) and **50** more found on labour.org.nz that policy.nz does not list separately (summaries written for this archive, marked *archive*).
- **138 full-text source documents** in [`documents/`](documents/): Labour's policy pages, PDFs, press releases and speeches on labour.org.nz, plus Scoop, RNZ and TVNZ pages that policy.nz cited. All were fetched from the original URLs (web.archive.org links in policy.nz were mapped back to the original pages) on **23 October 2026**. Text is verbatim; only the markup was turned into Markdown. Six policy PDFs (factsheets and policy documents linked from Labour pages) are also included as extracted text, and the PDFs themselves are kept in `raw/party-docs/labour/pdf/`.
- Structured data: [`policies.json`](policies.json). Scripts: `tools/policies_labour_fetch.py`, `tools/policies_labour_extract.py`, `tools/policies_labour_build.py`.
- How completeness was checked: every policy card on Labour's policy hub ([labour.org.nz/policy](https://www.labour.org.nz/policy)) and every page linked from it was captured. labour.org.nz has no sitemap.xml (it returns 404), so the list of all ~820 news items in the site's news menu was used instead: the ~300 most recent titles (roughly August 2025 to 22 October 2026) were read one by one and older ones were searched by keyword, and every release that announced a commitment was captured. Releases that only attack other parties, or report member's bills that have already passed, are not counted as policies.
- Each *archive* summary is a neutral restatement of what the linked Labour text says. Costings are Labour's own figures; none are independent estimates. Where sources disagree, this is noted.

## Headline commitments

These are the policy cards on Labour's [policy hub](https://www.labour.org.nz/policy), in Labour's order. Each description is Labour's own text (full page: [documents/policy.md](documents/policy.md)).

- **$20 Fare Cap**: $20 a week is the most you'll pay to get around by bus or train under Labour. In place by 1 July 2027.
- **Free Doctor's Visits**: Three free doctor's visits a year for everyone. Delivered with Labour's new Medicard.
- **Free Prescriptions**: Labour will remove the $5 fee on prescriptions to make them free for everyone. From 1 July 2027.
- **Free Maternity Scans**: Labour will make maternity scans free, everywhere in New Zealand with the Medicard. From July 2028.
- **Free Cervical Screening**: With Labour's Medicard, cervical screening will be free for ages 25-69. From 1 October 2027.
- **ED Recovery Plan**: Free GP visits, free prescriptions, more nurses and faster emergency care to ease pressure on emergency departments.
- **Family Doctor Loan Scheme**: Labour's Family Doctor Loan Scheme supports local GPs to buy into practices and stay in their communities. From 1 July 2027.
- **New Zealand Future Fund**: Backing New Zealand's potential and investing in a future made in New Zealand.
- **Capital Gains Tax**: A simple, targeted 28% tax on profit from selling investment property after 1 July 2027. Nine in 10 New Zealanders won't pay it.
- **Apprenticeship Boost**: Labour will support more tradies with extended employer payments, a $1,000 toolbox grant, and five new trades covered. From 1 July 2027.
- **SolarSaver**: Labour's SolarSaver is a fully-funded programme to help homeowners, renters and communities get solar without the upfront cost.
- **Small Business Action Plan**: Getting small business owners paid sooner, easier ways to invest, and less paperwork.
- **Small Business First**: A fair shot at work and less time dealing with the government.
- **Fiscal Plan**: Balance the books, return to surplus by 2028/29, reduce net debt towards 20% of GDP, and keep government spending and revenue sustainable.
- **AI in New Zealand's Interests**: Protect what matters, unlock our potential, and lead on our own terms with a new Office of AI and a framework to recognise and pay creators when AI uses their work.
- **Backing Māori into Skilled Work**: Māori-led training that helps more whānau Māori gain skills and move into good, well-paid work.
- **Graduate Nurse Job Guarantee**: Every eligible New Zealand-trained graduate nurse will be offered a job in our health system, from 2027.
- **Strengthening KiwiSaver**: Compulsory employer contributions rising to 6% by 2032, plus support during parental leave and over-65s.
- **Banning Price Gouging**: Making it illegal for big companies to overcharge for essentials.
- **Fairer Grocery Prices**: More competition, more choice, fairer prices by breaking open the supermarket duopoly's grip on wholesale supply.
- **Start Work Now**: Building homes, fixing schools and repairing hospitals - getting Kiwis back on the tools and nearly 30,000 apprentices into work.
- **Cutting Student Debt**: A 10% cut to eligible student loan balances, with small balances under $2,000 written off completely.
- **A Fair Go For Families**: No hidden fees. No traps. No rip-offs. Banning junk insurance and making cancelling as easy as signing up.
- **Pay Equity**: Restoring pay equity legislation and giving an immediate pay rise to care and support workers.
- **A Fair Go For Drivers**: Fairer fuel pricing, a right to repair law for cars, and a fairer deal at the car yard.
- **Fuel Tax Freeze**: Labour will freeze fuel excise tax for three years, saving an average family $520 a year by 2030.
- **Banking for Everyone**: Opening up banking competition, backing Kiwi fintech with a new digital banking licence, and keeping Kiwibank 100% New Zealand-owned.
- **A Better Deal When You Fly**: Real prices upfront, automatic refunds when things go wrong, and more airlines competing for your business.
- **Cutting Back-to-School Costs**: Cheaper uniforms, healthy school lunches back from Term 1 2028, rural school buses restored, and lower transport costs.
- **Keeping Kids off Screens**: Kids being kids for longer: sport grants, after-school activities, clearer screen rules in schools and support for parents.
- **Safer Online**: Basic rules for big tech: no social media for under-16s, more control over your feed, and a Digital Duty of Care.

## Fiscal plan and costings

Labour published its Fiscal Plan on 4 October 2026 ([labour.org.nz/fiscal-plan](https://www.labour.org.nz/fiscal-plan), full text: [documents/fiscal-plan.md](documents/fiscal-plan.md)). Its earlier fiscal strategy page is at [documents/fiscal-strategy.md](documents/fiscal-strategy.md). The key facts as Labour states them:

- Every election commitment is fully costed and fully funded, independently reviewed by Hall Chadwick
- Books return to an OBEGAL surplus in 2028/29
- Net debt down towards 20% of GDP over time
- Operating allowance held at $2.4 billion per Budget, as set at Budget 2026
- $12 billion in future capital allowances; Labour's commitments use $4.1 billion, leaving almost $8 billion unallocated
- $15.5 billion of additional health funding over the forecast period

## Health

### Remove $5 charge for prescriptions

`POL54665` · summary: *policy.nz* · also: Incomes & employment

**Why:** This party says that that last year, 155,000 New Zealanders didn't collect medicine they had been prescribed because they couldn't afford it. Not receiving treatments mean that health problems are exacerbated, and research shows that people who skip medication are more likely to end up in hospital. Making prescriptions free is an upfront cost for the government which removes pressure on the health system.

**How:** This party would remove the $5 fee for prescriptions of funded medication. This would apply to anyone who is entitled to free healthcare in New Zealand, instead of just people under age 14 and over age 65 and those with Community Services Cards. This would cost $74.5m a year, funded by a capital gains tax. This party estimates that this policy would cost $74.5 m a year.

**Costing (as stated):** This would cost $74.5m a year, funded by a capital gains tax. This party estimates that this policy would cost $74.5 m a year.

### Make up to three GP visits a year free

`POL54669` · summary: *policy.nz*

**Why:** This party says that many New Zealanders have to choose between medical care and other essentials like food. Some general practices charge nearly $100 per visit. Under the "Medicard" policy programme, anyone eligible for funded healthcare could go to the GP for free at least three times a year. This would be paid for by a capital gains tax.

**How:** This party would cover three visits a year to a doctor or nurse at the GP clinic where people are enrolled. Already free services like immunisations would not count towards the three-visit total. The "Medicard" card and app would link visits to people's National Health Index number. The three free visits are for each year and do not accumulate and roll over into the next year. This party estimates that this policy would cost $393.3 million in 2027/28, rising to around $553 million a year once fully in place.

**Costing (as stated):** This party estimates that this policy would cost $393.3 million in 2027/28, rising to around $553 million a year once fully in place.

**Full text:** ['Free Doctor's Visits with the Medicard', undated (accessed 11 August 2026)](documents/medicard.md) ([original](https://www.labour.org.nz/medicard)); [Affordable Healthcare - NZ Labour's Medicard Plan](documents/affordable-healthcare.md) ([original](https://www.labour.org.nz/affordable-healthcare)); [Labour's ED Recovery Plan](documents/ed-recovery-plan.md) ([original](https://www.labour.org.nz/ed-recovery-plan)); [Labour: Free doctor's visits (Medicard) - PDF](documents/pdf-labour-free-doctors-visits.md) ([original](https://www.labour.org.nz/media/vtbneplb/labour-free-doctors-visits.pdf))

### Ensure all referred maternity scans are free

`POL54686` · summary: *policy.nz* · also: Community & inclusion

**Why:** This party says that that pregnant people should not have to worry about money to know their baby is healthy, with each scan currently costing an average $90. No mum would have to skip a scan because they can't afford it.

**How:** This party would make every referred maternity scan by a doctor, midwife or specialist free in NZ. All a pregnant person would need is a Medicard. To grow the relevant workforce and ease the pressure on hospitals, 24 paid trainee sonographer positions would also be funded, costing less than $3 million a year. This policy would be funded by the party's targeted capital gains tax. This party estimates that this policy would cost $28.6 million a year.

**Costing (as stated):** To grow the relevant workforce and ease the pressure on hospitals, 24 paid trainee sonographer positions would also be funded, costing less than $3 million a year. This party estimates that this policy would cost $28.6 million a year.

### Implement free cervical screening for everyone aged 25-69

`POL54689` · summary: *policy.nz* · also: Community & inclusion

**Why:** This party says that that cervical cancer is one of the most preventable cancers but 85 percent of those diagnosed have never been screened or have missed screenings. Free cervical screening means earlier diagnosis, less pressure on hospitals and more lives saved.

**How:** This party would implement free cervical screening for everyone aged 25-69 from 1 October 2027. People's eligibility would be automatically loaded onto the Medicard, with screening available at their local doctor or community screening event. This would be funded from existing health budgets and would be in addition to the three free doctor's visits a year. This party estimates that this policy would cost $21.6 million for the first full year.

**Costing (as stated):** This party estimates that this policy would cost $21.6 million for the first full year.

### Provide loans to support local GPs to buy into practices

`POL54692` · summary: *policy.nz*

**Why:** This party says that doctors cannot always afford to buy their own practice. Yet private family doctors form the bulk of general practitioners in the health system. If more GPs can afford to open a practice in their local community, it will lower waiting times to see doctors and ease the pressure on busy hospitals.

**How:** This party would introduce a Family Doctor Loan Scheme providing up to 90% of the cost of buying into a practice. This would cap at $500,000 and would be interest-free for the first two years. A 3 percent annual interest rate would apply after that, with 10 years allowed to repay. Up to 50 loans would be available per year for owner-operated general practices. Funding would be supplied through the existing Small Business Cashflow Loan Scheme. This party estimates that this policy would cost $25 million a year if fully subscribed.

**Costing (as stated):** This party estimates that this policy would cost $25 million a year if fully subscribed.

**Full text:** ['Backing Your Family Doctor', undated (accessed 13 August 2026)](documents/familydoctorloanscheme.md) ([original](https://www.labour.org.nz/familydoctorloanscheme)); [Release: Labour backs family doctors](documents/release-labour-backs-family-doctors.md) ([original](https://www.labour.org.nz/news/release-labour-backs-family-doctors/)); [Labour: Backing local doctors (Family Doctor Loan Scheme) - PDF](documents/pdf-backinglocaldoctors.md) ([original](https://www.labour.org.nz/media/dbskvb1y/backinglocaldoctors.pdf))

### Guarantee jobs for NZ-trained graduate nurses

`POL54820` · summary: *policy.nz* · also: Incomes & employment

**Why:** This party says that graduate nurses are being trained here, but because of a lack of job openings, too many are being forced to look overseas instead of starting their careers in New Zealand. The pressure on the health system from the shortage of nurses spills over into patient healthcare.

**How:** This party would guarantee a job for every eligible New Zealand-trained nursing graduate, affecting about 2400 nurses. It would also address pressure on primary care because of the nursing shortage by doubling the funding available through the current recruitment subsidy. The current rate for rural nurses would be lifted from $20,000 to $40,000 and it would rise from $15,000 to $30,000 for nurses in urban areas. This party estimates that this policy would cost $525m.

**Costing (as stated):** This party estimates that this policy would cost $525m.

### Remove 10% of student loan balance from certain graduates

`POL55158` · summary: *policy.nz* · also: Incomes & employment

**Why:** This party says that student debt can hang over people for years, making it harder to save for a home, start a family or plan for the future. In launching this policy, this party hopes to encourage students to stay and work in NZ, rather than going overseas.

**How:** This party would write off 10% of eligible student loan balances. Students already finished and repaying their loan from 1 April 2027, and to current or future students three years after their studies, as long as they spent those years in NZ, would be eligible for the write-off. Eligible people would have to be up-to-date with their payments and have not received the now-defunct Fees Free incentive. Smaller loans of $2000 or less would be fully written off after the three years. This party estimates that this policy would cost nearly $40m a year once fully running in 2031.

**Costing (as stated):** This party estimates that this policy would cost nearly $40m a year once fully running in 2031.

### Ensure schools provide locally-made lunches
