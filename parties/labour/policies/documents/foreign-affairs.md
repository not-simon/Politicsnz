## Foreign Affairs

Labour will support multilateral institutions, defend peace and international law, and deepen New Zealand's relationships with the EU, Canada, ASEAN and the Pacific.

Key facts
- Multilateral institutions: review and mature the UN Charter, support the World Health Organization and adopt the updated WHO health regulations
- Peace and security: advocate for an ICJ advisory opinion on lethal fully autonomous weapons, establish clear parliamentary scrutiny if New Zealand joins armed conflicts, and strengthen oversight of payloads launched from New Zealand
- Relationships: prioritise the EU and Canada, deepen ties with ASEAN countries, and develop new market opportunities in Africa and South America
- Recognise the State of Palestine, place economic sanctions on Israel, and ensure the NZ Super Fund and ACC divest from holdings on the UN list of companies operating in the occupied territories
- Intervene in support of South Africa's case at the International Court of Justice in respect of a breach of the Genocide Convention
- Overseas development assistance progressively increased towards 0.7% of GNI as fiscal conditions allow
- Human rights: a programme to support human rights defenders, and advocacy for gender apartheid to be recognised in the Crimes Against Humanity Treaty

### Multilateral institutions

Labour will support the development of our multilateral institutions, including reviewing and maturing the UN Charter so it's fit for purpose.

Labour will support the good work of our multilateral organisations such as the World Health Organization and will adopt the updated WHO health regulations.

### Partnerships

Labour will prioritise growing our relationship with the EU and with Canada and exploring options for the development of those relationships in response to the challenges of today's world and in defence of international law, science and our values.

We will deepen our relationship with ASEAN countries, and develop new market opportunities, including in Africa and South America.

### Defending peace

In a world where technology is shifting and removing the offramps toward war, Labour will defend peace.

- We will advocate for an ICJ advisory opinion on the legality of lethal fully autonomous weapons and support international efforts to ban them
- We will establish clear parliamentary scrutiny if New Zealand joins armed conflicts
- We will strengthen oversight of payloads launched from New Zealand to ensure compliance with the current law

### Human rights

Labour will contribute to the development of the law that protects the rights of women and girls and advocate for gender apartheid to be recognised in the Crimes Against Humanity Treaty.

We will support those whose moral courage defies their reality by strengthening New Zealand's advocacy for democracy, peace and human rights, including in Myanmar; and establishing a programme to support human rights defenders, providing a safe haven for those acutely targeted precisely because of their potential to bring about positive change for human rights.

### International law

Labour will intervene in support of South Africa's case at the International Court of Justice in respect of a breach of the Genocide Convention.

Labour will recognise the State of Palestine.

Labour will place economic sanctions on Israel and ensure the NZ Super Fund and ACC divest from holdings on the UN list of companies operating in the occupied territories.

### Climate, development and the Pacific

Labour will re-engage strongly in international climate diplomacy and support the global transition away from fossil fuels. See also Labour's plan to fix the Emissions Trading Scheme.

Labour will progressively increase overseas development assistance towards 0.7% of GNI as fiscal conditions allow.

We will support closer economic integration across the Pacific and make it easier and cheaper to move money and data across the Pacific. Labour will also expand visa-waiver access for citizens of the Pacific Islands Forum nations.
