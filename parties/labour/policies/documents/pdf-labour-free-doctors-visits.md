FREE DOCTOR’S
VISITS FOR ALL
NEW ZEALANDERS
Labour will make it free to see your local doctor.
With a new Medicard, every New Zealander will get three free doctor’s
visits each year - at the practice they are enrolled in.
To see a doctor, all you will need is your Medicard, not your credit
card. This will save New Zealanders money every year.
Most Kiwis see their GP about two-and-a-half times a year, so
for many, a visit to the local doctor will be free. And for anyone
managing a long-term condition, it will take real pressure off the
household budget.
Three free doctor’s visits for all New Zealanders will be paid for by a
simple targeted tax on the profits earned after 1 July 2027 from the
sale of commercial and residential property (excluding the family
home).
Nine out of 10 New Zealanders do not own more than one property,
or a commercial property, so they won’t pay the tax. But every New
Zealander will get three free doctor’s visits a year with their new
Medicard.

HOW THE
MEDICARD WORKS
A Medicard will give all New Zealanders
access to three free doctor’s visits each year.
The three free visits will also be accessible
through a secure app.
People will receive their Medicard at birth,
or when they become a citizen, resident, or
otherwise eligible for healthcare.
Your Medicard can be used at the general
practice you’re enrolled with whenever you
need an appointment with a doctor or a
nurse.
The three free visits apply per year and don’t
roll over. If they aren’t used within the year,
they won’t carry into the next. You’ll use them
when you need them.
Your three free visits cover appointments with
a doctor or nurse at your enrolled general
practice. They don’t include services that
are already free (such as immunisations or
Access and Choice services), after-hours
care, or ACC visits.
To help free up appointments, Labour will
fund training and support for clinics to
introduce clinical triage systems. Using triage
will not count toward your three free visits.

FIXING THE FUNDING
PROBLEM
For more than two decades, general
practice has been funded mainly by a flat
payment for each enrolled person, with
clinics allowed to charge a patient copayment on top. That model is broken.
Independent analysis in 2022 found that
general practice was underfunded by
around 7.6 percent - about $137 million
against total practice income of $1.67
billion.
After decades of underfunding, practices
face impossible choices: raise fees on
patients, cut services, or close their doors.
Labour will fix the system to match funding
to need, so people can get care when and
where they need it.
As part of delivering three free doctor’s
visits, Labour is proposing to introduce a
new model known as independent pricing.
An Independent Pricing Authority would
be up and running by July 2028 and will set
an evidence-based national rate for GP
funding that reflects real costs and patient
need. When the rate goes up Health NZ
will be required to fund the increase the
following year - ending the cycle where
practices either absorb rising costs or pass
them on to patients.

Modelled on Australia’s Independent Health
and Aged Care Pricing Authority, the body
will use comprehensive data - practice costs,
staffing, patient mix, service delivery, and
sector-wide cost studies - to set sustainable
prices. This would make general practice
funding fairer and more transparent, so
practices don’t have to absorb costs
themselves or pass them on to the patient.
Money currently in co-payments will be
added to general practice funding, so clinics
won’t be worse off under the new system.
Fixing general practice funding means we
can focus on prevention, cut long-term costs
and avoidable suffering, and re-align the
health system to keep people well, not just
treat illness.

MORE PEOPLE WILL
GO TO THE DOCTOR
Three free doctor’s visits mean more people
will see their doctor or nurse - and that’s
exactly the point. When people get care
early, it takes pressure off our emergency
departments.
Labour has worked with general practice
representatives to design practical changes
that will free up around 4.58 million doctor’s
appointments every year.
These simple, affordable changes will give
doctors more time to see patients when they
need care.
Labour will support local clinics to manage
higher demand and prioritise those who need
care most. We want general practices focused
on providing the best medical care for their
patients, not finding new ways to charge fees
just to keep the doors open.
That’s why Labour will remove the pressure
to rely on fees by providing funding for
three free doctor’s visits per year. With
that support, clinics can focus on treating
patients efficiently - using phone triage
when appropriate and bringing in more
nurse practitioners and other skilled health
professionals to help meet demand.

Getting more doctors into practices
• Facilities: We will provide targeted funding
to ensure clinics in high-needs areas have
enough room to see patients. This could free
up 180,000 doctor’s appointments a year.
• Telehealth: We will review how the
Government supports telehealth to make
sure funding doesn’t pull doctors away from
where they’re most needed - providing faceto-face care in clinics.

Giving doctors more time
• Digital technology: We will roll out AI and
digital tools to save doctors time. General
practice is drowning in administrative work,
costing both time and money.
AI medical scribes are proving to be a gamechanger, freeing up time, reducing burnout,
and allowing doctors to focus on patients
over notetaking.
With patient consent, an AI tool can translate
conversations with patients into clinical
notes, summaries, or letters that can then be
incorporated into the patient’s health record.
A practice adopting AI scribes could save up
to two hours a day of clinician time across
the practice - around eight extra 15-minute
consults daily. The use of AI scribes and
other digital tools across the health system
could free up around 1.5 million doctor’s
appointments a year.


We will also introduce a Digital Innovation
Fund to support rapid adoption of proven
technologies and provide ongoing support
for general practices to adopt the next
generation of innovations.
• Clinical triage: We will support general
practices with training and funding to
introduce clinical triage systems.
Appointments are often allocated on a
first-come, first-served basis, which doesn’t
reflect need. Paying practices to staff a
telephone triage system will ensure patients
seeking same-day care are directed to
the most appropriate option. This system
has significantly reduced unplanned
appointments and provided timely advice,
prescriptions, and diagnostics in the
practices where it is already in place. This
could free up 1.9 million general practice
appointments a year and streamline a
further 2.9 million visits each year.

Lower operating costs for
general practices
• Extending access to the Health System
Catalogue: Primary care providers cannot
currently access New Zealand’s national
catalogue of products used across the
public health system. By extending access,
primary care could immediately benefit from
bulk procurement pricing already available
to Health New Zealand.
These solutions have been shown to work, to
help with wait times, and ensure doctors can
use their time most effectively.

Where a patient’s issue is resolved through

visits.
Improving available information: We will
support doctors to provide patients with
high-quality information to reduce lowcomplexity visits for those with long-term
