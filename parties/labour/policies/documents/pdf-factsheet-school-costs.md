Cutting
back-to-school
costs
Getting the kids ready for school will be
more affordable under Labour.
Every parent wants to give
their kids the best start in life.
But the cost of getting them
to school keeps going up.

Make school
uniforms affordable
You shouldn’t have to spend hundreds of
dollars on expensive uniforms just to send
your child to school.

A secondary school uniform
can cost as much as $1,000.
Then there’s the cost of
school lunches, bus fares
and petrol for the school
run. For families already
struggling with the cost of
living, it all adds up.

Labour will change the law to:

Labour will take practical
action to bring these costs
down and make life easier
for parents.

Schools can still have their own colours and
uniforms. But parents shouldn’t be forced to
buy expensive branded clothing when cheaper
alternatives will do the job.

 imit the number of branded uniform items
schools can insist parents buy.
L
 et families buy cheaper alternatives from
places like The Warehouse or Kmart.
R
 equire schools to consider what parents
can afford when setting uniform rules.
T
 rack uniform prices to make sure costs are
coming down.

The new rules will be in place by the first term
in 2028.


Bring back healthy
school lunches
National, ACT and New Zealand First took a
school lunch programme that was working
and made it worse.

Bring back rural
school buses
National has put extra costs on rural families
by cutting school bus routes.

Labour will bring back fresh, healthy, locally
made school lunches from Term 1 2028.

Many parents have been left driving further,
spending more on petrol, or trying to find
another way to get their kids to school.

Lunches will be prepared in school kitchens,
by iwi and hapū partners, or by local caterers,
with funding of $5 per meal and $6.50 for
students in Years 9 to 13.

Labour will work to restore the rural school
bus routes National cancelled. Where that’s
no longer possible, we’ll work with local
communities to find a practical alternative.

That means a decent lunch for kids, one less
cost for parents, and more work for local food
businesses.

Kids should be able to get to school safely,
without parents having to spend a fortune
getting them there.

Cheaper buses and
trains and a three-year
fuel tax freeze
Parents shouldn’t have to fork out hundreds
of dollars a year just to get their kids to school
by bus or train.
Labour will cap public transport fares at
$20 a week in Auckland, Wellington and
Christchurch, and $10 everywhere else.
Once you reach the weekly cap, the
remaining trips to and from school that week
will be free. That means cheaper travel for
kids getting to school and parents getting to
work.
And for families who rely on their cars, we
won’t add to the cost of filling up. Labour will
freeze fuel tax for three years.
These are practical, common-sense changes
that will make a real difference to family
budgets.
Cheaper uniforms. Better school lunches.
School buses back on the road. Lower
transport costs. Better starts now.
