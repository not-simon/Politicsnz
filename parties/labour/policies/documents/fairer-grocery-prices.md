### What's not working

New Zealand has lots of supermarket brands, but most are owned by just two companies: Woolworths and Foodstuffs. Together, they control more than 80 percent of the grocery market.

These two companies also control much of the wholesale supply that smaller grocers need to stock their shelves. That means a new or independent grocer often has to buy their products from the same supermarket group it is trying to compete with.

The big supermarket companies give their own stores better prices and better access to products. This often means smaller shops and independent supermarkets end up paying more, making it harder for them to compete and offer you a better deal.

Unless we tackle this problem, lower prices won't flow through to the checkout. New Zealand's grocery market is closed to protect the two big companies and lock out competition. Less competition means less choice and higher prices at the checkout.

National promised to fix the cost of living and bring grocery prices down. Since National took office, grocery prices have risen sharply:

- Butter is up 72%
- Bread is up 66%
- Milk is up 33%
- Beef mince is up 36%
- Cheese is up 31%

Despite talking big, National has had three years and done nothing. Now, with the election only weeks away, they say what we need is another review. New Zealanders can't eat a review. It's time for real action.

And Labour will act. We will open the market so more supermarkets can offer lower prices, giving shoppers more choice and a better deal at the checkout.

### What Labour will deliver

#### Better prices for shoppers

More grocers competing for your business will mean fairer grocery prices that last.

#### A better deal for small, independent supermarkets

Every supermarket and grocer will be able to buy products on better terms.

#### More choice at the checkout

It'll be easier for new supermarkets to open and for independent grocers to grow.

#### A better deal for food producers

We'll stop big supermarkets charging suppliers unfair fees when they receive nothing in return.

### How we'll do it

A Labour Government will deliver fairer grocery prices. Our first step to break up the supermarkets will be to free small grocers from anti-competitive restraints that keep prices high.

- End unfair contracts. We will remove the rules that stop individual stores, like Four Squares, lowering their prices, switching to another chain, or going independent.
- Ban junk fees. We will stop the big supermarkets charging food producers unfair fees for things that provide no real value to them, and that make pricing needlessly complicated.
- Back small supermarkets to grow and compete on price. Smaller stores will have guaranteed access to wholesale products. The big supermarket companies will not be allowed to cut off their supply when they begin gaining customers.
- Make payments simpler and fairer. We will simplify supermarket rebates and data charges so suppliers know what they are paying and what they receive in return.
- Start creating independent wholesalers. Woolworths and Foodstuffs will have to begin separating their wholesale supply businesses from their supermarket stores.

These steps will help smaller grocers compete and offer better prices, helping to build a genuinely competitive grocery market for the future.

Instead of the two supermarket companies controlling the market and shutting out smaller retailers, a Labour Government will create two independently run wholesalers: Foodstuffs Wholesale and Woolworths Wholesale. This will give smaller grocers more places to buy their products and make it easier for new supermarkets to open and grow.

We will know the plan is working when homegrown grocery chains are gaining market share, challenging the two big supermarket groups and offering shoppers a genuinely better price.

### Who it applies to

These new rules will apply to Woolworths and the two Foodstuffs cooperatives. They will not apply to local dairies, cafes or independent grocers. The changes are designed to help smaller businesses compete and offer shoppers a better deal.

### Better starts now with fairer prices for the future

Tackling the cost of living means finding long-term solutions to the drivers of high costs, but families also need a fair go now. That's why Labour will take immediate action to get the cost of living under control:

- Cap public transport fares at $20 a week in Auckland, Wellington and Christchurch, and $10 elsewhere
- Provide three free GP visits every year
- Make prescriptions free
- Lower bills by helping households install solar power with no upfront cost, saving around $1,000 a year
- Freezing fuel tax for three years

These changes will put money back in people's pockets now. Our grocery plan will deliver lasting change by giving shoppers more choice and ensuring fairer prices that last.

Frequently asked questions
Why are grocery prices so high in New Zealand?
New Zealand's grocery market is dominated by two groups, Woolworths and Foodstuffs, which together control more than 80 percent of the grocery market and much of the wholesale supply smaller grocers rely on. A new or independent grocer often has to buy products from the same supermarket group it is trying to compete with, and the big companies give their own stores better prices and access, making it harder for smaller shops to compete.

How much have grocery prices risen under National?
Since National took office, the price of bread has gone up 66%, butter 72%, beef mince 36%, cheese 31% and milk 33%. A basic basket of essentials has gone up in price by around 27% in three years. If you were spending $300 a week on groceries in 2023, you're now spending $381.

What will Labour actually do to bring grocery prices down?
- End unfair contracts that stop individual stores lowering their prices, switching chains, or going independent
- Ban junk fees charged to food producers
- Guarantee smaller supermarkets access to wholesale supply
- Simplify supermarket rebates and data charges
- Require Woolworths and Foodstuffs to begin separating their wholesale supply businesses from their supermarket stores

What are Foodstuffs Wholesale and Woolworths Wholesale?
Labour will create two independently run wholesalers, Foodstuffs Wholesale and Woolworths Wholesale, separated from Woolworths and Foodstuffs' supermarket stores. This will give smaller grocers more places to buy their products and make it easier for new supermarkets to open and grow.

Which companies will these rules apply to?
These rules will apply to Woolworths and Foodstuffs. They will not apply to local dairies, cafes or independent grocers. The changes are designed to help smaller businesses compete and offer shoppers a better deal.

How will this help food producers and suppliers?
Labour will stop big supermarkets charging food producers unfair fees for things that provide no real value to them, and will simplify supermarket rebates and data charges so suppliers know exactly what they are paying and what they receive in return.

How will we know if this policy is working?
Labour says the plan is working when homegrown grocery chains are gaining market share, genuinely challenging the two big supermarket groups, and offering shoppers a better price.

How does this relate to Labour's Banning Price Gouging policy?
Fairer Grocery Prices tackles the underlying lack of competition in the grocery market. Labour's separate Banning Price Gouging policy makes it illegal for large companies with market power, including supermarkets, to charge unfair and excessive prices for essentials, with the Commerce Commission able to investigate and order repayment to consumers.
