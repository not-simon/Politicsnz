## Pay Equity

Labour will restore pay equity legislation in its first 100 days and give an immediate pay rise to care and support workers.

Key facts
- Pay equity legislation restored in Labour's first 100 days
- $4 an hour immediate pay rise for care and support workers, from 1 January 2027
- Covers an estimated 65,000 care and support workers
- Worth around $8,320 a year, or $160 a week before tax, for an average full-time worker
- Fully funded within Labour's Fiscal Plan

Care and support workers do some of the most important work in our communities, and overwhelmingly they are women. This immediate pay rise is real money for people who are paying the mortgage, the rent, the power bill and the grocery shop.

An estimated 11,000 New Zealand children are currently in poverty because of National's cancelled pay equity claims. Labour says work should not be undervalued simply because it is work traditionally done by women.

### How it will work

Labour will reinstate pay equity legislation in its first 100 days in office. The reinstated legislation will accommodate the previous work already underway, so women aren't starting their claims from scratch.

From 1 January 2027, care and support workers will receive an immediate pay rise of $4 an hour, worth around $8,320 a year, or $160 a week before tax, for an average full-time worker.

Future pay equity settlements are different. Pay equity is determined through negotiation, and announcing in advance how much has been set aside for individual claims would undermine that process. Future settlements will be funded responsibly through future Budgets as agreements are reached and their costs become clear.

### Part of Labour's Fiscal Plan

The cost of the immediate pay rise for care and support workers is fully funded within Labour's Fiscal Plan, alongside every other election commitment Labour has made.
