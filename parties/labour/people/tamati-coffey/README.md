## Current status

Coffey announced in March 2023 he would retire, then in July 2023 replaced Kiri Allan as Labour's East Coast candidate; he lost to National's Dana Kirkpatrick by 3,199 votes and was not returned on the list ([NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/nz/tamati-coffey-named-labours-candidate-for-east-coast-electorate-after-allan-resignation/74EGANWAHBCDFHUNTNEVDUOWPI/); [2023 results](https://archive.electionresults.govt.nz/electionresults_2023/electorate-details-09.html)). No reliable source was found for his activities since.

## Biography

Born 19 September 1979 (Ngāti Porou, Ngāti Awa, Ngāti Whakaue, Tūhourangi, Ngāti Tūwharetoa) and raised in Lower Hutt, Coffey was the first in his family to attend university, graduating with honours in political science from Auckland. A TVNZ presenter on *What Now* and *Breakfast* and host of *New Zealand's Got Talent*, he won *Dancing with the Stars* in 2009 ([E-Tangata](https://e-tangata.co.nz/korero/tamati-coffey-labour-has-done-its-time-in-the-naughty-corner/)). He and his husband Tim Smith ran hospitality businesses in Rotorua and have two children born via surrogacy.

### Career summary

- Lost Rotorua in 2014. **Waiariki MP 2017–2020:** defeated Māori Party leader Te Ururoa Flavell by 1,321 ([2017](https://archive.electionresults.govt.nz/electionresults_2017/electorate-details-71.html)); lost Waiariki to Rawiri Waititi by 836 in 2020 and returned on the list ([2020](https://electionresults.govt.nz/electionresults_2020/electorate-details-72.html)).
- Chair of the Māori Affairs Committee (2020–2023).

## Record & notable actions

- **Surrogacy reform:** his Improving Arrangements for Surrogacy Bill passed its first reading unanimously in 2022 and was adopted as a government bill in May 2023 ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/491014/changes-to-surrogacy-laws-set-to-be-fast-tracked-after-government-adopts-bill)).

## Controversies

- **Rotorua representation bill (2022):** his local bill for the Rotorua Lakes Council, giving equal numbers of Māori-ward and general-ward councillors, was found by the Attorney-General to be inconsistent with the Bill of Rights; ministers withdrew support, it was paused, and the new council later dropped it ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/ldr/466019/labour-support-falters-as-rotorua-electoral-bill-fallen-foul-of-bill-of-rights)).
