## Current status

Williams is No. 24 in the shadow cabinet (commerce and consumer affairs, building and construction, youth) and led Labour's supermarket-competition announcements in the 2026 campaign ([Newstalk ZB](https://www.newstalkzb.co.nz/on-air/early-edition-with-ryan-bridge/audio/arena-williams-commerce-and-consumer-affairs-spokesperson-on-tackling-the-supermarket-duopoly/)). Her member's bill requiring banks and money-transfer firms to disclose international transfer fees passed its first reading in April 2026 with all parties except National ([The Post](https://www.thepost.co.nz/business/360995036/national-may-be-lone-opponent-bill-designed-lower-foreign-exchange-fees)). She is defending Manurewa at No. 41 ([policy.nz](https://policy.nz/2026/party-vote/parties/labour-party)).

## Biography

Born in 1990 (Te Aitanga-a-Māhaki, Ngāi Tūhoe, Ngāi Tahu), the daughter of Dr Jacqueline Allan and Sir Haare Williams, she grew up in Papakura and attended Diocesan School for Girls ([E-Tangata](https://e-tangata.co.nz/korero/arena-williams-making-changes-from-the-inside/)). She studied law and commerce at the University of Auckland and was AUSA president in 2012; she worked as a probation officer and at Chapman Tripp, and was appointed to the Waitematā DHB in 2019. She is married to Max Hardy and has two children.

### Career summary

- Contested Hunua in 2014 (Labour's youngest candidate). Won the 2020 Manurewa selection after incumbent Louisa Wall withdrew amid a disputed selection process ([NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/politics/news/article.cfm?c_id=280&objectid=12335909)).
- **Manurewa MP since 2020:** majorities 17,179 (2020) and 7,113 (2023) ([2020](https://www.electionresults.govt.nz/electionresults_2020/electorate-details-22.html); [2023](https://archive.electionresults.govt.nz/electionresults_2023/electorate-details-22.html)); chaired the Māori Affairs Committee; assistant whip 2023–2025.

## Controversies

- **Kāinga Ora advertisement (2021):** Kāinga Ora was criticised for featuring Williams, then a declared Labour candidate, in a 2020 taxpayer-funded advertorial; Housing Minister Megan Woods referred the agency to the Public Service Commission. Williams had told the agency of her political plans beforehand ([Newshub](https://www.newshub.co.nz/home/politics/2021/11/housing-minister-megan-woods-dobs-k-inga-ora-in-to-public-service-watchdog-over-cover-up.html)).
