## Current status

Henderson announced in March 2023 that she would retire at that year's election to return to legal work on court reform and sexual violence ([NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/northern-advocate/news/emily-henderson-why-im-standing-down-at-the-next-election/NQT24DNLVJGTLBSFIGG3UTA54M/)). She later disclosed violent threats made against her while in office ([NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/northern-advocate/news/outgoing-whangarei-mp-emily-henderson-reveals-violent-threats-made-against-her-during-time-in-office/CN2SSUHCFBG6PGFMWOEPDCAAD4/)).

## Biography

Born in 1972 and raised in Whangārei, Henderson holds a Master of Jurisprudence (Auckland, 1997) and a Cambridge PhD on cross-examination (2001). She was a family-law specialist at Henderson Reeves and a Crown prosecutor.

## Political career

### Career summary

- **MP for Whangārei 2020–2023**: trailed National's Shane Reti by 164 votes on election night but won by 431 after special votes ([Electoral Commission](https://www.electionresults.govt.nz/electionresults_2020/electorate-details-64.html)) — the first Labour win in the seat in 45 years.
- Deputy chair of the Social Services and Community Committee (2022–2023) and of the Justice Committee (2023); chaired a sub-committee on the conversion practices bill.

## Record & notable actions

- Cited her advocacy for Sexual Violence Courts, Whangārei Hospital rebuild funding and local state housing as achievements.
