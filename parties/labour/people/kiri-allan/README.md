## Current status

Allan did not stand in 2023 and left Parliament at that election. After leaving she announced a book deal with Penguin and set up a lobbying consultancy — registered while she was still an MP, which drew "revolving door" criticism given she had been responsible for lobbying reform as justice minister ([NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/nz/political-roundup-kiri-allan-latest-ex-labour-minister-to-move-into-lobbying/HBUTCMXLNRGQTGWLA2EFOGMGZY/)). She is not a 2026 candidate.

## Biography

Kiritapu Allan was born in 1984 in Te Karaka (Ngāti Ranginui, Ngāti Tūwharetoa) and raised whāngai by an aunt and uncle in Paengaroa and later Auckland, where she attended Auckland Girls' Grammar's reo Māori unit ([E-Tangata](https://e-tangata.co.nz/korero/kiri-allan-always-raising-eyebrows/)). She left school at 16, worked at KFC and as a fruit picker, then studied law and politics at Victoria University of Wellington, interning for Helen Clark and lawyer Annette Sykes. She worked at ChenPalmer and Kāhui Legal in Whakatāne. She has written about undergoing conversion therapy as a teenager ([The Spinoff](https://thespinoff.co.nz/politics/19-02-2022/kiri-allan-my-conversion-therapy-story)). She was treated for stage 3 cervical cancer in 2021 and declared cancer-free that December ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/national/457582/mp-kiri-allan-given-all-clear-after-months-of-cervical-cancer-treatment)).

### Career summary

- **List MP 2017–2020** after losing East Coast to Anne Tolley; junior whip, chair of Labour's rural caucus.
- **East Coast MP 2020–2023:** won the open seat by 6,331 votes over National's Tania Tapsell ([2020 results](https://www.electionresults.govt.nz/electionresults_2020/electorate-details-09.html)).
- **Minister:** Conservation and Emergency Management (Nov 2020 – Jun 2022) plus associate Arts, Culture and Heritage and Environment; Justice and Associate Finance from June 2022; under Hipkins (No. 10 in Cabinet) also Regional Development and Associate Transport, and a lead minister for the Cyclone Gabrielle response in Tairāwhiti ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/483394/prime-minister-chris-hipkins-reveals-cabinet-reshuffle)). Co-nominated Hipkins for the leadership in January 2023.

## Record & notable actions

- Fronted the March 2021 Kermadec earthquake tsunami evacuations on the day her cancer diagnosis was confirmed ([Stuff](https://www.stuff.co.nz/national/politics/300269974/kiri-allan-bit-of-a-superwoman-to-handle-tsunami-and-earthquake-response-on-same-day-as-medical-test-confirming-her-cancer-diagnosis)).
- Passed the law creating the Matariki public holiday (first observed 2022) ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/national/464833/matariki-public-holiday-passes-into-law)).
- As Justice Minister: repealed the three-strikes sentencing law (2022) ([Beehive](https://www.beehive.govt.nz/release/three-strikes-legislation-repeal-bill-passes-third-reading)); electoral law reforms (donations disclosure, Māori electoral option); expanded legal aid; announced a new ram-raid offence in July 2023.
- **Conscience votes:** voted for the third readings of the End of Life Choice Bill and Abortion Legislation Bill (having voted against the euthanasia bill at second reading).

## Controversies

- **RNZ speech (March 2023):** at the farewell of her then-fiancée Māni Dunlop from RNZ, she commented on RNZ's treatment of Māori staff; she accepted this could be seen as a minister directing a Crown entity and apologised ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/489511/rnz-releases-comments-from-kiri-allan-speech)).
- **Meng Foon donations (April 2023):** she had properly declared 2020 campaign support from Race Relations Commissioner Meng Foon, but was criticised for not flagging a conflict of interest when she became the minister responsible for him ([1News](https://www.1news.co.nz/2023/04/21/allan-declares-perceived-conflict-of-interest-after-foon-donation/)).
- **Staff treatment (June–July 2023):** the heads of DOC and NEMA raised concerns about how staff in her office were treated; she apologised and received coaching ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/493729/east-coast-mp-kiri-allan-to-resume-duties-after-speaking-with-pm-on-staff-allegations)).
- **Arrest and resignation (July 2023):** on 23 July 2023 she crashed her ministerial car into a parked car in Wellington, was arrested for careless driving and refusing to accompany a police officer, and received an excess-breath-alcohol infringement notice; she resigned all portfolios the next day and announced she would not stand again ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/national/494338/kiri-allan-charged-with-reckless-driving-and-resisting-arrest-resigns-as-justice-minister-pm-says); [1News](https://www.1news.co.nz/2023/07/25/kiri-allan-announces-she-wont-stand-for-re-election/)). In May 2024 she pleaded guilty to both charges; she was fined $300 and ordered to pay $5,296 reparation for careless driving and discharged on the second charge ([1News](https://www.1news.co.nz/2024/05/22/kiri-allan-pleads-guilty-on-day-trial-was-set-to-begin/)). She has spoken publicly about her mental health at the time and later disclosed ADHD and PTSD diagnoses ([1News](https://www.1news.co.nz/2024/01/26/kiri-allan-opens-up-about-days-that-led-to-her-political-downfall/)).
