## Current status

Webb announced on 21 October 2025 that he would not seek re-election ([NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/nz/politics/labour-mp-duncan-webb-to-resign-as-mp-wont-seek-re-election/XALPZELGH5DZDJK46P5UI2D7JY/)); he stood down as Deputy Shadow Leader of the House in March 2026 and holds no portfolio in the final shadow cabinet list. In November 2025 he lodged a member's bill to repeal the Regulatory Standards Act 2025, which Labour promises to repeal within 100 days ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/579942/labour-lodges-member-s-bill-to-repeal-regulatory-standards-act-asks-nz-first-for-support)). In June 2026 China banned him and three other MPs from China, Hong Kong and Macau for a year after a cross-party visit to Taiwan; Webb said he would "pay that price" ([AP](https://apnews.com/article/new-zealand-china-mps-banned-taiwan-beijing-3b2745d7fe9e9db7f26b56187d82b07e)).

## Biography

Born in 1967 in London and raised in Christchurch from age six, Webb studied law at Canterbury (LLB Hons 1989; LLD 2007), lectured at Massey and Victoria, was a law professor at Canterbury (2000–2008) and then a partner at Lane Neave. He worked with the Public Interest Project and the Howard League ([Scoop](http://www.scoop.co.nz/stories/AK1605/S00057/duncan-webb-nominated-for-christchurch-central.htm)). He has three sons.

### Career summary

- **Christchurch Central MP 2017–2026:** won the seat from National's Nicky Wagner (majority 2,871), then 14,098 (2020) and 1,841 (2023) ([2017](http://www.electionresults.govt.nz/electionresults_2017/electorate-details-04.html); [2023](https://archive.electionresults.govt.nz/electionresults_2023/electorate-details-05.html)).
- Chaired the Environment and later Finance and Expenditure committees; junior whip (2020), senior whip (June 2022).
- **Minister (outside Cabinet), Jan–Nov 2023:** Commerce and Consumer Affairs and State-Owned Enterprises ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/483394/prime-minister-chris-hipkins-reveals-cabinet-reshuffle)).
- Opposition justice spokesperson and Deputy Shadow Leader of the House (2023–2026).

## Record & notable actions

- As Commerce Minister appointed the first Grocery Commissioner, introduced mandatory unit pricing in supermarkets and announced a Commerce Commission market study into banking (2023) ([Beehive](https://www.beehive.govt.nz/release/first-grocery-commissioner-appointed-hold-sector-account)).
- His Companies (Directors' Duties) Amendment Bill (2021) passed in 2023 under Camilla Belich's name.
- **Conscience votes:** for the End of Life Choice Act (2019) and the Abortion Legislation Act (2020).

## Controversies

- Named by expelled MP Gaurav Sharma (2022) in bullying allegations against Labour's whips, which Labour rejected ([Stuff](https://www.stuff.co.nz/national/politics/129565407/labour-mp-gaurav-sharma-unloads-on-party-in-excoriating-social-media-post)).
- **Palestine advocacy:** his support for BDS drew criticism from the NZ Jewish Council and others; in August 2024 he posted video of a rally where protesters chanted "Hey Luxon... how many kids did you kill today?" — Luxon called it "beyond the pale" and Hipkins said he disagreed with the chants ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/524411/labour-mp-publishes-palestine-protest-video-asking-luxon-how-many-kids-did-you-kill)).
