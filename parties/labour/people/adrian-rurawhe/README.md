## Current status

Adrian Rurawhe died at Rātana Pā on 30 September 2026, aged 65 ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/politics/1654486/former-speaker-and-mp-for-te-tai-hauauru-adrian-rurawhe-dies); [NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/nz/politics/former-speaker-of-the-house-and-labour-mp-adrian-rurawhe-dies/664Z5QDSPZASBGDQGQLAMPAHTY/)). He had announced on 21 January 2026 that he would retire from Parliament on Waitangi Day; Georgie Dansey replaced him as a list MP ([NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/nz/politics/former-speaker-adrian-rurawhe-first-mp-to-confirm-retirement-ahead-of-election/6U7GXT2KAJFUZFVMKJP74RA3AA/)).

## Biography

Born in Taihape in 1961, Rurawhe was a great-grandson of Rātana founder T. W. Rātana; his maternal grandparents Matiu and Iriaka Rātana both represented Western Maori (1945–1969) ([NZ Herald](http://www.nzherald.co.nz/wanganui-chronicle/news/article.cfm?c_id=1503426&objectid=11173660)). With a background in health and education, he chaired Ngāti Apa for ten years and helped negotiate its 2011 Treaty settlement. He was a member of the Māori Party from 2004 to 2008.

### Career summary

- **Te Tai Hauāuru MP 2014–2023:** won the seat from the Māori Party after Tariana Turia's retirement; held it in 2017 and in 2020 (majority 1,053 over Debbie Ngarewa-Packer after a recount) ([Stuff](https://www.stuff.co.nz/national/politics/123446749/mori-partysought-recount-sees-fewer-votes-for-coleader-debbie-ngarewapacker)). List MP 2023–2026.
- Assistant Speaker (2017–2020), Deputy Speaker (2020–2022), and **Speaker of the House, 24 Aug 2022 – Dec 2023** — the second Māori Speaker after Sir Peter Tapsell ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/473418/gaurav-sharma-hijacks-speaker-ceremony-to-accuse-trevor-mallard)).
- Opposition spokesperson for Whānau Ora and associate Māori development (2023–2026).

## Record & notable actions

- His Official Information (Parliamentary Under-Secretaries) Amendment Bill, bringing under-secretaries under the OIA, became law in 2016 ([Parliament](https://www.parliament.nz/en/pb/bills-and-laws/bills-proposed-laws/document/00DBHOH_BILL64530_1/official-information-parliamentary-under-secretaries)).
- As Speaker, referred Education Minister Jan Tinetti to the Privileges Committee (2023) and ruled that Meka Whaitiri would sit as an independent without triggering the waka-jumping law ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/489210/speaker-rules-meka-whaitiri-not-affected-by-waka-jumping-law)).
- **Conscience votes:** against the End of Life Choice Act (2019) and the Abortion Legislation Act (2020).

## Controversies

- His Whaitiri ruling was criticised by National and ACT for avoiding the waka-jumping law (see above).
