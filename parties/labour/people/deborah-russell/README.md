## Current status

Russell is Labour's revenue and climate change spokesperson and associate finance spokesperson (No. 16 in the shadow cabinet). In June 2026 she announced she would stand list-only, having contested New Lynn at every election from 2017 to 2023; she is No. 27 on the list ([Wikipedia: 2026 NZ general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_New_Zealand_general_election); [policy.nz](https://policy.nz/2026/party-vote/parties/labour-party)).

## Biography

Born 14 January 1966 in Whangamōmona, Taranaki, Russell attended Sacred Heart Girls' College, New Plymouth, and holds a BCom (Hons) from Otago, a BA (Hons) from Massey and a PhD in philosophy from the Australian National University. She worked as an accountant and policy analyst and lectured in taxation and ethics, latterly as a senior lecturer in tax at Massey University; she co-wrote *Tax and Fairness* (2017) with Terry Baucher ([BWB](https://www.bwb.co.nz/books/tax-and-fairness/)). She is married to academic Malcolm Wright; they have three daughters.

### Career summary

- Lost Rangitīkei in 2014. **MP for New Lynn 2017–2023** (succeeding David Cunliffe; majorities 2,825 in 2017 and 13,134 in 2020); lost New Lynn to National's Paulo Garcia by 1,013 in 2023 and returned on the list ([2017](http://www.electionresults.govt.nz/electionresults_2017/electorate-details-31.html); [2023](https://www.electionresults.govt.nz/electionresults_2023/electorate-details-28.html)).
- Chaired the Environment Committee (2017–2019) and the Finance and Expenditure Committee (2019–2020); Parliamentary Under-Secretary for Revenue (2020).
- **Minister (outside Cabinet), Jan–Nov 2023:** Statistics, Earthquake Commission, associate justice and revenue; responsible for the 2023 Census ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/492870/minister-declares-census-a-success-despite-return-estimates-falling-short-of-target)).

## Record & notable actions

- **Member's bills passed:** Family Proceedings (Dissolution for Family Violence) Amendment Act 2024, letting victims of family violence divorce without the two-year separation period (passed unanimously, October 2024) ([AP](https://apnews.com/article/divorce-zealand-marriage-domestic-abuse-violence-women-42f9d84eb0706f43e4cd9ec199fc5024)); Companies (Address Information) Amendment Act 2025, letting directors keep home addresses off the public register ([LawNews](https://lawnews.nz/premium/directors-get-the-right-to-keep-home-addresses-secret/)); her sexual-harassment personal-grievance bill (12 months instead of 90 days) passed unanimously in 2023 under Marja Lubeck's name.
- **Conscience votes:** supported the Abortion Legislation Bill at all stages; voted for the End of Life Choice Bill at first reading but against it at its final stages, citing concerns for disabled people.

## Controversies

- **Small business remarks (April 2020):** told the Epidemic Response Committee that the COVID-19 lockdown had exposed structural weakness in the small-business sector; Grant Robertson distanced himself and critics called the remarks insensitive ([Stuff](https://www.stuff.co.nz/business/prosper/121173801/coronavirus-labour-mp-deborah-russell-blames-businesses-for-struggling-during-covid19-lockdown)).
- As an academic in 2016 she had tweeted against removing GST from fruit and vegetables, which became Labour policy in 2023; she said her view had changed as food prices became less stable ([Stuff](https://www.stuff.co.nz/national/politics/300949503/live-last-remaining-covid19-restrictions-removed)).
