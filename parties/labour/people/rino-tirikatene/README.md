## Current status

Tirikatene resigned from Parliament with effect from 28 January 2024, without a valedictory speech, saying he wanted to "reset and take up new opportunities"; Tracey McLellan took his list seat ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/te-manu-korihi/507639/labour-s-rino-tirikatene-retires-from-politics)). On 18 July 2026 he confirmed he had joined New Zealand First ([NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/nz/politics/former-labour-minister-rino-tirikatene-joins-nz-first/Y7PIEBVNH5DSZF66WK6GTNODMI/)); he does not appear among 2026 candidates on policy.nz.

## Biography

Born in Rangiora in 1972 (Ngāi Tahu, Ngāti Hine), Tirikatene is the grandson of Sir Eruera Tirikatene and nephew of Whetu Tirikatene-Sullivan, who together held Southern Maori for 64 years. He was a commercial lawyer at Simpson Grierson and worked in Māori economic development ([Stuff](http://www.stuff.co.nz/the-press/opinion/columnists/tahu-potiki/5990395/When-politics-is-in-the-DNA)).

### Career summary

- Stood in Te Puku O Te Whenua in 1996 after his father, the original candidate, died during the campaign.
- **Te Tai Tonga MP 2011–2023:** won the seat from the Māori Party's Rahui Katene by 1,475; majority 6,855 in 2020; lost to Te Pāti Māori's Tākuta Ferris by 2,824 in 2023 and returned on the list ([2011](http://electionresults.govt.nz/electionresults_2011/electorate-69.html); [2023](https://www.electionresults.govt.nz/electionresults_2023/electorate-details-71.html)).
- Chair of the Māori Affairs Committee (2017–2020); Parliamentary Under-Secretary (2020); **Minister for Courts and Minister of State for Trade** (Jan–Nov 2023).

## Record & notable actions

- **Conscience votes:** one of four Labour MPs who voted against marriage equality in 2013 ([NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/nz/gay-marriage-how-mps-voted/WLPKK4734FBZEKJIDHBFO4MWFA/)).
