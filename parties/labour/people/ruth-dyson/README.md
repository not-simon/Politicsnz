## Current status

Dyson retired at the 2020 election and was appointed QSO in 2021. In March 2023, as deputy chair of the Earthquake Commission and Fire and Emergency NZ, she was criticised for partisan social-media posts and admitted she had not read the Crown-entity code of conduct; she undertook to follow it ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/485535/former-labour-mp-ruth-dyson-caught-up-in-political-neutrality-crackdown)). No reliable source was found for her roles in 2026.

## Biography

Born in Lower Hutt on 11 August 1957, Dyson joined Labour in 1979, worked with Fran Wilde on homosexual law reform and was elected Labour Party president in 1988, defeating Jim Anderton.

### Career summary

- **MP 1993–2020:** Lyttelton (1993–1996), list (1996–1999), Banks Peninsula (1999–2008) and Port Hills (2008–2020).
- **Fifth Labour Government:** Disability Issues (1999–2000, 2001–2008), ACC (2002–2007), Labour (2005–2007), Social Development and Employment (2007–2008); resigned briefly in October 2000 after a drink-driving conviction ([NZ Herald](http://www.nzherald.co.nz/nz/news/article.cfm?c_id=1&objectid=157822)).
- Opposition spokesperson for health, conservation, senior citizens and Canterbury earthquake recovery; senior government whip (2017–2019) and Assistant Speaker (2019–2020); chaired the Abortion Legislation Committee.

## Record & notable actions

- Led development of the New Zealand Sign Language Act 2006, making NZSL an official language ([valedictory statement](https://www.parliament.nz/en/pb/hansard-debates/rhr/document/HansS_20200804_052560000/dyson-ruth)).

## Controversies

- Drink-driving resignation (2000); a 2015 tweet calling Speaker David Carter "incompetent, biased... lazy [and] sexist" ([TVNZ](https://www.tvnz.co.nz/one-news/new-zealand/labour-mp-under-fire-for-incompetent-lazy-sexist-twitter-comments)); and the 2023 political-neutrality criticism above.
