## Current status

Nash is not standing in 2026. He confirmed in April 2025 that he was no longer a Labour member ([NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/nz/politics/former-labour-mp-stuart-nash-not-ruling-out-running-for-nz-first-at-next-election/CXEEAFO32JHFBHNY42KXMBTYHY/)), addressed NZ First's 2025 conference, and in May 2026 was announced as NZ First's candidate for Napier ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/596231/former-labour-minister-stuart-nash-switches-to-nz-first)). After he said National MP Katie Nimon had been "lazy" for taking parental leave, Christopher Luxon said Nash would not be a minister in any government he led, and NZ First accepted Nash's resignation as a candidate on 22 July 2026; he later apologised to Nimon ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/politics/772673/significant-distraction-stuart-nash-resigns-as-nz-first-candidate-after-sexist-comments)). In September 2025 he had resigned from his job at recruiter Robert Walters and from the Taxpayers' Union board after a crude on-air definition of a woman on The Platform ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/572769/former-labour-mp-stuart-nash-resigns-after-comment-about-women)).

## Biography

Born in August 1967 and adopted by Jenny and Hal Nash — making him the adoptive great-grandson of Prime Minister Sir Walter Nash — he grew up in Napier, attended Napier Boys' High School, and gained master's degrees in law, forestry science and management from the University of Canterbury before working at AUT ([Parliament profile](https://www3.parliament.nz/en/mps-and-electorates/former-members-of-parliament/nash-stuart/)). He is married to Sarah Nash and has four children.

### Career summary

- Contested Epsom for Labour in 2005; **list MP 2008–2011**; lost Napier to Chris Tremain in 2011 and briefly served as chief of staff to Labour leader David Shearer.
- **Napier MP 2014–2023:** won with a 3,850 majority in 2014 (aided by Conservative Garth McVicar splitting the right vote), increased it in 2017, and held it by 5,856 in 2020 ([2014](http://www.electionresults.govt.nz/electionresults_2014/electorate-29.html); [2020](https://www.electionresults.govt.nz/electionresults_2020/electorate-details-26.html)).
- **Minister 2017–2023:** Police, Revenue, Small Business and Fisheries (2017–2020); Economic and Regional Development, Forestry, Tourism and Small Business (2020–2023); Police again (2023) until his resignation from that portfolio in March 2023 ([DPMC 2020](https://dpmc.govt.nz/sites/default/files/2020-11/ministerial-list-2-nov-2020.pdf)).

## Controversies

- **Police independence (March 2023):** resigned as Police Minister after disclosing he had phoned the Police Commissioner urging an appeal of a sentence he considered too light ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/486012/stuart-nash-has-resigned-as-police-minister-pm-hipkins-says)). It then emerged the Attorney-General had reprimanded him in 2020 for comments about a murder accused before trial, and that he had lobbied MBIE for a constituent; Hipkins demoted him to the bottom of Cabinet on a "final warning" ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/486178/stuart-nash-demoted-to-bottom-of-cabinet-now-on-his-final-warning-hipkins)).
- **Dismissal (28 March 2023):** sacked from all portfolios after it was revealed he had emailed two donors in 2020 about confidential Cabinet discussions on commercial rent relief ([Stuff](https://www.stuff.co.nz/national/politics/131627166/stuart-nash-sacked-as-minister-after-email-to-donors-emerged)). A Cabinet Office review in June 2023 found no further confidentiality breaches but identified an unmanaged conflict of interest over the appointment of donor Phil McCaw; the Chief Ombudsman found he had breached the Official Information Act by withholding the donor emails ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/492084/stuart-nash-did-not-share-confidential-information-with-other-donors-probe-finds); [Newsroom](https://www.newsroom.co.nz/stuart-nash-breached-official-information-laws-ombudsman)). He announced on 3 April 2023 that he would not stand again.
- **Groundswell remarks (2021):** said the farmer protest group's website promoted racism and vaccine hesitancy, which its organisers rejected ([Stuff](https://www.stuff.co.nz/national/127032009/nash-says-groundswell-stands-for-racist-antivax-sentiment)).
- **Post-politics:** criticised Labour's gang-asset law as too weak and alleged Kiri Allan had blocked tougher measures (2024); Willie Jackson accused him of grandstanding ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/510204/stuart-nash-claims-labour-cabinet-softened-its-gang-asset-seizure-policy)). Crude remarks about women (2025, 2026) — see Current status.
