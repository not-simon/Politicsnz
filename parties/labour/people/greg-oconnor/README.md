## Current status

O'Connor is leaving Parliament at the 2026 election. His Ōhāriu electorate was abolished in the boundary review; after failing to win the Wellington North selection he sought a list-only place, aiming to become Speaker, but he is not on Labour's 2026 list — The Post reported on 8 June 2026 that he decided not to put his name forward, criticising a list process that "makes choosing Pope look transparent", while his Wikipedia article describes it as Labour not selecting him ([NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/nz/wellington/greg-oconnor-confirms-re-election-bid-wants-to-become-speaker/XLJLC5ZA3JFF5K42WRGWYF5PKI/); [RNZ list](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/597538/who-s-in-who-s-out-labour-unveils-list-for-november-election); [The Post](https://www.thepost.co.nz/politics/361018627/greg-oconnor-not-standing-after-labour-list-process-makes-choosing-pope-look-transparent)). He then stood as an independent in a Wellington City Council by-election for the Takapū/Northern ward, backed by Mayor Andrew Little, and was elected unopposed when nominations closed on 1 October 2026 ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/regions/1672610/greg-o-connor-elected-unopposed-as-new-wellington-councillor)).

## Biography

Born 12 May 1958 in the Buller District and educated at Buller High School, O'Connor served almost four decades in the New Zealand Police (retiring as a senior sergeant), including undercover work, and was president of the Police Association for a record 21 years (1995–2016), during which he advocated arming frontline police ([NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/nz/greg-oconnor-a-life-on-the-front-line/6DJ7KPPHI5FOFVY4RLRB57IQII/)). He is married to Desley and has three children; Damien O'Connor is his cousin.

### Career summary

- **Ōhāriu MP 2017–2026:** won in 2017 after Peter Dunne withdrew (majority 1,051), then by 11,961 (2020) and 1,260 over Nicola Willis (2023) ([2017](http://www.electionresults.govt.nz/electionresults_2017/electorate-details-36.html); [2020](https://www.electionresults.govt.nz/electionresults_2020/electorate-details-33.html); [2023](https://archive.electionresults.govt.nz/electionresults_2023/electorate-details-33.html)).
- Chair of the Transport and Infrastructure Committee (2020–2022); **Deputy Speaker** from 25 August 2022; Assistant Speaker in the 54th Parliament, and spokesperson for courts and veterans.

## Record & notable actions

- Member's bill extending child-sex-offender registration to overseas travel reporting passed its first reading in August 2022 ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/national/programmes/the-house/audio/2018852160/a-week-knee-deep-in-bills)).
- **Conscience votes:** supported the End of Life Choice Act; supported the Abortion Legislation Bill at first reading but opposed it at second and third readings (2020).

## Controversies

- Criticised in July 2020 for a social-media post complaining about the COVID-19 MP pay cut ([NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/nz/labour-mp-greg-oconnor-slammed-for-tone-deaf-post-about-covid-pay-cut/6PRIOW4Q5LLS24IQFSRT34TU74/)).
