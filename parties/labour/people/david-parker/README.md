## Current status

Parker announced on 8 April 2025 that he would leave Parliament, denying it was due to internal disputes over tax policy; his resignation took effect on 12 May 2025 and Vanushi Walters replaced him as list MP and shadow attorney-general ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/557515/labour-stalwart-and-former-cabinet-minister-david-parker-resigns-from-parliament); [NZ Gazette](https://gazette.govt.nz/notice/id/2025-ps2424)). He has since told the Sunday Star-Times he backs cutting income tax in exchange for a capital gains tax, an annual wealth tax and an inheritance tax on estates over $1m ([The Post](https://www.thepost.co.nz/business/360688375/being-david-parker-bold-ideas-find-audience-years-later)). He is not a 2026 candidate.

## Biography

Born in 1960 in Roxburgh and raised in Dunedin (Otago Boys' High School), Parker graduated BCom and LLB from the University of Otago and co-founded the Dunedin Community Law Centre while a student ([Beehive biography](https://www.beehive.govt.nz/minister/biography/david-parker)). He practised resource-management and civil litigation law (managing partner of Anderson Lloyd), ran a café, and was a business manager for entrepreneur Howard Paterson, serving as founding chief executive of companies including A2 Corporation and Blis Technologies ([NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/nz/electricity-sparks-parkers-political-ambitions/5P5W47CPHOA3OFS7ODDM6IN3KQ/)). He has three children with his former wife, poet Sue Wootton.

### Career summary

- **Otago MP 2002–2005** (an upset win by 648 votes from list position 47); list MP 2005–2025 after losing Otago (2005), Waitaki (2008) and contesting Epsom (2011, 2017).
- **Fifth Labour Government:** Attorney-General, Minister of Transport, Energy and Climate Change Issues from October 2005; resigned in March 2006 over a Companies Office filing allegation, was cleared, and returned to Cabinet in May 2006; later Minister of State Services and acting Environment Minister.
- **Opposition 2008–2017:** finance spokesperson and economic policy architect for Labour's 2011 and 2014 platforms (capital gains tax, raising the pension age); deputy leader under David Cunliffe (2013–2014) and interim leader after the 2014 election; finished third in the 2014 leadership vote ([Stuff](http://www.stuff.co.nz/national/politics/63305825/Andrew-Little-new-Labour-Party-leader-by-a-whisker)). Later foreign affairs, trade, environment spokesperson and shadow attorney-general.
- **Sixth Labour Government 2017–2023:** Attorney-General throughout; Minister for the Environment (2017–2023), Trade and Export Growth (2017–2020), Economic Development (2017–2019), Associate Finance; from 2020 also Oceans and Fisheries and Revenue; briefly Transport (2023). Dubbed "the minister of almost everything" ([Stuff](https://www.stuff.co.nz/national/politics/103235167/national-portrait-minister-of-almost-everything-david-parker)).
- **Opposition 2023–2025:** shadow attorney-general and foreign affairs spokesperson; chair of the Regulations Review Committee.

## Record & notable actions

- **Foreign-buyer ban:** led the Overseas Investment Amendment Act 2018 banning most foreign purchases of existing homes ([Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2018/aug/15/tenants-on-our-own-land-new-zealand-bans-sale-of-homes-to-foreign-buyers)).
- **Trade:** signed the CPTPP (March 2018), led ratification of PACER Plus, launched UK FTA talks, and cancelled the Saudi "sheep deal" he had exposed in opposition ([NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/nz/government-axes-saudi-sheep-deal/7KXXQSKRAU26OR2A737MPGM2TY/)).
- **Resource management:** replaced the RMA with the Natural and Built Environment Act 2023 and Spatial Planning Act 2023 (passed 16 August 2023; repealed by the next government in December 2023) ([NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/nz/politics/government-passes-rma-replacement-bills-ending-30-years-of-rma/EUKOHYGWNNA3TDZYQYRI64446U/)); Essential Freshwater rules including winter-grazing controls (2020), criticised by farm lobbies as unworkable and by some scientists as too weak.
- **Emissions Trading Scheme:** as climate change minister in 2008 he legislated the NZ ETS ([NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/nz/climate-change-bill-becomes-law/BXWMGB3XSP4X6BJHJECTRAFI54/)).
- **Attorney-General:** steered the COVID-19 Public Health Response Act 2020 (the High Court later found the first nine days of the 2020 lockdown, while justified, were not lawful) ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/national/423917/high-court-rules-some-of-covid-19-level-4-lockdown-was-unlawful)); oversaw the appointment of Helen Winkelmann as Chief Justice and of Joe Williams, the first Māori Supreme Court judge; passed the Bill of Rights (Declarations of Inconsistency) Amendment Act 2022.
- **Tax fairness:** commissioned IRD research (2023) finding the wealthiest families paid a lower effective tax rate than most New Zealanders ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/national/488705/wealthiest-paying-tax-at-much-lower-rate-than-most-other-new-zealanders-ird-report)); passed the Tax Principles Reporting Act 2023, repealed under urgency in December 2023 ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/505298/parliament-repeals-tax-principles-reporting-law-under-urgency)).
- **Conscience votes:** for civil unions (2004), marriage equality (2013), and the End of Life Choice Act (2019); against the 2003 euthanasia bill.

## Controversies

- **2006 resignation:** resigned as Attorney-General and from Cabinet after a former business partner alleged he filed an incorrect Companies Office declaration; the Companies Office cleared him and he was reappointed ([NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/nz/parker-heads-back-into-the-cabinet/G5FSJ3CZB4UEZG7X4VFSGOKBJA/)).
- **Clare Curran hiring (2007):** the State Services Commission cleared him of improper pressure in suggesting the environment ministry hire Labour activist Clare Curran, though critics disputed the finding ([NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/nz/no-ministerial-pressure-in-curran-job-report/LXTB3L3NSF3CFOBVXXP6DMOXKY/)).
- **GST on KiwiSaver fees (2022):** a proposal to apply GST to KiwiSaver fees was dropped after a backlash; the 2022 cost-of-living payment was also criticised for paying people who were not eligible ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/473822/government-drops-plans-to-charge-gst-on-kiwisaver-fees)).
- **Resignation as revenue minister (July 2023):** quit the portfolio, calling it "untenable" after Hipkins ruled out his wealth/capital gains tax work; some commentators called the timing petulant, others principled ([Stuff](https://www.stuff.co.nz/national/politics/132611615/david-parker-untenable-to-remain-revenue-minister-after-wealth-tax-rejection); [NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/nz/politics/claire-trevett-david-parkers-ill-timed-bout-of-principle-on-taxes-delivers-a-headache-for-chris-hipkins/2R5I3YCCKJEM3JLBMLI3STGFEU/)).
- **EPA chair dismissal (2023):** removed Rob Campbell as Environmental Protection Authority chair after Campbell publicly criticised National's Three Waters stance in breach of neutrality expectations ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/485149/ousted-health-boss-rob-campbell-also-dumped-as-chair-of-epa)).
