## Current status

Edmonds is Labour's finance spokesperson — Labour's choice for Minister of Finance if it wins in 2026 — having taken over from Grant Robertson in February 2024 ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/509662/labour-reshuffle-edmonds-moves-up-as-finance-spokesperson)); since March 2025 she has also held economy, savings and investment and led Labour's economic team ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/544048/chris-hipkins-announces-new-labour-economic-team-tangi-utikere-promoted)). She is No. 3 on Labour's 2026 list and is standing in Kenepuru ([policy.nz](https://policy.nz/2026/party-vote/parties/labour-party)). In May 2026 Labour declined to release details of its "Future Fund" policy before the election; Edmonds said Treaty obligations attached to some state-owned enterprises were the reason ([Wikipedia: Shadow Cabinet of Chris Hipkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shadow_Cabinet_of_Chris_Hipkins)).

## Biography

Born in 1981 on Auckland's North Shore to parents who migrated from Samoa in 1978, Edmonds spoke Samoan as her first language; her mother died when she was four ([E-Tangata](https://e-tangata.co.nz/korero/barbara-edmonds-sacrifice-and-success/)). She was head girl of Carmel College (1998) and graduated LLB/BA from the University of Auckland in 2008 ([Newsroom](http://newsroom.co.nz/2024/02/29/labours-golden-girl-slated-as-future-prime-minister/)). A specialist tax lawyer, she worked at Inland Revenue, was seconded in 2016 as private secretary to National revenue ministers Michael Woodhouse and Judith Collins, and from 2017 was a political adviser to Labour minister Stuart Nash, working on the post-Christchurch firearms ban legislation. She and her husband Chris have eight children.

### Career summary

- **Mana, 2020–present.** Selected to succeed Kris Faafoi; won by 16,244 votes in 2020 and 7,324 (election-night count) in 2023 ([2020](https://www.electionresults.govt.nz/electionresults_2020/electorate-details-20.html), [2023](https://archive.electionresults.govt.nz/electionresults_2023/electorate-details-20.html)).
- **First term:** associate whip; deputy chair then (from June 2022) chair of the Finance and Expenditure Committee. The NZ Herald's Audrey Young ranked her the second-best of the 2020 intake ([NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/nz/politics/rookie-mps-the-best-from-the-class-of-2020/HN5LRPHI3VEZNOW5NSXJ73NUT4/)).
- **Minister, 2023.** Entered Cabinet under Hipkins on 31 January 2023 as Minister of Internal Affairs and Minister for Pacific Peoples (plus associate health and housing); added Associate Cyclone Recovery (Feb), Economic Development (Apr, after Stuart Nash's dismissal), Revenue and Associate Finance (Jul, after David Parker's and Kiri Allan's departures) ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/483394/prime-minister-chris-hipkins-reveals-cabinet-reshuffle); [1News](https://www.1news.co.nz/2023/07/24/hipkins-reveals-who-gets-kiri-allans-ministerial-posts/)).
- **Opposition:** economic development, infrastructure and associate finance spokesperson (Nov 2023), then finance (Feb 2024).

## Record & notable actions

- As revenue minister, announced with Grant Robertson a digital services tax on large multinationals (August 2023) ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/496826/government-unveils-digital-services-tax-aimed-at-multinationals)).
- Launched consultation on retirement-village law changes as associate housing minister ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/495009/government-proposes-changes-to-law-governing-retirement-villages)); led New Zealand's first all-female trade delegation to Australia ([NZTE](https://www.nzte.govt.nz/blog/new-zealands-first-all-female-trade-delegation)).
