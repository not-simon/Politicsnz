## Current status

Jackson is No. 4 on Labour's 2026 list (list-only) ([policy.nz](https://policy.nz/2026/party-vote/parties/labour-party)) and, since the 11 March 2026 reshuffle, Labour's spokesperson for Māori development, Māori–Crown relations and associate health (Māori) ([Stuff](https://www.stuff.co.nz/politics/360949687/hipkins-reshuffles-labour-portfolios-henare-departs-and-plan-hatched-maori-seats-race)). He has again led Labour's campaign for the Māori electorates; Labour won only one of the seven in 2023, after which he said he had considered quitting ([NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/kahu/senior-mp-willie-jackson-to-leave-politics-following-hefty-defeat-at-the-polls-but-only-if-hes-not-wanted/MTUJK4PQDJBKFNX7PZNZOCAPLY/)).

## Biography

Born in 1961 (Ngāti Maniapoto, Ngāti Porou), the son of urban Māori leaders Dame June Jackson and Bob Jackson; his uncles included activist Syd Jackson and lawyer Moana Jackson ([The Spinoff](https://thespinoff.co.nz/atea/28-02-2019/the-incredible-legacy-of-dame-june-jackson)). After leaving Māngere College he became a freezing worker and, at 21, president of the freezing workers' union. In the 1990s he ran Tangata Records and managed Moana and the Moahunters. A broadcaster for two decades, he ran Radio Waatea and Urban Māori Broadcasting, hosted *Eye to Eye* (TV One, 2004–2009) and talkback on RadioLive with John Tamihere. He chaired the National Urban Māori Authority (from 2003) and led the Manukau Urban Māori Authority (from 2009), whose subsidiary won a charter-school contract in 2017 ([NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/kahu/labour-candidate-willie-jackson-benefits-from-new-round-of-charter-schools/G676BTHOOKBLSAFRVUKYOFEZKE/)).

- **Alliance MP, 1999–2002** (Mana Motuhake); led Mana Motuhake from 2001 and was Alliance deputy leader at the 2002 election, when the party lost all its seats.
- **Labour list MP, 2017–present.** Recruited by Andrew Little to run Labour's Māori-seat campaign in 2017, when Labour won all seven Māori electorates ([RNZ](http://www.radionz.co.nz/news/political/323829/willie-jackson-to-stand-for-labour)).
- **Minister:** Minister of Employment (outside Cabinet) and Associate Minister for Māori Development, 2017–2020; Minister for Māori Development in Cabinet from November 2020, plus associate ACC and justice; Minister of Broadcasting and Media from June 2022 ([DPMC 2020](https://dpmc.govt.nz/sites/default/files/2020-11/ministerial-list-2-nov-2020.pdf)); co-chair of Labour's Māori caucus.
- **Opposition:** Māori development, broadcasting, employment and associate portfolios (2023); Māori development and social development (March 2025); Māori development, Māori–Crown relations and associate health (Māori) (March 2026).

## Record & notable actions

- **Public media merger:** introduced the Aotearoa New Zealand Public Media Bill to merge RNZ and TVNZ (2022); it was scrapped by the Hipkins government in February 2023 ([Parliament](https://www.parliament.nz/en/pb/bills-and-laws/bills-proposed-laws/document/BILL_125298/aotearoa-new-zealand-public-media-bill); [Stuff](https://www.stuff.co.nz/national/politics/131174007/rnztvnz-merger-gone-minimum-wage-rises-policies-delayed-in-government-refocus)).
- Apologised to Moriori on behalf of Ngāti Tama and Ngāti Mutunga as the Moriori Claims Settlement passed in 2021 ([NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/nz/moriori-praised-for-dedication-patience-as-18m-and-apology-settlement-with-crown-becomes-law/VR32NHE6MDBEHZE6ZL5Z2X5NDU/)).
- A leading opponent of ACT's Treaty Principles Bill; won a 2024 Oxford Union debate calling for the return of mokomōkai from the British Museum ([1News](https://www.1news.co.nz/2024/05/24/willie-jackson-calls-for-return-of-mokomokai-in-oxford-debate-win/)).

## Controversies

- **Roast Busters interview (2013):** he and John Tamihere were taken off air at RadioLive after an interview with a friend of an alleged victim in which the group's actions were described as "mischief" ([NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/nz/roast-busters-radiolive-hosts-taken-off-air/C4HJSTJI55U546Q2OUS4OA7WUY/?c_id=1&objectid=11155132)).
- **Jack Tame interview (Dec 2022):** a combative Q+A interview on the media merger, in which he questioned the host's motives, drew criticism including from Ardern; he gave a partial apology ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/480223/willie-jackson-gives-partial-apology-over-public-media-merger-interview)).
- **"Go to war" remark (Nov 2023):** said Māori would "go to war" if the incoming government pursued a Treaty principles referendum; ACT leader David Seymour responded that people who "threaten war" instead of debate "are not being a good actor" ([Newshub](https://www.newshub.co.nz/home/politics/2023/11/labour-mp-willie-jackson-warns-of-m-ori-uprising-over-act-s-proposed-treaty-referendum.html)).
- **Ejections from the House:** in December 2021 (refusing to withdraw calling ACT "right-wing fascists"), November 2024 (calling David Seymour a liar during the Treaty Principles Bill first reading) and May 2025 ([NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/nz/politics/maori-development-minister-willie-jackson-kicked-out-of-parliament-for-right-wing-fascist-comment/OIZQJ3QM42VSM3GSSLE7YLLIYY/); [RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/533792/watch-labour-s-willie-jackson-ejected-from-house-for-calling-david-seymour-a-liar-during-treaty-principles-bill-reading); [1News](https://www.1news.co.nz/2025/05/15/labours-willie-jackson-booted-from-house/)).
