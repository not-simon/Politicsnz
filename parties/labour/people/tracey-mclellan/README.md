## Current status

McLellan is Labour's junior whip (since March 2025) and corrections and Christchurch issues spokesperson (No. 22 in the shadow cabinet). She is contesting Banks Peninsula again at No. 28 on the list ([policy.nz](https://policy.nz/2026/party-vote/parties/labour-party)).

## Biography

Born in Sydney on 20 May 1970 and raised in a Southland state house, McLellan holds a master's from Massey and a PhD in psychology from the University of Canterbury, where she researched sports injuries and concussion; she later organised for the NZ Nurses Organisation ([Newsroom](https://www.newsroom.co.nz/2020/01/07/952026?slug=the-sure-things-tracey-mclellan)). A Labour member since 2011, she became party vice-president in 2019 and briefly acting president after Nigel Haworth resigned — she had sat on the panel that dismissed the initial complaints in the 2019 staffer misconduct case ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/398894/labour-abuse-allegations-investigator-brings-in-computer-expert)). She is a single mother of two sons.

- **Banks Peninsula MP 2020–2023:** won by 13,156; led on election night 2023 but lost to National's Vanessa Weenink on special votes, and was initially out of Parliament ([The Post](https://www.thepost.co.nz/politics/350103743/labours-tracey-mclellan-out-parliament-after-special-votes-counted)).
