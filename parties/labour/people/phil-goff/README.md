## Current status

Goff retired from elected politics in 2022 after two terms as Auckland mayor and served as High Commissioner in London from January 2023. On 6 March 2025 Winston Peters dismissed him after he questioned, at a Chatham House event, whether Donald Trump understood history (invoking Churchill's criticism of the Munich Agreement); Luxon backed the decision, Hipkins said Goff had "overstepped the mark", and Helen Clark accused the government of placating the Trump administration ([Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2025/mar/06/phil-goff-donald-trump-comments-new-zealand-high-commissioner-removed-chatham-house-ntwnfb); [Stuff](https://www.stuff.co.nz/politics/360604666/comment-cost-phil-goff-his-job-high-commissioner)). He later said he had "no regrets" ([1News](https://www.1news.co.nz/2025/04/04/no-regrets-phil-goff-breaks-silence-on-trump-remark-sacking/)).

## Biography

Born 22 June 1953 and raised in Three Kings, Auckland, Goff left home at 16 to attend university, working at a freezing works and as a cleaner to gain an MA (first-class honours) in political studies at the University of Auckland; he later organised for the Insurance Workers Union ([Newshub](https://www.newshub.co.nz/general/candidate-profile-phil-goff-2011100311)). He married Mary Ellen Goff in 1979; they have three adult children. He was appointed CNZM in 2017.

### Career summary

- **MP for Roskill/Mt Roskill 1981–1990 and 1993–2016.** Youngest member of David Lange's 1984 Cabinet (Housing, Environment, later Employment, Tertiary Education and Education).
- **Fifth Labour Government:** Minister of Foreign Affairs and Trade and Justice (1999–2005); Defence, Trade, Disarmament and Pacific Island Affairs, and Associate Finance (2005–2008).
- **Labour leader and Leader of the Opposition, 11 Nov 2008 – 13 Dec 2011**; Labour fell to 27.5% in 2011 and he stood down. Later spokesperson for foreign affairs, then defence and Auckland.
- Resigned from Parliament on 12 October 2016 after winning the Auckland mayoralty, triggering the Mt Roskill by-election won by Michael Wood ([NZ Gazette](https://www.gazette.govt.nz/notice/id/2016-ps5847)).

## Record & notable actions

- Signed the New Zealand–China Free Trade Agreement in Beijing on 7 April 2008 ([Stuff](http://www.stuff.co.nz/4466946a27013.html)); involved in the 2001 decision to accept 131 Tampa refugees.
- Introduced student fees and loans as tertiary education minister (late 1980s).
- As opposition leader campaigned against raising GST to 15% and against mining on conservation land (2010).
- As Auckland mayor (2016–2022) barred far-right speakers Lauren Southern and Stefan Molyneux from council venues (2018), prompting a free-speech legal challenge that was withdrawn ([RNZ](https://www.radionz.co.nz/news/national/362611/free-speech-coalition-withdraws-urgent-legal-action-over-goff-s-southern-molyneux-decision)), and opposed the Three Waters mandate (2021).

## Controversies

- **SFO inquiry (2020–2022):** the Serious Fraud Office investigated his 2016 mayoral campaign's declaration of auction donations; it closed the inquiry in April 2022 with no charges ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/national/464825/sfo-ends-two-year-probe-of-donations-to-auckland-mayor-s-election-campaigns)).
- **Coronation remarks (2023):** apologised after offending Kiingi Tūheitia's delegation in London by forgetting a karakia and saying no one present had experienced a coronation ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/489302/high-commissioner-to-the-uk-phil-goff-offends-maori-king-with-coronation-comments-forgotten-karakia)).
- **Trump remarks and dismissal (2025):** see Current status.
