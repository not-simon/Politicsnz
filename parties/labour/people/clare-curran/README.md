## Current status

Curran left Parliament in 2020. She has served on the University of Otago Council and the board of Network for Learning, chairs the Dunedin Night Shelter Trust, and has been chair and later co-general manager of Life Matters Suicide Prevention Trust; she has also been writing a crime novel ([The Post](https://www.thepost.co.nz/nz-news/350156526/life-after-politics-finding-new-buzz-beyond-beehive); [ODT](https://www.odt.co.nz/news/dunedin/health/curran-takes-hands-role-life-matters)).

## Biography

Born in 1960 and raised in Dunedin, Curran holds a BA from Otago and BA (Hons) in anthropology from Victoria. She worked in communications for Australian unions and in public relations in Dunedin, joined Labour in 2006 and sat on its national council ([Parliament profile](http://www.parliament.nz/en-NZ/MPP/MPs/MPs/4/2/4/50MP169711-Curran-Clare.htm)).

### Career summary

- **Dunedin South MP 2008–2020:** won selection over sitting MP David Benson-Pope in 2007; majorities 6,449 (2008) and 4,175 (2011) ([2011](http://www.electionresults.govt.nz/electionresults_2011/electorate-9.html)).
- Opposition spokesperson for communications and IT and broadcasting.
- **Minister 2017–2018:** Broadcasting, Communications and Digital Media; Government Digital Services; associate ACC and open government. Removed from Cabinet in August 2018 and resigned as a minister on 7 September 2018 ([Stuff](https://www.stuff.co.nz/national/politics/106908738/clare-curran-resigns-as-minister)).

## Controversies

- **Ministry for the Environment contract (2006):** a State Services Commission inquiry found the ministry failed to identify the conflict of interest when it hired her, as a Labour activist, on Minister David Parker's office's recommendation; the ministry's chief executive resigned ([Stuff](http://www.stuff.co.nz/dominion-post/news/politics/49148/Setchell-found-other-public-jobs-closed-to-her)).
- **Undisclosed meetings (2018):** a meeting with RNZ executive Carol Hirschfeld (who resigned after RNZ's account proved wrong) and a second unrecorded meeting with Derek Handley about the chief technology officer role led Ardern to remove her from Cabinet; after struggling to answer questions about use of personal Gmail for ministerial business she resigned, citing "relentless pressure" ([Newshub](https://www.newshub.co.nz/home/politics/2018/08/prime-minister-removes-clare-curran-from-cabinet.html)).
- In 2020 she described bullying she had experienced in politics, including a 2012 incident in which National MPs mocked her with a toilet seat bearing her photo ([The Spinoff](https://thespinoff.co.nz/politics/04-07-2020/clare-curran-interview-donna-chisholm/)).
