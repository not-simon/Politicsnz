## Current status

Twyford is No. 25 in the shadow cabinet (immigration, disarmament and arms control, associate foreign affairs) and is contesting Henderson at No. 40 on the 2026 list ([policy.nz](https://policy.nz/2026/party-vote/parties/labour-party)). He narrowly held Te Atatū in 2023 by 131 votes over National's Angee Nicholas ([2023 results](https://archive.electionresults.govt.nz/electionresults_2023/electorate-details-53.html)).

## Biography

Born 4 May 1963 in Auckland, Twyford attended Westlake Boys High School and graduated BA from the University of Auckland. He was the founding director of Oxfam New Zealand and earlier worked as a journalist and union organiser ([Labour profile](https://www.labour.org.nz/philtwyford)).

### Career summary

- List MP 2008–2011 after contesting North Shore (2005, 2008); MP for Te Atatū since 2011 (majority 10,508 in 2020, 131 in 2023) ([2020](https://www.electionresults.govt.nz/electionresults_2020/electorate-details-53.html)).
- **Opposition 2008–2017:** spokesperson roles in development assistance, disarmament, transport, housing (2013–2017) and building and construction; ranked 11th under David Shearer.
- **Minister 2017–2023:** Minister of Housing and Urban Development and Minister of Transport (2017–2019); after the June 2019 reshuffle, Urban Development, Transport and Economic Development (2019–2020); outside Cabinet from 2020 as Minister for Disarmament and Arms Control and Minister of State for Trade (2020–2023); dropped from the ministry by Hipkins on 31 January 2023 ([Stuff](https://www.stuff.co.nz/national/politics/113821469/pm-takes-housing-off-phil-twyford-in-first-major-reshuffle); [Stuff](https://www.stuff.co.nz/national/politics/131111121/the-winners-and-losers-in-prime-minister-chris-hipkins-cabinet-reshuffle)).

## Record & notable actions

- **KiwiBuild:** the flagship pledge to build 100,000 affordable homes by 2028. In January 2019 he conceded only about 300 of the first-year target of 1,000 homes would be completed by July 2019 ([Stuff](https://www.stuff.co.nz/national/politics/110113848/phil-twyford-says-only-300-kiwibuild-homes-are-due-to-be-finished-by-july)); housing was moved to Megan Woods in June 2019, and her September 2019 "reset" abandoned the 100,000-homes target ([Wikipedia: KiwiBuild](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KiwiBuild)).
- Championed the 2018 foreign-buyer ban on existing homes ([Newshub](https://www.newshub.co.nz/home/politics/2017/11/foreign-buyer-ban-will-work-phil-twyford-insists.html)) and, as Transport Minister, the initial Auckland light-rail process.
- **Member's bills:** Local Government (Protection of Auckland Assets) Amendment Bill (defeated 2009); Depleted Uranium (Prohibition) Bill (failed on a tied vote, 2012) ([NZ Herald](http://www.nzherald.co.nz/nz/news/article.cfm?c_id=1&objectid=10815942)).

## Controversies

- **"Chinese-sounding names" data (2015):** as housing spokesperson he released analysis claiming buyers with Chinese surnames were buying a disproportionate share of Auckland homes; it was widely criticised as flawed and racially stigmatising ([Stuff](https://www.stuff.co.nz/business/money/70155168/labours-halfbaked-property-data-turns-chinese-buyers-into-scapegoats)).
- **In-flight phone call (May 2018):** stripped of civil aviation responsibilities after making a phone call as his plane was taxiing for take-off, breaching civil aviation rules; Ardern declined his offer to resign as Transport Minister ([Stuff](https://www.stuff.co.nz/national/politics/104196693/minister-phil-twyford-apologises-for-civil-aviation-breach)).
- **KiwiBuild shortfall:** the programme's failure to meet its targets was a major political problem for the government ([NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/nz/news/article.cfm?c_id=1&objectid=12195042)).
