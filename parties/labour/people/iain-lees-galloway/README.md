## Current status

Lees-Galloway joined The Opportunity Party in 2024, saying he did not plan to stand, and by 2025 was its general manager; in August 2025 he advertised TOP's vacant leadership on Seek, and in February 2026 said TOP would run about 30 candidates in 2026 ([Stuff](https://www.stuff.co.nz/politics/360792451/leaderless-political-party-thats-turned-seek); [The Spinoff](https://thespinoff.co.nz/politics/16-02-2026/iain-lees-galloway-on-crashing-out-of-parliament-and-his-new-opportunity)).

## Biography

Born 18 September 1978 in Auckland, Lees-Galloway attended King's College, was Massey University Students' Association president (2005) and worked as an organiser for the NZ Nurses Organisation. He is married to Clare, a teacher; they have three children ([Beehive biography](https://www.beehive.govt.nz/minister/biography/iain-lees-galloway)).

### Career summary

- **Palmerston North MP 2008–2020:** majorities 1,117 (2008), 3,285 (2011), 2,212 (2014, over mayor Jono Naylor) and 6,392 (2017) ([2017](https://www.electionresults.govt.nz/electionresults_2017/electorate-details-39.html)).
- Junior whip (2013–2014); spokesperson for workplace relations, transport and veterans.
- **Minister 2017–2020:** Workplace Relations and Safety, Immigration and ACC; dismissed on 22 July 2020.

## Record & notable actions

- Led Labour's campaign that ended zero-hour contracts; the Employment Standards Legislation Bill passed unanimously in March 2016 ([Stuff](http://www.stuff.co.nz/national/politics/77653185/labour-and-national-agree-to-scrap-zero-hour-contracts)).
- Member's bills on tobacco display bans (2010) and a 0.05 drink-drive limit (2013) prompted government legislation achieving the same aims.

## Controversies

- **Karel Sroubek (2018):** criticised for granting residency to convicted Czech drug smuggler Karel Sroubek; he later said Immigration NZ had not given him key information ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/369790/minister-stands-by-czech-drug-smuggler-decision)).
- **Dismissal (July 2020):** Ardern sacked him after he admitted an inappropriate consensual relationship with a former staff member of one of his agencies, saying he had improperly used his position of power; a review of his spending found nothing inappropriate ([Stuff](https://www.stuff.co.nz/national/politics/122208470/workplace-relations-minister-iain-leesgalloway-dismissed-following-relationship-with-staffer)).
- **Southern/Molyneux visas (2018):** granted visas to the far-right speakers while describing their views as contrary to NZ values ([NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/nz/news/article.cfm?c_id=1&objectid=12092243)).
