## Current status

Walters returned to Parliament on 12 May 2025 when David Parker resigned, and is Labour's shadow attorney-general; in March 2026 she also took foreign affairs, NZSIS and GCSB (No. 12 in the shadow cabinet) ([NZ Gazette](https://gazette.govt.nz/notice/id/2025-au2550); [Stuff](https://www.stuff.co.nz/politics/360949687/hipkins-reshuffles-labour-portfolios-henare-departs-and-plan-hatched-maori-seats-race)). She is Labour's Waitākere candidate at No. 8 on the 2026 list ([policy.nz](https://policy.nz/2026/party-vote/parties/labour-party)).

## Biography

Born in Sri Lanka in August 1981 and raised in New Zealand from age five, Walters is a great-granddaughter of Ratnasothy Saravanamuttu, the first native Mayor of Colombo ([Sri Lanka NZ](https://www.srilankanz.co.nz/2020/06/introducing-vanushi-walters-first-sri.html)). She holds BA/LLB (Hons) from the University of Auckland and a master's in international human rights law from Oxford. A human rights lawyer, she was general manager of YouthLaw Aotearoa, a member of Amnesty International's international board, a senior manager at the Human Rights Commission and a Foundation North trustee. She is married to Rhys Walters, has three sons and lives in Titirangi.

### Career summary

- **Upper Harbour MP 2020–2023:** won by 2,392 over National's Jake Bezzant — New Zealand's first Sri Lankan-born MP; chaired the Justice Committee; lost to National's Cameron Brewer by 11,192 in 2023 and was out of Parliament until May 2025 ([2020](https://www.electionresults.govt.nz/electionresults_2020/electorate-details-55.html); [2023](https://archive.electionresults.govt.nz/electionresults_2023/electorate-details-55.html)).
- Led New Zealand's delegation to the 2023 Inter-Parliamentary Union assembly, where she drafted an emergency resolution on Russia's invasion of Ukraine ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/national/programmes/the-house/audio/2018888391/backbench-mps-take-on-russia)).
