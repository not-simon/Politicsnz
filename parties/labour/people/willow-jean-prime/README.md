## Current status

Prime is No. 8 in the shadow cabinet (social development, children, associate education – Māori) and No. 7 on Labour's 2026 list. She was selected on 5 February 2026 to contest the Māori electorate of Te Tai Tokerau, held by Te Pāti Māori's Mariameno Kapa-Kingi ([Stuff](https://www.stuff.co.nz/politics/360931874/greens-labour-and-te-pati-maori-gear-intense-te-tai-tokerau-battle)). In the 11 March 2026 reshuffle she moved from education to social development ([NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/nz/politics/election-2026-labour-party-announces-caucus-reshuffle/VH25T6XHZ5HCDDN7DZPFFR7IQI/)).

## Biography

Born in 1983 (Te Kapotai, Ngāti Hine, Ngāpuhi), Prime grew up in Northland and attended Bay of Islands College; her father was a train driver. She holds BA, LLB, PGDip and LLM degrees from the University of Waikato and worked as a solicitor in Wellington before returning north ([Waikato University](http://www.waikato.ac.nz/study/success-stories/willow-jean-prime)). She was elected to the Far North District Council in 2013, its youngest member. She lives near Pakaraka with her husband Dion and two children.

### Career summary

- Ran in Northland in 2014, the 2015 by-election (won by Winston Peters, with Labour's leader signalling tolerance of strategic voting) and 2017; list MP from 2017 ([Stuff](http://www.stuff.co.nz/national/politics/67099070/Andrew-Little-prods-Northland-to-vote-for-Winston-Peters)).
- **Northland MP 2020–2023:** won by 163 votes on special votes over National's Matt King; lost to National's Grant McCallum in 2023 and returned from list No. 9 ([2020](https://www.electionresults.govt.nz/electionresults_2020/electorate-details-32.html); [2023](https://archive.electionresults.govt.nz/electionresults_2023/electorate-details-32.html)).
- **Minister 2023:** Minister of Conservation and Youth from 31 January 2023, entering Cabinet in April 2023 (when Cabinet reached a 10/10 gender balance) ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/483394/prime-minister-chris-hipkins-reveals-cabinet-reshuffle)).
- **Opposition:** children and youth (2023), education (March 2025 – March 2026), social development (from March 2026).

## Record & notable actions

- As Conservation Minister announced tripled Hauraki Gulf protection areas and a bottom-trawling ban across most of the Gulf, a rewrite of the Wildlife Act 1953, and (with Rachel Brooking) six new marine reserves off the south-east South Island coast (2023) ([Beehive](https://www.beehive.govt.nz/release/govt-triples-hauraki-gulf-protection-area-conservation-future-generations); [RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/election-2023/499457/government-announces-six-new-south-island-marine-reserves)).
