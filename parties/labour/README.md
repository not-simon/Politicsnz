## History

**Origins (1916–1935).** Labour was formed in 1916 as the political wing of the union movement. Its early leaders (Harry Holland, Peter Fraser, Bob Semple, Paddy Webb) opposed WWI conscription and some were jailed. It won 8 seats in 1919 and became the official Opposition in 1926; radical policies such as the "usehold" (state leasehold) land policy were dropped during the 1920s ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_Labour_Party)).

**First Labour Government (1935–1949)** under Michael Joseph Savage and Peter Fraser built the modern welfare state (state housing, social security, free health care). **Second (1957–60)** under Walter Nash and **Third (1972–75)** under Norman Kirk/Bill Rowling were single-term governments.

**Fourth Labour Government (1984–1990)** under David Lange and Finance Minister Roger Douglas carried out sweeping market liberalisation ("Rogernomics") — deregulation, corporatisation and privatisation — which split the party and its union base, and also made New Zealand nuclear-free. Membership collapsed in the 1980s–90s ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_Labour_Party)).

**Fifth Labour Government (1999–2008)** under Helen Clark (three election wins) governed in MMP coalitions/support arrangements (Alliance, Progressives, United Future, NZ First, Greens). It introduced KiwiSaver, Working for Families, the NZ Super Fund, Kiwibank, interest-free student loans and NCEA; the 2004 foreshore and seabed controversy led to the breakaway Māori Party ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_Labour_Party)).

**Opposition (2008–2017)** — four leaders in nine years (Goff, Shearer, Cunliffe, Little). The 2014 result (25.13%) was Labour's worst since 1922. Policies run in 2011 and 2014 — a capital gains tax, raising the pension age to 67, NZ Power, KiwiAssure — were all dropped by Andrew Little after 2014 (see [track record](./track-record.md#1-opposition-era-promises-2011--2014)). With Labour polling at 24%, Little stood down on 1 August 2017 and Jacinda Ardern took over seven weeks before the election ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_Labour_Party)).

**Sixth Labour Government (2017–2023)** — see [Government periods](#government-periods-2008-2026) below.

**Opposition again (2023–2026).** Hipkins was re-elected leader after the 2023 defeat; veterans Grant Robertson, Kelvin Davis and Rino Tirikatene (2024) and David Parker (2025) left Parliament. Labour called for recognition of Palestinian statehood (April 2024) and Hipkins said a Labour government would not be part of AUKUS (December 2024). Several Labour members' bills passed with cross-party support (e.g. Camilla Belich's wage-theft and pay-secrecy bills, Deborah Russell's divorce-wait exemption for family-violence victims) ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_Labour_Party); [54th Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/54th_New_Zealand_Parliament)).

## Ideology and principles

Labour's 1916 objective was "the socialisation of the means of production, distribution and exchange"; the socialist objective was dropped in 1951. The current constitution lists democratic-socialist principles — management of natural resources for all, equal access regardless of wealth, co-operation in economic relations "to ensure a just distribution of wealth", the right to work, people "more important than property", **honouring Te Tiriti o Waitangi**, international co-operation, and equality regardless of race, sex, sexuality, gender identity, age, religion or disability. Observers describe its policy as pragmatic social democracy with social-liberal positions ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_Labour_Party)).

Core voter base today: young people, urban workers, public servants, and Māori and Pasifika communities ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_Labour_Party)).

## Organisation and union links

- **Structure:** electorate branches and special branches (Māori, Pasifika, women, rainbow, university, etc.) → Labour Electorate Committees → six regional conferences → **Annual Conference** (called Congress in election years), the supreme body. The **New Zealand Council** is the governing executive; a **Policy Council** elected each term writes the platform and manifesto ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_Labour_Party)).
- **Caucus elects Cabinet:** unusually, Labour's caucus — not the leader — elects its ministers ([Wikipedia: Leader of the Labour Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leader_of_the_New_Zealand_Labour_Party)).
- **Leadership rules (2021):** a candidate needs two-thirds of caucus; failing that, an electoral college of caucus 40% / members 40% / **affiliated unions 20%** decides ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leader_of_the_New_Zealand_Labour_Party)).
- **Affiliated unions:** 11 affiliated unions in 2023, paying a levy by membership; the CTU president speaks at the annual conference ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_Labour_Party)). Union influence over leadership votes is a recurring criticism. Policy closely aligned with unions in government: Fair Pay Agreements, doubled sick leave, pay equity settlements, repeal of 90-day trials for larger firms.
- **Young Labour** is the youth wing. The party's handling of 2018 Young Labour camp assaults and of 2019 complaints against a staffer drew heavy criticism; party president Nigel Haworth resigned in 2019 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sixth_Labour_Government_of_New_Zealand)).
- **Party presidents** (recent): Andrew Little (2009–11), Moira Coatsworth (2011–15), Nigel Haworth (2015–19), Claire Szabó (2019–22), Jill Day (2022–). *Dates from Wikipedia's list of presidents; not independently re-verified.*

- **$3,273,137.93** in declared electoral donations from 1 January 2025 to late October 2026 — behind National ($8.47m) and ACT ($4.79m). It includes $125,000 from the Mills Family Trust and $219,178 from the estate of Murray George Jones. ## 2026 pitch and headline promises

Slogan **"Better Starts Now"**, launched 24 August 2026 ([1News](https://www.1news.co.nz/2026/08/24/labour-reveals-its-2026-election-campaign-slogan/)). Labour is campaigning on cost of living, health access, jobs and "changing the government"; National has focused its attack on the capital gains tax. Headline commitments (full list and verbatim documents in [policies/](./policies/README.md)):

- **Capital gains tax** of 28% on residential and commercial property, excluding the family home and farms, from July 2027 — funding **up to three free GP visits a year** (Medicard), free maternity scans and free cervical screening ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/577021/labour-to-campaign-on-narrow-capital-gains-tax-no-wealth-tax)).
- **NZ Future Fund** to invest in infrastructure and NZ firms (details withheld until after the election) ([1News](https://www.1news.co.nz/2025/10/20/nz-future-fund-labour-reveals-first-major-policy-for-next-election/); [NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/nz/politics/labour-admits-it-will-not-release-cornerstone-policy-costing-and-job-modelling-until-after-the-election-blames-treaty-of-waitangi-concerns/QNGXJK4SHZFOJB52WFCVHPLR4E/)).
- **$20/week public transport fare cap** in the three main cities ($10 elsewhere) ([Stuff](https://www.stuff.co.nz/politics/360990643/labour-reveals-public-transport-fare-cap-long-awaited-election-policy)).
- **Fiscal strategy:** surplus by 2029/30, net debt ~20% of GDP, an independent budget office ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/politics_election-2026/1081146/labour-releases-fiscal-strategy-promises-to-slash-debt)).
- **$4b for 6,000+ social housing places, school and hospital repairs** ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/politics_election-2026/1552827/labour-pledges-4-billion-for-state-houses-school-repairs-and-hospitals)); **10% student-loan write-off** ($583.4m) ([NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/nz/politics/labour-kicks-off-campaign-after-national-hammers-home-tax-attack/JJ6SXVR3HJH2RB7BF4567UTSDI/)); **restore pay equity** and a $2.5b pay rise for care and support workers ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/politics_election-2026/1718141/labour-releases-its-fiscal-plan-promises-pay-rise-for-care-and-support-workers)).
- **Cost of living / competition:** split supermarket wholesale from retail, ban price gouging, a "fair go" law on hidden fees, airline and bank competition measures ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/politics_election-2026/1478631/labour-proposes-splitting-foodstuffs-and-woolworths-from-their-wholesale-arms)).
- **KiwiSaver:** compulsory employer contributions, 6% minimum ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/politics_election-2026/1356180/labour-to-make-employer-contributions-to-kiwisaver-compulsory-if-elected)).
- **Reversals of coalition policy:** repeal the Regulatory Standards Act within 100 days; scrap youth boot camps; pause the NCEA/curriculum overhaul; restore school boards' Treaty obligations; restore locally made school lunches and rural school bus routes.
- **Rule-outs:** no GST rise in 2027, oppose fuel excise rises, no change to landlord interest deductibility.
- **Foreign policy:** recognise Palestine, sanctions on Israel, stay out of AUKUS, aid towards 0.7% of GDP ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/politics/1528401/labour-sums-up-its-foreign-policy-approach-as-independent-principled-and-unafraid)).
