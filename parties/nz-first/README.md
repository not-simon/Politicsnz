# New Zealand First (NZ First)

> Nationalist, populist, socially conservative and economically interventionist party, founded in 1993 by **Winston Peters**, who has led it ever since. It has been a junior partner in the National-led coalition since November 2023.
> **Leader:** Winston Peters: Minister of Foreign Affairs; Deputy Prime Minister 27 Nov 2023 – 31 May 2025; Minister for Rail. **Deputy leader:** [Shane Jones](./people/shane-jones/README.md) (elected by caucus 17 Sept 2025): Minister for Regional Development, Resources, Oceans and Fisheries.

## History: the kingmaker

- **1993–1996.** Peters won the 1993 Tauranga by-election as an independent. NZ First won 8.4% and two seats in 1993. In 1996, the first MMP election, it took 13% and 17 seats, including all five Māori seats (the "Tight Five").
- **1996: coalition with National.** Peters campaigned strongly against National, and many voters expected a deal with Labour. He chose National, becoming Deputy PM and Treasurer. The coalition broke up in August 1998 when Jenny Shipley sacked him over the sale of Wellington Airport. Half the caucus split off to keep supporting National, and in 1999 the party fell to 4%.
- **2002–2005.** It recovered to 10.4% and 13 seats in 2002, campaigning on immigration, Treaty costs and crime. In 2005 it fell to 5.72%, and Peters lost Tauranga. During the campaign he had spoken of staying on the cross-benches and rejecting the "baubles of office", but he then took Foreign Affairs (outside Cabinet) in a confidence-and-supply deal with Helen Clark's Labour. That deal produced the **SuperGold Card** (launched 2007).
- **2008: out.** The party was hit by a donations controversy involving Owen Glenn, the Vela family and Bob Jones. The Serious Fraud Office and Police found no wrongdoing, but Parliament's Privileges Committee censured Peters for knowingly misleading Parliament by not declaring a donation. John Key ruled NZ First out, and it won 4% and no seats.
- **2011–2017: return.** It came back with 6.59% (8 MPs) in 2011, helped by the "teapot tape" affair, and won 8.66% (11 MPs) in 2014. Peters won the 2015 Northland by-election.
- **2017: kingmaker again.** With 7.2% and 9 MPs, NZ First negotiated with both major parties and on 19 October 2017 chose **Labour** (with Green confidence and supply). Peters became Deputy PM and Foreign Minister; Shane Jones ran the $3b Provincial Growth Fund; Ron Mark was Defence Minister; Tracey Martin held Children and Internal Affairs.
- **2020: out again.** It won 2.6% and lost every seat. That year the **NZ First Foundation** case broke (below).
- **2023: return and coalition with National.** Peters ruled out Labour before the election and campaigned on "Take our country back". NZ First won 6.08% and 8 list MPs, and on 24 November 2023 signed a coalition agreement with National. That made it part of New Zealand's first three-party coalition under MMP, alongside ACT.

## The NZ First Foundation case (2020–2022)

- In February 2020 the SFO began investigating the NZ First Foundation. The allegation was that donations (about half a million dollars, 2017–19) had been channelled through it to pay party expenses without being declared as party donations.
- The SFO charged two people in September 2020. At the 2022 trial the Crown alleged that $746,881 had been obtained by deceiving donors, the party secretary and the Electoral Commission.
- **Both defendants were acquitted on 22 July 2022**, and their names are permanently suppressed. Peters was not charged and has always denied wrongdoing.
- Labour's Justice Minister then announced tighter donation laws. Third-party entities were brought within the definition of donations, and the disclosure threshold was lowered.

## In government, 2023–2026

**Ministers:** Peters (Foreign Affairs, Racing; later Rail; Deputy PM until 31 May 2025); Shane Jones (Regional Development, Resources, Oceans and Fisheries); Casey Costello (Customs, Seniors); Mark Patterson (Rural Communities, Associate Agriculture); Jenny Marcroft (Under-Secretary, Media and Communications).

**Main coalition wins delivered** (details and sources in the [track record](./track-record.md)):
- NZ Super age kept at 65, and no foreign buyer tax.
- A **$1.2b Regional Infrastructure Fund**: $1,070m committed, $858m approved, but only $153m paid out at 31 July 2026.
- **English Language Act 2026**, passed 30 July 2026 with Labour's support; English-first names for government agencies.
- **Māori ward referendums** restored: in October 2025, 24 of 42 councils voted to remove their wards.
- A broadened **COVID-19 inquiry** (phase-two report released 10 March 2026).
- Te Aka Whai Ora abolished; smokefree generation law repealed; relationships and sexuality education guidelines withdrawn.
- **Fast-track Approvals Act 2024**, and the offshore oil and gas exploration ban reversed (Crown Minerals Amendment Act 2025).
- As Rail Minister, Peters handled the aftermath of the cancelled iReX mega-ferries. Total cost of the cancelled project: $671m. Replacement ferries were ordered at a fixed price of US$335m, for service from 2029 ([1News](https://www.1news.co.nz/2025/08/15/how-much-it-cost-to-cancel-irex-ferry-contract/)).

**Blocking, permitting and "agree to disagree":**
- **Permitted then blocked ACT's Treaty Principles Bill:** voted for it at first reading (Nov 2024), as agreed, then against it at second reading (Apr 2025).
- **Voted for the Regulatory Standards Act** at its third reading (13 Nov 2025), then pledged a week later to repeal it.
- **Invoked "agree to disagree"** over:
  - the scope of the COVID inquiry (mid-2024);
  - new work-residency pathways (Sept 2025);
  - the India free trade agreement (Dec 2025; NZ First voted against its legislation in 2026, which passed with Labour support);
  - the under-16 social media ban (Aug 2026).
- Opposed the Gene Technology Bill unless it was changed (Nov 2025), and opposed legislation allowing advertising on certain public holidays (Oct 2025).

**Controversies (factual):**
- In March 2024 Peters compared co-governance to Nazi race ideology; Luxon called the remarks unhelpful.
- In April 2024 Shane Jones attacked the Waitangi Tribunal's summons process.
- In 2023, media reported conspiracy-linked views held by several NZ First candidates.
- In August 2026 Peters told Green MP Lawrence Xu-Nan to "go back to your own country". Labour then formally ruled out working with NZ First, citing "overt racism" ([1News](https://www.1news.co.nz/2026/08/10/labour-rules-out-nz-first-hipkins-says-caucus-call-was-unanimous/)).

## 2026 pitch and bottom lines

Peters' State of the Nation speech (Tauranga, 22 March 2026) and the campaign that followed focused on cost of living, national identity, "anti-co-governance" and immigration. Former MPs Alfred Ngaro (NewZeal), Michael Laws, Ron Mark and Harete Hipango are on the 2026 list. Headline promises, all sourced in [`nz-first.json`](../../data/promises/nz-first.json) and [policies](./policies/README.md):

- **A binding referendum on the Māori seats** (revived from 2017). Also: abolish the Waitangi Tribunal and replace it with a Treaty of Waitangi Completion Commission; disestablish Auckland's Independent Māori Statutory Board.
- **Citizens-only voting**; **NZ Super only for citizens from 2029**; social housing for citizens first; a ten-year benefit stand-down for migrants; tighter visas; a two-year amnesty for overstayers who leave voluntarily.
- **Superannuation at 65** stays a defining commitment (it was secured in the 2023 deal).
- Split power companies' generation and retail arms; split Foodstuffs into two co-operatives; buy back **BNZ** and merge it with Kiwibank; KiwiSaver enrolment at birth; a $5,000-a-year "Kiwi Kids Grant".
- **Repeal the Regulatory Standards Act**; amend the fast-track regime; a Special Economic Zone at Marsden Point; a 20% company tax rate for small businesses.

NZ First has not published a formal list of 2026 coalition "bottom lines". Earlier campaigns treated the Māori-seat referendum (2017) and super at 65 as conditions.

## Coalition stance for 2026

- Peters ruled out Labour well before the campaign. In August 2026 Labour ruled out NZ First, saying the caucus decision was unanimous ([1News](https://www.1news.co.nz/2026/08/10/labour-rules-out-nz-first-hipkins-says-caucus-call-was-unanimous/)). That leaves a renewed National-led arrangement as NZ First's only realistic route to government.
- Peters defended strategic vote-splitting when National urged its voters not to split, accusing National of "desperation".
- Relations with ACT are tense: Seymour accused NZ First of bad faith over its pledge to repeal the Regulatory Standards Act.
