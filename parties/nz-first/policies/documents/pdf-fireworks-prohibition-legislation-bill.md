Fireworks Prohibition Legislation Bill
Member’s Bill
Explanatory note
General policy statement
This Bill seeks to prohibit the public sale and use of fireworks in New Zealand. Under
the Bill, approved public displays will continue to be permitted.
The Bill aims to address the widespread public concern about the adverse effects of
fireworks on animal welfare, public safety, and the environment. Advocacy groups
have raised concerns regarding these risks, and there have been popular public peti‐
tions supporting control.
Fireworks can potentially cause severe distress to livestock, wildlife, and domestic
pets. The sudden loud sounds and flashes of light have a tendency to lead to panic,
injury, and even death. Veterinarians commonly report increased cases of anxiety-
related disease and lost pets following firework events.
Abuse of fireworks have resulted in property damage, personal injury, and an
increased burden on emergency services. Fire hazards, particularly during dry condi‐
tions, pose a serious threat to towns and countryside communities. By restricting pub‐
lic access to fireworks, the Bill aims to reduce these threats and improve community
safety overall.
Clause by clause analysis
Clause 1 is the Title clause.
Clause 2 is the commencement clause and provides for the Bill to come into force the
day after Royal assent.

Part 1
Amendments to the Hazardous Substances and New Organisms Act
1996
Clause 3 identifies the Hazardous Substances and New Organisms Act 1996 as the
Act being amended by this Part.
Clause 4 inserts new section 25E to prohibit the retail sale of fireworks, and the
importation or manufacture of fireworks for retail sale.
Clause 5 and 6 make provision for the offence of non-compliance with the prohib‐
itions in new section 25E and provide a penalty for that offence.
Clause 7 and the Schedule provide for transitional matters.
Part 2
Amendment to the Summary Offences Act 1981
Clause 8 identifies the Summary Offences Act 1981 as the Act being amended by this
Part.
Clause 9 amends section 35 to increase the fine for setting off or throwing fireworks.
It also expands the section to apply to animals.
Part 3
Revocation of Hazardous Substances (Fireworks) Regulations (SR
2001/121)
Clause 10 revokes the Hazardous Substances (Fireworks) Regulations (SR 2001/121).

The Parliament of New Zealand enacts as follows:
1 Title
This Act is the Fireworks Prohibition Legislation Act 2025.
2 Commencement
This Act comes into force the day after Royal assent.
Part 1
Amendments to the Hazardous Substances and New Organisms Act
1996
3 Principal Act
This Part amends the Hazardous Substances and New Organisms Act 1996.
4 New section 25E inserted (Retail sale of fireworks prohibited)
After section 25D, insert:
25E Retail sale of fireworks prohibited
(1) The retail sale of fireworks is prohibited.
(2) To this end,—
(a) no fireworks may be imported into or manufactured in New Zealand for
retail sale; and
(b) no approval may be issued to import into or manufacture in New Zea‐
land fireworks for retail sale.
(3) In this section, fireworks do not include—
(a) bonbon crackers snaps, or similar pyrotechnic novelties or noise makers
containing less than 1.7 mg of pyrotechnic substance; or
(b) amorces, crackshots, or similar pyrotechnic novelties or noise makers
containing less than 5 mg of pyrotechnic substance; or
(c) party poppers, streamer bombs, handblasters, or similar pyrotechnic nov‐
elties or noise makers containing less than 20 mg of pyrotechnic sub‐
stance.
5 Section 109 amended (Offences)
After section 109(1)(c), insert:
(ca) sells, imports, or manufactures any firework in contravention of section
25E; or
6 Section 114 amended (Penalties)
In section 114(1), after “paragraph (c) or” insert “paragraph (ca) or”.

Provisions relating to Fireworks Prohibition Legislation Act 2025
14 Interpretation
In this Part,—
amendment Act means the Fireworks Prohibition Legislation Act 2025
distributor means a person engaged in the business of selling fireworks other‐
wise than at retail only
retailer means a person engaged in a business that includes the sale of fire‐
works for any reason other than for resale by the person to whom the fireworks
are sold
transitional period means the 6–month period starting at the commencement
of the amendment Act.
15 No transitional period for manufacturers or importers
There is no transitional period for a manufacturer or an importer of fireworks.
16 Transitional period for distributors or retailers
A distributor or retailer does not commit an offence against this Act in the 6–
month transitional period.
