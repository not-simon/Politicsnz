# NZ First Announces Overhaul To Regulate Combat Sports

*Thursday, 15 October 2026, 1:35 pm Press Release: New Zealand First Party* — [https://www.scoop.co.nz/stories/PA2610/S00133/nz-first-announces-overhaul-to-regulate-combat-sports.htm](https://www.scoop.co.nz/stories/PA2610/S00133/nz-first-announces-overhaul-to-regulate-combat-sports.htm)

## Full text

New Zealand First will back Combat Sports and establish a statutory Combat Sports Commission to enforce participant safety, eliminate unsanctioned events, and block criminal influence.

The lack of modern regulation creates severe risks for athletes and undermines legitimate clubs.

Following coronial recommendations after the tragic death of Kain Parsons, alongside growing concerns over gang-affiliated bare-knuckle fights and unsanctioned prize-money events, New Zealand First will deliver decisive reform.

### New Zealand First will:

- Establish a Combat Sports Commission to oversee nationwide event licensing and contestant registration.

- Introduce minimum health and medical standards, such as pre- and post-fight medical examinations, serological clearances, attending medical practitioners at all events, and mandatory stand-down period between contests determined by medical professionals.

- Empower the Commission to focus on licensing events and promoters.

- Introduce a penalty regime for illegal and unsanctioned events.

- Formalise recognition and partnership with Sport New Zealand to support legitimate promoters and athletes.

This comprehensive reform ensures safer ringsides, clean competition, and public confidence across New Zealand’s combat sports sector.

### BACKGROUND

- Combat sports have grown significantly in New Zealand over the past two decades, particularly MMA, kickboxing, Muay Thai and Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu, alongside traditional boxing and wrestling. New Zealand athletes have achieved success on the international stage, contributing to increased participation and commercial interest in the sector.

- The principal legislation governing combat sport contests remains the Boxing and Wrestling Act 1981, was enacted more than 40 years ago and has since been repealed. The Act regulated boxing and wrestling contests but does not adequately cover the ‘Asian Martial Arts’ or modern combat sports such as MMA and kickboxing. We need a fit for purpose piece of legislation.

- As a result, many combat sports operate under a mixture of voluntary codes, private promoters and sporting organisations, with no consistent national framework governing all combat sports.

- Increased commercialisation, online promotion, prize money competitions and spectator interest have heightened concerns regarding participant safety, event oversight and public confidence in the sector.

### PROBLEMS TO ADDRESS

- New Zealand's regulatory framework has not kept pace with the emergence and growth of modern combat sports, leaving significant gaps in oversight and enforcement.

- There is currently no consistent nationwide system governing fighter registration, event licensing, medical requirements or participant safety standards across all combat sports.

- Combat sports carry a heightened risk of concussion, head trauma and other serious injuries. Though many practitioners, coaches, and promoters ensure the necessary safety equipment, there is no minimum standard across all different codes.

- The death of Kain Parsons following a charity boxing match prompted coronial recommendations for stronger participant education, concussion management and event safeguards.

- Recent reporting has highlighted the emergence of unsanctioned combat events operating outside recognised sporting structures, raising concerns regarding participant welfare, legal liability and public safety.

- There have also been concerns about gang-affiliated fighting events and the potential for organised crime to exploit combat sports competitions, creating law and order risks alongside athlete welfare concerns.

- These issues risk undermining legitimate combat sports clubs and promoters that operate safely and professionally.

### POLICY SOLUTIONS

- Establish a statutory Combat Sports Commission as a Crown entity responsible for regulating combat sports nationwide.

- Require organisers to obtain a contest licence before promoting or conducting a combat sports contest. Unlicensed events would be subject to fines of up to $10,000 for individuals and $20,000 for organisations.

- Require all contestants to be formally registered before participating in regulated combat sports contests.

- Give the Commission authority to refuse licences where events or promoters have direct or indirect gang associations, or where Police advise there are significant public safety risks.

- Introduce mandatory medical safeguards including:

- Pre-fight medical examinations.

- Post-fight medical examinations.

- Current serological clearances.

- Mandatory attendance of medical practitioners at events.

- A mandatory medical stand-down period between contests.

- Allow the Commission to create sport-specific rules and safety standards for different combat disciplines.

- Formalise engagement with Sport New Zealand and improve recognition of legitimate combat sports organisations within the wider sports sector.
