Financial Markets Conduct (Preventing Payment Scams)
Amendment Bill
Member’s Bill
Explanatory note
General policy statement
This Bill would make New Zealand’s financial system a safer place for consumers by
addressing payment scams. Even though the New Zealand Banking Association Code
of Banking Practice includes five commitments intended to reduce fraudulent transac‐
tions — like pre-transaction warnings, check of payee systems, and improved report‐
ing on scams — these are all focused on the responsibilities of a sending bank and do
not apply to receiving banks or financial service providers.
Receiving banks, who can receive stolen funds, and who may inadvertently play a
central role in money laundering operations, are not currently required to filter incom‐
ing payments for suspicious activity. Such a limitation significantly diminishes the
efficacy of scam prevention.
Financial service providers handling large sums of money on behalf of consumers are
not verified by banks as legitimately able to carry out such transactions, leaving
impersonators able to carry out fraudulent transactions undetected.
To address these shortcomings, the Bill proposes amending the Financial Markets
Conduct Act 2013 to introduce a duty to protect consumers from payment scams.
This change meets the aims of the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing
of Terrorism Act 2009, combatting organised crime, and is founded on international
best practice. By making receiving banks as accountable as sending banks for identi‐
fying and combating fraud, and extending duties of care for financial service provid‐
ers, the Bill intends to provide a better and more equitable system for keeping New
Zealanders safe from payment scams.
Equally, all institutions involved in reducing financial fraud will be held accountable
for timeliness response to alerts.

Clause by clause analysis
Clause 1 is the Title clause.
Clause 2 is the commencement clause and provides that the Bill comes into force on
the day after Royal assent.
Clause 3 provides that the Bill amends the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013.
Clause 4 places a new duty on financial institutions to protect consumers from pay‐
ment scams and lists some methods of compliance with this duty. It also provides for
how loss resulting from payment scams will be attributed.
Clause 5 inserts new definitions for the purposes of subpart 6A of Part 6.
Clause 6 provides for regulations to be made regarding the new obligations imposed
under clause 4.

4 New section 446JA and 446JB and cross–heading inserted
After section 446J, insert:
Duty to protect consumers from payment scams
446JA Duty to protect consumers from payment scams
(1) Every financial institution must take all reasonable steps to protect consumers
from payment scams.
(2) The requirement to protect consumers includes all of the following:
(a) ensuring a confirmation of payee system is operational:
(b) ensuring an identity verification process that meets the requirements of
the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act
2009 is operational:
(c) educating consumers about the risk of payment scams:
(d) ensuring staff have the requisite knowledge, expertise, and training to
identify and warn consumers about the risk of payment scams:
(e) identifying vulnerable consumers and taking appropriate precautions to
mitigate those consumers’ heightened vulnerability to payment scams:
(f) providing advice and assistance to consumers who believe they have
been subject to a payment scam, including ensuring that such advice and
assistance is easily accessible at any time of day:
(g) implementing and continually improving systems that align with inter‐
national best practice standards to detect and warn consumers where
there is evidence of particular payment scams occurring within New
Zealand:
(h) implementing systems to monitor consumer activities to ensure that the
financial institution—
(i) is not being used by its consumers to carry out payment scams,
fraud, money laundering, or financing of terrorism:
(ii) is able to detect and prevent its consumers from becoming victims
of payment scams:
(i) where an account has been identified as being used for the purpose of
conducting a payment scam, sharing relevant information with other
financial institutions and government authorities to assist in preventing
further payment scams from occurring:
(j) taking urgent action, including freezing relevant consumer accounts if
any of the following activities have been identified in respect of that
account:
(i) signs of unusual account activity:

446JB Responsibility for losses arising from payment scams
(1) If a consumer of a financial institution is subject to a payment scam, and has
suffered loss as a result, every financial institution must comply with the fol‐
lowing general principles in respect of attributing liability for those losses:
(a) where a financial institution has failed in their duty to take reasonable
steps to protect their customers from payment scams, the starting pos‐
ition is that any loss arising from a payment scam must not be attributed
to the consumer:
(b) regulations made under section 546(1)(oec) may specify circum‐
stances where a consumer may be liable for losses incurred in respect of
a payment scam, including where the consumer has—
(i) acted fraudulently:
(ii) acted with gross negligence:
(iii) unreasonably failed to follow any instructions or advice the finan‐
cial institution issued to the consumer personally relating to the
payment scam:
(iv) unreasonably delayed in reporting the payment scam and as a
result the financial institution has been unable to mitigate the los‐
ses.
(2) Every financial institution must comply with the regulations made under sec-
tion 546(1)(oec) (which relate to the attribution of loses from payment
scams).
(3) For the avoidance of doubt, this section applies to both sending and receiving
financial institutions.
