## Current status

Leader of New Zealand First, Minister of Foreign Affairs, Racing and Rail in the National–ACT–NZ First government. He was Deputy Prime Minister from November 2023 until 31 May 2025, when the role rotated to David Seymour under the coalition deal. He is **#1** on NZ First's 2026 list and is not contesting an electorate. He has "permanently" ruled out working with Labour leader Chris Hipkins (May 2025).

## Biography

Born in Whangārei on 11 April 1945 to a Māori father (Ngāti Wai, Ngāti Hine, Ngāpuhi) and a Scottish mother; raised on a farm at Whananaki. Trained as a teacher, worked in Australia as a blast-furnace worker and tunneller, then graduated BA/LLB from the University of Auckland (1973) and practised law at Russell McVeagh (1974–1978). Brothers Ian and Jim were also MPs. Long-time partner Jan Trotman.

## Political career

- **National Party**: entered Parliament in 1979 for Hunua after an electoral petition; MP for Tauranga from 1984. Exposed the 1986 Māori loan affair. Minister of Māori Affairs from 1990 until sacked by Jim Bolger in October 1991 for criticising the government's economic policies.
- Resigned from National and Parliament in 1993, won the Tauranga by-election as an independent and founded **New Zealand First** on 18 July 1993.
- 1996: NZ First won 17 seats and held the balance of power; Peters chose a coalition with National and became Deputy PM and Treasurer. Sacked by Jenny Shipley in August 1998, ending the coalition.
- 2005–2008: Minister of Foreign Affairs and Racing outside Cabinet under Helen Clark (confidence and supply). Out of Parliament after NZ First fell to 4.07% in 2008.
- **2011–2026 (50th–54th Parliaments)**: returned with 8 MPs in 2011 and 11 in 2014; won the 2015 Northland by-election (lost the seat in 2017). Formed a coalition with Labour in 2017: Deputy PM, Foreign Affairs, SOEs, Racing; Acting PM during Jacinda Ardern's maternity leave. NZ First was ousted in 2020 (2.6%). Returned in 2023 (6.08%, 8 seats) to form a three-party coalition with National and ACT.

## Record & notable actions

- Champion of the SuperGold Card (launched 2007) and of the elderly vote; long-standing advocate of binding citizens-initiated referenda; supported MMP in 1992–93.
- 2017–2020: the *Herald* assessed NZ First as having fully or partly achieved 80% of its 70 coalition promises (including the Provincial Growth Fund); insisted on a referendum for the End of Life Choice Act and sought one on abortion law reform.
- As Foreign Minister since 2023: "Pacific reset" travel; closer ties with the US and exploration of AUKUS Pillar 2 (criticised by Helen Clark); travel bans on Israeli ministers Ben-Gvir and Smotrich (2025); declined to recognise Palestine at the 2025 UN General Assembly; suspended then restored aid to the Cook Islands over its China partnership (2025–26); dismissed High Commissioner Phil Goff over comments about Donald Trump (March 2025); abstained on a UN pandemic-preparedness resolution (October 2026).
- As Racing Minister announced a ban on greyhound racing by July 2026; as Rail Minister contracted China's COMEC shipyard (October 2025) for two rail-enabled Interislander ferries due in 2029 and confirmed $144m compensation to Hyundai Mipo for the cancelled iRex project.
- Voted for the Regulatory Standards Act (November 2025) under the coalition deal but pledged NZ First would repeal it; invoked "agree to disagree" over the COVID-19 inquiry (2024) and the India FTA (2025). Cabinet agreed to introduce NZ First's bill making English an official language (2025).

## Controversies

- **"Go back to your own country" (30 July 2026):** told Green MP Lawrence Xu-Nan, who was born in China, to go back to his own country; the Race Relations Commissioner called it racist and PM Luxon called it "very offensive and completely inappropriate" but kept him as Foreign Minister. China lodged a complaint. Peters denied it was racist.
- **Rhetoric on immigration and race:** a long record of remarks about Asian immigration (e.g. the 2005 Orewa speech on "imported criminal activity"); in 2023 said Māori were not indigenous; in March 2024 likened co-governance to Nazi-era "race-based theory", criticised by the Holocaust Centre; in 2026 criticised Teanau Tuiono for saying "Aotearoa".
- **2008 donations affair:** censured by Parliament on 23 September 2008 for "knowingly providing false or misleading information" about a $100,000 donation from Owen Glenn; the Serious Fraud Office later cleared him of fraud and police laid no charges.
- **Superannuation privacy case:** sued ministers and officials over the 2017 leak of his pension overpayment (repaid ~$18,000); the court found his privacy had been deliberately breached but could not attribute the leak to the defendants, and ordered him to pay $320,000 in costs (appeal dismissed 2021).
- 2020 controversies included arranging an Antarctica trip for wealthy associates (to seek Scott Base sponsorship) and the NZ First Foundation donations case (the people charged were acquitted; Peters was not charged).
- In 2022 he visited the anti-mandate occupation of Parliament grounds and was briefly trespassed; Bob Carr announced a defamation suit after Peters called him "a Chinese puppet" (2024).
