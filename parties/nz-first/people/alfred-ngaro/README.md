## Current status

Former National list MP (2011–2020) and former Pacific Peoples Minister. Led the conservative NewZeal party from July 2023 until resigning in March 2026, when he was introduced as a NZ First candidate. Standing in **Glendene** and **#5** on NZ First's list for 2026.

## Biography

Raised in Te Atatū by Cook Islands parents (a labourer/union delegate from Aitutaki and Pukapuka, and a cleaner from Mangaia); his grandmother was of Cook Islands and Polish-Jewish descent. A qualified electrician with a theology degree from the Bible College of New Zealand (Laidlaw). Worked in community-led development and governance (Tāmaki Transformation, Healthy Village Action Zones; Sir Peter Blake Emerging Leader Award), chaired Auckland DHB's Pacific committee and Tamaki College's board. Married to Mokauina; four children.

## Political career

- Citizens & Ratepayers candidate for Auckland Council in 2010.
- National list MP 2011–2020 — the first MP of Cook Islands descent; contested Te Atatū in 2014, 2017 and 2020.
- One of the "Four Amigos" backbenchers who backed Bill English in 2016; promoted to Cabinet as Minister for Pacific Peoples and Community and Voluntary Sector (December 2016 – October 2017).
- Explored forming a Christian party in 2019 but stayed with National; lost his seat in 2020.
- NewZeal leader 2023–2026 (0.56% in 2023; third in the 2023 Port Waikato by-election).

## Record & notable actions

- Member in charge of the Local Government (Auckland Council) Amendment Act 2016 (the "Warren Flaunty bill"), barring candidates from serving on more than one local board.
- Helped secure formal recognition of Cook Islands WWI soldiers; increased Polyfest funding as minister.
- Left National citing its support for gender self-identification (2021) and the conversion-practices ban (2022).

## Controversies

- **May 2017 funding remarks:** told a National conference he could cut funding to organisations associated with Labour's Willie Jackson if they campaigned against the government; he apologised, PM Bill English reassured community agencies and ordered a review of Ngaro's decisions.
- **2020 Facebook attack ad** claiming a vote for Labour's Phil Twyford meant decriminalising all drugs and "unlimited abortion"; deleted after criticism, and National leader Judith Collins distanced the party from it.
