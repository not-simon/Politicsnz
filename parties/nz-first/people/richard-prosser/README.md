## Current status

Deceased (died 10 June 2022). Former NZ First list MP (2011–2017).

## Biography

Born in Henderson on 15 January 1967 and raised in Waikato; worked as a photographic technician, barman, engineering contractor, truck driver and salesman, then as a winemaker in Otago (certificate from EIT, 2001) and an irrigation-firm manager in Canterbury. Wrote the "Eyes Right" column for *Investigate* for ten years and the book *Uncommon Dissent* (2012), describing himself as a "Kiwi Nationalist".

## Political career

- Democrats for Social Credit candidate in Otago (2005); last in Central Otago mayoral and council races (2007); promoted South Island separatist pressure groups.
- Joined NZ First in 2010; elected at list #4 in 2011 (Waimakariri) and #3 in 2014; demoted to #15 in 2017 and lost his seat.

## Record & notable actions

- Advocated a separate South Island parliament, compulsory military training, arming shopkeepers and taxi drivers, and a burqa ban; voted against marriage equality (2013).

## Controversies

- **"Wogistan" column (February 2013):** wrote in *Investigate* that young Muslim men, or men who "look like a Muslim", should be barred from Western airlines, using slurs; it drew international attention and condemnation across Parliament. He initially refused to apologise, describing his style as "shock jock", but later admitted the article lacked balance, apologised for the offence and stopped writing for *Investigate*. The UN Committee on the Elimination of Racial Discrimination said in March 2013 that it "regrets" the remarks.
- His 2012 book's claim that Western society had been "hijacked by a conspiracy of Silly Little Girls" was criticised by feminist groups; in 2020 he promoted a claim that COVID-19 was a global elite conspiracy.
