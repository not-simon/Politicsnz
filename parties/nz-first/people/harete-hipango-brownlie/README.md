## Current status

Former National MP. Joined NZ First after losing in 2023; standing in **Whanganui** and **#7** on NZ First's list for 2026.

## Biography

Harete Makere Hipango was born in Whanganui (1964 or 1965) and raised at Pūtiki; Te Āti Haunui-a-Pāpārangi, Ngāti Apa, Ngā Rauru Kītahi, Ngāti Tamakōpiri and Ngāti Whitikaupeka; descendant of Hoani Wiremu Hīpango and relative of Rere-ō-maki, a Treaty signatory. LLB (Auckland, 1991); lawyer in Whanganui for over 25 years, including during the 1995 Pākaitore occupation; joined the 2004 foreshore and seabed hīkoi. Catholic. Married to Dean MacFater; three children.

## Political career

- Won **Whanganui** for National in 2017 (the first Māori woman National chose for a seat it held); lost it to Labour's Steph Lewis in 2020.
- Returned on 11 June 2021 as a list MP replacing Nick Smith.
- National candidate for Te Tai Hauāuru in 2023 (third; first National candidate in a Māori seat since 2002); not re-elected at list #31.
- Joined NZ First in 2024.

## Record & notable actions

- Strongly opposed the End of Life Choice Act 2019 and the Abortion Legislation Act 2020.
- Took part in the 2020 caucus move that replaced Simon Bridges with Todd Muller.

## Controversies

- Claimed the abortion law allowed full-term abortions, which critics said was incorrect (post-20-week abortions require two practitioners' sign-off).
- Posted an image with a quote falsely attributed to Jacinda Ardern (2020), which she defended as "a construction of key words".
- Attended anti-vaccination protests in Whanganui (November 2021 and a Voices for Freedom rally in January 2022); National leader Christopher Luxon distanced the party.
- In January 2022 acknowledged asking a staffer to delete the controversies section of her Wikipedia article, which she said she regretted.
- In June 2021 the *Herald* reported furniture and a television bought with Parliamentary Service funds were delivered to her home; Judith Collins said she had paid for or correctly coded items.
