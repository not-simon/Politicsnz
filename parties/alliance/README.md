## History

The Alliance was Jim Anderton's left-wing grouping that won 18% of the vote (but only two seats under first-past-the-post)
in 1993, won about 8% and ten seats in 1999 and entered coalition with Labour, introducing paid parental leave and
Kiwibank. It split in 2002 when Anderton's faction left (the Progressives), and
the rump lost all its seats. [1]

**2011–2015.** When Anderton retired from Wigram in 2011, former MP Kevin Campbell stood there (fourth, 793 votes). The
party won 0.05% (1,209 votes per the official count; Wikipedia's party article gives 1,069). In 2014 it ran no list and
one electorate candidate (Napier, 59 votes), and it was deregistered at its own request on 26 May 2015, though it never
formally dissolved. [1][2]

**Relaunch (2025–26).** In June 2025 the party announced a relaunch and stood a candidate for Christchurch City Council
(unsuccessful). It announced in January 2026 that it would stand electorate candidates nationwide. On 7 June 2026 the
**Maritime Union of New Zealand** voted to affiliate (while keeping its Labour affiliation); Billot, a former MUNZ
communications staffer, called it a "gamechanger". The party was re-registered on 5 August 2026. [1][3]
