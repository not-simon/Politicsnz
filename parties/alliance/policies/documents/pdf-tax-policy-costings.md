4. Wealth Tax
An annual tax of 2.5 percent on individual net wealth above $2 million. A couple pays
nothing below $4 million of combined net worth.

The gross base is the portion of net worth sitting above the $2 million threshold, not the
total net worth of everyone above it. It is allocated across asset classes in proportion to
the holdings of individuals above the threshold. The gap between gross and retained
base allows for valuation, avoidance, and migration.
Above a $2 million individual threshold, much of the wealth held in New Zealand sits in
housing equity. A couple owning a $3 million house outright with no other substantial
assets holds $1.5 million each and pays nothing.
The base is drawn from Stats NZ individual net worth data for the year ended June
2024.

5. The Other Measures
Trust tax: $0.11b. Trust assets are attributed to their settlors for the wealth tax, so most
trust wealth is already counted in Section 4. Of a $100b trust base, 10 percent is treated
as distinct from that attributed wealth and taxed at 1.5 percent, with 75 percent
compliance. The measure closes an avoidance route. The yield reflects that..
Capital gains tax: $0.54b rising to $2.22b. A 33 per cent rate on realised gains,
modelled on a $420b asset base accruing at 4 percent nominal. No inflation indexing.
Gains at death are carried over. A realisation-based capital gains tax reaches only gains
accruing after it begins, which is why the revenue ramps to year 10 rather than arriving
at once.
Corporate rate change: $0.85b. A 30 percent rate on a $60.4b base, with 1 percent of
the base assumed to shift offshore per percentage point of increase.
Financial transactions tax: $0.64b. A 0.1 percent levy on a retained base of $644.25b,
covering equity and onshore foreign exchange turnover. The retained base is what
remains after relocation offshore.

Interest deductibility reversal: $0.75b. Treasury costed this when the change was

Bright-line (transitional): $0.30b. The bright-line test continues to apply to property

The two measures do not tax the same gain.
Multinational enforcement: $0.22b. Scaled from Australian recoveries.

The revenue-maximising rate is 44 per cent. The Alliance rate is 47 per cent, and raises
$0.04b.
The top rate corrects the shape of the scale, which currently flattens well below the level
of genuinely high incomes. It is a distributional measure. The package does not depend
on it for revenue.
The framework is Treasury’s, applied to Inland Revenue’s published distribution. It
produces the same finding about the existing 39 percent rate.

7. Data and Method
Income tax is a grouped-data estimate. Each schedule is applied to the average income
in every band of Inland Revenue’s published taxable income distribution, covering
4,693,920 individuals, and summed. Running the actual 2024 schedule through the
same code reproduces Inland Revenue’s reported take to within 0.29 percent. It is not a
person-level microsimulation.
All figures are static estimates in 2024 dollars. Fiscal drag, population growth, and
growth in the asset bases over the ten years are excluded, except where the capital
gains tax accrual is stated.
The income tax mechanics, the corporate tax base, and interest deductibility are settled
figures. The capital gains and wealth tax bases are estimates built from published data.
The financial transactions tax carries the widest margin, because much of the base is
already offshore and the behavioural response is unmeasured.
