## Full text (verbatim)

Alliance Party of Aotearoa New Zealand

Te Tiriti o Waitangi, Democracy, and
Constitutional Policy
Democracy, Sovereignty, and the Power of the People
The Alliance believes that democracy must mean more than voting every three
years.
A genuinely democratic society is one in which people have a meaningful ability to
shape the decisions that affect their lives, their communities, and their future.
Political power must not be concentrated in the hands of governments, multinational
corporations, wealthy individuals, or distant bureaucracies. It must be accountable to
the people.
The Alliance seeks to build a participatory democracy in which decisions are made
as close as practicable to the people affected by them, while maintaining national
institutions capable of ensuring equality, social justice, and the protection of national
interests.
Democratic government must extend into economic and social life. Political
democracy is weakened when economic power is concentrated in a small number of
private hands.
Our constitutional policy is therefore founded on four principles:
- Popular sovereignty: political authority ultimately belongs to the people.
- Participatory democracy: people and communities must have meaningful
control over decisions affecting them.
- Social justice: democracy must protect equality, human rights, and the
dignity of every person.
- Upholding Te Tiriti o Waitangi: the constitutional relationship between
tangata whenua and the wider population of Aotearoa must be honoured and
strengthened.

1. Te Tiriti o Waitangi: The Constitutional Foundation
The Alliance recognises Te Tiriti o Waitangi as the foundational constitutional
document of Aotearoa.
In particular, the Māori text of Te Tiriti o Waitangi must be recognised as the primary
expression of the agreement entered into between Māori and the Crown.

---

A just democratic society cannot be built upon the dispossession or marginalisation
of tangata whenua.
The Alliance therefore rejects institutional racism and supports constitutional
arrangements that recognise mana motuhake and the distinct rights, interests, and
responsibilities of Māori, while ensuring a democratic society based on cooperation
and mutual respect.
The relationship established by Te Tiriti o Waitangi must be reflected throughout the
machinery of government, legislation, and public decision-making.

Te Tiriti o Waitangi Obligations
The Crown must honour its obligations under Te Tiriti o Waitangi.
This includes:
- recognising Māori participation in constitutional and governmental
decision-making;
- protecting Māori rights and interests;
- addressing the consequences of historical Crown breaches;
- eliminating institutional discrimination; and
- ensuring that public institutions operate consistently with the principles of Te
Tiriti o Waitangi.
Honouring Te Tiriti o Waitangi is not simply a matter of consultation. It must be
reflected in the structure and operation of the state.

2. Waitangi Tribunal and Historical Grievances
The Alliance will strengthen the Waitangi Tribunal and ensure that it has the
resources and independence necessary to perform its constitutional role effectively.
Māori must have a meaningful role in the appointment of Māori members of the
Tribunal, including consideration of mechanisms through which iwi can directly select
or nominate those members.
The Government will commit to acting upon the recommendations of the Waitangi
Tribunal relating to Treaty breaches, environmental resources, and other matters
within its jurisdiction.
Historical grievances must be addressed fairly and transparently.
The purpose of Treaty settlement is not simply to rectify historical breaches, but to
establish a durable constitutional relationship for the future.

3. Participatory and Community Democracy
The Alliance will decentralise political power wherever practicable.
Decisions should be made at the lowest effective level, provided that
decentralisation does not undermine equality, universal rights, or the ability of central
government to protect the public interest.
Local communities should have genuine control over matters that directly affect
them.
We will strengthen:
- local democratic institutions;
- community participation in public decision-making;
- public consultation and deliberation;
- community ownership of essential assets and services;
- mechanisms for direct citizen participation; and
- accountability of elected representatives to the communities they represent.
The Alliance rejects the idea that citizens should participate in democracy only by
casting a vote at election time.

4. A Proportional and Representative Parliament
The Alliance supports the continuation of Mixed Member Proportional
representation (MMP) as the basis of parliamentary democracy.
Parliament should reflect the diversity of political opinion within Aotearoa, rather than
artificially concentrating power in the hands of parties that receive only a minority of
the popular vote.
MMP will be protected and strengthened.

The Party Vote Threshold
The current party vote threshold is 5 percent, which is among the highest of any
proportional system. A party can be the choice of one voter in twenty and receive
nothing.
Two independent reviews have found the threshold higher than it needs to be. The
Electoral Commission's 2012 review of MMP recommended lowering it to 4 percent.
The Independent Electoral Review recommended 3.5 percent in its 2023 final report,
concluding that a lower threshold would broaden representation while still allowing
stable parliaments to form. Neither recommendation has been implemented.

3

---

The Alliance will lower the party vote threshold to 3.5 percent, in line with the most
recent independent recommendation. Over time, we believe that the removal of the
threshold and a simple percentage for each seat is the most democratic option, and
will represent the implementation of a proportional system reaching maturity.
A threshold set high enough to discard one voter in twenty is protecting incumbents
rather than stability.
Any future changes to the electoral system must preserve the fundamental principles
of proportionality, representation, and political pluralism.

5. Strengthening Parliament
Parliament must be an effective check on executive power.
The Alliance will strengthen the ability of parliament and its select committees to
scrutinise government decisions, public expenditure, and the performance of
government agencies.
Government budgets should be accompanied by independent assessments of their:
- social impacts;
- economic impacts;
- environmental impacts; and
- distributional consequences.
Select committees will be strengthened so that they can properly question ministers,
officials, and public agencies.
Officials should be required to provide parliament with accurate and comprehensive
information, subject to legitimate protections for privacy, national security, and other
clearly defined public interests.

6. Reforming Cabinet Government
The Alliance will reform the conventions surrounding cabinet collective
responsibility.
Ministers should not be prevented from expressing disagreement with government
policies, or from participating openly in parliamentary debate.
Collective responsibility should operate where necessary to ensure coherent
government, but it should not become a mechanism for suppressing legitimate
political debate or preventing parliament from properly scrutinising government
decisions.
Greater transparency within government will strengthen rather than weaken
democratic government.

7. A Written Constitution
New Zealand's constitutional arrangements have developed through legislation,
convention, judicial decisions, and historical practice.
The Alliance will initiate a comprehensive constitutional review to examine
whether Aotearoa should adopt a formal written constitution.
The review will be nationwide, participatory, and transparent to build a constitution
democratically from the ground up.
It should consider:
- the constitutional status of Te Tiriti o Waitangi;
- the recommendations of the Matike Mai report;
- the rights and responsibilities of citizens;
- the relationship between parliament, the executive, and the judiciary;
- the powers and limitations of government;
- the role of local government;
- the protection of democratic rights;
- mechanisms for constitutional amendment; and
- appropriate checks and balances on state power.
Any proposed written constitution must emerge from a genuine public process,
rather than being imposed by one government or political party.

8. Reviewing the Structure of Parliament
As part of the constitutional review, the Alliance will examine whether the present
structure of parliament provides the best possible system of democratic
representation and accountability.
This review will consider the possible establishment of a second legislative
chamber, including its purpose, composition, powers, and democratic legitimacy.
A second chamber is only worth having if it improves scrutiny. An appointed house of
former ministers would not achieve this.
The review will also consider appropriate mechanisms for ensuring regional,
community, and other forms of representation are properly reflected within the
constitutional system.

9. Citizens’ Initiatives
The Alliance supports greater opportunities for citizens to directly participate in the
legislative process.
The constitutional review will examine the establishment of Citizens’ Initiatives,
allowing citizens to propose legislative or policy changes through an appropriate
democratic process.
Any such system will include safeguards to ensure:
- adequate public information;
- meaningful debate;
- protection of minority and individual rights;
- clear and workable questions; and
- democratic legitimacy.
Direct democracy should complement representative democracy rather than replace
it.

10. The Future of the Monarchy
The Alliance believes that the constitutional status of the Crown should be subject to
democratic consideration.
As part of the national constitutional review, the people of Aotearoa should have the
opportunity to determine the future constitutional relationship between Aotearoa New
Zealand and the monarchy.
The Alliance therefore supports consideration of a referendum on the future of the
monarchy as part of a wider constitutional process.
Any constitutional change should be determined democratically by the people of
Aotearoa New Zealand.

11. Open and Transparent Government
Democracy cannot function effectively when citizens are denied access to
information about decisions made in their name.
The Alliance will strengthen the principle that government information belongs to
the public, subject only to clearly justified exceptions.
The Official Information Act will be extended to cover publicly listed companies in
which the Crown holds a controlling stake, which currently sit outside it, and the
grounds for withholding will be narrowed across the entities already covered.
Public information should be:

- freely accessible;
- provided promptly;
- available in understandable forms;
- proactively released wherever possible; and
- withheld only where there is a compelling and legally recognised reason.
The burden should be on government to justify secrecy, not on citizens to justify their
right to know.

12. Political Integrity and Conflicts of Interest
Public office exists to serve the public.
The Alliance will introduce stronger requirements to prevent conflicts of interest and
the undue influence of private wealth over political decision-making.
Candidates for public office will be required to publicly disclose relevant financial
interests and assets.
Elected representatives and senior public officials will be subject to clear and
enforceable conflict-of-interest rules.
The objective is not to prevent people with legitimate business, professional, or
community interests from entering politics. It is to ensure that those interests are
transparent and cannot secretly influence public decisions.

13. Political Donations and Democratic Equality
Political influence must not be determined by the ability to make large financial
contributions.
The Alliance will establish strict transparency requirements for political donations.
All significant individual and corporate donations to political parties will be publicly
disclosed.
The Alliance will also consider appropriate limits on political donations to prevent
wealthy individuals, corporations, and other organisations from acquiring
disproportionate political influence.
Political parties must be accountable for the sources of their funding.
The democratic process should be based on equal political citizenship, not the
financial power of particular interests.

14. Democracy and Economic Power
Political democracy cannot be separated from economic democracy.

7

---

A society in which a small number of corporations control essential infrastructure,
natural resources, employment, and investment cannot provide genuine democratic
control simply by holding regular elections.
The Alliance will therefore pursue democratic control of essential economic
resources through:
- public ownership;
- cooperative ownership;
- community ownership;
- worker participation;
- strong trade union rights; and
- democratic economic planning.
This principle connects our constitutional policy with our economic, environmental,
and social policies.
The people must have the power to determine the direction of society, rather than
being subordinate to the decisions of concentrated private economic power.

15. Democracy, Environment, and Future Generations
Democratic government has a responsibility not only to those who can vote today,
but also to future generations.
Environmental decisions must therefore be subject to democratic scrutiny and
long-term assessment.
Communities and tangata whenua must have a meaningful role in decisions
concerning:
- land;
- water;
- forests;
- minerals;
- energy;
- biodiversity; and
- other natural resources.
Environmental democracy and stewardship means that decisions affecting the
commons cannot be left solely to corporations, private investors, or central
government agencies.

8

---

This principle reinforces the Alliance commitment to public and community control of
essential resources, and to the protection of the natural environment for future
generations.

16. A Democratic Constitution for Aotearoa
The Alliance's constitutional vision is ultimately about where power resides.
We reject a political system in which citizens are expected to hand their democratic
authority to governments every three years and then have little meaningful influence
over the decisions that follow.
We seek a society in which:
- parliament represents the people proportionally;
- government is transparent and accountable;
- representatives are subject to recall by democratic means;
- communities have genuine democratic power;
- tangata whenua exercise their constitutional rights under Te Tiriti o Waitangi;
- citizens can participate directly in important decisions;
- political influence is not determined by wealth;
- public resources are protected from private exploitation; and
- economic power is subject to democratic control.
The Alliance will work towards a constitutional system that reflects the character of
Aotearoa as a democratic, diverse, and Treaty-based nation.
Our goal is not simply a better government. It is a more democratic society, but
one in which power is placed in the hands of the people and exercised in the
interests of all.
