# ACT New Zealand

> Party founded in 1994; Wikipedia's infobox describes it as classical-liberal and right-libertarian, and places it on the right. It has been a junior partner in the National-led coalition since November 2023.
> **Leader:** [David Seymour](./people/david-seymour/README.md) (since October 2014). He has been Deputy Prime Minister since 31 May 2025 and is also Minister for Regulation.
> **Deputy leader:** [Nicole McKee](./people/nicole-mckee/README.md) (since 28 June 2026, replacing Brooke van Velden, who is retiring at the election). **President:** John Windsor.

## History

**Origins (1993–2004).** ACT grew out of the "Backbone Club" of Labour MPs who backed Roger Douglas's free-market reforms ("Rogernomics"). It began as a pressure group and became a party to contest the first MMP election. Its platform was based on Douglas's book *Unfinished Business*. Under Richard Prebble (leader 1996–2004) it won 6.1% in 1996, 7.0% in 1999 and 7.1% in 2002.

**Collapse and the Epsom lifeline (2005–2011).** After Prebble left, ACT fell to 1.5% in 2005. It survived only because Rodney Hide won Epsom. In 2008 it took 3.65% and five MPs and gave National confidence and supply: Hide became Minister of Local Government and Regulatory Reform, and Heather Roy a minister outside Cabinet. Former National leader Don Brash joined ACT and took over in April 2011 when Hide stepped aside, amid internal pressure on Hide's leadership.

**The one-MP era (2011–2020).** From 2011 to 2020 ACT had a single MP, every time through Epsom, where National signalled that its voters should give ACT their electorate vote:

| Election | Leader | Party vote | Seats | Role |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2011 | Don Brash (John Banks led after the election) | 1.07% | 1 (Banks, Epsom) | Confidence and supply with National. Banks became Minister for Regulatory Reform and Small Business. The deal brought in charter (partnership) schools |
| 2014 | Jamie Whyte (Seymour from 3 Oct 2014) | 0.69% | 1 (Seymour, Epsom) | Confidence and supply. Seymour became Parliamentary Under-Secretary for Education (charter schools) and Regulatory Reform |
| 2017 | David Seymour | 0.50% | 1 | Opposition |
| 2020 | David Seymour | 7.58% | 10 | Opposition |
| 2023 | David Seymour | 8.64% | 11 | Coalition with National and NZ First |

- The 2011 "teapot tape" meeting between John Key and Banks in Epsom made the Epsom arrangement a national controversy. In 2014 John Key again encouraged National voters in Epsom to back Seymour, and about 60% of National voters there split their vote.
- John Banks retired as leader in 2014 to fight electoral-fraud charges over his 2010 Auckland mayoral campaign return. He was convicted in 2014, but the Court of Appeal quashed the conviction in 2015 and the Crown did not seek a retrial ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Banks_(New_Zealand_politician))).
- Leader Jamie Whyte (2014) attracted controversy over remarks on incest laws and on Māori "legal privilege". He resigned after the election.
- ACT's main achievement as a one-MP party was Seymour's **End of Life Choice Act**. It passed in 2019, was approved at the 2020 referendum and came into force on 7 November 2021.

**Breakthrough (2020).** ACT's rise to 7.6% and 10 MPs had three causes, according to analysts cited by Wikipedia. Seymour was the only MP to vote against the 2019 semi-automatic firearms ban, which drew gun owners (and firearms advocate Nicole McKee) to ACT. He was also a sharp critic of the COVID-19 response. And National was in leadership turmoil, while some NZ First voters were unhappy with Labour.

**2023 and coalition.** ACT won 8.64% and 11 seats, its best result. Brooke van Velden won Tāmaki, ACT's first electorate outside Epsom. In 2022 Seymour had said a Treaty-principles referendum would be a condition of coalition. In the end ACT signed a coalition agreement with National on 24 November 2023 that did not include a referendum (see below).

## In government, 2023–2026

**Ministers:** Seymour (Regulation; Associate Finance, Health (Pharmac) and Education; Deputy PM from 31 May 2025), Brooke van Velden (Internal Affairs; Workplace Relations and Safety), Nicole McKee (Courts; Associate Justice (firearms)), Karen Chhour (Children; Prevention of Family and Sexual Violence), Andrew Hoggard (Biosecurity; Food Safety; Associate Agriculture). Simon Court was Parliamentary Under-Secretary for Infrastructure and RMA reform.

**Main coalition wins delivered** (details and sources in the [track record](./track-record.md)):
- **Ministry for Regulation**, established 1 March 2024 and funded by abolishing the Productivity Commission.
- **Regulatory Standards Act 2025**, passed 13 November 2025 (68–55), in force 1 January 2026.
- **Charter schools return**: legislation passed 25 September 2024; 18 schools open by July 2026, against Budget 2024 funding for 50.
- **Three-strikes law reinstated** (December 2024, introduced by McKee) and an **Arms Act rewrite** (Arms Act 2026, passed 29 July 2026).
- Interest deductibility for landlords restored (80% from April 2024, 100% from April 2025), pet bonds, 90-day trials for all employers, repeal of Fair Pay Agreements, and a Reserve Bank inflation-only mandate.
- Repeal of section 7AA of the Oranga Tamariki Act (April 2025), and a "need not race" directive for public services (September 2024).
- Deputy PM rotation: Seymour succeeded Winston Peters on 31 May 2025.

**What ACT did not get:**
- The **Treaty Principles Bill** was introduced and passed its first reading, as agreed. It drew a record 300,000 written submissions, about 90% opposed, and the Hīkoi mō te Tiriti. It was **voted down 112–11 at its second reading on 10 April 2025**, with National and NZ First voting against.
- No return of the public service to 2017 size, no abolition of "demographic" ministries, no repeal of the Zero Carbon Act, no reversal of the live-export ban, and no four-year-term referendum.
- ACT used the "agree to disagree" clause on the Fair Digital News Bargaining Bill (July 2024), the scope of the firearms-registry review (May 2025) and the under-16 social media ban (August 2026). ACT also opposed the modern slavery bill (January 2026) and a gift-card expiry bill, and refused government backing for National's social-media bill in May 2025.

**Controversies (factual):**
- In June 2024 Stuff reported allegations of bullying and a "culture of fear" during ACT's 2023 campaign. The party said an independent review had been carried out and its recommendations accepted.
- The 2025 Equal Pay Amendment Act, led by Brooke van Velden as Workplace Relations Minister, passed under urgency. It raised the thresholds for pay-equity claims and discontinued the 33 claims then in progress ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equal_Pay_Amendment_Act_2025)).
- The redesigned school lunch programme was criticised for meal quality.
- In June 2026 Porirua candidate Lyra Yan Zhang left the list after failing to declare past membership of the China Zhi Gong Party.

## Money: donations

- In 2022 ACT received over $2 million in large donations, including several rich-list donors giving $100,000 each ([Newshub](https://www.newshub.co.nz/home/politics/2023/05/electoral-donations-for-2022-national-received-more-than-10-times-what-labour-got.html)).
- ACT declared about $4.3m in donations in its 2023 return, behind National's $10.4m and Labour's $4.8m ([Weekend Herald](https://www.pressreader.com/new-zealand/weekend-herald/20240504/281578065741385)).
- In the 2025 annual returns ACT overtook Labour in donations ([RNZ, 7 May 2026](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/594551/national-tops-party-donations-list-act-overtakes-labour)).
- Wikipedia's 2026 election tracker lists about $4.79m in declared donations as of late October 2026. Donors include Nick Mowbray ($225,000), Brian Cartmell ($200,000), Greymouth Petroleum subsidiary GMP Environmental ($100,000) and Van Den Brink Karaka ($100,000). ACT reported $2.35m raised in 2026 by 24 September ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_New_Zealand_general_election)).

## 2026 pitch

The campaign was launched at Shed 10, Auckland, on 28 June 2026 under the theme **"Lock Labour Out, Unlock New Zealand's Potential"**. Seymour named three focuses: government waste, cutting business restrictions, and equal rights under the law ([Spinoff](https://thespinoff.co.nz/politics/29-06-2026/aura-edits-and-protests-field-notes-from-the-act-partys-big-flash-rally), [speech on Scoop](https://www.scoop.co.nz/stories/PA2606/S00205.htm)). Broadcaster Paul Henry is fourth on the list. Headline promises, all sourced in [`act.json`](../../data/promises/act.json) and [policies](./policies/README.md):

- **Fiscal plan (11 October 2026):** gradually raise the NZ Super age by three months a year; pause Super Fund contributions; return non-welfare spending to 2017 per-capita levels; $653m in disability-benefit savings through independent medical certification; abolish demographic ministries; cut foreign aid ([1News](https://www.1news.co.nz/2026/10/11/act-sets-out-plan-to-raise-retirement-age-cut-debt-and-spending/)).
- Freeze the adult minimum wage for three years and introduce a training wage; no tax on KiwiSaver earnings; repeal the Zero Carbon Act and net-zero target; abolish the Climate Change Commission.
- Disestablish the Waitangi Tribunal once historical claims are settled; entrench "equal rights" in constitutional statutes; end "race-based hiring" in the public service.
- Immigration restrictions: deport serious non-citizen offenders, a five-year benefit stand-down for new residents, a $6-a-day surcharge on temporary work visas, and abolition of the permanent-resident visa category.
- Add burglary to three strikes; a $2b National Cancer Centre (Henry); raise Pharmac's share of the health budget; let pharmacists treat more conditions; supermarket competition through a one-stop consenting body, while opposing a forced break-up.

## Coalition stance for 2026

- ACT is campaigning to re-form the National-led government and keep Labour out ("Lock Labour Out").
- Seymour defended voters' right to split their vote, after National's John Key and Christopher Luxon urged National supporters not to.
- ACT and NZ First have clashed publicly. After NZ First pledged to repeal the Regulatory Standards Act it had voted for, Seymour accused Peters of bad faith and of positioning for a deal with Labour ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regulatory_Standards_Act_2025)).
