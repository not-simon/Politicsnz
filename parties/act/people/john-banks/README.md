## Current status

Former MP and former ACT leader. Resigned from Parliament in June 2014 after a High Court conviction for filing a false electoral return, which was later overturned; in May 2015 the Court of Appeal declined to order a retrial after new evidence showed Kim and Mona Dotcom's account had been fabricated. Not a 2026 candidate.

## Biography

Born in Wellington on 2 December 1946; his parents were imprisoned for procuring abortions when he was a child and from age two he was raised by an aunt and uncle among many foster children, later attending Avondale College. Worked in pharmaceutical market research, commercial property development and restaurants. Birkenhead Borough councillor from 1977. Married Amanda Medcalf in 1987 (later separated); they adopted three children from St Petersburg. CNZM (2011), QSO (2000). A long-time talkback host (Radio Pacific 1992–2007; later MagicTalk).

## Political career

- National MP for Whangarei 1981–1999; Minister of Police, Tourism and Sport from 1990; left Cabinet in 1996 rather than serve alongside coalition partner Winston Peters; retired in 1999.
- Mayor of Auckland City 2001–2004 (lost to Dick Hubbard) and 2007–2010; lost the 2010 Auckland "super city" mayoralty to Len Brown.
- Joined ACT in May 2011 and won **Epsom** in 2011 (after the "cup of tea" with John Key) as ACT's only MP; ACT leader from February 2012 to 2014 (succeeded by Jamie Whyte, who was not an MP). Minister outside Cabinet in John Key's government until resigning his portfolios in October 2013 when committed for trial.

## Record & notable actions

- As a 1990s minister he said he could not persuade Cabinet to ban semi-automatic guns after Aramoana; after the 2019 Christchurch attacks he said he was "haunted" by that.
- As ACT leader secured, under the 2011 confidence-and-supply deal, the introduction of partnership (charter) schools.
- Voted for the 2013 marriage equality law, saying that "if I knew then what I have since learnt, I would have acted differently" — reversing his 1986 opposition to homosexual law reform.
- The Local Electoral Amendment Act 2013 (the "John Banks bill") tightened rules on anonymous local-election donations in response to his 2010 return.

## Controversies

- **False electoral return (2010–2015):** charged by private prosecution over two $25,000 donations from Kim Dotcom declared as anonymous; convicted on 1 August 2014 (two months' community detention), conviction quashed on appeal in November 2014, and no retrial ordered in May 2015.
- Long record of contentious remarks: opposing 1986 homosexual law reform ("sad and sickening day"), comments about Asian immigrants (2004) and Māori men in a safe-sex campaign (2006); in January 2021 MagicTalk dropped him after he agreed on air with a caller that Māori were a "stone age" people.
- The 2011 "teapot tape" — a journalist's recording of his private conversation with John Key — became a major election-campaign controversy.
- In a 2016 paternity case brought by Antony Shaw, the High Court declared Banks to be Shaw's birth father; Shaw's mother alleged in an affidavit that Banks had pressured her to take drugs to induce a miscarriage in 1969, which Banks had denied.
