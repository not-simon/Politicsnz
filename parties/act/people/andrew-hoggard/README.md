## Current status

ACT list MP since 2023 and minister outside Cabinet (Biosecurity; Food Safety; Associate Agriculture and Environment). Standing in **Rangitīkei** and **#3** on ACT's list for 2026.

## Biography

Born 1974 or 1975; educated at Heretaunga College, Upper Hutt, and Massey University (Bachelor of Agricultural Economics, 1996). Dairy farmer at Kiwitea, Manawatū, from 1998; Taranaki–Manawatū Young Farmer of the Year (2003). Held Federated Farmers leadership roles from 2014 — dairy chair, vice-president, then president from June 2020 until stepping down early on 8 May 2023 — and sat on the International Dairy Federation board. As president he opposed Labour's planned pricing of agricultural emissions.

## Political career

- ACT member since 2019; announced as ACT's Rangitīkei candidate the day after leaving Federated Farmers. Third in Rangitīkei in 2023; elected at list #5.
- Minister for Biosecurity and Food Safety and Associate Minister of Agriculture and Environment from November 2023.

## Record & notable actions

- Suspended (announced March 2024) councils' obligations to identify new Significant Natural Areas for three years during RMA reform.
- Halted poultry exports after an H7N6 avian influenza outbreak near Moeraki (December 2024).
- Rewrote pig welfare rules (October 2025): farrowing crates retained with tighter rules and a 10-year transition rather than banned — released papers showed the National Animal Welfare Advisory Committee had disagreed.
- Confirmed in March 2026 that the coalition would not reverse the live-animal export ban this term, as Cabinet consensus could not be reached.
- Backed investor-funded voluntary carbon and nature markets (May 2026).

## Controversies

- **SNA announcement (March 2024):** his statement that the SNA requirement was suspended was described by law professor Andrew Geddis as "misleading at best, and borderline unlawful at worst" because a minister cannot remove a statutory duty by announcement; Hoggard clarified that no legal change had yet taken effect.
- **Infant formula standards (2024–25):** his decision not to adopt the joint trans-Tasman infant formula standard was welcomed by industry but criticised by the College of Midwives; RNZ reported he had been lobbied heavily by dairy companies (including through his sister), after which the government reconsidered.
