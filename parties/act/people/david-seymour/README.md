## Current status

Leader of ACT, MP for Epsom since 2014, **Deputy Prime Minister** since 31 May 2025 (under the coalition's job-share with Winston Peters) and the first **Minister for Regulation**. Standing again in **Epsom** and **#1** on ACT's list for 7 November 2026.

## Biography

Born in Palmerston North on 24 June 1983 and raised in Whangārei; has Ngāpuhi (Ngāti Rēhia) ancestry through a great-great-great-grandmother. Educated at Auckland Grammar and the University of Auckland (BE in electrical and electronic engineering and BA in philosophy, 2006). Worked in engineering and then for about five years as a policy analyst at Canadian conservative think tanks (Frontier Centre for Public Policy, Manning Centre) and completed the Atlas Network's 2008 "MBA for Think Tanks" programme. Former president of ACT on Campus. Contestant on *Dancing with the Stars* (2018).

## Political career

- ACT candidate in Mt Albert (2005), list-only (2008) and Auckland Central (2011).
- Adviser to ACT leader John Banks (2011–2014), working on the partnership schools legislation.
- Won **Epsom** in 2014 (with John Key's tacit endorsement) as ACT's sole MP and became leader on 3 October 2014; Parliamentary Under-Secretary for Education and Regulatory Reform 2014–2017.
- Sole ACT MP again in 2017; led ACT to 10 seats in 2020 and a record 11 seats (8.64%) in 2023, after which ACT joined the National-led coalition.

## Record & notable actions

- **End of Life Choice Act 2019**: his member's bill legalising assisted dying passed its third reading 69–51 with a referendum requirement added for NZ First; approved by 65.9% in the 2020 referendum.
- **Regulatory Standards Act**: as Minister for Regulation sponsored the Regulatory Standards Bill (passed third reading along party lines on 13 November 2025), setting principles for good law-making emphasising individual and property rights; criticised by legal academics such as Jane Kelsey.
- **Treaty Principles Bill**: ACT's bill redefining the principles of the Treaty of Waitangi was supported to select committee under the coalition deal but defeated at second reading 11–112 on 10 April 2025; it prompted the Hīkoi mō te Tiriti, one of the largest protests in New Zealand history.
- As Associate Education Minister reintroduced charter (partnership) schools ($153 million in Budget 2024 for 35 conversions and 15 new schools) and redesigned the free school lunch programme (Ka Ora, Ka Ako) to a cheaper bulk-supplied model — whose 2025 rollout drew complaints about food quality.
- As Associate Health Minister restored over-the-counter pseudoephedrine sales (2024) and directed Pharmac to stop factoring the Treaty of Waitangi into decisions.
- The only MP to vote against the 2019 semi-automatic firearms ban (119–1); removed "safe zones" from the 2020 abortion law (later reinstated with his support in 2022); passed a 2015 bill allowing early-morning bar openings for the Rugby World Cup.

## Controversies

- **Māori and co-governance:** a vocal opponent of co-governance, He Puapua and the Māori Health Authority; critics including Te Pāti Māori, Labour's Willie Jackson and many iwi leaders accuse him of divisive, majoritarian politics, while supporters see a defence of equal citizenship.
- **Māori vaccination code (2021):** publicly tweeted a booking access code intended for Māori at a Whānau Waipareira vaccination site; criticised by CEO John Tamihere and others.
- **"Guy Fawkes" remark (2023):** joked on Newstalk ZB about sending "a guy like Guy Fawkes" into the Ministry for Pacific Peoples; condemned by PM Chris Hipkins and others; he refused to apologise and police found no offence.
- **Golriz Ghahraman (2019):** called her a "menace to freedom" and likened her hate-speech views to tactics of Mao, Stalin and Hitler.
- **Snapchat with teenagers (2024):** reports that he had exchanged Snapchat messages and "streaks" with school students since about 2016, including an ACT-branded condom photo captioned "Be Safe Kids"; he said he saw no issue.
- **Atlas Network:** called questions about his links a "crazy conspiracy theory", although he had completed an Atlas programme and referred to "my old friends at the Atlas Network" in 2021.
- In December 2022 PM Jacinda Ardern was caught on a hot mic calling him an "arrogant prick"; she apologised and they auctioned a signed copy of Hansard for charity.
