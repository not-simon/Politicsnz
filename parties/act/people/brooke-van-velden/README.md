## Current status

MP for Tāmaki and a Cabinet minister (Internal Affairs; Workplace Relations and Safety) in the National–ACT–NZ First government. **Retiring** at the 2026 election (announced 24 March 2026); replaced as ACT deputy leader by Nicole McKee on 28 June 2026.

## Biography

Born in Auckland on 15 October 1992 to a nurse and a mechanic who later ran car-sales businesses; educated at St Cuthbert's College and the University of Auckland (BA/BCom in economics and international trade, 2016). Worked for lobbying firm Exceltium, then as a staffer for David Seymour, spending two years lobbying MPs and helping draft the End of Life Choice Bill. Says she was a Green voter before switching to ACT at university.

## Political career

- ACT candidate for Auckland Central in 2017 (list #3, not elected).
- Elected ACT deputy leader in June 2020 aged 27; entered Parliament on the list in 2020 (ACT whip; health, housing, foreign affairs and trade spokesperson).
- Won **Tāmaki** in 2023 by 4,158 votes, ending National's hold since 1960.
- Became one of the youngest Cabinet ministers in New Zealand history in November 2023.

## Record & notable actions

- Repealed Labour's Fair Pay Agreements Act (December 2023).
- Set minimum wage increases (2% to $23.15 in April 2024); expanded the terms of reference of the Royal Commission into COVID-19 lessons.
- **Equal Pay Amendment Act 2025**: passed under urgency on 6–7 May 2025, raising thresholds for pay-equity claims and stopping 33 existing claims; strongly opposed by unions and opposition parties.
- **Employment Relations Amendment Act 2026** (passed 17 February 2026): "gateway test" for contractors, tighter personal-grievance rules, no remedies for dismissals for serious misconduct, removal of the 30-day rule.
- Holidays Act overhaul toward hours-based accrual of leave; online casino licensing bill (2025); a citizenship test from mid-2027.
- WorkSafe "road cone tipline" (June–December 2025), shut down after ~1,300 notifications at a cost of about $150,000.
- Moved the 2021 parliamentary motion on human-rights abuses against Uyghurs in Xinjiang (adopted unanimously after Labour removed the word "genocide").

## Controversies

- The pay-equity changes were a major controversy; a Labour branch's Facebook post depicting her in a Nazi uniform was deleted and Labour's leadership apologised. In a May 2025 debate she quoted an expletive from a Stuff column criticising her, drawing criticism from Winston Peters.
- A 2023 comment that during COVID New Zealand "completely blew out what the value of a life was" was criticised by opponents.
