## Current status

ACT list MP since 2020 and Cabinet minister (Children; Prevention of Family and Sexual Violence). Standing in **North Shore** and **#5** on ACT's list for 2026.

## Biography

Of Ngāpuhi descent; born in Australia and raised by grandparents in Kaeo before returning to her mother on Auckland's North Shore at five. She ran away from home repeatedly as a child and spent time in state/foster care, which she cites as the reason for her focus on child welfare. Worked in property management before politics.

## Political career

- Contested Upper Harbour in 2020 and 2023 (fourth both times); elected at list #7 in 2020 and re-elected in 2023.
- Minister for Children and Minister for the Prevention of Family and Sexual Violence from November 2023.

## Record & notable actions

- **Repeal of section 7AA of the Oranga Tamariki Act** (Treaty obligations/whānau placement): she introduced a member's bill in 2022 and, as minister, the government bill passed its third reading on 3 April 2025. She argued the clause meant "children being put second"; the Waitangi Tribunal's interim report said the repeal would harm vulnerable children.
- **Waitangi Tribunal summons (2024)**: the Tribunal summonsed her to give evidence; the High Court set aside the summons, but the Court of Appeal reinstated it on 13 May 2024, the day the repeal bill was introduced (which ended the inquiry).
- Launched a military-style academy pilot for serious youth offenders (July 2024), criticised by the Greens, Te Pāti Māori, IHC and academics; introduced a Youth Serious Offender declaration and expanded monitoring powers.
- Created an independent Child Protection Investigation Unit (November 2024).
- Opposed Labour's Smokefree generational tobacco ban as unworkable (2021).

## Controversies

- In September 2022 Children's Minister Kelvin Davis told her to "enter the Māori world" and stop looking through a "vanilla lens"; he apologised the next day.
- In 2024 she said she faced bullying and an "unsafe work environment" at Parliament after Te Pāti Māori posts linked her views to her uplifting as a child and MP Mariameno Kapa-Kingi called her an ACT "puppet"; ACT accused the Speaker of not acting on her complaint.
