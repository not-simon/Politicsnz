## Current status

ACT list MP since 2023 (previously a National list MP 2014–2020). Standing in **Pakuranga** and **#10** on ACT's list for 2026.

## Biography

Born in India in 1970, the daughter of an Indian Air Force officer; master's in biochemistry in India, moved to New Zealand in 1995 and gained a PhD in biological sciences from the University of Auckland (2003). Worked as a scientist, co-directed a confectionery company and hosted current-affairs talkback on Radio Tarana; served on the Film and Video Labelling Body and the Families Commission board. The first Indian-born woman to represent a party in the New Zealand Parliament.

## Political career

- National candidate for Mount Roskill in 2014, the 2016 by-election and 2017 (second each time); National list MP 2014–2020; lost in 2020 and was not selected by National for 2023.
- Switched to ACT on 31 May 2023; ACT candidate for Pakuranga (third) and elected at list #9.

## Record & notable actions

- As a National MP: Newborn Enrolment with General Practice Bill (passed first reading unanimously in 2017, defeated at second reading 2018); Patents (Advancement Patents) Amendment Bill (defeated 2018); a private bill fixing Auckland Regional Amenities funding reporting (law July 2020).
- As ACT education spokesperson opposed University of Auckland Māori and Pasifika "safe spaces" (2024) and its compulsory Waipapa Taumata Rau course (2025); introduced a member's bill (March 2025) to bar universities offering ethnicity-based services including scholarships and accommodation.
- In June 2026 asked the Police Commissioner to investigate Brian Tamaki's remarks about purging non-Christians.

## Controversies

- In 2014 Labour's Rajen Prasad questioned her remaining on the Families Commission board after she was photographed wearing a National rosette; she resigned on being confirmed as a candidate.
- In April 2026 she was the target of a haka by former Te Pāti Māori president Che Wilson at a regional kapa haka competition that included the phrase "bloody Indian curry" and mocked Indian accents; she called it "deeply troubling". Te Matatini and Whakaata Māori removed the footage, and several Māori commentators condemned the performance while also criticising ACT's policies.
