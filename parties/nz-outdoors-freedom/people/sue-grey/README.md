## Current status

Leader of the NZ Outdoors & Freedom Party, **#1** on its 2026 list and candidate for **West Coast-Tasman**. Never an MP.

## Biography

Susan Jane Grey holds law and science (microbiology, biochemistry) degrees; admitted in 1990; a self-employed lawyer in Nelson known for environmental and medicinal-cannabis work. In 2008–2010 she represented wool growers in the Saxmere case, which led to Justice Bill Wilson's resignation; she was dismissed from a Department of Conservation legal role over the case.

## Political career

- Co-leader of the NZ Outdoors Party from January 2020 (declined a 2020 takeover approach from Billy Te Kahika); contested the 2022 Tauranga by-election.
- Joined Freedoms NZ as co-leader with Brian Tamaki in 2023 (#2 on its list; 0.33% party vote); seventh in West Coast-Tasman.

## Record & notable actions

- Campaigns against 1080 poison, 5G and COVID-19 vaccination; advocates medicinal cannabis access.

## Controversies

- Described by Wikipedia and mainstream media as a conspiracy theorist who has spread COVID-19 vaccine misinformation, leading to Facebook bans and Law Society complaints; a disciplinary tribunal found in August 2023 that her comments, made as a private citizen, were not misconduct.
- In the 2022 "Baby W" case she falsely claimed blood from vaccinated donors was unsafe; the High Court granted Health NZ guardianship for surgery.
- Briefly held in custody for contempt of court in December 2022 after disrupting a trial; she apologised.
- Led the 2017 Brook Valley legal challenge to an aerial poison drop near Nelson, during which protesters assaulted MP Nick Smith (Grey was not accused of the assault).
