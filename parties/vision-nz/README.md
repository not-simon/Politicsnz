## History

Vision NZ is the third party linked to the Tamakis' Destiny Church, after Destiny New Zealand (2003–07) and the Family
Party (2007–10). Its positions have included opposition to abortion and homosexuality, a call to ban construction of
new mosques, a (later qualified) two-year halt to immigration and refugees, removing non-citizen permanent residents'
right to vote — alongside support for a Māori-owned bank, Tūhoe ownership of Te Urewera and government funding for
Destiny Church programmes. Labour and National ruled out working with it by December 2019. [1]

In 2020 it ran a five-person list and won **0.15%**; Hannah Tamaki came third in Waiariki (1,171 votes). After Brian
Tamaki launched [Freedoms NZ](../freedoms-nz/README.md) in August 2022, Vision NZ became a component party; in 2023 it
contested all seven Māori electorates but the party vote went to Freedoms NZ (0.34%). Hannah Tamaki came last of five
in Tāmaki Makaurau (829 votes). In August–September 2023 Vision candidate Karl Mokaraka repeatedly disrupted Labour,
National and ACT campaign events. After Freedoms NZ deregistered (May 2025), Hannah Tamaki stood for Vision NZ in the
September 2025 Tāmaki Makaurau by-election (175 of 10,688 votes). [1][2]

A Vision member's High Court challenge to Wellington's Cuba Street rainbow crossing was dismissed in March 2025 with
costs awarded against the plaintiffs. [1]

## 2026 campaign

- **List (6):** 1 Hannah Tamaki (Papakura), 2 Heker Robertson, 3 Sawan Jerry (Epsom), 4 Karl Mokaraka (Ōtāhuhu), 5 Naomi
  Maclean (Southland), 6 Anne Williamson. [3] On 26 September 2026 Mokaraka confronted Labour leader Chris Hipkins in
  Ōtara. [4]
- policy.nz: "promoting Christian family values, a smaller role for government, pausing immigration and taking
  conservative positions on indigenous rights"; 2025 donations $6,718.43; polling "<0.5%". Broadcasting allocation
  $78,887 (amended $82,011). [5][4]
