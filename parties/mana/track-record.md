# Mana Movement — track record (2011–2014)

Mana held a single opposition seat (Hone Harawira, Te Tai Tokerau) from June 2011 to September 2014 and never had a
confidence-and-supply or coalition agreement, so it had no way to implement its programme directly. Its record is
therefore about what its MP pursued in Parliament and whether it kept its electoral undertakings.
Structured data: [`data/promises/mana.json`](../../data/promises/mana.json).

## Platform (2011)

Mana's 2011 platform included: government-funded breakfast and lunch in decile 1–2 schools; abolishing GST and
replacing it with a financial-transactions ("Hōne Heke") tax; nationalising monopolies and duopolies; full employment;
10,000 new state houses a year; a living wage of $18.80/hr; free education from pre-school to tertiary; an amnesty for
Pacific overstayers; and te reo Māori as a core curriculum subject. [1]

## What happened

| Commitment | Outcome | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Free breakfast and lunch in decile 1–2 schools ("Feed the Kids") | Harawira's Education (Breakfast and Lunch Programmes in Schools) Amendment Bill was drawn from the ballot on 8 Nov 2012. It did not become law while Mana was in Parliament (its detailed parliamentary history could not be re-checked because parliament.nz / legislation.govt.nz pages were not fetchable from the research environment). [2] | abandoned (not enacted; Mana left Parliament in 2014) |
| Opposition to state-asset sales | Mana activists campaigned against the partial sale of energy companies; the sales went ahead under the mixed-ownership model (National + ACT + United Future majority). [1][3] | broken (outcome not achieved — Mana in opposition) |
| Abolish GST / financial-transactions tax; 10,000 state houses a year; $18.80 living wage | No legislation by Mana on these reached a vote in our sources. | not verified |
| 2014 Internet Party MoU: Mana to hold list places 1, 3 and 4; review within five weeks of the election | Delivered as agreed; the parties reviewed and separated on 13 Dec 2014 "with no regrets". [4] | kept |
| 2017 MoU with the Māori Party: Mana would not stand against it in other seats in return for a clear run in Te Tai Tokerau | Mana stood only in Te Tai Tokerau and Māngere. [1] | kept |

## Assessment

Mana's influence came from protest and community campaigning (Glen Innes state housing, Māngere motorway, school
breakfasts) rather than from legislation. Its biggest strategic decision — the Dotcom-funded Internet MANA alliance —
increased its party vote (1.08% → 1.42%) but cost Harawira his seat when National and NZ First leaders encouraged
tactical votes for Labour's Kelvin Davis, removing Mana from Parliament. [1][3][4]
