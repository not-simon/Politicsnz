## Current status

Former MP (2005–2014). Led the Mana Movement until May 2021. Not a 2026 candidate.

## Biography

Born in Whangārei on 6 January 1955 to John and Titewhai Harawira (a prominent activist); of Ngāti Hau, Ngāti Wai, Ngāti Hine, Te Aupōuri, Ngāpuhi and Ngāti Whātua descent. Raised in West Auckland; attended St Stephen's School and the University of Auckland. A long-time activist — He Taua (1979), the 1981 Springbok tour protests, land occupations, Māori language and broadcasting, and the 2004 foreshore and seabed hīkoi. Married to Hilda Halkyard-Harawira.

## Political career

- Won **Te Tai Tokerau** for the Māori Party in 2005 and 2008.
- Clashed with the party over its support for the National-led government and the foreshore and seabed replacement law; suspended from caucus and left the party in February 2011.
- Founded the **Mana Party** (April 2011), resigned to force a by-election and won it (June 2011, majority 1,117); re-elected in November 2011.
- In 2014 Mana ran jointly with Kim Dotcom's Internet Party; Harawira lost to Labour's Kelvin Davis after National and NZ First urged tactical votes for Davis. Lost again to Davis in 2017.

## Record & notable actions

- **"Feed the Kids" Bill** (Education (Breakfast and Lunch Programmes in Schools) Amendment Bill, drawn 2012): free meals in decile 1–2 schools — a forerunner of later school-lunch policy.
- Arrested in 2012 at a Glen Innes state-house demolition protest; organised free breakfasts in South Auckland.
- Led iwi COVID-19 checkpoints into the Far North in 2020–21 (some shut down by police).

## Controversies

- A long record of inflammatory remarks: a 2009 profane email to Buddy Mikaere about "white motherf***ers" after skipping a European conference for Paris (he apologised for the wording, not the sentiment); calling John Howard a "racist bastard"; describing National's Māori MPs as John Key's "little house niggers" (2012); praising Osama bin Laden as a man who "fought for... his people" (2011, later apologised for how he expressed it); and in 2017 calling for the death penalty for Chinese methamphetamine importers.
- Said in 2010 he "wouldn't feel comfortable" if his children brought home a Pākehā partner.
