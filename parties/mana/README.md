## History

**Formation (2011).** Hone Harawira, MP for Te Tai Tokerau since 2005, left the Māori Party in February 2011 after its
disciplinary committee recommended his expulsion; he had opposed the party's stance on the foreshore and seabed
replacement law and its relationship with National. He launched the Mana Party on 30 April 2011 with backing from
activist John Minto and former Green MPs Nándor Tánczos and Sue Bradford, plus Annette Sykes and Matt McCarten. [1][2]
He resigned his seat to force a by-election (25 June 2011), which he won for Mana with a 1,117-vote majority, then held
the seat at the November 2011 general election (majority 1,165 over Labour's Kelvin Davis). [1][3][4]

**Opposition MP, 2011–14.** Harawira's member's bill to fund breakfast and lunch in decile 1–2 schools (the "Feed the
Kids" bill) was drawn from the ballot on 8 November 2012. Mana activists were prominent in protests against state-asset
sales, the TPPA and state-house demolitions in Glen Innes (Harawira was arrested there in October 2012). Mana's Te
Hamua Nikora came second (26.1%) in the 2013 Ikaroa-Rāwhiti by-election and John Minto ran for Auckland mayor in 2013
on free public transport. [1][3]

**Internet MANA (2014).** In May 2014 Mana formed an electoral alliance with Kim Dotcom's Internet Party — see
[Internet Party](../internet-party/README.md). Sue Bradford resigned in protest. The joint list won 1.42% but
Harawira lost Te Tai Tokerau to Kelvin Davis by 743 votes after National and NZ First leaders urged supporters to vote
tactically for Davis, so the alliance won no seats. It was dissolved on 13 December 2014. [1][3][5]

**2017 and after.** Under a memorandum of understanding with the Māori Party, Mana stood only in Te Tai Tokerau (and
Māngere); Harawira lost again to Davis (12,673 to 7,866) and Mana won 0.14%. It did not contest 2020, endorsing the
Māori Party, and was deregistered at its own request in 2021. [1][3]

## MPs

- **Hone Harawira** — MP for Te Tai Tokerau 2005–2014 (Māori Party 2005–11, independent Feb–June 2011, Mana
  2011–14). No person folder exists in this archive. [3]

## Notable controversies (factual)

- Harawira's 2011 by-election was criticised by Labour, the Greens and the Māori Party as a costly "publicity stunt"
  (estimated $500,000). [1]
- In September 2012 Harawira described National's Māori MPs on Facebook using a racial slur ("house niggers") and
  defended the remark; in June 2017 he called for the death penalty for Chinese methamphetamine importers. [3]
- The Internet MANA alliance with Kim Dotcom (whose money funded it) was controversial on the left; Dotcom said on
  election night that "the brand Kim Dotcom was poison". [1][5]

## Current status

Deregistered (2021). Harawira led Far North COVID-19 checkpoints in 2020–21. [1][3]
