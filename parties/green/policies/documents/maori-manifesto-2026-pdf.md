Kaupapa Māori
By Māori, for Māori

The Green Party will support Māori to make decisions
about the destiny of their own whānau, hapū and iwi.
Tangata Whenua lived undisturbed for centuries as hapū, creating tikanga
and protocols unique to the whenua , with the central focus being the health
and sustainability of their whakapapa. Supporting ongoing kaupapa Māori
approaches for Māori to fulfil their aspirations was affirmed and guaranteed by
Te Tiriti o Waitangi and provides better outcomes for all who live in our country.
When Te Tiriti o Waitangi was signed, it was assured that the relationship
between Tangata Whenua and the Crown would mean no change in the way
Māori choose to live their lives. However, successive governments continue
to implement harmful actions which have eroded Māori social structures,
contributed to the loss of traditional knowledge, and created intergenerational
inequities across many key metrics in Aotearoa, New Zealand.
The current Governments legislative assaults have targeted tangata whenua
from all sides; Health, Justice, Tamariki, Education, Social services, and Te
Tiriti o Waitangi. They have consistently ignored independent experts, the
Waitangi Tribunal, and their own advisors, who have warned that their actions
will only deepen existing inequities for Māori and contribute to further racial
divide within the country.

Our plan
 Devolve power and redistribute resources back to tangata
whenua in recognition that Māori are the decision makers and
facilitators of the destiny of their own whānau, hapū and iwi.
 Invest in outcomes that minimise the disparities and inequities
tangata whenua face across the majority of social and economic
outcomes.
 Resource marae through a Marae Resilience Fund for Māori
to lead initiatives for their people, wāhi tapu, and whenua in
accordance with their own tikanga. Create ongoing funding for
provisions for civil emergencies in recognition of being consistent
community lifelines.
 End Perpetual Leases on Whenua Māori and amend the Public
Works Act 1981 to prevent the acquisition of Māori freehold land,
Māori Customary land, and lands within a reasonable proximity
to marae, urupā, and wāhi tapu.
 Expand the learning, speaking, and the promotion of te reo Māori
in both formal and informal settings to encourage the widespread
use of the language nationwide.

The Green Party support tangata whenua-led processes, actions and decisionmaking by devolving power and investing resources in all matters that affect
Māori. We know that Te Tiriti and democracy are not contradictory, they are
complementary. Decision-making with iwi, returning whenua to its kaitiaki,
and facilitating by Māori, for Māori solutions to target inequities and minimise
disparities are all ways we can uphold te Tiriti while honouring the democracy
this country should have been built on.

Kaupapa Māori

It is time to remove the burden from Māori to keep reminding the Crown of their
promises and for the Crown to take accountability for their ongoing injustices
against te Tiriti and using te iwi Māori as a political football.

Children

Thriving tamariki and rangatahi
The Green Party will work to build a system that puts
tamariki, rangatahi and whānau first.
Tamariki Māori are disproportionately impacted by a child protection system
that excludes meaningful partnership and underinvests in culturally grounded,
whānau-led solutions. Whakapapa and identity must be centred with whānau
and hapū resourced to lead decisions affecting their tamariki. We reflect our
commitment to Te Tiriti o Waitangi by addressing the inequities tamariki Māori
face in everyday life and in the state care system.

Our plan
 Prioritise workforce safety by ringfencing training and workforce
development strategy prioritising Māori, Pacific, Rainbow,
Disabled, and community provider pathways.

Climate action:
resilience + adaptation
The Green Party will prepare all New Zealanders for the
impacts of climate change.
Climate change is one of the biggest challenges of our time and the impacts
are already being felt by communities all around the rohe. Severe weather,
with more frequent flooding, and more frequent droughts, already are having
devastating consequences. Often, marae and community groups are on the
front lines of the emergency and clean up response, but these groups are not
supported by the Government to adequately plan for adaptation. People who
are worried about rising insurance prices due to living in areas exposed to
floods or slips have not received any clarity from the Government as to the help
that will be provided when disaster strikes, or when the areas where they live
becoming increasingly unliveable.

Our plan
 Ensure climate adaptation planning affirms tino rangatiratanga
and upholds the mana motuhake of whānau, hapū and iwi over
whenua, wai, and taonga, by appropriately funding a Māori
Climate Resilience Fund.
 Establish a dedicated fund for central and local government
to implement climate adaptation and resilience measures,
including funding for partnerships with tangata whenua and
community organisations.

Conservation
+ biodiversity

Protecting and restoring plants,
animals and ecosystems
The Green Party will protect our precious whenua and
taonga species from corporate exploitation.
Over this term, the Luxon Government has made it easier for corporations to
plunder our moana and tear up the whenua—putting private profits ahead of
our precious native wildlife. Meanwhile, many of our native plants and animals
are on the brink of collapse, and without urgent action, we’re at risk of losing
them forever.
The Green Party are committed to protecting 30% of land and oceans by
2030 because we know the mauri and health of te taiao is foundational to the
wellbeing of our people, our climate, and our communities. We will achieve this
by giving effect to Te Tiriti o Waitangi and recognising tangata whenua as the
original and ongoing kaitiaki of Aotearoa.

Our plan
 Reform conservation and wildlife protection laws to give effect to
Te Tiriti o Waitangi, prioritise indigenous-led land stewardship,
and to ensure the conservation management system prioritises
halting and reversing nature loss in New Zealand.

Conservation and biodiversity

Aotearoa is home to natural taonga found nowhere else in the world, with our
ngahere, awa, and moana central to our national identity and way of life.

Disability

An accessible Aotearoa for all
The Green Party will build an Aotearoa where disabled
people are valued, where they and their whānau live with
dignity and autonomy.
Our current laws, policies, and social norms mean disabled people are
often denied the same rights as others and are excluded from places and
activities non-disabled people take for granted. These barriers are often
worse for tāngata whaikaha Māori and for disabled people from marginalised
communities.
The current Government has made it harder for disabled people to access the
support they deserve and that enables them to participate equally in life and
their communities.
The Green Party is ambitious in its plan to create a more accessible Aotearoa
by building off the Enabling Good Lives framework and grounding our
approach in Te Tiriti o Waitangi. Our approach will operate on a high-trust
model and apply to all disabled people, including those with disabling
conditions that do not currently access support.

Our plan

Disability

 Introduce a Tiriti-based disability rights law with enforceable
accessibility standards.

Drug law reform

Putting health first to keep people safe
The Green Party will take an evidenced, health-based
approach to alcohol and other drugs to reduce harm in
our communities.

Our plan
 Ramp up funding of harm reduction and addiction support for
people who want to reduce their drug intake, including through
kaupapa Māori, Rainbow, youth, and harm reduction programmes.

 Phase out all alcohol advertising and sponsorship, including at
sporting and cultural events and use funding from the alcohol levy
to establish a well-resourced national harm-reduction campaign.
 Implement stronger regulation for vape retailers to protect young
people and children, including by restricting maximum nicotine
concentrations, restricting flavours, and keeping vape stores out of
the vicinity of schools and kura.

Drug law reform

 Enable a licensing system for psychedelic treatment and recognise
taonga status of our native psylocibin species.

Education

An Aotearoa where everyone

The Green Party will support an inclusive education
system that uplifts everyone.
The Green alternative is grounded in Te Tiriti o Waitangi and universal

embraces te reo Māori and mātauranga Māori, and ensures Māori voices and

foundation. Disabled people, rainbow communities, migrants and refugees,

thrive. Educators’ voices, and grounded expertise are central to decisions at
all levels.

assessment reforms, reverting to the original direction of Te
Mātaiaho and ensuring qualification reform follows curriculum
reform

 Support rangatiratanga to provide an education system driven by
whānau needs, allowing space for whakapapa-based decisions,
and working towards a Māori Education Authority governing and
funding all Māori Medium and Kaupapa Māori education.

Education

towards providing a warm and nutritious locally made lunch to all

Health

Accessible care to support
everyone’s health
The Green Party will ensure our health care system is
equitable, accessible and properly supported.
Undoing actions of redress for Māori such as the disestablishment of Te Aka
Whai Ora - The Māori Health Authority, removing requirements for culturally
safe care, and stripping power from iwi-Māori Partnership has not only reversed
decades of progress to provide equitable healthcare after decades of neglect,
but continues to force Māori into a health system that is known to underserve
them and is misaligned with their hauora aspirations.
Private provider costs are locking whānau out of accessible primary health care
which causes health issues to unnecessarily escalate because they are not
detected early. Meanwhile, Hauora Māori and social service providers that are
focused on whānau-wide holistic and preventative care remain underfunded
and restricted by overly prescriptive contracts.
Good health and dental care allow all of us to live enjoyable, meaningful lives.
Our plan will expand access to essential primary care and dental, and work with
and fund kaupapa Māori approaches to end health inequities in the system, and
prevent ill health and mental distress in the community.

Our plan

 Work towards a publicly delivered, fully free provision of dental
and primary care services, including by-Māori for-Māori
community clinics contracted through a redeveloped Te Aka
Whai Ora.
 Stop overly prescriptive Hauora service contracts and commit
to working with Iwi Māori Partnership Boards and Hauora Māori
to simplify and improve funding arrangements for providers of
health services for whānau, hapū iwi and hapori.

Health

 Fund mobile healthcare and mental health care clinics,
diagnostics services and dental vans to ensure rural
communities are able to access health services regularly,
including during and after civil emergencies.

Oceans and fisheries
Abundant oceans teeming
with kaimoana

The Green Party will restore the mauri (life force) of our
moana through bold marine protection initiatives.
Aotearoa’s moana is a taonga and New Zealanders deserve to have healthy
and thriving oceans, with abundant marine life that provides for sustainable
kaimoana. Oceans are the lifeblood of our planet and our largest public
good—an abundant ocean belongs to all of us.
The Green Party will put Te Tiriti o Waitangi at the centre of our approach
to restoring the abundance of our moana, because when we invest in local
knowledge, we get closer to restoring the right of every New Zealander to
gather kaimoana and find abundant life in our seas for generations to come.

 Protect at least 30% of New Zealand’s ocean territory by 2030
through reforming marine protected areas legislation to ensure it
is fit-for-purpose and enabled Tiriti-consistent and tikanga-led
tools and approaches to improve ocean health outcomes.
 Ban seabed mining in the coastal marine area and the exclusive
economic zone; and maintain pressure for a moratorium on
seabed mining in international waters.
 Review the Quota Management System and move towards an
ecosystem-based fisheries management model, in line with the
Māori fisheries settlement.
 Resource iwi and hapū to lead ocean protection by embedding
kaitiakitanga, tikanga, and mātauranga in policy operational
management and ocean conservation projects.

Advancing an Aotearoa that
embraces Te Tiriti

The Green Party will put Te Tiriti o Waitangi at the heart
of a modern Aotearoa founded on real partnership.
We envision an Aotearoa where our governing structures uphold the rangatiratanga
of iwi and hapū, providing dignity for all. We can achieve this by demonstrating true
partnership between tangata whenua and the Crown as intended by Te Tiriti.
Successive governments have consistently breached Te Tiriti. The resulting harm,
lack of accountability and denial of Māori as equal partners mean that the Crown has
not upheld its side of the partnership. When Te Tiriti breaches are ongoing, apologies
for past harms are not enough.
It is our responsibility to acknowledge, take accountability and put right the
wrongdoings committed by the Crown. These have led to worse outcomes for Māori
in too many ways. We can’t allow government to pretend that everyone is equal in this
country when the exact systems that were created to oppress Māori are still operating.
The Green Party understands that He Whakaputanga and Te Tiriti are the foundation
for an enduring constitutional partnership. We acknowledge the need for ongoing
dialogue to build the awareness, understanding and vision that will give life to Te Tiriti
in Aotearoa.

Our plan
 Amend the Treaty of Waitangi Act to recognise that Te Tiriti is the
authoritative text and that Te Tiriti works in conjunction with He
Whakaputanga o te Rangatiratanga o Nu Tireni.
 Implement genuine Te Tiriti-based decision-making models across
government and resource iwi and hapū to participate as partners rather than
as advisors.
 Reform the Treaty Settlements process by amending the settlement policy
guide for negotiators to enable fair negotiations and just reparations without
cessation of sovereignty, removing any clauses that refer to ‘Full and
Final settlement’, ending the ‘large natural grouping’ approach to Treaty
settlements, and validating hapū rangatiratanga.
 Increase resourcing for the Waitangi Tribunal, providing them with the
power to make binding recommendations for the return of Crown and
Private land to iwi, whānau and hapū.
 Facilitate the return of whenua outside of Treaty Settlements that was
wrongfully alienated from tangata whenua, including through exploring a
right-of-first-refusal process that enables the return of private land to iwi,
hapū and whānau at point of sale.
