The Green Party

The Green Party is the only party with
a concrete plan to bring down the price
of food. In a country that could feed its
own population eight times over,
nobody should go hungry.
The Green Party’s plan will take on the
supermarket duopoly, bring down the cost
of food and groceries, and ensure everyone
has easy and affordable access to good,
nutritious kai.

Food Policy 2026

Food connects us.
Everyone needs to eat.
We’re a nation of 5 million
people, producing enough
to feed 40 million people.
No one in Aotearoa should
go hungry.
Despite this, one in five children live in
households that run out of food, food costs
have increased by 29% over the last five years,
and demand for food banks and emergency
food grants is the highest it has been at any
time, except during the pandemic.

Food is a basic human necessity, and
good nutrition is essential to being able
to lead a good life.
The Green Party will create the third
supermarket competitor, owned by
and for New Zealanders.
There will be more choice for both buyers
and sellers, and supermarkets will no longer
make extreme profits as they’re forced to
genuinely compete.
People will be protected by stronger rules, and
the Commerce Commission will be equipped
to enforce those rules. Being unknowingly
ripped off at the checkout will stop.

Our food system could ensure that every
New Zealander has access to affordable and
nutritious kai.

Anti-competitive behaviours including data
harvesting, undercutting with home brands,
and loyalty schemes will be regulated.

Instead, it’s controlled by two big supermarket
chains who exploit their market power to
prioritise their exorbitant profits, often for
their overseas shareholders, crushing our
farmers and growers at one end, and ripping
off the rest of us at the other. Only the two big
corporate giants - and their shareholders
- are winning in this system.

Access to affordable, healthy kai cannot be
left in the hands of profit-driven privately
owned corporations. The Green Party will fix
the system to give all of us lower prices, more
choice, and greater security.

Local food producers are too often held
hostage by the supermarket duopoly.
Supermarkets prioritising their own ‘home’
brands and stocking fewer local products
is contributing to New Zealand-based
producers, like the Heinz and Wattie’s
factories, shutting up shop.
The lack of competition means growers and
producers have few options but to accept
rock-bottom prices and conditions, risking
their commercial viability and stripping the
country of farming and horticulture resilience
and expertise.
This model is structured to leave people
hungry, growers operating on the edge of
viability, and competitors unviable, while
delivering massive profits to two enormous
corporations. The current system is harming
our health and our bank balances, and is
undermining our national food independence.

Food Policy 2026

Break up the duopoly
and create competition
with KiwiMart
Successive governments have explored
breaking up the supermarkets and forcing
them to sell their stores to a new supermarket
operator to improve competition, but
neither National nor Labour have been
willing to commit to restructuring the
supermarket duopoly.

People will see lower prices at the checkout
thanks to KiwiMart’s affordability mandate
and a more competitive grocery sector,
even if they shop at Woolworths, New World,
or Pak’nSave.
Local suppliers and food producers will be less
reliant on just the two major supermarkets,
making it harder for the duopoly to squeeze
local processors and growers out of the
market – like we’ve seen with the McCain and
Wattie’s factory closures.

The Green Party will establish a
publicly owned supermarket chain,
breaking up the Woolworths and
Foodstuffs duopoly.

KiwiMart will start with the initial acquisition
of distribution centres, and build additional
distribution centres as needed.

The Green Party will finish where National and
Labour left off and require the supermarket
duopoly to divest at least 120 of their stores
and distribution centre capacity in both the
North and South Islands.

Protect people from unfair
pricing in supermarkets and
other sectors

These will be sold into public ownership to
establish KiwiMart, a new publicly owned
supermarket chain. This will ensure the
new competitor is collectively owned by
New Zealanders, and working in the public
good to deliver lower prices for all of us
– as recommended by Consumer NZ.

The two large supermarket chains,
Foodstuffs and Woolworths, have been
making $1 million a day in excessive profits on
top of a reasonable margin. They can only
do this because of their dominant position in
an uncompetitive market, which lets them
charge ordinary people too much for their
food and groceries.

This supermarket will operate as a
self-sustaining and commercially viable
competitor in the grocery market, like
KiwiBank’s role in the banking sector
– but at a competitive scale.

The Green Party will immediately ban
supermarkets from exploiting their market
share to charge unfair prices above their
costs and a reasonable margin.

We’ll task the Commerce Commission to
work with the duopoly to identify which
stores should be divested to KiwiMart based
on local market share, and to determine fair
compensation without merely rewarding them
for their dominant market position.
KiwiMart will have a clear and single
mandate to prioritise affordability
for consumers.

We will require the supermarkets to
share their pricing information with the
Commerce Commission so they can monitor
and enforce the price gouging ban. We’ve
already introduced legislation to do this.
This would bring us into line with Australia,
which has also recently introduced legislation
to prohibit supermarkets from charging
excessive prices.

The Green Party

We will extend the ban on price gouging
to other uncompetitive sectors, including
energy and fuel.
The Commerce Act used to explicitly prohibit
any business from charging unreasonably
high prices, but this was stripped from our
laws during the deregulation of the 1980s.
We know that supermarkets aren’t the
only sector where businesses abuse their
market position to charge unreasonably high
prices. Local food producers and processors
have also struggled with high electricity
and fuel prices.
We will restore a broad prohibition on
excessive pricing as part of a wider review
of the Commerce Act.

Empower the
Commerce Commission
to enforce the rules
Supermarkets too often overcharge
consumers, which adds up over time to
significant costs for consumers. In 2023,
Consumer NZ filed a formal complaint with
the Commerce Commission, which laid
criminal charges against Woolworths and two
Pak’nSave stores for misleading pricing. In
2024, the Commerce Commission estimated
that pricing errors were costing consumers
tens of millions of dollars every year.
The Green Party will introduce a
mandatory supermarket pricing
accuracy code, including automatic
compensation requirements when
shoppers are overcharged.
Misleading or incorrect pricing isn’t the
only way the supermarket duopoly gives
consumers a bad deal. Every day, the
supermarket duopoly uses loyalty card
schemes to obscure pricing, drive up prices,
discourage shopping around, and track
customer purchasing habits.

06

Consumer NZ has warned that supermarkets
could use the loyalty card data they collect to
dynamically change prices throughout the
day, as is increasingly common in European
grocers, potentially targeted to each shopper.
They use store brands like Essentials and
Pams to squeeze out local producers,
reducing range and competition across the
market. We’ve already seen this contribute
to the closure of the McCain and Wattie’s
factories in the Hawkes Bay.
The Green Party will empower the
Commerce Commission to directly regulate
the use of loyalty cards, store brands, and
other anti-consumer practices to protect
competition and transparency.
We’ll also explore extending the Consumer
Data Right framework to the supermarket
sector, allowing consumers to share their
loyalty data with other supermarkets and
trusted price comparison tools and use their
own purchase history to get better offers and
compare baskets. This would give consumers
control over their information and help level
the playing field for new competitors who lack
the duopoly’s decades of information.
To limit the shopping data collected and to
prevent against ‘surveillance shopping’, we’ll
give consumers greater rights to choose what
data supermarkets can collect about their
shopping habits, how long they can keep it,
and what supermarkets can do with it.
The Greens will ensure a strong Commerce
Commission that is resourced and
empowered to hold the supermarkets
and other big corporates to account. In
contrast, over the last three years, the Luxon
Government has reduced funding for the
Commerce Commission by millions of dollars.
The Green Party will increase funding for
the Commerce Commission, increase fines
for breaking the rules and empower the
Commission to issue binding directions.

Food Policy 2026

We’ll increase funding for the Commerce
Commission by $25m to put them in a
stronger position to enforce our competition
laws, including doubling the Commission’s
litigation budget.

Canada, Scotland, the United Kingdom, and
the European Union have all established
coordinated strategies and frameworks that
recognise the intersections of feeding their
people, climate, health, and food resilience.

We’ll raise the maximum penalties available
under the Commerce Act. Currently, the
maximum penalty is the greater of $10m,
three times the commercial gain, or 10%
of turnover during the breach period.
We will raise this to the greater of $50m,
three times the commercial gain, and 30%
of turnover. This brings us closer into line
with Australia’s penalties.

Our National Food Strategy will embed
Māori kai sovereignty principles, supporting
the re-indigenisation and re-localisation of
our food systems. This means ensuring
Māori governance and leadership in shaping
food policy, and could include supporting
marae-based food distribution, protecting
traditional food sources, and creating
opportunities to grow, share, and prepare
food locally through the Fair Food Fund.

We’ll also give the Commerce Commission
the power to issue binding directions in
response to a market study or investigation,
like they can in the United Kingdom
– not just recommendations.
These reforms will put the Commerce
Commission in a stronger position to enforce
competition not just in the grocery sector, but
across the whole economy.
We’ll deliver these priority interventions to
address supermarket competition as part of a
wider overhaul to ensure the Commerce Act is
fit for purpose and stops giving big companies
a free pass.

Create a National Food
Strategy for the whole
food system

The National Food Strategy will underpin
and connect food policy across all sectors,
ensuring there is more equitable access
to affordable and nutritious food, that our
food system is resilient to domestic and
international shocks, and that we can produce
food within environmental and climate limits.
It will establish a legislative right to adequate
and nutritious food, and make sure schools,
hospitals and other public institutions
prioritise local food procurement.
The National Food Strategy will require
large food distributors like supermarkets
to reduce or donate surplus food to food
rescue organisations. It will also ensure that
food regulations are proportionate to scale,
so small-scale local and community food
production can operate under reasonable
and fair conditions.

The control of our food system has been
handed over to the big producers, processors,
and retailers, and leaves the needs of everyday
New Zealanders and our environment
unprotected from corporate greed.
The Green Party will introduce a
National Food Strategy in collaboration
with food producers, food distributors,
public health experts, Māori, and the
community food sector.

The Green Party

Support community
food security so no one
goes hungry
New Zealand’s food system is already much
broader than just the supermarket model,
and supermarkets alone will not deliver
the food security New Zealanders need.
Community organisations across the
country are building alternative models
for food processing and distribution.
The potential for community solutions
to contribute to local food security and
resilience is immense. The greatest
constraint these organisations and
potential initiatives face is resourcing.
Nationwide organisations like the
New Zealand Food Network collect and
distribute rescued food at an unprecedented
scale, supported by many local food rescue
and food distribution organisations.
Many communities have established food
hubs, providing everything from storage and
collection to processing and kitchens.
Frontline organisations have also been
forced to start up social supermarkets or
expand their food bank operations to meet
the ever-increasing demand for emergency
food support.
The Green Party will establish a $150m
Fair Food Fund to expand community-led
food security initiatives.
This fund will support community-led
initiatives that can improve food security,
resilience, and reduce waste, in line
with our National Food Strategy. It will
support initiatives at the local, regional,
and national levels.

08

This fund could support the establishment
of regional and local food hubs, markets,
community cold storage, marae-based
food infrastructure, seed banks, social
supermarkets, online food distribution
platforms, Garden to Table or Pā to Plate
programmes, and other innovations that
provide better access to food.
The fund could also support the financing
of community and co-operative ownership
of local food processing. It will endeavour
to provide longer-term funding that
ensures projects have certainty and time
to demonstrate success.
This will replace the existing $1 million
‘Community Food Security Initiatives Fund’,
currently administered by Ministry of Social
Development, which will be reprioritised into
other Food Secure Communities funding.
The Green Party will also ensure that
everyone can access the food they need
in an emergency by increasing funding for
food banks and doubling the cap on food
grants from Work and Income.
Food banks and food grants should not be
necessary, but it will take time to reform our
food system to ensure food security for all of
us. Expanding access to emergency support
in the short-term is critical to ensure no
immediate need goes unmet.
We will increase the funding for regional
food distribution hubs and community food
providers from up to $8 million to $25 million
- restoring funding to at least 2023/24 levels.
This will include restoring a high-trust
approach for accessing emergency food
grants, so nobody has to jump through
hoops to access the support they need.

Food Policy 2026

Provide better lunches
for more children
Imagine a future where every child receives
a nutritious lunch at school, prepared locally
and with locally sourced ingredients. School
lunches are not just about food. They are
about investing in our tamariki and our future.
The Luxon Government has slashed funding
per meal, taken lunch provision away from
local communities, and handed it over to a
few big companies that prioritise their profit
margins over feeding our kids.
That move alone destroyed up to 2,000 local
jobs. Half of lunches now fail to meet nutrition
standards and much more food is wasted than
before. And they’ve only committed funding
until the end of 2027.
School lunches should be there to meet
the needs of our kids. Research shows that
students who miss meals are two to four
years behind in maths, reading, and science
achievement compared to their classmates
who never miss meals.

The Green Party will extend the programme
to schools with an equity index over 450,
ensuring that it continues to target the most
disadvantaged communities first. We’ll raise
funding per lunch to at least $6.50 to ensure
meals are nutritious and tasty, and don’t just
go to waste.
Restoring local provision will restore up to
2,000 jobs lost when the government gutted
the scheme, and expanding the scheme will
create up to 1,900 more jobs.
We’ll also empower schools, particularly those
opting for in-house provision, with resources
to integrate the growing of produce and the
production of meals with classroom learning
and hands-on education. Schools will be
supported with kitchen fit-outs and other
necessary facilities to deliver the programme.
Restoring and expanding Ka Ora, Ka Ako
is the first step towards providing a warm,
nutritious, and locally made lunch to all
schoolchildren.

When more and more families are struggling
to put food on the table and one in five
children live in households that run out
of food, we can’t afford to continue the
Luxon Government’s inadequate school
lunch programme.
The Greens will restore the Ka Ora,
Ka Ako programme so nutritious
lunches are provided through schools
or local providers.
We’ll expand the programme to around
400 more schools, ensuring 150,000 more
children enjoy a nutritious lunch every day,
and fund the programme permanently.
