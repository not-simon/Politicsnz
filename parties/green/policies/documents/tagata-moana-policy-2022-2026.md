# Tagata Moana Policy (Green Party policy document)

<!-- page 1 -->

Tagata Moana Policy
Vision
The self-determination of tagata moana is upheld and whakapapa connections are recognised
across the Pacific.

Summary
In this policy, we strengthen the community and cultural connections of those who live in the
islands and those who call Aotearoa home. We support the rights of tagata moana 1 to
decolonise and reindigenise their identity, including the use of their own Indigenous names.

Values and Principles
Te Tiriti o Waitangi: We prioritise the use of tagata moana (people of the sea) to reflect our
historical and whakapapa connections to tangata whenua (people of the land). This rich
tapestry of interactions and relationships forms the foundation for solidarity with Indigenous
Peoples across the Pacific.

Non-Violence: We recognise that tagata moana have always resisted Western imperialism and
colonisation, and have their own cultural practices for conflict resolution and peace-making.

Appropriate Decision-making: We express solidarity with Indigenous movements and small
independent island nations. We recognise the tradition of movements such as the Nuclear Free
and Independent Pacific (NFIP) and independence struggles such as those in West Papua and
French-occupied Polynesia.

Ecological Wisdom: We recognise the ancestral wisdom of tagata moana to care for the moana,
and the flora and fauna of their islands and waters.

Social Responsibility: We uphold the collective rights of tagata moana to health, education,
justice, housing, and democracy in Aotearoa and in the islands.

Strategic Priorities
The Green Party’s strategic goals include:

“As a Party we strive to create a more connected, compassionate and equal Aotearoa,
free from structural biases that discriminate against groups and individuals. We want an
Aotearoa that draws on our shared wisdom to meet the needs of all.”
1

Actions in this policy that work towards this include:
- Support climate action led by and centred around Indigenous leadership, and
relationships between tangata whenua and tagata moana. (8.1)

- Build communities with houses designed for multi-generational family units and with
greater access to public transport and other public amenities. (4.1)

- Promote Pasifika-led health-care models based on tagata moana concepts, practices,
and values. (3.1)

- Dismantle the Recognised Seasonal Employer2 (RSE) scheme and replace it with a
scheme that gives Pasifika workers access to residency pathways, fair wages, housing,
and essential services. (7.1)

- Develop equitable and culturally-appropriate pathways and programmes for students,
for tertiary and other education. (2.4)

Connected Policies
This policy is connected to the following policies: Immigration, Rainbow, Education, Marine,
Global Affairs, Climate Change, and Housing and Sustainable Communities.

Arts and Culture

Issues
We recognise the expertise of Indigenous knowledge and language holders of our Pasifika
communities. Indigenous knowledge refers to the understandings, skills, and philosophies
developed by societies with long histories of interaction with their natural surroundings. It
encompasses language, systems of classification, resource use practices, social interactions,
ritual, and spirituality. We support an equitable approach to:

2.

1.1.

Establish and maintain tagata moana cultural hubs and Pasifika festivals across the
country.

1.2.

Fund programmes that revitalise and maintain Indigenous Pāsifika languages and
dialects.

1.3.

Develop and maintain vocational pathways for tagata moana artists and their
collaborative initiatives.

1.4.

Record and research Pāsifika histories by Pasifika peoples.

Education

Develop teaching and learning resources around tagata moana knowledge systems,

Amend the Teaching Council criteria of measuring Pacific Teaching qualifications to

Arts can be easily accessed.

2.6.

Ensure that children who hold part-time jobs to contribute to their households are
provided with sufficient and timely support to continue their education.

Health

Issues
Tagata moana face systemic discrimination within the healthcare system, which means they
are less likely and less able to access healthcare. Young tagata moana suffer from poor mental
health in both their age groups and ethnic identities.

Actions

4.

3.1.

Promote Pasifika-led health-care models based on tagata moana concepts,
practices, and values.

3.2.

Develop and improve access to culturally appropriate, safe, and free health, dental,
and mental health care for tagata moana delivered by tagata moana.

3.3.

Increase PHARMAC funding to ensure equitable access to address the
disproportionate health needs faced by tagata moana.

3.4.

Increase access to training for Pasifika nursing, midwifery, and medical students.

Housing

Issues
Housing and community design should reflect the cultural practices of tagata moana that
ensure that our children and young people grow up with the guidance of their parents, elders,
and extended family.

Actions
4.1.

Build communities with houses designed for multi-generational family units and
with greater access to public transport and other public amenities.

4.2.

Develop and implement accessible pathways to Pasifika home-ownership and
resourcing Pasifika housing providers.

5.

Pasifika Rainbow

Issues
Tagata moana cultures have always been fluid with respect to our conceptualising and
practising of gender roles. Traditionally, mahu, vakasalewa, palopa, fa’afafine, akava’ine,
fakaleiti (leiti), and fakafifine (or MVPFAFF+) have held places of respect. The colonising forces
of Christianity have historically disrupted, eroded their standing, and that of all Pasifika who
identify with diverse genders, sexualities, and sex characteristics.

Actions

6.

5.1.

Ensure equitable access for MVPFAFF+ and Rainbow Pasifika to education,
healthcare, and mental health care that honours all of their identities.

5.2.

Support Rainbow Pasifika-led initiatives, organisations, and research projects such
as F’INE and the Manalagi Project.

Immigration

Issues
Aotearoa New Zealand’s policies and practices must now begin to address the historical and
contemporary injustices perpetuated in New Zealand’s inequitable relationships with other
Pacific nations. Concepts and notions such as “deportation,” “overstaying,” and “aid” are
foreign to Oceania. We acknowledge the generations of trauma caused to tagata moana by the
1974-1976 Dawn Raids era whereby both the Labour and National Governments sought to
maintain a “white nation state” through racist immigration policies.

Actions

7.

6.1.

Actively support Māori aspirations for a Tiriti-based immigration system by
devolving resources to hapū, iwi, and whānau to link with Pasifika communities.

6.2.

Advocate for visa-free travel to allow for free movement between all Pacific islands
and Aotearoa.

6.3.

Create a broad Amnesty Programme for people who overstay.

Workers’ Rights

Issues
For successive generations, tagata moana have been brought to New Zealand only as a cheap
labour force. Combatting New Zealand’s exploitation of tagata moana workers is very much
part of the International Workers’ Rights struggle. We affirm the rights of workers to be
unionised.

Actions
7.1.

Dismantle the Recognised Seasonal Employer (RSE) scheme and replace it with a
scheme that gives Pasifika workers access to residency pathways, fair wages,
housing, and essential services.

Climate Justice

Issues
Environmental and climate challenges are inherently social justice and human rights issues and
are linked to the tradition of Indigenous Peoples’ movements in the Pacific.

Actions

9.

8.1.

Support climate action led by and centred around Indigenous leadership, and
relationships between tangata whenua and tagata moana.

8.2.

Support global action that supports small island nations already experiencing
climate catastrophe.

8.3.

Ensure workers from communities on the frontlines of climate change are prioritised
as part of a Just Transition.

8.4.

Ensure that tagata moana have active representation on He Pou Rangi, Aotearoa
New Zealand’s Climate Change Commission.

8.5.

Finance climate change mitigation and adaptation, loss and damage compensation
for Pasifika communities.

Democracy

Issues
The political system in New Zealand does not widely or substantially represent tagata moana,
and therefore, many do not engage in mainstream democracy.

Actions
9.1.

Resource “by tagata moana for tagata moana” mentoring and leadership
development programmes.

9.2.

Support culturally safe outreach to tagata moana communities who have low voter
enrolment.
