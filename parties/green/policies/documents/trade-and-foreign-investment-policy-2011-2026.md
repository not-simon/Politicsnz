# Trade and Foreign Investment Policy (Green Party policy document)

<!-- page 1 -->

Trade and Foreign Investment Policy
The Green Party will work toward a global trade regime that respects ecological limits and local
cultures, and which contributes to sustainable development. This should encourage good global
citizenship and corporate responsibility, including through enforceable international law to
protect human rights and the environment. Economic decision-making should be democratic and
transparent.

Vision
Trade and foreign investment prioritise social, economic, cultural, and environmental justice in
the pursuit of sustainability.

Values and Principles
Trade and foreign investment agreements and policies must be underpinned by the following
values and principles:
- Honour Te Tiriti o Waitangi: Māori as Treaty partners and the indigenous people of
Aotearoa New Zealand, should have their rights and interests upheld in all trade
negotiations between Aotearoa New Zealand and the rest of the world.

- Ecological Wisdom: The purpose of trade and foreign investment should be to achieve
environmental and social objectives that keep us within our planetary boundaries, and
ensure the equitable, sustainable, and responsible use of natural resources.

- Social Responsibility: Equity and intergenerational responsibility should be cornerstones
of trade and foreign investment, and exploitation rejected.

- Appropriate Decision-Making: Trade and investment agreements should be transparently
and democratically mandated, negotiated, agreed upon, and reviewed.

- Non-Violence: Human rights and fair labour standards must be upheld, and the
precautionary principle should be adopted where there is uncertainty or concern about
potential harm.

- Global citizenship: The specific needs, sovereignty, biodiversity, and cultural differences
of emerging economies should be respected.

economic activity [...] will predominate.”

Connected Policies
Managing trade activity is one role of the Government in the Economy, as part of Global Affairs.
Trade and foreign investment must support Biodiversity and Environmental Regeneration
(including addressing biosecurity), Climate Change responses, and Human Rights. Dependence
on trade can be mitigated with self-sufficiency in Food and Energy, and reorientation of key
sectors such as Agriculture, Tourism, and high-emitting industries. Any foreign-owned Business
operating in Aotearoa New Zealand must be held to the same standard as those that are locally
owned.

Sovereignty, International Trade Governance, and Global Citizenship

Issues
The large power imbalances among states engaging in consensus decisions in international trade
governance often leads to slow or stalled processes that protect the most powerful and so erode
trust. Trade agreements enhance international market access but can undermine national
decision-making through shared regulations, opaque dispute mechanisms, and strong investor
protections.

economic growth and efficiency over environmental safeguards increases resource extraction,
displaces pollution, and accelerates biodiversity loss.

Foreign Investment in Aotearoa New Zealand

Issues
Foreign investment, especially when it is directly into businesses, assets, or projects in Aotearoa
New Zealand, while offering economic opportunities, also presents economic and democratic
risks. A foreign investor or multinational company could transfer profits out of the country,
thereby draining foreign exchange, reducing the funds available for local reinvestment, and
limiting the domestic benefits of foreign investment. Foreign ownership of assets, such as land
and infrastructure, erodes the national benefit from and influence over those assets, risking
decisions that ignore domestic priorities and democratic decision-making that reflects the
economic influence of foreign vested interests. These risks must be mitigated.

Action
3.1.

3.2.

Ensure that foreign investment in Aotearoa New Zealand is ethical, appropriate, and
supports sustainability, by:
3.1.1.

Strengthening controls on speculative and other non-productive foreign
investment (e.g. land banking) to minimise its negative effects

3.1.2.

Prioritising investment into sustainable enterprises;

3.1.3.

Strengthening the approval criteria to consistently limit profit repatriation,
protect the rights of tangata whenua, support community sovereignty, protect
ecosystems, and support a stable climate

3.1.4.

Requiring international businesses conducting significant operations here to
establish local subsidiaries that are incorporated within Aotearoa New Zealand
or otherwise create a local ownership stake in their activities;

3.1.5.

Reserving land ownership for Aotearoa New Zealand citizens and permanent
residents;

3.1.6.

Applying the national interest test to all foreign investment proposals

3.1.7.

Reducing the threshold for oversight and review of approved investments where
the purchasing entity is 10% or more foreign-owned
