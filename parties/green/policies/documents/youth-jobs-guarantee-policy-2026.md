Jobs Policy 2026

Every 16-to-24-year-old who
has been out of work for six
months will be guaranteed a
pathway into a locally based
job or training.
There is no shortage of work to do in New
Zealand—things that need fixing, nature that
needs protecting, and people who need to be
cared for.
Every community can identify stop banks
that need upgrading before the next storm,
a kaumātua who needs company, and a local
playground that needs repairs. All around
the motu, there is infrastructure and nature
in need of maintenance and renewal, and
critical community services held back by
a lack of labour.

A long period without a job at the start of a
working life lowers a person’s wages for years
afterwards. Thousands of young people are
in that position now. The Government can
choose to have an active role in making sure
there are good jobs for them, before young
people are condemned to either a future of
low wages or a new life in Australia.
We can choose to fix this with programmes
that already work in our communities.
The Mayors Taskforce for Jobs already helps
thousands of 16 to 24-year-olds into work
with local employers, with support for driver
licensing, training and work gear.

When there is so much work to be done, it
doesn’t make sense for nearly 100,000 young
people to not be in employment, education or
training. Our young people are being denied
important opportunities at a critical time in
their lives.
Youth unemployment is at nearly 15 percent,
roughly three times that of the wider working
age population, which is at an 11-year high.
Too many young graduates are applying for
hundreds of jobs in a year, only to win a couple
of job offers at best—with some of them
leaving Aotearoa New Zealand when the
hunt becomes too hard.
It’s not surprising when Wellington City alone
has lost nearly 10,000 jobs over the past three
years. There aren’t enough jobs in Luxon’s
economy for all the young people who are
desperately applying for them.
The harm falls hardest on Māori and
Pasifika young people. Over 21 percent
of young Māori and Pasifika are not in
employment, education, or training,
compared to 13.8 percent for all young
people. Some regions are worse off than
others. Youth unemployment in Northland
is more than twice the rate in Canterbury.

An economy for
people and places
Local government jobs to
support water infrastructure
in Kāpiti
In Kāpiti, the Mayors Taskforce for
Jobs supported a young person
into a paid internship and then a
long-term job in the Council’s water
and wastewater lab, giving them
practical scientific experience
while helping to protect essential
local infrastructure.
Our Jobs for Young People
programme will fund more roles
like this in local government.

Hāpu partnerships to build
homes in South Taranaki

Restoring native landscapes
in Hauraki
The Mayors Taskforce for Jobs
supported a young person in Hauraki,
after nine months of unemployment to
get their drivers license and connect
them with a role on Tiaki Whenua
Trust’s native planting team. This led
to a permanent position with Waihi
Native Plants, beginning a long-term
career working to restore native
forests. The local Hauraki scheme
has placed dozens of young people in
trade apprenticeships and other roles.
Our Jobs for Young People
programme will fund more
roles like this provided by
non-profit organisations.

In South Taranaki, Ōkahu-Inuawai
Hapū partnered with WITT to
train young people in construction
by building new homes on hāpu
whenua. The Mayors Taskforce
for Jobs has helped to connect
young people to this training
opportunity, with rangatahi
graduating with a construction
qualification and supported into
longer-term employment.
Our Jobs for Young People
programme will fund more roles
like this provided by iwi and hapu.

Jobs Policy 2026

An assessment of the Mayors Taskforce for
Jobs in six rural districts estimated that every
dollar invested returned $5.60 in social value.
It created just over 1,000 opportunities
for young jobseekers in the past year.

The Green
Party will:

The Green Party will fund all 67 councils
to get 32,000 young people into local jobs.

Guarantee decent work
for young people

Jobs for Nature, set up under the previous
Government, employed more than 15,900
people over five years in pest control, planting
and restoration work in their own regions.
The Green Party will widen its scope
beyond conservation to create a Jobs for
Young People programme.
With government providing policy direction
and funding, people in each community
will decide what work needs doing, whether
that is restoring local bush, building climateresilient infrastructure, or caring for people.
Every job under the guarantee will be
additional, this means no job will replace
an existing worker.
The Green Party will make sure every
young person who wants a job has the
chance to do decent work where they live.
