# Overseas New Zealanders Policy (Green Party policy document)

<!-- page 1 -->

Overseas New Zealanders Policy
Vision
The Green Party celebrates the large and diverse diaspora of Aotearoa New Zealand, fosters
mutually-beneficial and meaningful links to home, and safeguards our rights and well-being.

Values and Principles
Waiho i te toipoto, kaua i te toiroa
Let us keep close together, not far apart
This policy is grounded in the Green Party charter, international Green values and elementary
policies of fairness, equity and human rights.
- Honour Te Tiriti o Waitangi: Tangata whenua should lead all kaupapa related to Māori living
overseas.

- Ecological Wisdom: Overseas New Zealanders should be supported with sustainable and
environmentally-conscious connections with home.

- Social Responsibility: Overseas New Zealanders should have human rights, democratic
rights and access to the principles of citizenship, regardless of where we live.

- Appropriate decision-making: Overseas New Zealanders should have the opportunity to
contribute to our home country and have their voices heard.

- Non-violence: States have a basic duty of care to their citizens. This must include overseas
New Zealanders and be free of political bias, favouritism or status based on perceived
worth to our home country.

Connected Policies
The Aotearoa New Zealand diaspora is impacted by policy in several areas including Governance,
Disability, Health, Global Affairs, Immigration, Livelihoods, Education, Te Tiriti o Waitangi, and
Workforce.

Issues
Māori hold a distinct and special status as the indigenous people, or tangata whenua, of Aotearoa
New Zealand. The particular rights of Māori living overseas, outlined by the articles of te Tiriti o
Waitangi, must be fully explored and upheld, enabling Māori leadership in supporting the Māori
diaspora.

Actions
1.1.

Guaranteeing Aotearoa New Zealand citizenship to all individuals with Māori
whakapapa living or born overseas; and

1.2.

Establishing a tikanga pathway to citizenship that allows for hapū and iwi to assess
whakapapa evidence, as recommended in Waitangi Tribunal’s “He Tangata, he
Whenua: The Citizenship Report” (Wai 3513)."

2.

Services, Support and Human Rights

Issues
Our interconnected world creates situations where many migrants do not have access to services,
support, or basic rights in either their country of residence or country of origin. New Zealanders
living overseas are no exception, and their basic needs must be met, including in times of crisis.
Our ability to return home must be equitable and accessible.

Actions
2.1.

Affirm the right of New Zealanders to hold multiple nationalities.

2.2.

Affirm the rights of New Zealanders to access pathways to citizenship in other
countries, including by advocating against health and disability cost-based
discrimination.

2.3.

Ensure that measures to address risks from current and future crises protect the
rights of New Zealanders to return home and do not impose additional costs on
them.

2.4.

Provide adequate resources to allow diplomatic staff to act as advocates or provide
support for New Zealand citizens in emergency situations.

2.5.

Investigate digital or embassy-based health services for New Zealanders living
overseas.

2.6.

Allow New Zealanders living overseas to claim a one-off emergency loan payment
in the event they have to leave their country of residence due to hardship or other
circumstances.

3.

Meaningful Connections

Issues
The relationship between New Zealanders living overseas and our home is affected by
Government policy and must be strengthened and supported. The government must be
responsive and represent the interests of the diaspora by understanding our unique and diverse
characteristics.

Actions

4.

3.1.

Establish a government institution with responsibility for the Aotearoa New Zealand
diaspora.

3.2.

Establish a formal channel for overseas New Zealanders to communicate with the
government directly on all issues relevant to them.

3.3.

Ensure that demographic data collection for New Zealanders overseas gives an
accurate understanding of our diaspora.

3.4.

Remove the requirement for people to be physically present in Aotearoa New
Zealand to renew or replace government-issued documentation and ensure renewal
processes are accessible, including to disabled people.

3.5.

Lengthen the repayment holiday for overseas student loan borrowers.

3.6.

Abolish border arrests for student loan repayment violations.

3.7.

Restore the ten-year eligibility-threshold for time spent in Aotearoa New Zealand
to be eligible for a full or partial superannuation.

3.8.

Ensure that people who leave Aotearoa New Zealand intending to return within six
months, but are unable to do so for exceptional reasons, remain eligible for
superannuation.

3.9.

Ensure that assessment of applications to be paid superannuation while overseas
for more than 26 weeks takes full account of financial position, family obligations,
and cultural considerations.

Issues
Our ability to participate in the democracy of Aotearoa New Zealand must be upheld and
improved. We must have a voice and both ourselves and our families should feel part of Aotearoa
New Zealand society.

Actions
4.1.

Lengthen the time spent outside Aotearoa New Zealand before losing voting
eligibility.

4.2.

Establish accessible, secure enrolment and voting mechanisms for overseas voters.

4.3.

Ensure that sufficient accessible voting options are available so that all overseas
voters can vote, regardless of barriers (such as disability).

4.4.

Make it easier for overseas partners and immediate family members of New
Zealanders to become residents.

4.5.

Hold New Zealanders accountable for any international crimes committed at home
or overseas, while:
4.5.1.

Upholding the fundamental protections provided by international and
Aotearoa New Zealand’s criminal and human rights laws; and

4.5.2.

Ensuring prosecutions are not undertaken for partisan purposes.
