# Greens Promise To Reverse National’s Public Service Cuts

**Thursday, 15 October 2026, 1:48 pm**
**Press Release: [Green Party](https://info.scoop.co.nz/Green_Party)**

The Green Party would cancel the National Party's $2.4 billion cuts to the public service if elected, says Green Party Co-leader Marama Davidson.

"National's reckless cuts to public services have left New Zealanders with crumbling essential services, and job losses that have devastated our local economy, including the hospitality and retail sectors."

"Instead of investing in our public service, and the people who bring their skills, talent and dedication to the sector, the National Party is proposing cutting another 8,700 more roles and replacing them with AI."

"The Green Party knows that a strong public service is essential to addressing the economic and environmental challenges that lie ahead for Aotearoa New Zealand."

“The Greens will always stand on the side of a strong, skilled, hard-working public service that works for all of Aotearoa.”

"That's why, when in Government we will reverse and cancel Luxon’s public service cuts and instead invest and support the sector."

"The Greens are the only party campaigning on no public service cuts.”

"The Green Party will make sure New Zealand has a strong, well-resourced public service that can address the issues our country faces, and that values the people who do this work," says Marama.

Notes:

- In May 2026, the National government announced that they would reduce the public service headcount by 8,700 roles, by increasing the use of AI and other digital tools
- They reduced public service baselines for most public service agencies by 2% in Budget 2026, and committed to cutting baselines by 5% for most agencies next year, and by a further 5% in 2028/29.
- Their cuts will impact every public service agency except for Oranga Tamariki, law and order, health, education, and defence and intelligence agencies.
- In Budget 2026, from 2026/27 to 2029/30, these cuts were slated to save $2.39 billion. From 2027/28 to 2030/31, cancelling these will cost $3.17 billion.
