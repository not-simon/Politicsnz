About this Easy Read

This Easy Read is by the Green
Party of Aotearoa New Zealand
manifesto.

The Green Party of Aotearoa New
Zealand is a political party.

A political party is a group of people
with the same ideas that want to be
part of the Government.

In this document the Green Party of
Aotearoa New Zealand will be called
the Greens.

Where it says we / us in this
document this means the Greens.
3

<!-- page 5 -->

A manifesto tells everyone what a
political party:
- believes in

- wants to happen.

You can read our full manifesto at
this website:
www.greens.org.nz/manifesto_2026

This website is not in Easy Read.

This Easy Read document has
information about:
- why you should vote for us

- how to vote for us.

Why vote for the Greens?

The Greens want:
- the Government to support
people

- nature to do well.

We want everyone to have the things
they need like:
- food

- electricity / power

- housing

- transport

- doctors.

5

<!-- page 7 -->

We want to take care of the
environment which means the things
around us like:
- water

- forests

- air

- soil

- climate / weather.

We want a fair Government.

We want a Government that works
for everyone.

We do not want a Government that
just works for the people with the
most money.
6

<!-- page 8 -->

We want the Government to always
respect the rights of Māori.

Rights are things that the
law says every person should:
- have

- be able to do.

These rights were agreed on in
Te Tiriti o Waitangi.

Te Tiriti o Waitangi is an important
agreement between:
- Māori
and

- the Crown / Government.

7

<!-- page 9 -->

Te Tiriti o Waitangi:
- kept Māori independent which
means they make their own
decisions

- made a government for everyone
else.

Te Tiriti o Waitangi also says the
rights of Māori are:
- all the rights everyone else has
and

- control over their:
o

lands

o

customs / ways of doing
things

o

everything else that is
important to them.
8

<!-- page 10 -->

We want the people with the most
money to pay more tax.

Tax is money that goes to the
government.

Tax is used by the government to pay
for things like:
- schools

- roads

- disability support services.

The people we want to pay more tax
are:
- big businesses

- people who are very rich.

9

<!-- page 11 -->

With the extra money from taxes we
will look after:
- people

- nature / the environment.

10

<!-- page 12 -->

What will the Greens do for disabled
people?

Disabled people are important to the
Greens.

We want disabled people to live with
dignity.

Dignity means:
- being treated with respect

- having all the things you need to
live a good life.

Disabled people should be able to
make the same choices as everyone
else.

11

<!-- page 13 -->

We want disabled whānau / families
to have good lives.

Disabled people should have the
same rights as everyone else.

Disabled people should be able to
access things like:
- transport to get the places they
want to go

- housing in the community they
want to live in.

There are lots of barriers for
disabled people.

A barrier is something that makes it
harder for you to do something.
12

<!-- page 14 -->

Things are very hard for:
- tāngata whaikaha Māori / Māori
disabled people

- disabled people who are treated
unfairly for other reasons.

The Government we have now has
made things worse.

The Greens will make sure that all
disabled people get the support they
need to be part of their communities.

We want disabled people to have
great lives.

We want to make things better for
people who face the most barriers.

Important policies for disabled people

1. Disability rights law
We will make a strong disability
rights law which respects Te Tiriti o
Waitangi.

This law will make sure disabled
people can get easy access to:
- places

- services.

2. Support decisions
We will support disabled people to
make their own decisions about
their lives.

14

<!-- page 16 -->

3. Enough money
We will make sure disabled people
have enough money for:
- the extra things they need to
live

- medical / healthcare costs.

This money will be given to
disabled people no matter:
- how they became disabled

- who they live with.

15

<!-- page 17 -->

4. Make services better
We will improve how important
services work with disabled people
like:
- healthcare

- education

- disability services.

We will use Enabling Good Lives.

16

<!-- page 18 -->

Enabling Good Lives is a
different way of thinking about
disability support.

Enabling Good Lives is about
having:
- choice

- control

- support

- a good life.

5. Support children with
disabilities
We will make sure all children with
disabilities get the support they
need early in their lives.

17

<!-- page 19 -->

This includes:
- getting a diagnosis

- support with learning at school.

Here diagnosis means naming the
medical / learning condition
someone has after doing tests.

6. Choice of where to live
We will give disabled people real
choices about where they can live.

We will make rules that make more
accessible housing.

Accessible means something
works well for everyone including
disabled people.
18

<!-- page 20 -->

7. Easier to get around
We will make it easy for disabled
people to get around like using
public transport.

We will make Total Mobility taxis
cheaper again.

8. Good access to education after
high school
We will make sure disabled people
can access a good education after
high school.

This includes going to:
- university

- wānanga / Māori places of
learning.
19

<!-- page 21 -->

9. Jobs in the Government.
We will support disabled people to
get jobs in the Government.

We will train Government staff how
to treat disabled people in fair
ways.

10. Support for disabled people
from other countries
We will make it easier for disabled
people from other countries to live
here.

We will get rid of rules that say it
costs too much to have them here.

<!-- page 22 -->

11. Ban hate speech
We will make it illegal to use hate
speech that attacks someone for
things like their:
- disability

- religion

- gender.

Hate speech is saying horrible
things about someone.

Hate speech can also be:
- pictures

- writing

- on the internet.

21

<!-- page 23 -->

Hate speech can make:
- the person feel unsafe

- other people think wrong
things about the person.

Gender is if you are:
- a man

- a woman

- another gender like nonbinary.

We will work with disabled people
on safety plans.

This includes plans for:

o

earthquakes

family and sexual violence.

About the 2026 general election

A general election is happening this
year.

Here a general election is when you
can vote for who you want to be in
Parliament.

Making a vote is when you choose
who you think should win an election.

Enrol to vote
You need to enrol before you can
vote.

Here enrol means you fill in a form
to add your name to the electoral
roll.

25

<!-- page 27 -->

The electoral roll is a list of all the
people in New Zealand who are
enrolled to vote.

Enrolments close on 25 October
2026.

You cannot enrol when voting starts.

You can find out more about how to
enrol:
- by phone:
0800 36 76 56

- at this website:
https://vote.nz/accessible-2026

26

<!-- page 28 -->

Voting
Voting in the general election
happens:
- from 26 October 2026

- to 7 November 2026.

You can go to any voting place in
Aotearoa New Zealand.

There will be orange signs outside a
voting place to show you where to
go.

At the voting place you will be given a
voting paper.

Here a voting paper is where you
choose who you want to vote for.
27

<!-- page 29 -->

The 2 votes you get on the voting
paper are the:
- party vote

- electorate vote for a person.

Here a party vote is choosing the
political party you want to be in
Government.

An electorate is an area of the
country set up for voting.

Each electorate has about the same
number of people in it.

Here an electorate vote is choosing
the candidate / person for your area
who you want to be in parliament.

28

<!-- page 30 -->

You can only vote for 1 party.

You can use your party vote to
support the Greens to be in
Government.

How to vote for us
1. Get your voting paper
Staff at the voting place can give
you your voting paper.

2. Party vote
Tick the circle next to the Green
Party.

This is on the left side of the paper.

29

<!-- page 31 -->

3. Electorate vote
Tick the circle next to the name of
the person you want to vote for in
your electorate.

This is on the right side of the
paper.

4. Put your voting paper in the box
Staff at the voting place can show
you the correct box to put your
voting paper in.

If you find voting hard
There are other ways to vote if you
find voting hard.
