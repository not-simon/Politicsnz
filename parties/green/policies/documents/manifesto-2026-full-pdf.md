Tiriti o Waitangi, where power and wealth sit
with our communities, and where people and
the rest of nature thrive together.
Aotearoa has enough for all of us. Our
government should work for people and
planet, not the greed of corporations and their
shareholders.

Government on the side of the super-rich and
the most powerful corporations.

Paid for by a plan that will tax the super-rich

services, and cut income tax for 96 percent of
people.

Climate action cut, protections for nature

turning up demanding action.

healthy nature and real democracy.

Thriving tamariki and rangatahi
The Green Party will work
to build a system that puts
tamariki, rangatahi and
whānau first.
Our tamariki and rangatahi deserve to be
loved, nurtured and safe. They should be
connected with their whānau, cared for and
free to express their authentic selves, no
matter where they live. We know we have
enough to ensure tamariki are free from
poverty and supported through every step
of their unique journey. Supporting tamariki
means ensuring whānau and communities
have everything they need to support their
wellbeing.
We have a shared responsibility to make
sure tamariki voices are heard, centred and
reflected in all decisions that impact them.
Tamariki deserve a skilled, well-supported
workforce that is trauma informed, culturally
responsive and properly trained.
Tamariki Māori are disproportionately
impacted by a child protection system
that excludes meaningful partnership and
underinvests in culturally grounded, whānauled solutions. A functioning system should
centre whakapapa and identity, with whānau
and hapū resourced to lead decisions
affecting their tamariki.

Children

The Green Party reflects our commitment
to Te Tiriti o Waitangi by addressing the
inequities tamariki Māori face in everyday life
and in the state care system.

6

Our plan
1. Legislate so that the child’s voice is
central in all decisions that matter
to them by using the Child Impact
Assessment Tool consistently and fully
incorporating the Rights of the Child into
legislation.
2. Increase the Best Start payment and
extend it to every child under three as a
universal child benefit for the early years.
3. Elevate workforce safety by funding
training and a workforce development
strategy prioritising Māori, Pacific,
rainbow, disabled and community
provider pathways.
4. Improve access to both diagnosis
and support services for children with
disabilities or learning support needs.
5. Amend the Oranga Tamariki Act so that
it protects tamariki Māori and explicitly
embeds whānau participation in
decision-making to exercise iwi or hapū
authority.

Climate action:
emissions reduction

Bold action and solutions that
hold polluters to account
The Green Party will drive
meaningful action, cutting
climate pollution and making
big polluters pay.
We will ensure that polluting corporations
pay for Aotearoa to electrify our economy.
By rapidly phasing out fossil fuels, we can
ease cost of living pressures and decrease
our vulnerability to global price shocks.

Our plan
1. Strengthen the Zero Carbon Act by
requiring the government Budget
process to be consistent with limiting
global warming to 1.5 degrees, gross
emissions reduction targets and
international commitments.

Under the leadership of a Green Minister
for Climate Change, emissions began to
drop. But now, the current Government is
deliberately making choices to take us off
that track. In Aotearoa, progress towards
our domestic climate targets is at risk. Our
international reputation has been tarnished
due to this Government’s decisions to
weaken our climate targets and exempt
our biggest polluters from paying for their
greenhouse gas emissions, undermining
international agreements we have signed
up to.

2. Empower the Climate Change
Commission to directly set unit supply
in the Emissions Trading Scheme, in
line with emissions budgets. Align the
government Budget process with the
National Adaptation Plan and Emissions
Reduction Plans.

Decades of climate inaction shows
that a hands-off government will fail to
decarbonise our economy and secure the
future everyone deserves. The market
will not respond to the climate crisis at the
scale or pace we need, and won’t give fair
outcomes for those most affected.

4. Reform the Emissions Trading Scheme
(ETS) to restore credibility to the market
by phasing out the free allocation of
units, removing and forestry offsetting
so the system functions as a genuine
‘cap-and-trade’ system and redirecting
funding from the reformed ETS to an
improved Government Investment in
Decarbonising Industry Fund.

We need political leadership that stops
emissions at source, makes polluters pay,
and focuses all government institutions on
limiting global warming to 1.5 degrees.
The Green Party will implement climate
solutions that tackle the root causes and
honour Te Tiriti o Waitangi. We will remove
economic incentives that encourage and
enable climate pollution.

3. Introduce a price on agricultural
emissions to ensure methane reduction
targets are met, with financial and
technical support for on-farm emissions
reduction.

5. Ensure Aotearoa meets our Paris
Agreement commitments through
strong domestic action and, if
necessary, environmentally and socially
responsible initiatives overseas.
6. Improve public transparency on the
costs of inaction by including the fiscal
impact of the Nationally Determined
Contribution obligation in the financial
statements.

Keeping Aotearoa safe and prepared

Climate action: resilience and adaptation

The Green Party will prepare
all New Zealanders for the
impacts of climate change.

8

We will ensure that everyone is
comprehensively supported through both
the transition to a zero emissions economy
and increasingly severe weather events like
cyclones.
Climate change is one of the biggest
challenges of our time. Across Aotearoa,
we are already feeling the impacts of more
intense and frequent storms, flooding and
drought with devastating consequences.
Marae and community groups are often
on the frontlines of the emergency and
clean-up response, but these groups are not
supported by the Government to proactively
plan and adapt.
People are worried about rising insurance
prices, and what happens when disaster
strikes, particularly those living in
increasingly flood and slip exposed areas.
They have not received any clarity from
the Government about help that might be
available down the track when they need it.

Our plan
1. Ensure climate adaptation planning
affirms tino rangatiratanga and upholds
the mana motuhake of whānau, hapū
and iwi over whenua, wai and taonga by
appropriately funding a Māori Climate
Resilience Fund.
2. Expand the role of the Natural Hazards
Commission to include cover for climaterelated weather events and ensure it
plays a key role in managing communityled adaptation and retreat.
3. Prioritise long-term, nature-based
solutions over short-term engineering
solutions, and increase funding for hapū,
iwi and community organisations to
deliver these.
4. Ensure planning frameworks give effect
to the need for climate adaptation in the
approach to land and water use, together
with supporting ecosystem restoration
and conservation.

Climate change means that some areas are
not safe to build in, nor to keep living in, and
so retreat is the only safe option in the long
term. But again, the Government has failed to
help communities facing this uncertain future
with any support, plans or guidance.

5. Establish a dedicated fund for central and
local government to implement climate
adaptation and resilience measures,
including funding for partnerships
with tangata whenua and community
organisations.

The Green Party will provide this muchneeded clarity.

6. Scale up support for, and work with,
neighbouring countries in the Pacific to
adapt to climate change, and advocate for
an international agreement on the rights
of people displaced by climate change

We will ensure that any National Adaptation
Framework is anchored by Te Tiriti o
Waitangi. We will support the autonomy
and self-determination of hapū, iwi and
community organisations to develop
and implement their own climate change
response plans.
The Green Party will ensure that local
government and communities are
comprehensively funded to respond to their
climate adaptation challenges.

Conservation
and biodiversity

Protecting and restoring plants,
animals and ecosystems
The Green Party will protect
our precious whenua, moana
and taonga species from
corporate exploitation.
Aotearoa is home to natural taonga found
nowhere else in the world. Our ngahere
(forests), awa (rivers) and moana are central
to our national identity and way of life.
The Luxon Government has made it easier
for corporations to plunder and tear up te
taiao (nature), putting private profits ahead
of our treasured native wildlife. Meanwhile,
many of our native species are on the brink of
collapse. Without urgent action, we’re at risk
of losing them forever.
The Green Party will work towards protecting
30% of land and oceans because we
know the mauri and health of te taiao is
foundational to the wellbeing of our people,
our climate and our communities. We will
achieve this by giving effect to Te Tiriti o
Waitangi and recognising tangata whenua as
the original and ongoing kaitiaki of Aotearoa.
New Zealanders deserve to experience our
national parks and hear the calls of our native
manu for generations to come. That’s why
the Green Party will ensure conservation
decisions are made for the public good, not in
the interests of private profiteers.
We won’t just restore what’s been lost. We
will invest in an Aotearoa where people and
nature flourish together.

Our plan
1. Reform conservation and wildlife
protection laws to give effect to Te
Tiriti, elevate Indigenous-led land
stewardship and ensure the conservation
management system prioritises halting
and reversing nature loss in Aotearoa.
2. Ban new mining on conservation land,
including stewardship land.
3. Expand funding for Jobs for Nature
– Mahi mō te Taiao to protect our
biodiversity and create thousands of
regional jobs.
4. Protect all remaining natural wetlands in
legislation, including coastal wetlands
and estuaries, and implement a plan to
restore at least 30% of degraded wetland
area, prioritising areas most important
for biodiversity recovery and community
resilience.
5. Fund Action for Nature, the
implementation plan for the biodiversity
strategy, and implement national
guidance to protect indigenous
biodiversity, including the protection of
taonga species.
6. Increase overall public conservation
funding, including increasing funding and
support for the Predator Free 2050 goal.

An Aotearoa that values artists
The Green Party will invest in
the arts, creativity and culture
that make Aotearoa vibrant
and unique.
Our art, artists and creatives tell the stories of
our communities and help make sense of the
world around us.
This government has starved the sector
of support. Too many of our creatives are
working without the certainty of a stable
income, and many lack access to the training
and education needed to foster their skills
and talents. Steep cuts to Māori arts and
broadcasting have left them to scale down or
to fend for themselves.

Culture and creativity

Those governments have seen the arts as
a nice-to-have, rather than central to our
identity as New Zealanders. They’ve rolled
out the red carpet for big overseas studios,
sending funds offshore, while local and
Indigenous artists are left to struggle.

10

Arts, culture and creativity are vital for
creating vibrant cities and communities. Like
other community infrastructure, the arts,
culture and creative sectors need proper
support.
It is time for artists and creatives to be valued,
respected and recognised for their work.
The Green Party will fund the arts properly
and ensure artists have the long-term
sustainability, training and venues that
are necessary to build an audience and a
meaningful livelihood.
Together, we can support the people behind
the books, music, movies, shows and images
that tell our stories.

Our plan
1. Require that all publicly funded projects
pay at least the living wage to artists,
and expand paid artist residencies.
2. Provide stable funding for creative
organisations and events such as Te
Urungi, including long-term funding for
significant creative initiatives such as Te
Matatini and Polyfest.
3. Provide sustainable long-term arts
funding that is not reliant on gambling
revenue, alongside alcohol-free venues
for performers.
4. Support and invest in repatriating
taonga currently overseas back to iwi,
hapū and whānau Māori, as well as
keeping taonga in Aotearoa.
5. Establish infrastructure across
ministries to strengthen our creative
and cultural economy and maximise
opportunities for local artists.
6. Increase funding for quality, noncommercial public media that nurtures
and promotes our talent and creativity
as a nation, including sustainable
funding for Radio New Zealand and
Whakaata Māori.
7. Increase resourcing for student,
community, regional, Māori and Pacific
media and radio.

Democracy

Politics for people, not big money

The Green Party will
ensure that our democracy
represents the people, not
wealthy corporate interests.
Our democracy should work for everyone, not
just the wealthy few.
Around the world, democracy is under threat
by would-be dictators and corporate power.
Successive governments have meddled
with our electoral rules for their own selfinterest and failed to curb the influence
of big corporate interests in politics. This
government has actively invited those big
corporates in to write the rules in their own
favour.
The richest few can spend millions trying to
influence Ministers, while politicians weaken
our donation laws, make it harder to vote and
strip thousands of people of their right to vote
in breach of their human rights.
The Green Party is committed to a
democracy that upholds Te Tiriti o Waitangi
and the vision of Matike Mai Aotearoa,
creating a decision-making process that
reflects genuine partnership.

Our plan
1. Resource a national conversation
for constitutional transformation
that upholds He Whakaputanga and
Te Tiriti o Waitangi as our founding
constitutional documents, utilising
Matike Mai Aotearoa to facilitate
community discussions.
2. Uphold the right to vote by extending
the voting age to 16, extending the right
to vote to all people in custody and
restoring enrolment up to election day.
3. Entrench the Māori electoral seats
to guarantee Māori representation in
Parliament and extend the time in which
Māori can switch rolls.
4. Reform electoral funding by limiting
donations to registered voters,
increasing public financing of
campaigns and capping a donor’s
donations at a level that does not risk
donor influence over policy.
5. Strengthen the rules and transparency
over political lobbying, including
through a lobbyist register and a code of
conduct.

We can create a more representative
democracy by transforming our constitution,
expanding voting rights and banning big
money from politics.

Technology helping, not harming
The Green Party will hold
digital platforms accountable
to protect us and our
information.
Digital environments should support our
wellbeing, connect our communities, protect
our privacy and respect our sovereignty and
nature’s limits.
The choices we make on how to deploy and
use technology should be under democratic
control, not defined for us by overseas
corporations who prioritise shareholders
over citizens. All of us have been exposed to
harmful and toxic content that damages our
mental health, weakens trust between people
and splinters our shared reality.

Digital technology

Data centres running AI models threaten
to suck the world’s energy and clean water
sources dry. Intellectual property and
Māori culture are exploited and stolen by
unaccountable platforms. Companies from
social media giants to those storing our
health records collect more information
about us than required while proving that
they can’t be trusted with it.

12

Many of these platforms have become digital
infrastructure necessary to access essential
services, yet aren’t adequately regulated
nor owned by Aotearoa. Many of us still face
barriers to using these platforms at all.
Successive governments have left these
platforms alone because they think it’s too
hard, or they’ve wasted their time on false
solutions.
The Green Party will take a harm-reduction
approach to digital technology. We will hold
digital platforms accountable to protect us
and our information, so all of us can access
and enjoy the benefits of digital services
without compromising our privacy and our
online safety.

Disability

An accessible Aotearoa for all

The Green Party will build
an Aotearoa where disabled
people are valued and they
and their whānau live with
dignity and autonomy.

1. Introduce a disability rights law based
on Te Tiriti with enforceable accessibility
standards.

An Aotearoa where disabled people and
whānau have access to the services they
need — like transport and good quality
housing — means everyone can thrive in
caring communities.

2. Provide easily accessible and timely
income support to meet all disabilityrelated living and medical costs,
regardless of relationship status and
cause of disability.

3. Crack down on tax avoidance and the
offshoring of profits by multinational
tech giants such as Meta, Amazon and
Microsoft.

Our current laws, policies and social norms
mean disabled people are often denied the
same rights as others and are excluded
from places and activities non-disabled
people take for granted. These barriers are
often worse for tāngata whaikaha Māori
and for disabled people from marginalised
communities.

3. Address homelessness and work
towards all disabled people being able
to live in safe, accessible, affordable
housing in the community of their
choice, including by setting robust
universal design standards for public
and private housing.

4. Adopt and implement the Māori Data
Governance Model and Māori AI
Governance Framework from Te Kāhui
Raraunga across the public sector to
protect Māori data sovereignty.

The current Government has made it
harder for disabled people to access the
support they deserve and which enables
them to participate equally in life and their
communities.

5. Strengthen obligations on data-holders
to protect user and biometric data,
increase penalties for privacy breaches,
and strengthen enforcement powers
for the Privacy Commissioner to better
protect personal information.

The Green Party is ambitious in its plan
to create a more accessible Aotearoa
by building on the Enabling Good Lives
framework and grounding our approach
in Te Tiriti o Waitangi. Our approach will
operate on a high-trust model and apply
to all disabled people, including those with
disabling conditions who do not currently
access support.

Our plan
1. Regulate social media platforms
to address harmful content and
behaviour by introducing a Digital Duty
of Care. This will require algorithmic
transparency and accessible and
responsive complaints systems, as well
as strengthening enforcement with an
independent national regulator.
2. Regulate artificial intelligence to ensure
its use is safe, transparent and accurate,
including stronger rules around
‘deepfakes’ and scam material.

6. Expand media and digital literacy
education, and equip New Zealanders to
navigate AI-generated media and other
emerging digital technologies.
7. Reduce barriers to digital inclusion and
ensure all communities have access to
the internet, suitable devices and offline
alternatives for essential services.

The Green Party is committed to an Aotearoa
where disabled people and whānau thrive in
caring communities.

Our plan

4. Make getting around accessible,
including by continuing to fund the
75% subsidy for the Total Mobility
taxi scheme, making it easier to use
and increasing the number of mobility
vehicles to meet demand.
5. Draw on the Enabling Good Lives
approach to guide how government
agencies work with disabled people.
Remove barriers to accessing the
essentials like health care, transport,
inclusive education and disability
support for all who need it.
6. Ensure national and local emergency
response plans address the needs of
disabled people.
7. Uphold legal capacity for everyone,
moving from legal capacity assessments
to rights-based systems of supported
decision-making.

Putting health first to keep people safe
The Green Party will take an
evidenced, health-based
approach to alcohol and other
drugs to reduce harm in our
communities.
Successive governments have tinkered
around the edges of our drug laws but largely
left them unchanged from the ‘war on drugs’
approach of 50 years ago. Newsflash: they’re
losing the war on drugs. This approach has
been punitive and has not reduced harm
or demand. Instead, it fills our courts and
prisons, diverts money away from public
health services and leaves frontline services
chronically under-resourced. It makes the
problem worse.
Drugs exist and people use them. It’s a
political decision to either try and reduce the
harm that can arise and help make people’s
lives safer, or leave people faced with stigma
and fear that stops them from reaching out
for help.

Drug law reform

It’s past time to overhaul our drug laws
and replace them with an evidence-based
approach that prioritises the health of people.
We will increase funding for mental health
and addiction services and put a fence up at
the top of the cliff, instead of handcuffs at the
bottom.

14

Our plan
1. Repeal and replace the Misuse of Drugs
Act with an evidence-based approach
that reduces harm, treats drug use as a
health issue and provides for regulated
access to cannabis for personal use.
2. Ramp up funding of harm reduction
and addiction support for people who
want to reduce or stop their drug intake,
including through kaupapa Māori,
rainbow, youth and harm reduction
programmes.
3. Work with local government and service
providers — focused on safe drug
use, addictions and social housing —
to support town centres negatively
impacted by drug use.
4. Enable a licensing system for
psychedelic treatment and recognise
taonga status of our native psilocybin
species.
5. Pass Good Samaritan laws that allow
people to call for help without fear of
punishment. Legalise and fund other
overdose measures including increasing
the availability of naloxone and medically
supervised consumption spaces to
reduce harm and save lives.
6. Implement stronger regulation for
vape retailers to protect young people
and children, including by restricting
maximum nicotine concentrations,
restricting flavours, and keeping
vape stores out of the vicinity of schools
and kura.
7. Replace the Land Transport (Drug
Driving) Amendment Act 2025 with an
impairment approach to reduce harm
from all causes of driving impairment.
8. Phase out all alcohol advertising and
sponsorship, including at sporting and
cultural events, and use funding from
the alcohol levy to establish a wellresourced national harm-reduction
campaign.

Education

An Aotearoa where everyone can
reach their full potential
The Green Party will support
an inclusive education system
that uplifts everyone.

Education is a public good and a lifelong
right, not a commodity. The Green Party
believes education must foster curiosity,
creativity and confidence. Education should
support every person to discover who they
are and how they can contribute to a just,
sustainable world.
Education plays a vital role in building social
cohesion and democratic life, and should be
planned and funded to deliver this. Schools,
kura and early learning centres should be
places of belonging where diverse cultures,
identities and experiences are valued.
Unfortunately, today’s education system
treats education as a competitive market
and learning as a means to a narrowly
defined end. When decisions are driven by
standardisation, ranking and market forces,
it erodes trust, sidelines creativity and
reinforces exclusion.
The Green Party champions an education
approach grounded in Te Tiriti o Waitangi
and universal human rights, where inclusion
is foundational. This system honours tino
rangatiratanga, embraces te reo Māori
and mātauranga Māori, and ensures Māori
voices and communities are central to
decisionmaking. Disabled people, rainbow
communities, migrants and refugees, and
others facing structural barriers are fully
supported to learn, belong and thrive.
Educators’ voices and expertise are central to
decisions at all levels.
In the well-resourced system we will build,
education supports lifelong learning,
strengthens wellbeing, and is a force for
transformation of individuals, communities
and society as a whole.

Our plan
1. Make early childhood education free and
provide it as a public good like schooling,
removing the market and stopping
corporate interests from running forprofit centres.
2. Reset the Government’s rushed and
ideological curriculum and assessment
reforms, reverting to the original
direction of Te Mātaiaho and ensuring
qualification reform follows curriculum
reform.
3. Expand the criteria for access to learning
support. Establish a single agency to
coordinate and resource specialist
supports for learners with health and
disability support needs. Implement all
actions in the ‘Framework for Action: A
quality education for all’.
4. Expand Ka Ora, Ka Ako as a first step
towards providing a warm and nutritious
locally made lunch to all schoolchildren.
5. Support rangatiratanga to provide an
education system driven by whānau
needs, allowing space for whakapapabased decisions, and working towards
a Māori Education Authority governing
and funding all Māori medium and
Kaupapa Māori education.
6. Support the workforce so all teachers
and educators have fair wages and
staffing levels that support quality
teaching. Co-develop a long-term
workforce plan with the sector.

Energy

Powering Aotearoa with
affordable renewable energy
The Green Party will fix our
broken power system so that
people, not profits, come first.
Aotearoa can be a nation with energy
independence, where everyone has access
to affordable, reliable, clean renewable
energy. The Green Party will ensure our
energy system prioritises public good, so the
benefits of our renewable resources stay with
people, rather than driving corporate profit.
Our reliance on both fossil fuels and a profitdriven market exposes households to global
shocks and rising costs. Instead of fixing
these, the current Government has reopened
fossil fuel drilling, handed out fossil fuel
subsidies and slowed the transition to clean,
homegrown energy that we urgently need.
Regular people are left paying more for a
system that serves corporate interests first.

Our plan

Workforce rights

into insecure, low-paid or unsafe work.

Zero waste

Building a sustainable circular economy

The Green Party will take
action to reduce waste and
protect the environment from
pollution.
In a truly sustainable system, nature is
protected rather than treated as a dumping
ground. We can build a circular economy
where waste is designed out and resources
are valued and reused.
Under the current Government, progress
towards our zero-waste future has stalled.
Action on plastic pollution has ground to a
halt, and funding for solutions has dried up.
Corporations continue to make products that
won’t last or can’t be repaired, filling up our
landfills. Communities are left to deal with the
pollution and impacts.
The Green Party has shown what’s possible.
In government, we banned single-use plastic
bags and reduced food waste going to
landfill. We increased the waste levy for the
first time in over a decade and introduced
mandatory product stewardship schemes.
We also started work on a container return
scheme.
We will build on this legacy by holding
corporations accountable for the waste they
create. This means both reducing waste, and
requiring the recovery, recycling, reuse and
redistribution of materials.
By supporting communities to lead local
solutions, we will accelerate the transition
to a sustainable and circular economy that
allows people and nature to thrive.

Our plan
1. Establish strong product policy
measures, including mandatory
product stewardship, a beverage
container return scheme, phaseouts of problematic products and
comprehensive right-to-repair.
2. Direct revenue from the waste disposal
levy into waste minimisation initiatives
and stop diverting the local government
portion to other purposes. Increase the
levy to match international best practice
and apply a consistent rate across all
landfill classes.
3. Introduce a moratorium on wasteto-energy plants and explore energy
generation from biogas associated with
animal and plant wastes.
4. Build on proposed reforms of the Waste
Minimisation Act to implement timely,
binding, measurable and ambitious
waste reduction targets. Ensure
legislation gives effect to Te Tiriti o
Waitangi. Introduce comprehensive
duty-of-care systems for the waste and
resource recovery sector covering all
waste streams, including hazardous
waste.
5. Make greater use of discarded soil.
Divert soil from landfills, where safe and
appropriate to do so.
6. Prioritise local, mana whenua and
community-driven solutions to
resource recovery, implementation of
national zero-waste strategies, and
organic waste reduction, diversion and
processing.
