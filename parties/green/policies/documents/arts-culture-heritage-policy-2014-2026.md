# Arts, Culture and Heritage Policy (Green Party policy document)

<!-- page 1 -->

Arts, Culture and Heritage Policy
The Green Party will support the arts and cultural heritage sector to develop to its highest
standard and be properly resourced. A Green government will collaborate with funders, the
industry, and other stakeholders, including tangata whenua, who have the right to protect
traditional and contemporary Māori art. We will nurture talent in our communities by providing
educational opportunities in schools and communities and by expanding platforms for arts and
cultural heritage.

Vision
Creative, cultural and heritage work is integrated within our communities in myriad innovative
ways that empower people, spread happiness, and encourage intercultural appreciation and
understanding.

Values and Principles
- Honour Te Tiriti o Waitangi: Revitalising and celebrating toi Māori, tikanga Māori and te
reo Māori is key to ensuring a thriving arts industry. The Crown has a responsibility to
ensure the protection and return of taonga, including Māori arts, culture, intellectual
property and heritage sites. The Crown must support and contribute to but not determine
the revitalisation of toi Māori, tikanga Māori and te reo Māori.

- Ecological Wisdom: The arts play a part in enabling the transition to a life-sustaining
society by changing hearts and minds. Mātauranga Māori, as expressed through art and
cultural practice, is inextricably linked to the natural world.

- Social Responsibility: The arts foster community and improve our daily well-being,
empathy, equality and cultural diversity, as well as contributing economically.

- Appropriate Decision-Making: Those involved in arts and cultural heritage work must be
involved in developing guidelines for funding. Local communities must be given the ability
to protect places, buildings and heritage collections that are important to them historically
and culturally. The tino rangatiratanga of hapū and iwi over their taonga must be affirmed,
through Matike Mai mechanisms.

- Non-Violence: All art forms practised and recognised in Aotearoa New Zealand are
included in this policy and their interconnectedness is acknowledged. The art forms of
marginalised cultures and groups should be equitably supported.

- Resilience: Arts, culture and heritage are essential social infrastructure, not luxuries. The
Government should help build their resilience in our communities and at professional
levels.

- Professional: A vibrant and diverse professional arts sector contributes to all our lives, as
well as the identity of Aotearoa New Zealand on the international stage.

Strategic Priorities
The Green Party’s strategic goals include:

“People will be part of caring communities with a strong heritage fabric”
Actions in this policy that will help achieve this include:
- Ensure cultural heritage infrastructure and funding provisions are sufficient to increase
participation in community arts, provide arts and cultural heritage education, and support
the professional arts to reach the highest standard. (1.2)

- Support and contribute to the revitalisation of toi Māori (2.1)

- Develop and resource the implementation of an ongoing arts education strategy from
early childhood to tertiary level. (3.3.1)

- Developing a network of Art Spaces and heritage equivalents in every community that are
accessible to all cultures and levels of ability, including programmes in prisons, and care
and refuge spaces. (3.11)

Connected Policies
Providing platforms through Media to celebrate the arts is important for arts, culture and
heritage to thrive. Ensuring sufficient income for those working in the arts, cultural and heritage
sectors is part of our Livelihoods and Workforce Policies. The Crown’s responsibility to ensure
the protection and revitalisation of natural and other taonga is explored in our Biodiversity and
Environmental Regeneration and Kaupapa Māori Policies.

Resourcing the arts, culture and heritage sector

Issues
Inadequate resourcing of the arts, culture and heritage sector risks a downward spiral in which
organisations close or cut back, long‑running programmes disappear, inequities deepen, and the
sector loses diversity and capacity it may not get back. The sector faces persistent structural
problems with few full-time jobs, low pay, and an expectation that creative professionals will
work for free or for ‘exposure’. Burnout and financial stress are common. Toi Māori has long been
structurally under‑resourced relative to its cultural significance and the scale of its activity especially considering language revitalisation, marae‑based arts, intergenerational teaching, and
community‑driven practice.

Ngā toi Māori

Issues
National strategy and funding systems were not designed for kaupapa that are relational,
long‑horizon and whakapapa‑based, such as whare wānanga, customary arts, and iwi‑driven arts
infrastructure. They fail to uphold tino rangatiratanga or to represent the breadth and diversity
of te ao Māori, instead encouraging simplified, commodified, or misappropriated works.

Actions

3.

schools.

Economic Development and the Arts, Culture, and Heritage

Issues
Creative industries make a significant economic contribution. Rising economic disparities,
unstable income streams, and intergenerational knowledge gaps hinder creative start-ups and
innovation. Reliance on corporate funding risks eroding artistic autonomy and public trust.
Creative and cultural exporters face significant trade barriers, and there is a lack of scalable midsize businesses.

Protecting and Preserving our Heritage

Issues
There are significant challenges in protecting and preserving built heritage, including cost,
seismic and climate risks, and pressures from urban development. Neglected buildings require
expensive fixes to avoid demolition. Negative and systemic biases towards built and colonial
heritage sideline and erode pre-colonial history, wāhi tapu, stories, and skills. Heritage losses are
irreplaceable.

Actions
5.1.

5.2.

Strengthen heritage policy and funding by:
5.1.1.

Establishing national direction for heritage, requiring local authorities to place
more weight on recognising and supporting heritage places in district plans; and

5.1.2.

Providing funding schemes for the maintenance and earthquake strengthening
of privately-owned heritage buildings.

Enhance Māori and community leadership and engagement in heritage protection by:
5.2.1.

Providing programmes that enable communities to safely explore the diversity
and tensions in local heritage and traditions;

5.2.2.

Increase the capacity of Te Papa's National Services to provide effective
assistance, training and other services to local museums and art galleries in the
care and management of heritage collections;

5.2.3.

Prioritising areas of cultural value to communities, whānau, hapū and iwi when
resourcing climate change adaptation; and

5.2.4.

Resourcing artistic and community activities that seek to challenge entrenched
harmful narratives and enhance understanding of a positive, sustainable and
climate-safe future.
