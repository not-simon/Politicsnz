# Media Policy (Green Party policy document)

<!-- page 1 -->

Media Policy
The Green Party will support an independent media and enable community-based media. We will
support publicly owned broadcasting services and fund locally produced content, prioritising
those communities that are not well served by commercial interests, including Māori and
children.

Vision
A strong, diverse and independent media contributes to the maintenance of effective
democracy, and to our social, cultural, economic and environmental wellbeing.

Values and Principles
Decisions relating to broadcasting and media must uphold the following values and principles:
- Honour Te Tiriti o Waitangi: Māori media should be prioritised and recognised as a
platform to uphold the culture of tangata whenua in Aotearoa New Zealand.

- Ecological Wisdom: The environmental impact of the production and storage of media
content should be minimised.

- Social Responsibility: The media should be accessible to people of all abilities, and used
to inform, educate and entertain in a manner that supports citizens to participate
effectively in democracy and appreciate each others’ needs, interests and aspirations.

- Appropriate Decision-Making: The right to broadcast or publish is balanced with
expectations to act in a responsible and inclusive manner.

- Non-Violence: Non-violent resolution of political and social conflict is based on the
knowledge, acceptance and understanding of diverse communities, and of their needs,
interests and aspirations. Media should act as a means of checks and balances against
the misuse or abuse of social, economic and political power.

- Independence: A vigorous, independent and diverse media is the cornerstone of a free
society: Public broadcasting and community-based media initiatives should be supported;
and directives by the state, or ownership and control by international media
conglomerates, or domination by local commercial monopolies should be avoided.

Connected Policies
A freely available and politically independent media makes a significant contribution to the
effectiveness of Governance, Research, Science and Technology, and Education, through the
provision of political debate, diverse perspectives and information about the world we live in. It
is a key mechanism for celebrating the Arts, Culture and Heritage of Aotearoa New Zealand. It is
increasingly entwined with the development of Digital technologies. Its Workforce must be
protected.

Policy Positions
1. Free and Independent Media
Issues
Ownership of media in Aotearoa New Zealand is dominated by transnational mediacommunication conglomerates and private financial organisations. National media is not immune
to global trends that threaten free and independent media, leading to a shift of audiences and
advertising revenue from traditional mass media to social media, and the purchased influence of
far-right populists. This risks undermining public knowledge and increasing the polarisation of
views.

Inclusive and accessible media

Issues
New Zealanders are heavily exposed to commercial and imported content. By comparison, locally
made content that shows Aotearoa New Zealand’s cultures, languages and values is relatively
limited. This reduces opportunities for children to see themselves and their communities, and
Māori as tangata whenua, positively reflected on screen. It is hard for Māori to exercise
rangatiratanga and be heard in the media. Expressions of the indigenous culture of Aotearoa
New Zealand, which is the birthright of every Māori, and the heritage of every New Zealander,
are diluted. Access gaps for disabled people and speakers of minority languages, for example,
can deepen social exclusion and limit participation as audience or creator. Meanwhile, access to
harmful content has impacts on mental health, attitudes to relationships, and discrimination especially for young people.

Actions
2.1.

broadcasting and streaming by diverse Aotearoa New Zealand artists, producers,

2.1.1.

Requiring Government-funded broadcasters and streaming services to employ
a workforce that reflects the communities it serves, and employ best practice
for media coverage of marginalised communities;

2.1.2.

Investigating minimum Aotearoa New Zealand content quotas for public
broadcasters and streaming services and incentives for commercial
broadcasters and streaming services to provide higher levels of local content;
and
