# Waste and Hazardous Substances Policy

[View our Priorities](https://www.greens.org.nz/waste_and_hazardous_substances_policy#waste_and_hazardous_substances_policy_strategic_priorities)
[View the Full Policy](https://assets.nationbuilder.com/beachheroes/pages/65046/attachments/original/1778624620/Policy-Greens_Waste_Hazardous_Substances_Policy_2022-2025.pdf)

## Summary

This policy seeks to transform all sectors of the Aotearoa New Zealand economy into a circular economy in which the production of unnecessary waste and hazardous substances is eliminated and, where this is not possible, they are effectively managed through reuse and recycling, safely, effectively, and responsibly, to ensure the wellbeing of current and future generations.

OUR VISION

> All activities eliminate waste, circulate resources, and regenerate nature.

## Values and Principles

Decisions relating to waste and hazardous substances should uphold the following values and principles:

##### Honour Te Tiriti o Waitangi:

Tangata whenua have primary decision-making power over the elimination and reduction of waste and hazardous substances production, as well as their management and disposal, and all decisions in this area show respect to the taonga of te taiao.

##### Ecological Wisdom:

Material growth should be limited, such that waste production is avoided or redirected for use in a circular economy.

##### Social Responsibility:

The management of waste and hazardous substances should not further disadvantage or burden marginalised groups within or outside Aotearoa New Zealand.

##### Appropriate Decision-Making:

Decisions regarding the management of waste should be based on mātauranga Māori and western science, and determined in partnership with whānau, hapū and iwi.

##### Non-Violence:

A precautionary approach to hazardous material should be taken to ensure that no person, ecological environment or native species is harmed by the production, management or disposal of hazardous materials.

## Strategic Priorities

The Green Party’s strategic goals include:
“Our nation will be in transition to a zero waste economy, where single use plastics are largely eliminated, wasteful consumption is ended, and all-of-lifecycle product stewardship is in place across the economy.”
Actions in this policy that will help achieve this include:

- Establishing a Tiriti-based national circular economy strategy that sets out a clear pathway for the phasing out of landfills (1.2.3)
- Promoting thoughtful and moderated consumption and restricting advertising that promotes hyperconsumption (1.5.2)
- Resourcing the implementation of zero-waste strategies and associated legislation (1.2.1)
- Prohibiting most single-use plastic products and non-recyclable and non-home-compostable packaging, with necessary exceptions (2.2.1)

- Developing a national resource recovery network that resources existing local solutions and replicates them in other areas (2.4.1)
- Requiring all products that include plastics and rare earth metals to be readily recyclable (2.5.1)
- Leading international efforts to clean up plastic and other harmful waste from the Pacific Ocean (2.9.5)
