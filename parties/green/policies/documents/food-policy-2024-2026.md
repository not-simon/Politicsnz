# Food Policy (Green Party policy document)

<!-- page 1 -->

Food Policy
The Green Party supports everyone’s right to food that is safe, accessible, and affordable. For
Māori, this includes the right to access and prepare food in traditional ways. Aotearoa New
Zealand needs a national strategy for the whole food system that builds local resilience, reduces
waste, and protects the climate. When there is enough food, and it is nourishing, it supports our
health and wellbeing. We want to prepare and share meals with our family and friends. This will
sustain and strengthen our family traditions and cultural practices.

Vision
Nourishing food is always local, affordable, safe, and sustainable for future generations.

Values and Principles
Food policy must follow these values and principles:
- Whakamana: Central and local government must support Māori to restore their food
practices.

- Rangatiratanga: Everyone should have the right to autonomous decision-making over
nourishing food systems.

- Puawaitanga: Nutritious food should be affordable for all, via a fair and robust supply
chain.

- Manaakitanga: Everyone should have access to enough good food wherever they live.

- Tikanga: Food should be safe and nourishing. All steps of the food supply chain should be
transparent and support cultural safety.

- Ngā Hapori Tōnui: Those who work within the supply chain should be the first to benefit.

- Kaitiakitanga: Food systems should promote healthy and biodiverse ecosystems and a
safe climate.

- Hauora: Food should improve wellbeing for family and community. “He kai te rongoā, he
rongoā te kai.”

- Manawaroa: Food systems must be resilient and sustainable during natural and humanmade disasters.

Strategic Priorities
The Green Party’s strategic goals include:

“...regenerative practices in all areas of economic activity, including land use and food production,
will predominate…”

Actions in this policy that will work towards this goal include:
- Create a national strategy for the whole food system that honours Te Tiriti o Waitangi and
prioritises the health of people and the planet over economic goals. (1.3.)

- Provide free nutritious lunches for every school child, emphasising wholefood and plantbased options. (4.2.)

- Update the Eating and Activity Guidelines. Take a 'whole system' approach to food that
includes environmental, social, and cultural issues. (4.3.)

- Set up a cross-government body with iwi, academics, and non-government organisations.
This should oversee and lead food-related policies. (5.4.)

- Amend the food processing and selling regulations to make them fair and affordable for
small local suppliers. (6.3.)

- Support higher levels of nutrition education for all healthcare providers. They should use
that knowledge to meet individual health needs. (8.2.)

Policy Positions
1.

Whakamana

Our Vision
We value the life-giving qualities of food and honour its connection to the natural world. We
receive support to use our traditional ways and/or mātauranga Māori approaches to preparing
and consuming wholefoods. This nurtures people and gives us hope for the future of the planet.

Actions

1.2.
1.3.

Expand programs that teach children to grow and harvest food from school
gardens, and teach them to prepare and share nutritious meals.
Strengthen and prioritise restoring the Māori-led kaitiakitanga of mahinga kai.
Create a national strategy for the whole food system that honours Te Tiriti o
Waitangi and prioritises the health of people and the planet over economic goals.

Rangatiratanga

Our Vision
Māori food sovereignty is strong and supports food security and resilience. The agency of all
consumers is upheld. We can make informed food decisions, supported by clear, accessible
information. A wide range of options close to where we live lets us choose foods that meet our
cultural needs. We know how to prepare and eat nourishing foods.

Puawaitanga

Our Vision
The foods that we need every day are affordable. We have easy access to nutritious food that is
plentiful and fresh. We have the resources we need to access and share the food we want, without
being reliant on others to meet that need. This means that we all enjoy high levels of economic,
physical, and social wellbeing.

Manaakitanga

Our Vision
Everyone can access nourishing and diverse food close to where we live and work. There is
enough food to support our health and wellbeing. We can also provide hospitality to friends,
family, and visitors. As a country, we prioritise our domestic supply of the food we produce to
feed our own people first.

Tikanga

Our Vision
Food and drinks meet our health and cultural needs. They must also meet our spiritual and/or
religious requirements. Government agencies check that imported and commercial foods meet
high standards of safety. There are regulations that prioritise the public health and wellbeing of
all.

Actions
5.1.

5.2.
5.3.
5.4.

6.

Renegotiate the Australia and New Zealand Food Standards Treaty. This must let
Aotearoa New Zealand, including iwi, take full control over our own food
regulations.
Improve the enforcement of imported foods. Hold these to the same standards as
those required by Aotearoa New Zealand food producers.
Strengthen the Food Act to address longer-term risks to public health.
Set up a cross-government body with iwi, academics, and non-government
organisations. This should oversee and lead food-related policies.

Ngā hapori tōnui

Our Vision
We have a reliable, year-round food supply from local farmers’ markets and community gardens.
The food is in-season, fresh, and produced in a sustainable way. Local suppliers' prices are
competitive with supermarkets, which provides ongoing food security.

Actions
6.1.

Support community-led and tangata whenua-led approaches to growing resilient
local food economies.
6.2.
Include food-sensitive and urban design concepts in council planning policies by
signing up to the Milan Food Pact so that they:
6.2.1.
Cover fresh and wholefood outlets in areas that don't have many, and
6.2.2.
Support communities to access land to grow food.
6.3.
Amend the food processing and selling regulations to make them fair and
affordable for small local suppliers.
6.4.
Prioritise local food suppliers for public institutions (e.g. prisons, hospitals,
schools).

Kaitiakitanga

Our Vision
Each step in the food supply chain achieves net-zero greenhouse gas emissions. We act to
reduce the food supply chain’s impacts on the natural world and on climate change. This informs
our dietary choices, and our diet is primarily wholefood and plant-based.

Hauora

Our Vision
Nourishing food is easy to find, especially high-nutrient, minimally processed, plant-based
options. This supports our physical and mental health and family and community wellbeing.
Access to these food options has an extra benefit of lowering demand on our health system.

Actions

Support higher levels of nutrition education for all healthcare providers. They
should use that knowledge to meet individual health needs.
Create a National Policy Statement to regulate food marketing to protect public
health.

Manawaroa

Our Vision
We can rely on a sustainable food supply in a natural disaster. A strong local food system
supports the resilience of our community in an emergency. Disruptions will not affect the supply
of our varied nutritional needs from food.

Actions
9.1.
9.2.

9.3.

Support marae and other hubs to stockpile food to meet all community food
requirements, during and following emergencies.
Support food distributors and retailers to provide rapid and secure food supplies
in an emergency. This requires well-rehearsed systems, supply chain resilience,
and trained personnel.
Continue distributing food if cash or card systems are not available in an
emergency. Compensate all affected food providers as soon as possible
afterwards.
