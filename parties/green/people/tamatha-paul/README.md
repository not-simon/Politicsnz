## Current status

Green MP for Wellington Central since 2023. Following boundary changes she is standing in **Wellington North** and is ranked **#4** on the Green list for 2026.

## Biography

Tamatha-Kaye Erin Paul was born in South Auckland in 1997 and grew up in Christchurch and Tokoroa; her father is of Waikato-Tainui and Ngāti Awa descent. Dux of Tokoroa High School (2015); diagnosed with lupus at 12. BA in international relations and political science from Victoria University of Wellington (2018, First in Family scholarship) and a Master of Resource and Environmental Planning from Massey (2022). The first Māori woman to be president of the Victoria University of Wellington Students' Association (elected 2018, served 2019).

## Political career

- Elected to Wellington City Council (Pukehīnau/Lambton) in 2019 as part of a "youthquake" of young candidates; joined the Greens in 2022 and topped the ward poll (5,206 votes) at the 2022 election.
- Won **Wellington Central** in 2023 — previously a Labour stronghold held by Grant Robertson — by 6,066 votes over Labour's Ibrahim Omer, becoming its first Green and first Māori MP. Resigned from council effective 10 November 2023.

## Record & notable actions

- On council chaired the Environment and Infrastructure Committee; led the Housing Action Plan and a campaign that helped low-income tenants access rent subsidies; backed the inner-city density spatial plan; secured an extra $100 million over ten years for Wellington's cycle network through Let's Get Wellington Moving; held climate (Te Atakura – First to Zero) and city-safety (Pōneke Promise) portfolios.
- As MP, opposed the National-led government's reinstatement of 90-day no-cause tenancy terminations and tougher Kāinga Ora eviction rules, and its roading/tunnel priorities.

## Controversies

- **Comments on police (March 2025):** said at a University of Canterbury event that police beat patrols made some communities feel less safe and later that beat police "walk around all day waiting for homeless people to leave their spot". Labour leader Chris Hipkins called the remarks "ill-informed... unwise, in fact... stupid" and PM Christopher Luxon defended police; Paul stood by them, citing constituent experiences. She drew further criticism for a CubaDupa DJ set including KRS-One's "Sound of da Police".
- A March 2025 TikTok in which she said the "vast majority" of prisoners were jailed for non-violent, poverty-related offences was disputed by critics.
