## Current status

Former MP and former Green co-leader. Stepped down as co-leader in March 2024 (succeeded by Chlöe Swarbrick) and left Parliament on 5 May 2024 (replaced by Francisco Hernandez). Now works in climate-related business and governance roles, including Morrison & Co. Not a 2026 candidate.

## Biography

Born in Wellington on 6 May 1973 and raised mainly by his mother Cynthia Shaw and later also her partner Susanne Jungersen. Attended Wellington High School and Victoria University of Wellington, then spent 12 years in London, where he completed an MSc in sustainability and business leadership at the University of Bath (2005) and worked in PwC's consulting division. Returned to Wellington in 2010 and worked for HSBC on environmental-leadership programmes and at the Akina Foundation social enterprise. Married to Annabel; lives in Aro Valley.

## Political career

- Volunteered for the Greens from 1990; stood for Wellington City Council at 19 (1992).
- Green candidate in Wellington Central 2011, 2014, 2017 and 2020; list MP from 2014.
- Elected male co-leader on 30 May 2015 after seven months as an MP, beating Kevin Hague (54% to 44% of first preferences). Sole leader through the 2017 campaign after Metiria Turei's resignation; co-leader with Marama Davidson from 2018.
- Minister for Climate Change, Minister of Statistics and Associate Minister of Finance (outside Cabinet) from 2017 under the Labour–NZ First–Green arrangement; retained Climate Change and gained Associate Environment (Biodiversity) under the 2020 cooperation agreement.
- Re-elected co-leader 116–4 against James Cockle (2021). Not reconfirmed in the July 2022 annual vote, triggering a contest; re-elected with 142 of 145 delegate votes (97%) on 10 September 2022.
- Announced in January 2024 he would step down as co-leader; his Bill of Rights amendment adding a "right to a sustainable environment" was defeated at first reading on 10 April 2024.

## Record & notable actions

- **Climate Change Response (Zero Carbon) Amendment Act 2019**: introduced by Shaw in May 2019, it set net-zero targets for long-lived gases by 2050, created the independent Climate Change Commission and a system of emissions budgets. It passed with near-unanimous support.
- Backed the 2018 decision to end new offshore oil and gas exploration permits ("nuclear-free moment of our generation").
- Represented New Zealand at UN climate conferences (COP) during 2017–2023.
- Described himself as part of a "pro-market" Green tradition that believes markets can be reformed to incorporate sustainability.

## Controversies

- **Green School funding (2020):** approved $11.7 million from the COVID-19 "shovel-ready" fund for a private school in Taranaki, contrary to Green policy against state funding of private schools; criticised by teachers' unions, principals and former Green MPs. He apologised on 1 September 2020 calling it "an error of judgment"; the grant was later converted into a loan. Education Minister Chris Hipkins disputed Shaw's account that he had given verbal approval.
- **2018 census**: as Statistics Minister he was criticised over the census's low response rate.
- Criticised in 2018 for the highest ministerial international airfare spending in one quarter (NZ$77,771), which he attributed to climate conferences; and in 2021 for MIQ places allocated to his COP26 delegation.
- On 14 March 2019 he was assaulted while walking to Parliament; a 47-year-old man was charged. (Shaw was the victim.)
