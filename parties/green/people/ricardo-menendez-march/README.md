## Current status

Green list MP since 2020 and the party's musterer (whip). Standing in **Mount Albert** and ranked **#9** on the Green list for 2026.

## Biography

Moved to New Zealand from Tijuana, Mexico, in 2006. Worked for a decade as a film projectionist in Auckland until made redundant by digitisation, then in hospitality and migrant advocacy. From late 2017 he coordinated Auckland Action Against Poverty, becoming a frequent media critic of benefit levels, housing costs and KiwiBuild under the Labour-led government. Openly gay; describes himself as a "proud socialist".

## Political career

- Young Greens co-convenor (2016); Green candidate for Mount Roskill in 2017 (list #21, not elected).
- Elected a list MP in 2020 (list #10; third in Maungakiekie); re-elected 2023 (third in Mount Albert with 9,296 votes).
- Became Green musterer and spokesperson for social development, employment and immigration in November 2023.

## Record & notable actions

- Advocate for higher benefits and targeting housing assistance at low-income families.
- In February 2025 lobbied Associate Immigration Minister Chris Penk on behalf of Davan Kumar, an 18-year-old born in New Zealand to overstayer parents; Penk granted the teenager residency (but not his parents).
- During the 2020 campaign publicly criticised co-leader James Shaw's Green School funding decision.

## Controversies

- **Travel to Mexico during COVID-19 (December 2020):** travelled to care for seriously ill family members, with party permission; criticised by National leader Judith Collins, and National's Chris Bishop revealed he had twice sought MIQ places on "national interest" grounds.
- **"From the river to the sea" (2021, 2023–24):** the NZ Jewish Council criticised his use of the slogan as associated with Hamas and antisemitism; Alternative Jewish Voice and Golriz Ghahraman defended it as a call for equal rights.
- He has been the target of remarks by government MPs: in 2016 Young NZ First members chanted "Build the Wall" at him (they apologised); in July 2024 Trade Minister Todd McClay told him "you're not in Mexico now" and was ordered to withdraw and apologise; in January 2025 NZ First's Shane Jones shouted "send the Mexicans home" in the House, prompting a complaint from the Mexican Embassy.
