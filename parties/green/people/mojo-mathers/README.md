## Current status

Former MP (2011–2017). Appointed chief executive of the Disabled Persons Assembly (DPA) in 2023. Not a 2026 candidate.

## Biography

Born Mojo Celeste Minrod in London on 23 November 1966 and named after Muddy Waters' "Got My Mojo Working"; granddaughter of legal philosopher H. L. A. Hart. Profoundly deaf from birth, she lip-reads and later also used sign language. She holds an honours degree in mathematics and a master's in conservation forestry, ran a forestry-management business (2001–2006) and was a senior policy adviser for the Green Party (2006–2011). In Coalgate, Canterbury, she was spokesperson for the community opposing the Central Plains Water irrigation dam and a founder of the Malvern Hills Protection Society, which halted the project. Three children. MNZM (2019 New Year Honours) for services to people with disabilities.

## Political career

- Green candidate for Rakaia (2005) and Christchurch East (2008, 2011).
- Elected in 2011 (list #14) as New Zealand's first deaf MP; re-elected 2014. Lost her seat in 2017 when the Green vote fell, despite an unchanged list ranking.
- Sat on the Commerce, Government Administration, and Local Government and Environment committees.

## Record & notable actions

- Her election prompted Parliament to provide an electronic note-taker and moves toward captioning of House proceedings.
- Commerce (Supermarket Adjudicator and Code of Conduct) Amendment Bill (2015), to set up an adjudicator for supermarket–supplier disputes; defeated at first reading (supported by Greens, Labour, NZ First, Māori Party; opposed by National, ACT, United Future).

## Controversies

- In 2012 there was a public dispute over funding for her electronic note-taker, which was provided after some delay.
