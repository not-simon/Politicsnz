## Current status

Green list MP since 2020 and Third Assistant Speaker since December 2023. Standing in **Palmerston North** and ranked **#3** on the Green list for 2026.

## Biography

Born in Henderson, West Auckland, on 25 December 1972 to a Māori mother (Ngāpuhi, Ngāi Takoto) and a Cook Islands Māori father from Atiu; grew up in Te Atatū and Ōtāhuhu, with two years in Rarotonga. BA in Māori Studies and a law degree from the University of Auckland; credits a paper taught by Jeanette Fitzsimons with drawing him into environmental activism. An education consultant, he has worked for the United Nations and was activist-in-residence at Massey University. Organised protests against GCSB legislation and the TPPA, helped translate Facebook into te reo Māori, and has spoken of his home being raided in the 2007 police "anti-terror" raids.

## Political career

- Green candidate 2017 (list #16, not elected); second in the 2019 Palmerston North mayoral race.
- Elected a list MP in 2020 (list #8; third in Palmerston North) — the Greens' first Pasifika MP. Re-elected 2023.
- Appointed Third Assistant Speaker on 7 December 2023 — the first Green MP in the Speaker's team.

## Record & notable actions

- **Citizenship (Western Samoa) (Restoration) Amendment Act 2024**: his member's bill restoring citizenship eligibility to Samoans born 1924–1949 under New Zealand administration passed its third reading unanimously on 20 November 2024, after National switched to support following 24,000+ submissions.
- Lodged the Tohorā Oranga Bill (February 2026) to grant legal personhood to whales.
- Supports regenerative agriculture and allowing MPs to swear allegiance to Te Tiriti o Waitangi instead of the King.

## Controversies

- On 18 February 2026 Winston Peters twice criticised him in question time for using "Aotearoa", asking why someone "who comes from Rarotonga" would rename New Zealand; Chris Hipkins called this racism and Speaker Gerry Brownlee ruled the question unacceptable. (Tuiono was the subject, not the instigator.)
