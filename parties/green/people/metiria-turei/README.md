## Current status

Former MP and former Green co-leader. After leaving Parliament in 2017 she studied art at the Dunedin School of Art; as of 2025 she lectured in law at the University of Otago, was promoted to associate professor in 2026, and writes a regular political column in the *Otago Daily Times*. Not a 2026 candidate.

## Biography

Metiria Leanne Agnes Stanton Turei (Ngāti Kahungunu, Āti Hau nui a Pāpārangi) grew up in a working-class Māori family in Palmerston North. She left school without passing her examinations, worked as a kitchen-hand, was a founding member of the Random Trollops performance-art troupe, and later studied law at the University of Auckland, becoming a corporate lawyer at Simpson Grierson. She is married to Warwick Stanton and has a daughter.

## Political career

- Stood for the McGillicuddy Serious Party (1993, 1999) and the Aotearoa Legalise Cannabis Party (1996); Green candidate for Mayor of Auckland in 2001 (2.05%).
- Green list MP from 2002 to 2017 (contested Tāmaki Makaurau 2002, Te Tai Tonga 2005, Dunedin North 2008 and Te Tai Tonga 2017).
- Elected female co-leader of the Green Party on 30 May 2009, ahead of Sue Bradford; co-leader alongside Russel Norman and then James Shaw until 9 August 2017.
- Resigned as co-leader and withdrew from the party list on 9 August 2017; lost Te Tai Tonga to Labour's Rino Tirikatene and retired.

## Record & notable actions

- Member's bills: Misuse of Drugs (Medicinal Cannabis) Amendment Bill (defeated 84–34 at first reading, 2009); Liquor Advertising (Television and Radio) Bill; Marine Animals Protection Law Reform Bill; Crown Minerals bill to protect Schedule 4 conservation land; a bill applying criminal proceeds recovery to casinos (2012).
- Led the Green campaign in 2009–10 against the National government's proposal to allow mining in national parks.
- Voted for the Marriage (Definition of Marriage) Amendment Act 2013.
- In 2017 campaigned for higher benefits for solo parents and low-income families.

## Controversies

- **2017 benefit and electoral disclosures.** At the Green campaign launch on 16 July 2017 Turei said that in the early 1990s, while on the Domestic Purposes Benefit, she had not told Work and Income she was receiving rent from flatmates. She later also acknowledged having been enrolled to vote, at 23, in an electorate where she did not live. The disclosures polarised opinion; she agreed to repay what she owed. Green MPs Kennedy Graham and David Clendon withdrew from caucus over her handling of the issue, and she resigned as co-leader on 9 August 2017, citing the scrutiny on her family.
- In 2017 she said NZ First leader Winston Peters had a "very racist approach to immigration"; Peters and Tracey Martin rejected the claim.
