## Current status

Deceased. Fa'anānā Efeso Collins died on 21 February 2024 after collapsing during a ChildFund charity run on lower Queen Street, Auckland, less than a week after delivering his maiden speech. His list seat passed to Lawrence Xu-Nan.

## Biography

Born 27 May 1974 in Ōtara, South Auckland, the youngest of six children of Samoan (and part-Tokelauan) immigrants — a bus driver and Pentecostal pastor, and a factory worker and cleaner. He held the matai title Fa'anānā. Educated at Tangaroa College and the University of Auckland (BA 1997, MA 1999), where in 1999 he became the first Pasifika president of the students' association and later ran a Pacific student outreach programme for 15 years. He also worked as a youth worker, broadcaster and in the public service. Married Fia in 2011; two daughters.

## Political career

- Labour member for many years; elected to the Ōtara-Papatoetoe Local Board in 2013 (chair) and to Auckland Council for Manukau in 2016, topping the ward poll in 2019.
- Ran for Mayor of Auckland in 2022 as an independent endorsed by Labour, the Greens and outgoing mayor Phil Goff, on a platform including fare-free public transport; lost to Wayne Brown (124,802 votes, 30.85%, to 181,810).
- Joined the Green Party in 2023; contested Panmure-Ōtāhuhu and was elected as a list MP (list #11).

## Record & notable actions

- On council led work on homelessness ("rare, brief and non-recurring") and won support to lobby for a ban on public fireworks sales.
- In 2021 called for an amnesty for overstayers to encourage Pasifika COVID-19 testing, and criticised TVNZ's *Police Ten 7* for its portrayal of Māori and Pasifika (after which he received a death threat).
- In 2010 he was stood down as host of *Talanoa Pacific* after questioning a $4.8 million government grant to the Pacific Islands Economic Development Agency; the grant was later cancelled.

## Controversies

None of significance; he was the target of death threats in 2021 and 2023 (a 58-year-old man admitted the 2023 threat and went through a restorative justice process).
