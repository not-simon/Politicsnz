## Current status

Former MP (2017–2024). Resigned in January 2024 after shoplifting allegations, was convicted on four charges and fined in June 2024, and lodged an appeal in October 2024. Not a 2026 candidate.

## Biography

Born in Iran in 1981; her family fled in 1990 when she was nine and claimed asylum at Auckland Airport during a stopover, being accepted as refugees. Educated at Auckland Girls' Grammar School and the University of Auckland (LLB, BA in history), and holds an MSt in international human rights law from Oxford. Worked as a criminal-defence barrister and with UN-backed tribunals for Rwanda, Cambodia and the former Yugoslavia (on defence and prosecution teams), and argued a Supreme Court case that led police to overhaul rules on undercover operations. She has multiple sclerosis. Author of *Know Your Place* (2020).

## Political career

- Elected in 2017 (list #8, Te Atatū candidate) after special votes lifted the Green tally — New Zealand's first MP who had been a refugee.
- Re-elected 2020 and 2023 (list #7 both times; contested Mount Roskill and Kelston).
- Stood aside from portfolios on 10 January 2024 and resigned effective 18 January 2024; replaced by Celia Wade-Brown.

## Record & notable actions

- **Electoral (Strengthening Democracy) Amendment Bill** (2019, drawn 2022): proposed lowering the MMP threshold to 4%, prisoner voting, donation caps, a foreign-donation ban, free Māori roll switching and voting at 16; defeated at first reading in September 2022, though several elements were later adopted in other legislation (foreign donation ban 2019, short-term prisoner voting 2020, Māori electoral option reform 2022).
- Co-sponsored, with women MPs from three other parties, the 2020 law strengthening the ban on female genital mutilation.
- Sponsored a 2021 motion recognising Palestinian self-determination (opposed by National and ACT; Labour declined to support).

## Controversies

- **Shoplifting (2023–24):** charged with stealing items worth a total of about $9,978 from boutiques in Ponsonby and Wellington, plus a fourth charge for a $389 cardigan from a Newmarket store. She pleaded guilty to all four on 13 March 2024 and was convicted and fined $1,600 plus costs on 27 June 2024, having been refused a discharge without conviction. She attributed her actions to "an extreme stress response" linked to previously unrecognised trauma, and apologised.
- **Tribunal defence work:** in 2017 it emerged she had worked as an intern on defence teams for accused war criminals including Radovan Karadžić and Simon Bikindi; she said fair trials required defence counsel and she would "do it again".
- **Comments on Israel–Palestine:** in 2019 she apologised after the NZ Jewish Council said a tweet calling Mary and Joseph "Palestinian refugees" denied Jewish connection to the land; in 2021 the Council criticised her comments on the Sheikh Jarrah dispute.
- In 2019 ACT leader David Seymour called her "a menace to freedom" over her views on hate speech, comparing such views to tactics of Mao, Stalin and Hitler; the comments were widely criticised across Parliament.
