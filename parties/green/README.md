## 1. Summary

The Greens are New Zealand's third-largest party. They have passed the 5% threshold at every election since leaving the Alliance in 1997. Their weakest result was 5.3% in 2005 and their best was 11.6% in 2023. They have helped form three Labour-led governments. Under MMP they have never joined, or given confidence and supply to, a National-led government. The Greens hold four values: ecological wisdom, social justice, grassroots democracy and non-violence. They also accept Te Tiriti o Waitangi as the country's founding document.

The Greens have had two spells with ministers, in 2017–23. Their biggest achievement was the **Zero Carbon Act (2019)**, along with a cannabis referendum and a range of climate, housing and welfare measures. The cost was a run of compromises their own members disliked. Examples are the 2018 vote for NZ First's waka-jumping law and the 2020 Green School grant (see the [track record](./track-record.md)).

In opposition since 2023, the Greens have:
- polled at record highs, up to 16% in 2026;
- moved their economic message towards cost of living and wealth taxes;
- been hit by a run of MP scandals and departures, more than any other caucus this term.

## 3. Values: the Charter

The Green Charter has four principles:
- **ecological wisdom**
- **social justice**
- **appropriate decision-making** (grassroots democracy; the wording follows secondary sources, which call this principle "grassroots democracy")
- **non-violence**

The party also accepts Te Tiriti o Waitangi as New Zealand's founding document and recognises Māori as tangata whenua. Members disagree about whether the Charter calls for eco-socialism or a broader "ecological economics". This shows up as a long-running tension between the party's pragmatic wing (Norman's "green growth", Shaw's 2017 Budget Responsibility Rules) and its left wing (the GreenLeft network, and former MPs Sue Bradford and Catherine Delahunty). In 2025–26 The Spinoff and other commentators described the economic platform as a "dramatic break" with the Shaw era.

## 4. Co-leadership model and co-leaders

The party has six equal office-holders: two parliamentary co-leaders, two party co-convenors and two policy co-convenors. **Since May 2022** the co-leaders must be one woman and one person of any gender, and at least one must be Māori. Before that the rule was one man and one woman. Members' delegates confirm the co-leaders every year at the AGM. A 25% vote to reopen nominations triggers a contest, which is what happened to Shaw in July 2022.

## 5. In government, 2017–2023

**Confidence & supply (October 2017 to 2020).** The Greens supported the Labour–NZ First coalition from outside it.

| Minister | Role |
|---|---|
| James Shaw | Minister for Climate Change; Minister of Statistics; Associate Minister of Finance |
| Julie Anne Genter | Minister for Women; Associate Minister of Health; Associate Minister of Transport |
| Eugenie Sage | Minister of Conservation; Minister for Land Information; Associate Minister for the Environment |
| Jan Logie | Parliamentary Under-Secretary to the Minister of Justice (domestic and sexual violence) |

Items in the agreement included:
- a Zero Carbon Act and an independent Climate Change Commission;
- a cannabis referendum by 2020;
- a "Green Transport Card";
- rail and cycling investment, and light rail to Auckland Airport;
- more funding for the Department of Conservation;
- an end to "excessive" benefit sanctions;
- closing the gender pay gap;
- a rent-to-own scheme within KiwiBuild;
- re-establishing the Mental Health Commission.

The agreement also obliged the Greens to let the Labour–NZ First coalition deal be implemented. That is why they voted for the waka-jumping bill.

**Cooperation agreement (31 October 2020 to 2023).** Labour governed alone with a majority. Two Green ministers sat outside Cabinet:
- James Shaw: Minister for Climate Change, and Associate Minister for the Environment (biodiversity);
- Marama Davidson: Minister for the Prevention of Family and Sexual Violence, and Associate Minister of Housing (homelessness).

The Greens also got one select-committee chair and one deputy-chair role. Former MPs Russel Norman, Sue Bradford and Catherine Delahunty said the deal gave the party too little leverage.

What the Greens actually delivered in these terms, and where they fell short, is assessed in the [track record](./track-record.md).

## 6. Caucus departures and discipline, 2023–2026

The Greens have lost or disciplined more MPs this term than any other party. In date order, with sources:

- **Elizabeth Kerekere** (list MP 2020–23). In April 2023 the party opened an investigation after she called Swarbrick a "crybaby" in a leaked group chat. RNZ then reported bullying allegations from at least seven current and former staff. She **resigned from the party on 5 May 2023** before the process ended and sat as an independent. In her valedictory speech she accused Shaw and Davidson of an "epic failure of leadership" and of denying her natural justice. Māori, Pasifika and Rainbow members defended her.
- **Golriz Ghahraman**. Accused of shoplifting from Auckland and Wellington boutiques in late 2023. She stood aside on 10 January 2024 and **resigned from Parliament on 16 January 2024**, citing an "extreme stress response". She pleaded guilty to four charges (goods worth $9,978 in total) and was convicted and fined $1,600 in June 2024. Her bid for a discharge without conviction was refused, and she lost her appeal in October 2024. Celia Wade-Brown replaced her.
- **Fa'anānā Efeso Collins** died on 21 February 2024 during a charity run. Lawrence Xu-Nan replaced him.
- **James Shaw** retired in May 2024. Francisco Hernandez replaced him.
- **Darleen Tana** (list MP from 2023). Suspended on 15 March 2024 over migrant-exploitation allegations at her husband's e-bike business. An independent investigation by barrister Rachel Burt found she played a major role in the business and knew of the complaints before she became a candidate. She **resigned from the party on 8 July 2024** but refused to leave Parliament. Swarbrick said her conduct was "completely at odds with our party's values". The Greens then used the **Electoral (Integrity) Amendment Act 2018**, the waka-jumping law they had long opposed, to remove her. 185 delegates agreed by consensus on 17 October 2024, and the Speaker declared her seat vacant on **22 October 2024**. This was the first expulsion under the 2018 Act. Three senior Pasifika members resigned in protest, saying Tana and Kerekere had been mistreated. Benjamin Doyle replaced her.
- **Julie Anne Genter**. On 1 May 2024 she crossed the chamber and shouted "Read the report!" at minister Matt Doocey. On **1 August 2024 she was found in contempt**, censured and ordered to apologise. The party called her conduct "unacceptable". Wellington business owners also made separate complaints about confrontations over cycleways.
- **Benjamin Doyle** (Parliament's first non-binary MP, from November 2024). In early 2025 they became the target of a campaign over a 2023 Instagram post. Doyle reported death threats against them and their child, and Police charged more than 10 people. Doyle **resigned effective 3 October 2025**, citing safety, and called Parliament "hostile and toxic". Mike Davidson replaced them.
- **Tamatha Paul** (MP for Wellington Central). In March 2025 she said "police beat patrols" made some communities feel less safe, and in a TikTok video she said most prisoners were jailed for non-violent, poverty-related offences. She later said she regretted the prison comment. Labour's Hipkins called her police comments "ill-informed… stupid" and the Prime Minister also criticised them. She stood by her police comments. She is No. 4 on the 2026 list.
- **Chlöe Swarbrick**:
  - December 2023: refused, then gave, an apology for calling a Prime Ministerial statement a "demonstrable lie".
  - 12–13 August 2025: ordered out for saying "If we find 6 of 68 Government MPs with a spine…" during a Palestine debate. She refused to apologise and was "named", which meant a suspension with docked pay.
  - 4 August 2026: ejected for accusing the Speaker of "double standards" over Winston Peters telling Green MP Lawrence Xu-Nan to "go back to your own country".
- **Marama Davidson**. In 2023, as a minister, she said at a protest that "white cis men" cause violence, then clarified the remark. She was ejected in June 2023 for accusing ACT of racist questioning. She was treated for breast cancer from June 2024 and returned at Waitangi in February 2025.
- **NZ First remarks about migrant-background Green MPs**, 2025–26. Targets included Ricardo Menéndez March ("sending Mexicans home"), Xu-Nan, Hernandez and Teanau Tuiono. The Prime Minister called Peters' July 2026 remark racist but kept him as Foreign Minister.
- **October 2026: Mongrel Mob meeting**. Green MP Hūhana Lyndon and Te Pāti Māori MPs met Mongrel Mob members at a South Auckland election meeting and were photographed with them. National attacked the meeting, and Newsroom said it could put the Greens' poll gains at risk. The Greens were "unapologetic".

Not every episode above involves wrongdoing. Doyle, Collins and the NZ First remarks do not. They are listed because together they shaped the caucus this term. Integrity issues measured against Green values are discussed in the [track record](./track-record.md#5-actions-vs-values).

## 9. 2026 polling

In polls from August to October 2026 the Greens ranged from about **12% to 16%**. 1News–Verian had them at 16% on 23–27 September 2026, which it called their best result ever. The last 1News–Verian poll before this snapshot (15–19 October) also had them on 16%. Other recent results:
- Freshwater Strategy (9–19 October): 14.3%
- Taxpayers' Union–Curia (1–5 October): 13.3%
- RNZ–Reid Research (24 September to 1 October): 14.8%

On these numbers the Greens would get roughly 17–20 seats (our estimate). With Labour, the left bloc is roughly level with the governing coalition, and the Opportunity Party is often described as the possible kingmaker. Full series: [`data/polls-2026.json`](../../data/polls-2026.json).

## 10. 2026 pitch and headline promises

The campaign slogan is **"For All of Us"**. The Greens' manifesto was launched at its AGM in Auckland in July 2026. It combines climate and environment policy with cost-of-living policies funded by taxing the very wealthy and corporations to pay for a "floor" of food, shelter and jobs. Headline commitments:

- **Tax**:
  - 2.5% tax on net wealth over $10m, plus an inheritance tax on large estates;
  - 33% company rate for the biggest corporations;
  - new top bracket above $160,000;
  - first $10,000 of income tax-free.
- **Housing**: cap annual rent increases at 2%; a rental warrant of fitness; tens of thousands of new public homes.
- **Cost of living**: "KiwiMart", a government-owned supermarket chain; restore and expand free school lunches; fare-free public transport for young people, disabled people and Community Services Card holders, with a $2 adult fare cap.
- **Energy and climate**: "Kiwipower", a Crown entity investing in renewables; "electrify everything"; repeal the Fast-track Approvals Act and revoke the mining consents granted under it; a one-year pause on new AI data centres.
- **Water and oceans** ("Drink, Swim, Fish"): phase out synthetic nitrogen fertiliser, reduce stock numbers, protect 30% of the oceans.
- **Work**: restore pay equity ($4.5b for care workers); automatic union membership; abolish the training wage.
- **Transport**: $4b for intercity rail; removing Auckland level crossings ($2.5–3.5b).
- **Te Tiriti and te reo**: $200m for te reo Māori; a $2.4b marae resilience trust; entrench the Māori seats.
- **Democracy**: a $30,000 donation cap and no corporate donations; a lobbying register.
- **Foreign policy**: recognise Palestine and sanction Israel.

The full list is in [./policies/README.md](./policies/README.md).

## 11. Coalition stance

The Greens will only support a **Labour-led government**. In February 2026 at Waitangi, Hipkins, Swarbrick and Davidson announced that Labour and the Greens would work together during the campaign and in government. Te Pāti Māori is asking its voters to give their party vote to Labour or the Greens, so a centre-left government would probably need Labour, the Greens and Te Pāti Māori, and possibly the Opportunity Party.

Some sticking points are already public:
- Labour has ruled out supporting Te Pāti Māori's Te Tiriti Commission, which the Greens say fits their own policy.
- In 2020 Ardern ruled out a wealth tax.
- Labour did not adopt rent caps in 2017–23.

No sources found so far report any Green 2026 bottom lines.
