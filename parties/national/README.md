# New Zealand National Party (Rōpū Nāhinara o Aotearoa)

National is New Zealand's main centre-right party. It is the senior partner in the current coalition government with ACT and NZ First, and it has led a government six times since 1936, more often than any other party. Its 2026 slogan is **"Fixing the Basics. Building the Future."**

## History

- **Formation (1936).** United, which drew its support from city business, and Reform, which drew on farmers, had governed together in 1931–35 and lost heavily to Labour in 1935. They merged in May 1936 to oppose Labour. National's first leader was Adam Hamilton, followed by Sidney Holland in 1940 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_National_Party)).
- **First government (1949–57)**, under Holland and then Holyoake. It kept Labour's welfare state, and defeated the unions in the 1951 waterfront dispute.
- **Second government (1960–72)**, under Holyoake and then Marshall. This was a 12-year period of stable, broadly consensual government.
- **Third government (1975–84)**, under Robert Muldoon. It was economically interventionist: "Think Big" energy projects, and a wage and price freeze. It lost the 1984 snap election.
- **Fourth government (1990–99)**, under Bolger and then Shipley. It continued the free-market reforms begun by Labour's Roger Douglas: Ruth Richardson's "Ruthanasia" budgets, and the Employment Contracts Act. It formed the first MMP coalition, with NZ First in 1996, which collapsed in 1998. Jenny Shipley was New Zealand's first female Prime Minister (1997).
- **Opposition (1999–2008).** National's worst result came in 2002 (20.9%) under Bill English. Under Don Brash it recovered to 39.1% in 2005, campaigning on tax cuts and the "Orewa" race-relations speech. John Key became leader in 2006.
- **Fifth government (2008–17)**, under Key and then English. This was a minority government, with confidence and supply from ACT, United Future and the Māori Party. It steered the country through the global financial crisis and the Canterbury earthquakes, carried out a GST/income-tax switch, sold minority stakes in state energy companies, held the flag referendums, and returned the budget to surplus from 2014/15. See [Wikipedia: Fifth National Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fifth_National_Government_of_New_Zealand).
- **Opposition (2017–23).** In 2017 National won 44.4%, the largest party, but NZ First chose Labour. Four leaders followed in four years: Bridges, Muller, Collins and Luxon. The party slumped to 25.6% in 2020, its second-worst result ever.
- **Sixth government (2023–present)**, under Luxon. New Zealand's first three-party coalition under MMP, with ACT and NZ First. See [Wikipedia: Sixth National Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sixth_National_Government_of_New_Zealand) and the [track record](track-record.md).

## Ideology and factions

National's constitution lists these values: loyalty to country and Sovereign; "recognition of the Treaty of Waitangi as the founding document of New Zealand"; national and personal security; equal citizenship and opportunity; individual freedom and choice; personal responsibility; competitive enterprise; limited government; strong families; and sustainable development ([Wikipedia summary of party principles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_National_Party)).

National is usually described as a **broad church**:
- a **conservative** wing, with rural, provincial and socially conservative members;
- a **liberal** wing, both economic (classical-liberal) and social;
- smaller **populist** and **libertarian** tendencies.

The party has a free conscience vote on moral issues. Its MPs split on same-sex marriage, and mostly opposed the Abortion Legislation Act 2020. The Key government was relatively centrist. Commentators describe the Luxon government as more right-leaning: welfare sanctions, deregulation, reversing many Labour-era Treaty and co-governance measures, and a return to oil and gas exploration.

## Organisation

- National has two parts. The party organisation (members, run by an elected Board of Directors and a president) and the parliamentary caucus, which elects the leader.
- The annual conference sets policy and elects board members. Each electorate committee sends delegates.
- Membership was about 200,000 in the mid-1970s and had fallen below 30,000 by the early 2000s ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_National_Party)). No recent membership figure has been published.
- National has led New Zealand party fundraising in every recent year (see Quick facts). In 2023 its disclosed donors included Graeme Hart's Rank Group ($400,000) ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/election-2023/498251/billionaire-graeme-hart-s-700k-in-donations-to-right-wing-parties)). Donations under the $5,000 disclosure threshold are not itemised ([Spinoff](https://thespinoff.co.nz/politics/08-05-2026/three-and-a-bit-years-and-50m-of-political-donations-visualised)).

## Periods in government and arrangements

**Fifth National Government (19 Nov 2008 – 26 Oct 2017)**
- National governed as a minority, with confidence-and-supply agreements with ACT, United Future and the Māori Party in all three terms. A memorandum of understanding with the Greens in 2009 lapsed in 2011.
- Support-party leaders held ministerial posts outside Cabinet: Rodney Hide and Heather Roy (ACT), Tariana Turia and Pita Sharples (Māori Party), and Peter Dunne (United Future).
- Source: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fifth_National_Government_of_New_Zealand).

**Sixth National Government (27 Nov 2023 – present)**
- **Structure.** National signed two separate coalition agreements, one with ACT and one with NZ First.
- **Deputy Prime Minister.** The role was split: Winston Peters held it until 31 May 2025, then David Seymour.
- **Cabinet.** 13 National ministers, 3 from ACT and 3 from NZ First.
- **What National gave up.** Its foreign-buyer tax and its plan to raise the super age to 67, both conceded to NZ First.
- **What National took on from its partners:**
  - from ACT: faster restoration of interest deductibility, the Treaty Principles Bill to select committee, the Regulatory Standards Bill, and charter schools;
  - from NZ First: the Regional Infrastructure Fund, the English-language law, an expanded COVID inquiry, and moving fees-free to the final year.
- **"Agree to disagree".** Clauses in the agreements let partners vote against government bills. They have been used several times: ACT on the Fair Digital News Bargaining bill and the under-16 social media ban; NZ First on new residence pathways and the India free trade agreement.
- Sources: [1News coalition explainer](https://www.1news.co.nz/2023/11/25/nationals-coalition-deals-which-policies-made-the-cut/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sixth_National_Government_of_New_Zealand).

## The 2026 campaign: pitch and headline promises

National launched its campaign in Auckland on 27 September 2026. Luxon's "personal commitment" list ([National](https://www.national.org.nz/news/260927-christopherluxon-1)) was:

1. Economic growth "that delivers new jobs and higher wages"
2. **No new taxes**
3. **Compulsory KiwiSaver**, with higher contributions and a **$1,500 Baby Boost**
4. **Paid parental leave** extended from 26 to 30 weeks
5. Better school results, by **replacing NCEA**, with regular assessment and "teaching the basics brilliantly"
6. Tougher consequences for criminals, and more police patrols
7. Faster healthcare and more medicines, on top of 33 new cancer medicines already funded
8. Better infrastructure: Roads of National Significance, new classrooms, and new and upgraded hospitals
9. A better deal at the supermarket, through more competition (including structural separation of Foodstuffs)
10. **Books in surplus**: an OBEGALx surplus in 2028/29, debt below 40% of GDP, and spending moving towards 30% of GDP

Later campaign releases added more ([plan for young adults](https://www.national.org.nz/news/20261023-young-adults); [Kiwi kids](https://www.national.org.nz/news/20261021-christopherluxon); [retirement](https://www.national.org.nz/news/20261022-retirement)):
- a lower student-loan repayment rate (10%);
- wider 5%-deposit First Home Loans;
- a ban on social media for under-16s;
- keeping students at school to the end of Year 11;
- no screens in early childhood education;
- 20% of the Housing Flexible Fund ring-fenced for seniors' housing;
- an 0800 Seniors helpline;
- employer KiwiSaver contributions for over-65s from July 2027.

The pitch rests on National's first-term record and on attacking the alternative:
- **The record National claims**: tax relief "for the first time in 14 years"; 2.6% growth; ram raids down; school phone ban.
- **The attack**: a "Labour–Greens–Te Pāti Māori–Opportunity" coalition with "at least nine new taxes".
- **The independent picture** is more mixed. It is set out in the [track record](track-record.md): inflation was back up to 3.7% (year to September 2026), unemployment around 5.6%, and the deficit was still being closed.

## Coalition stance and bottom lines for 2026

- **Preferred partners: ACT and NZ First.** National's message is "three more years to finish the job" with the current arrangement ([National](https://www.national.org.nz/news/20261022-nicolawillis)).
- **Opportunity: ruled out.** Luxon ruled out working with Opportunity (TOP) on 27 July 2026, citing its tax plans, including a land tax and a universal basic income: "When you want to spend more, tax more, borrow more — we ain't doing business with you". His office confirmed this was a rule-out ([1News](https://www.1news.co.nz/2026/07/27/luxon-appears-to-rule-out-national-working-with-opportunity-party/)).
- **Te Pāti Māori.** In 2023 Luxon ruled out any coalition with Te Pāti Māori ([interest.co.nz](https://www.interest.co.nz/public-policy/121148/christopher-luxon-fends-friendly-fire-act-shutting-down-possibility-forming)). This archive found no 2026 statement reversing that. National's campaign material treats Te Pāti Māori as part of the opposing bloc.
- **Labour.** National does not present a grand coalition as an option. It has, however, passed legislation with Labour's votes: the India free trade agreement, the National Infrastructure Plan, the first readings of the RMA replacement bills, and a co-sponsored modern-slavery bill.
- **Stated bottom line: no new taxes.** This is the main condition National itself has set out ([National: no new taxes](policies/documents/policy-no-new-taxes.md)).
- **Friction with its current partners:**
  - NZ First has vowed to repeal ACT's Regulatory Standards Act;
  - NZ First opposed the India free trade agreement;
  - both ACT and NZ First oppose National's under-16 social media ban ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sixth_National_Government_of_New_Zealand)).
