## Full text (reading order)

Building the Future:

Mill Road

National knows that when Auckland succeeds,
New Zealand succeeds.
Auckland is New Zealand’s capital city of growth. It is
home to one third of New Zealand’s population and
contributes nearly 40% to our national GDP. It has
higher labour productivity than the rest of New Zealand,
and is home to some of New Zealand’s most exciting
growth-industries, with 116 of our country’s top 200 tech
firms calling Auckland home.
We are not going to be successful in growing our
economy if we don’t think carefully about how we
enable Auckland, as our largest and most important
city, to thrive. If it is congested and hard to get around,
the whole country pays for it through increased prices
being passed on to consumers.
Under this National-led Government, Auckland has
started moving again. But there is more work to do.
We have to keep investing in the state highway network
that our growing city depends on.

Building on National’s investments in
Auckland
National has already put the foundations in place to
support Auckland’s growth.
•

We scrapped Auckland’s Regional Fuel Tax on
30 June 2024, taking 11.5 cents a litre off the price
of petrol and diesel for Auckland motorists.

•

Invested in critical new Roads of National
Significance in Northland and Waikato, to improve
regional connectivity for people and freight heading
to and from Auckland.

•

The City Rail Link is open - National funded it,
and National has seen it completed. The CRL
will fundamentally change the way Auckland’s
rail network operates, with faster journeys, more
frequent services, and better connections across
the city.

Authorised by J de Joux, ps@national.org.nz

•

We have delivered the reforms to Auckland
transport governance restoring democracy and
accountability to transport in our largest city and
laying the foundations for a joint Crown/Council
30-year transport plan.

•

The Third Main Line, electrification to Pukekohe,
and new stations at Drury and Paerātā are now
open, with a third to open at Ngākōroa next year.
That means more reliable train journeys, and rail
access for the fastest-growing communities in
the south.

•

We are also investing in the wider rapid transit
network, so Aucklanders can get to where they
need to go faster and more reliably, with the $1.4
billion Eastern Busway under construction, and
the Northwestern Busway has been progressed
through fast-track.

•

Funded the acceleration of level crossing removals
in Takanini and Glen Innes, improving safety
for pedestrians and efficiency for motorists by
unlocking the full benefits of City Rail Link.

A re-elected National Government will:
•

Fund construction of Mill Road Stage One,
between Manukau and Alfriston.

•

Progress route protection work on Mill Road
Stages Two and Three, between Alfriston
and Drury.

Mill Road Stage One (Manukau to
Alfriston)

Mill Road Stages Two and Three
(Alfriston to Drury)

South Auckland is one of the fastest-growing parts of the
country, and Drury is at the centre of it. But the roading
network hasn’t kept up. People travelling between
Drury, Papakura and Manukau are funnelled onto an
already congested Southern Motorway, and roads like
Redoubt Road and Mill Road were built for a semi-rural
community, not tens of thousands of new households.

National is committed to completing Mill Road all the way
to Drury. Stages Two and Three will connect the corridor
directly into the heart of the Drury growth area.

Mill Road will change that. Once complete, it will be a
new four-lane, 21.5-kilometre corridor running alongside
State Highway 1 from Manukau to Drury, crushing
congestion, improving safety, and unlocking land for the
homes and jobs the region needs.
Mill Road Stage One is one of the highest ranked
projects on the Government’s Major Transport Project
Pipeline. Published by this government, the pipeline sets
the order for major transport projects based on value for
money, and how quickly they can be delivered. Funding
this project, which has already been identified as ready
to build, is the responsible next step.
This project is also identified as a priority project in the
Auckland Regional Deal, as well as identified in the NZTA
Auckland Motorway Strategic Plan.
Stage One is expected to deliver:
•

a four-lane corridor, with a westbound bus lane at
the northern end.

•

two new and six upgraded intersections between
the SH1 interchange and Murphys Road, plus three
new roundabouts.

•

new bridges across Puhinui Creek and Cheesman’s
Bush.

•

a 30 percent reduction in congestion on the
corridor, 25 percent faster journey times, and a
more than 60 percent reduction in deaths and
serious injuries.

For too long, landowners along the route have lived with
uncertainty about where the road will go and how their
properties will be impacted. That isn’t fair on them, and it
holds back development in the area.
A National Government will allocate $100 million to NZTA
to progress route protection for Stages Two and Three
as soon as possible, giving landowners, developers
and Auckland Council the certainty they need to
plan properly.
Progressing route protection for Stages Two and
Three was a key commitment in the Auckland Regional
Deal. We will also continue to work with Auckland
Council, through the new Auckland Regional Transport
Committee, to confirm the timing of Stages Two and
Three in Auckland’s 30-year transport plan.

Fiscal impact
National will commit $1.75 billion of Crown capital
in Budget 2028, held in tagged contingency for the
construction of Mill Road Stage One (Manukau to
Alfriston).
$1.75 billion will be provided based on the 2025
NZTA Investment Case. Any additional costs will
be met from the State Highway Improvements Activity
class of National Land Transport Fund. This is the same
approach taken in Budget 2026 to fund Cambridge
to Piarere.
$100 million of Crown capital is being allocated to
progress route protection on Mill Road Stages Two
and Three (Alfriston to Drury). This cost is consistent
with recent NZTA funding approvals for comparable
Roads of National Significance.

It also demonstrates strong value for money, returning
up to $3.10 in benefits (untolled) or $2.20 in benefits for
every dollar invested (tolled).
National will fund Mill Road Stage One through a Crown
Capital contribution in Budget 2028.
