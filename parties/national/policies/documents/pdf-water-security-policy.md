## Full text (reading order)

Building the Future:

Securing water and fertiliser
to back NZ farmers
Reliable access to water and fertiliser is critical to
on-farm productivity. National will remove roadblocks
to new water storage infrastructure and reduce our
exposure to volatile global fertiliser markets.
New Zealand’s farmers and growers are the best in
the world. When they do well, the whole country does
well. The primary sector produces 82 per cent of
New Zealand’s exports and that money pays for the
hospitals, schools and roads every community relies
on. When farming grows, wages lift and our towns
grow with it.
In much the same way as New Zealand ensures we
have security of fuel and energy, security of water
and fertiliser enables our farmers to produce food
and generate the exports that are currently worth
$65.2 billion to New Zealand’s economy.
When farmers and growers have reliable water, they
produce more, take on more staff and more money
flows into the local town, which means more jobs in
regional communities.
National have an ambitious agenda for growth and
the world wants what we produce. In three years, we
have signed trade deals with India and the United Arab
Emirates and completed an agreement with the Gulf
states, and brought our deal with the European Union
into force early. The job now is to produce more of it.
National back our farmers to get on with what they
do best.

Authorised by J de Joux, ps@national.org.nz

Water Security
Water is one of New Zealand’s greatest strategic
advantages. It underpins our food and fibre production,
supports regional jobs and earns the export income
that pays for hospitals, schools and roads.
We get more rainfall and renewable freshwater per
person than almost any other developed nation. Yet too
many regions still face water shortages. We have the
water. We just don’t store enough of it. New Zealand
captures and uses about two per cent of its available
water resources. We can and should do better.
In our first term, National made water storage easier to
build. We gave water infrastructure 35-year permits, cut
compliance costs for small dams, opened the Primary
Sector Growth Fund to feasibility studies, passed the
Fast-track Approvals Act and appointed New Zealand’s
first Associate Minister of Agriculture responsible for
water security.
National believes we should plan for water security
the same way we plan for fuel and energy. That means
storing water when it is plentiful and using it well when
it is scarce. It means knowing where water will be
needed and giving regions a clear path to build the
storage they need.

Get schemes built
A re-elected National Government will commit $300
million in concessional loans to support water storage
projects to be built. The Primary Sector Growth Fund
will continue to provide support for pre-construction
studies for productive water storage projects.
Water projects can spend years moving between
hydrology, engineering, environmental assessment,
land access, user commitments, consenting and
finance. National want to see water storage projects
get the necessary approvals and get built quicker.
Our promise of concessional loan support will help
bridge the gap.

Behind those five, the register will build a longer,
nationally prioritised pipeline. Further priority areas
identified include:
•

Bay of Plenty orchards

•

Central Otago and Waitaki fruit growing

•

Upper South Island: Marlborough and Nelson

•

Southern dryland areas, where stored water is
the difference between a normal season and
destocking.

A proportionate approach to on-farm water
storage and irrigation

Deliver New Zealand’s first long-term water
infrastructure pipeline

We value on-farm storage and want more of it, built
well. A routine farm pond is not a high-consequence
hydro dam and should not be regulated as if it were.

New Zealand has many promising water infrastructure
proposals, but there is no single national view of
existing storage, future demand, climate risk or project
readiness. Work is often commissioned one project at
a time; information is gathered to different standards
and specialist capability is lost between projects.

National will make it easier for farmers and growers to
build water storage on their own land, by developing
a National Standard for on-farm water storage under
the new planning system. This will allow on-farm water
storage infrastructure to be built without the need for
costly resource consents.

A national water infrastructure pipeline will allow
connected storage, distribution, managed aquifer
recharge, renewal and on-farm options to be
considered together. Projects will be compared on the
value created per unit of water and per Crown dollar,
climate resilience, environmental outcomes, regional
benefits, affordability and readiness to proceed.

National will also make it easier to get consents
by introducing a National Standard for Irrigation to
ensure irrigation systems are designed and operated
to recognised best practice, improving water-use
efficiency, reducing environmental risk and providing
farmers with a simpler, nationally consistent set of rules.

We will start by prioritising five key regions
•

Northland

•

Tairāwhiti

•

Hawke’s Bay

•

Wairarapa

•

Canterbury

Each of these regions depend heavily on reliable water
for high-value food and fibre production. Each faces
growing pressure from drought and climate variability.
And each region already has storage work underway,
so Crown investment can get schemes built sooner
rather than starting from scratch.
The national water storage register will identify the
project/s in each of these regions to be prioritised for
funding from the $300 million in concessional loans.

A re-elected National Government will:
•

Commit $300 million in concessional loans
to get water storage projects built.

•

The Primary Sector Growth Fund will continue
to provide grants for feasibility studies.

•

Deliver New Zealand’s first long-term water
infrastructure pipeline, in partnership with
farmers, growers, councils and iwi.

•

Publish the first National Rural and
Provincial Water Use Strategy in 2027,
backed by a national water storage register.

•

Introduce a National Standard for on-farm
and other lower-risk off-stream water
storage, and

•

Introduce a National Standard for Irrigation
to make consenting easier for farmers and
growers.

Fiscal impact

Strengthening supply partnerships

The water storage concessional loan scheme will
be structured as four $75m tranches of lending, with
the write down cost of this lending managed through
the existing Primary Sector Growth Fund. The annual
write down cost of this lending is expected to be $16.3
million per annum. Since these loans are expected to
be repaid within ten years, we do not anticipate an
impact on the Government’s capital allowance.

New Zealand imports around two-thirds of its urea,
with significant volumes sourced from the Gulf,
particularly Saudi Arabia. Recent conflict and disruption
in the Middle East have shown how quickly shipping
risks can flow through to global fertiliser prices and
availability. Other key nutrients, including phosphate
rock, potash and sulphur, are also exposed to
concentrated production, commodity cycles and
long transport routes.

Critical soil nutrients
Fertiliser is a critical nutrient input into New Zealand’s
food and fibre economy. Reliable access supports soil
fertility, farm productivity, export earnings and rural
communities. A serious supply disruption would be felt
well beyond the farm gate through lower production,
higher costs and weaker export returns.

National will treat fertiliser as a strategic priority in
trade, diplomatic and investment engagement. Using
the New Zealand–Singapore Agreement on Trade in
Essential Supplies as a blueprint, National will pursue
arrangements with key supplier countries focused on
reliable access, market diversification, early warning
and rapid government-to-government coordination.

New Zealand already plans for fuel and energy security,
but as events in the Middle East this year has shown,
we need to be planning for fertiliser security too.

A re-elected National Government will:

New Zealand uses over two million tonnes of fertiliser
to grow food each year. Our fertiliser production relies
on imported phosphate, which is then processed
on shore. This leaves our primary sector exposed
to international supply shocks. National will reduce
New Zealand’s exposure by supporting domestic
production, strengthening supply relationships, and
attracting investment in strategic infrastructure.
This is about ensuring a continued, stable supply of
fertiliser in the country. We will back measures to support
farmers to responsibly manage their nutrient use to
better support productive farming and the environment.

•

Negotiate fertiliser security agreements
with key supplier countries, using the
New Zealand–Singapore Agreement on Trade
in Essential Supplies as a model.

•

Establish a $10 million Critical Agri Inputs
Fund to co-invest in fertiliser production,
processing, storage and distribution projects
from the Primary Sector Growth Fund.
