Building the Future: Our Plan for Hunting
and Fishing
Hunting and fishing are part of the Kiwi way of life. They put food on the table,
bring families and communities together, support regional jobs and tourism,
and connect New Zealanders with the outdoors.
Over this term, National has given hunters and fishers
a stronger voice in government and delivered practical
progress to improve access and game management.

Recognising valued introduced species
New Zealand’s wild animals are managed through
a fragmented collection of laws, agencies and plans.
Responsibilities are often unclear, while species valued
by hunters, anglers and local communities can be
treated simply as pests.
A re-elected National Government will establish a
coordinated wild animal management framework and
formally recognise valued introduced species in law.
This will include introduced species that provide
recreational, economic, environmental or cultural value
to New Zealanders. They will not be treated as pests
by default simply because they were introduced.
Management will remain practical and evidence
based. Action will still be taken where populations
cause unacceptable environmental damage,
but species will be managed according to
local conditions.

Strengthen the Game Animal Council and Fish
& Game
National will strengthen the Game Animal Council
as the statutory manager of valued game animals.
The Council will have a clearer role in managing
herds of special interest, developing national and
regional game animal management plans, and
supporting practical hunter-led conservation. It
will also help coordinate recreational, guided and
commercial hunting on public conservation land,
improving monitoring and reducing conflict between
different users.
National will also pass legislation to modernise Fish
& Game New Zealand. The reforms will simplify
administration, strengthen accountability to licence
holders and ensure resources are directed to the
regions, habitats and fisheries where they are
most needed.
Expand hunter-led conservation
This term, National delivered two herds of special
interest. This reflects that National believes that
hunters should play a greater role in managing
animal populations and protecting the places in
which they hunt.
A re-elected National Government will seek proposals
for additional herds of special interest, building on
the wapiti and sika models. The final selections will
be evidence-based and require strong local and
hunter support.
National will also replace the outdated Tahr Control
Plan 1993 with a modern management plan developed
alongside the tahr hunting community.

Protect recreational access
Access to public land, rivers, lakes and the coast
is fundamental to hunting and fishing. National
recognises this, this is why National launched an
Access Charter to ensure better access for recreational
hunting and fishing on public conservation land across
New Zealand.
A re-elected National will strengthen the Charter by
adding the Game Animal Council, Land Information
New Zealand and the Overseas Investment Office
as signatories.
We will change the current policy that generally
prevents recreational hunting on Crown land
managed by Land Information New Zealand.
Suitable Crown land that would ordinarily be
accessible to the public will be opened for hunting and
fishing under rules similar to those applying on public
conservation land. Existing arrangements for Crown
pastoral leases and other land that is not ordinarily
publicly accessible will remain unchanged.
National will also ensure existing recreational access
is protected when sensitive land is purchased by
overseas investors and improved where practical.
Growing hunting-related jobs and exports
Hunting supports the economy. guides, outfitters,
taxidermists, accommodation providers, tourism
operators and rural businesses throughout regional
New Zealand.
National will promote New Zealand’s premium
guided-hunting offer in key overseas markets and
simplify trophy-export permits, veterinary certification,
taxidermy requirements and customs processes.
National will also develop clearer rules for recreational
hunting, guided hunting, aerial hunting and commercial
wild animal recovery. These activities serve different
purposes and should be managed in ways that reduce
conflict and protect conservation, recreational and
economic outcomes.

Other initiatives to ensure as many New Zealanders
can go hunting and fishing as possible
Investigate options for sustainable funding of national
hunter training programmes, alongside the Firearms
Safety Authority, to help deliver the training and
education of the next generation of hunters.
Direct Fish and Game regions to review their existing
regional boundaries to better align with
the Government’s Resource Management Act and
local government reorganisation reforms, as well as
to resolve existing issues in cross-boundary
catchment management.
Partner with DOC and community organisations to
maintain and enhance access to public conservation
huts and facilities.
Give hunters and fishers a voice by replacing an
existing Conservation Authority and Conservation
Board seat with a permanent representative from the
hunting and fishing sector.
Ensure that the Outdoor Access Commission’s
public access maps are considered as part of the
Government’s investment into a new planning and
environmental online management system.
Simplify the consenting system for shooting ranges
to more accurately reflect a risk-based approach
and implement a pre-existing right to operate where
there is no ability to complain if someone moves to
the nuisance.
Pilot a Government-backed biodiversity credit
to recognise the environmental benefits of
hunter-led conservation.
