Building the Future:

National backs the Waikato
Waikato is New Zealand’s productive heartland.
It is where much of our food is grown, our energy
is generated, our exports are made and our freight
moves through. Hamilton and the surrounding
towns are growing quickly, while rural and coastal
communities depend on strong connections and
reliable services.
National is backing that growth with the infrastructure
Waikato needs for the future.
That investment is anchored by two major projects.
National is extending the Waikato Expressway from
Cambridge to Piarere with $1.773 billion of funding,
and building the new Waikato Medical School
in Hamilton, which will provide 120 additional
medical training places each year from 2028.
But the investment goes much further. We are
progressing Hamilton Southern Links, expanding
Hamilton Airport, strengthening transport resilience
in the Coromandel, adding classrooms and specialist
education capacity, increasing health capacity,
supporting more housing, and backing major
productive infrastructure across the region.
We also invested a record $1.9 billion into the
Waikato’s roads through the 2024-27 National
Land Transport Fund, ensuring the region’s roads
are up to standard. Construction has also begun
on replacing the SH25 Pepe Stream bridge, the
SH25 Ramarama Stream Bridge and the SH27
Ohinekaua Stream bridge – improving the resilience
of the wider Waikato state highway network and
keeping local communities, freight and emergency
services moving.

Authorised by J de Joux, ps@national.org.nz

We are building the housing our most vulnerable in the
Waikato needs, with more than 900 additional social
houses being delivered in the Waikato under National.
We are also working to make Waikato communities
safer. Ram raids have fallen sharply from their peak,
theft and robbery are down, Police foot patrols have
more than doubled, and tougher laws are giving Police
stronger tools to target gangs and repeat offenders.
There is more to do. National will keep building the
infrastructure Waikato needs to grow, while making
sure families, businesses and communities can share
in the benefits..

A re-elected National Government will:
• Deliver Cambridge to Piarere, creating a
safer, faster and more reliable connection
through one of New Zealand’s most important
economic corridors.
• Continue to progress Hamilton Southern
Links, and further explore options for a staged
delivery of this important project, so some of
the benefits can be felt, sooner.
• Deliver the Waikato Medical School, building
a stronger pipeline of doctors for rural and
regional New Zealand.
• Keep investing across the region, in transport,
health, education, housing and productive
infrastructure.
• Keep Waikato communities safer, with visible
Police, tougher consequences for repeat
offenders and continued pressure on gangs
and retail crime.

Two big builds for Waikato’s future
Waikato is growing, and National is investing in the
infrastructure the region needs for decades to come.
At the heart of that programme are two major projects:
extending the Waikato Expressway from Cambridge
to Piarere and building New Zealand’s new medical
school in Hamilton.
Together, they will strengthen the connections and
workforce that underpin Waikato’s future growth.

Extending the Waikato Expressway
National is getting on with the next major extension
of the Waikato Expressway.
Budget 2026 invested $1.773 billion in the Cambridge to
Piarere Road of National Significance, a new 16-kilometre,
four-lane expressway from the end of the existing Waikato
Expressway to the SH1/SH29 intersection at Piarere.
This is one of New Zealand’s most important freight and
economic corridors, connecting Auckland, Waikato and
the Bay of Plenty with the central and lower North Island.
Early works for the project began in January, both major
construction contracts are now in procurement, and
the first phase of construction is expected to begin in
early 2027.

University of Waikato, backed by $82.85 million from
the Government and more than $150 million from the
University and philanthropic partners.
From 2028, the school will provide 120 additional
medical training places every year, with a strong focus
on primary care and rural health. Students will spend
their first year studying in Hamilton before undertaking
three years of clinical training across regional and
rural communities.
In Waikato, that training will stretch from Waikato Hospital
to North Waikato, Hauraki and Thames-Coromandel,
South Waikato, Waipā and the King Country. That
means more doctors training in the communities that
need them, helping build a stronger pipeline of GPs and
frontline clinicians for rural and regional New Zealand.
National is also planning ahead for the wider region’s
future health needs. A 13-hectare site has now been
secured at Drury for a future major health facility,
following work specifically identifying the rapid
growth of South Auckland and north Waikato.
Combined with the new Medical School, this is about
building health capacity ahead of growth rather than
waiting for existing services to come under even
more pressure.

For Waikato families this means safer, faster, and
more reliable journeys. For farmers, manufacturers
and exporters it means a stronger connection to ports,
markets and customers.

Building New Zealand’s new medical school

• Construction underway on the Waikato
Medical School, creating 120 additional doctor
training places every year from 2028.

National is also making one of the biggest investments
in New Zealand’s rural and regional health workforce in
a generation.
National campaigned on the Waikato Medical School
in 2023, after the previous Labour Government

extending the Waikato Expressway with
16 kilometres of safer, four-lane highway.

• A stronger rural health workforce, with
medical students training throughout Waikato,
and rural and regional New Zealand.
• Planning ahead for future hospital capacity,
with land secured at Drury to support the
long-term health needs of South Auckland
and north Waikato.

Building across Waikato
A productive region needs infrastructure that can
keep up.
Across Waikato, National is upgrading key transport
links, expanding health and education capacity, and
backing the infrastructure that supports more homes,
jobs and investment. From Hamilton to the Coromandel
and the King Country, we are making sure the region
has what it needs to keep moving forward.

Connecting the region
• Progressing Hamilton Southern Links, a major
future transport connection linking the Waikato
Expressway at Tamahere with western Hamilton
and opening up access to new housing, businesses
and Hamilton Airport. The route has been designated,
the investment case was endorsed by the new NZTA
board in 2025, and initial site investigations have
been completed.
• Expanding Hamilton Airport, supporting a
255-metre runway extension, apron and turning-bay
expansion and upgraded lighting, strengthening
Waikato’s ability to move people and freight.
• Began construction of bridge replacements: SH25
Pepe Stream bridge, the SH25 Ramarama Stream
Bridge and the SH27 Ohinekaua Stream bridge,
supporting a more resilient transport network
for Waikato.
• Delivered a new Road of Regional significance in
the Waikato, improving safety and reliability at the
State Highway 1 and State Highway 29 intersection,
building a new roundabout to replace the former
T-intersection.

• Fixing the roads Waikato already has, including the
accelerated SH1 maintenance programme between
Tīrau and Waiouru, which has rebuilt or resurfaced
more than 120 lane kilometres of state highway.
• Extended rail connectivity from Hamilton to Auckland,
with NZTA extending the Te Huia trial by 12 months
until June 2027.

Infrastructure for growing communities
• Delivering more social homes, with 929 net
new social homes added across Waikato since
November 2023.
• Fewer Waikato families are living in emergency
housing, with the number of households falling from
564 in November 2023 to 120 in September 2026,
a 79 per cent reduction.
• Unlocking new housing in Hamilton, by approving
an Infrastructure Funding and Financing levy for
Te Awa Lakes, helping to deliver the water and
roading infrastructure needed for 1,500 new homes.
• Building more classrooms where rolls are growing,
including four new classrooms at Hamilton East
School, two at Te Wharekura o Kirikiriroa and four
more at Berkley Normal Middle School.
• Expanding specialist education, with four new
classrooms at Hamilton North School and new
satellite classrooms at Te Aroha College and Fairfield
Intermediate, giving more students with high and
complex needs access to purpose-built learning
spaces closer to home.

• Adding health capacity, with a new 28-bed
assessment ward being built next to Waikato
Hospital’s emergency department and due for
completion in 2027, Hospital in the Home being
expanded to support up to 40 patients receiving
hospital-level care in their own homes, 10 new
forensic mental health beds opened at Waikato
Hospital alongside eight community step-down
beds, a new 64-bed acute mental health facility due
to open shortly, and extra security staff in Waikato
Hospital’s emergency department around the clock.
• Bringing specialist care closer to rural communities,
with a new infusion service at Te Kūiti Hospital
reducing the need for King Country patients to
travel to Hamilton for cancer treatment, and the new
Waikato Regional Renal Centre at Waikato Hospital,
70 per cent larger than the old centre, providing 53
dialysis chairs for kidney patients across the region
centre, providing 53 dialysis
chairs for kidney patients across the region.

Infrastructure that grows the economy
• Backing local manufacturing, with Green Steel
at Hampton Downs approved through Fast-track.
The new plant will process around 200,000
tonnes of recycled steel each year, strengthening
New Zealand’s domestic infrastructure supply chain.
• Unlocking billions in future exports at Waihi,
with the Waihi North project extending mining
operations and supporting significant new
investment and economic activity in the region.

• Future-proofing Mystery Creek, upgrading its
ageing water network to support Fieldays, which
attracts more than 110,000 visitors and generates
an estimated $213 million in Waikato each year.
• Supporting Ruakura’s growth, by progressing the
consenting and detailed design of a new four-lane
road at Ruakura, helping complete the Ruakura
Inland Port and opening up land for new homes
and businesses in Hamilton’s east.

Next, National will:
• Keep building the transport connections
Waikato needs, progressing Southern Links
and investing in safer, more resilient roads
across the region.
• Keep infrastructure ahead of growth,
delivering more classrooms, health capacity,
housing and essential services as Hamilton
and Waikato’s fastest-growing communities
expand.
• Keep unlocking productive infrastructure,
getting worthwhile manufacturing, energy,
mining and development projects moving faster.
• Keep investing across the whole region, from
Hamilton and the Waikato growth corridor to
rural communities and the Coromandel.

Law and order: safer Waikato communities
Waikato businesses and communities were hit hard by
the surge in ram raids, retail crime and gang offending.
National has focused on restoring consequences and
putting Police back into communities. Ram raids in the
Waikato Police District have fallen 79 per cent from
their 2022 peak, while between 2023 and 2025 theft
across the Waikato region fell 11 per cent, robbery,
blackmail and extortion fell 6 per cent, and burglary
fell 2 per cent. Police foot patrols have increased
106 per cent.
Retailers are also getting a more targeted response.
Waikato’s dedicated Retail Crime Teams laid 172
charges in their first six weeks, targeting prolific
offenders responsible for repeated harm to local
shops and businesses.
National has also banned gang patches in public,
introduced dispersal notices and non-consorting
orders, toughened sentencing for gang offenders,
and put nearly 1000 additional gang members
behind bars.

National will
• Keep driving down ram raids and retail crime,
targeting the repeat offenders causing the most
harm to local businesses.
• Keep Police visible in Waikato communities,
building on the 106 per cent increase in
foot patrols.
• Keep the pressure on gangs, enforcing the
gang patch ban, dispersal notices and nonconsorting orders, and putting more serious
gang offenders behind bars.
