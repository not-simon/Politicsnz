Building the Future:

SH1 Belfast to Pegasus Motorway
and Woodend Bypass
More New Zealanders are choosing to make
Canterbury home, drawn by affordable housing,
good jobs and a great lifestyle. National is committed
to supporting Christchurch and the wider region
to grow.

•

Began construction of the Second Ashburton
Bridge, keeping our promise to start it in our first
term. This is a key route for ensuring our people
and goods can get up and down the South Island
and needs to be future proofed.

Canterbury is the engine room of the South Island.
It is home to around 695,000 people, roughly one
in eight New Zealanders, and is our second-largest
regional economy, generating $55.5 billion of GDP
in the year to March 2025.

•

Invested $38.5 million towards the replacement
of Christchurch’s nearly 100-year-old Pages Road
Bridge, the lifeline and evacuation route for New
Brighton and Southshore, carrying more than
13,000 vehicles a day.

North Canterbury is at the forefront of that growth.
The Waimakariri District has more than doubled in size
since the early 1990s to around 70,000 people, making
it one of the fastest-growing districts in the country.

•

Began work on the SH1 Rolleston Access
Improvements project, making it safer and
easier to enter or exit Rolleston and providing
a less congested connection for freight into the
industrial area.

•

Successfully launched the National Ticketing
Solution in Canterbury, the first city in the country
to benefit from this $1.36 billion investment to
provide an easier and more convenient way for
passengers to pay for public transport.

•

Completed major roading improvements for
State Highway 75/Halswell Road in Christchurch
competed months ahead of schedule, increasing
resilience and supporting housing growth in the
southwest of Christchurch.

But the roads haven’t kept up. They face long queues
at peak times, a main street clogged with trucks,
and a poor safety record. Under this National-led
Government, the Belfast to Pegasus Motorway and
Woodend Bypass is finally moving after two decades
of talk, and if re-elected, the next National government
will fund it and get it built.

Building on National’s investments in
Canterbury
National has a proud history of delivering for
Canterbury.
•

Increased transport investment in Canterbury
to $1.8 billion, around 50 per cent more than
under Labour.

•

Funded upgrades to SH76 Brougham Street, one
of Christchurch’s busiest roads and the main freight
route to Lyttelton Port, carrying more than 45,000
vehicles a day. Construction began in July 2025.

•

Progressed Belfast to Pegasus and the
Woodend Bypass, allocating $127 million to preimplementation, property purchase and early
works. Early physical works are already under
way, and the project secured fast-track approval
in July 2026.

Authorised by J de Joux, ps@national.org.nz

National’s Canterbury Transport Plan
A re-elected National Government will:
•

Fully fund construction of the Belfast to
Pegasus Motorway and Woodend Bypass,
so NZTA begin to deliver this important project
for Canterbury.

•

Commit $800 million in our first Budget to
deliver the project, with construction starting
next year.

Belfast to Pegasus Motorway and
Woodend Bypass
The Belfast to Pegasus Motorway and Woodend Bypass
will extend the Christchurch Northern Motorway around
11 kilometres north, from just south of the Waimakariri
River to beyond Pegasus and Ravenswood.
This much needed road will boost economic growth,
reduce congestion, and improve safety. SH1 approaching
Woodend currently carries around 21,500 vehicles per
day, of which nine percent is freight. The traffic volume
is expected to reach 28,000 vehicles per day by 2048.
There were 280 crashes on SH1 through Woodend
between 2014 and 2023, with three fatalities and 25
serious injuries.
During peak times it can take up to 20 minutes to travel
through Woodend, and it’s difficult for people to access
the shops, or drop off and pick up children from the local
primary school.
This project will improve travel times, reduce congestion,
and improve safety. It includes:

Delivering this project has substantial benefits, including
up to 10 minutes time saving at peak. It is expected to
reduce traffic through Woodend from 21,500 vehicles
per day to 8,000, and a reduction in deaths and serious
injuries by 78%.
This project demonstrates strong value for money,
returning up to $2.30 in benefits for every dollar
invested.
Critically, it will also make the corridor more reliable for
people and freight travelling to and from Christchurch,
Christchurch Airport and Lyttelton Port.
For locals, the bypass means getting their town centre
back. With through-traffic and heavy trucks moved from
Woodend onto the new motorway, enabling a safer and
more vibrant main street.
Because of National’s work in Government, the project
is ready to go and is the next investment-ready project
on the Major Transport Projects Pipeline. We will fund
the Belfast to Pegasus Motorway and Woodend Bypass
directly in our first budget, allowing construction to
commence in 2027.

•

Widening the southern section of the existing SH1
from two to four lanes.

Fiscal impact

•

A new four-lane motorway bypass in the northern
section.

•

A grade separated interchange at the Williams
Street intersection with SH1.

National’s Canterbury Transport Plan commits
$800 million of Crown capital, held in tagged
contingency and released once the project is ready
to proceed.

•

Replace the Pegasus roundabout with an
overbridge and signalised intersection.

•

Kaiapoi Bridge seismic strengthening and widening.

•

Construction of new bridges over the Cam River
and overbridges at Woodend Beach Road and
Gladstone Road.

$800 million will be provided to deliver construction
of the Belfast to Pegasus Motorway and Woodend
Bypass, based on the 2025 NZTA Investment
Case. Any additional costs to be met from the State
Highway Improvements Activity class of National Land
Transport Fund.
