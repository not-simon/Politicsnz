Education Manifesto 2026

Building the Future:

Education Manifesto 2026
Our vision
Every child deserves an education that opens doors.
The knowledge to understand the world, the skills
to contribute to it, and the confidence to build a
successful future.
National’s vision is a world-leading education system
that raises achievement and gives every young
New Zealander the opportunity to succeed.
A child’s background, postcode, or learning needs
should never set the limit on what they can achieve.
We believe in high expectations for every child,
excellent teaching in every classroom, and additional
support when needed.
A great education is the foundation of opportunity
for individuals and of stronger economic growth and
prosperity for New Zealand.
That starts in the early years, with children developing
the language, skills and confidence they need for school.

Authorised by J de Joux, ps@national.org.nz

It continues through a clear, knowledge-rich curriculum,
effective teaching, regular assessment, and warm,
dry classrooms.
It means parents knowing how their child is progressing,
teachers having the training and resources to teach
well, and young people leaving school with credible
qualifications and pathways into further study, training
and employment.
Over the past three years, National has begun a
transformation of education. We have restored a focus
on the basics and backed teachers with the tools to
deliver them.
Encouraging results are beginning to emerge. Our
next term will build on that foundation: helping children
arrive at school Set Up for Success and giving them
More Time Learning once they get there.

1. Reforms to date
When National came into Government, too many
children were missing school or reaching secondary
school without strong foundations in reading, writing
and mathematics. The curriculum lacked clarity,
assessment was inconsistent, and teachers were too
often left without the resources and specialist support
they needed.
The warning signs had been clear for years.
New Zealand’s PISA mathematics score fell from

523 in 2003 to 479 in 2022 equating to a loss of
two years of learning for our 15-year-olds in 2022
compared to 15-year-olds in 2003.
Our response has been to put ambition, achievement
and evidence at the centre of the education system,
with a target of 80 per cent of Year 8 students at
or above the expected level in reading, writing and
mathematics by 2030.

Teaching the Basics Brilliantly
Our focus

What we have delivered or funded

Strong foundations

An hour a day of reading, writing and mathematics; structured literacy
and a new mathematics curriculum.

Practical classroom resources

More than one million mathematics books and resources, alongside
decodable readers, teacher guides and curriculum support.

Clear information for parents

20- and 40-week phonics checks and twice-yearly assessment in
reading, writing and mathematics for Years 3–8.

Support where it is needed

Nearly $750 million for the largest expansion of learning support in a
generation, alongside targeted literacy and mathematics intervention.

Better places to learn

Around $2.7 billion across three Budgets for school property, with nearly
1,600 additional classrooms and learning spaces fully funded.

Focused classrooms

Phones away during the school day, and end to open plan classrooms
and a renewed focus on attendance and learning.

A clear, knowledge-rich national
curriculum

Knowing how every child is
progressing

National has rebuilt the curriculum around the
knowledge and skills children should learn, year by
year. Internationally benchmarked content and clear
progressions give teachers, parents and students
greater certainty about what success looks like.

Parents deserve clear information about their child’s
learning. Teachers need to identify gaps early and
know what to teach next. National has made regular
assessment and clear reporting a core part of the
education system.

New primary English and mathematics content
was introduced from the beginning of 2025. We
have developed the wider Years 0–13 curriculum
and are supporting implementation with practical
teaching materials.

• Phonics checks at 20 and 40 weeks of schooling
to identify children who need additional reading
support.

Maths workbooks, textbooks and teacher guides, literacy
resources, science and music kits, and lesson-planning
support help teachers spend more time teaching.
A nationally consistent curriculum gives every child
access to a world-leading education, wherever they live
and whichever school they attend.

Transforming reading, writing and
mathematics
We have required an hour a day each of reading,
writing and mathematics in primary and intermediate
schools and mandated structured literacy, including
phonics.
Children are explicitly taught the building blocks of
reading, alongside vocabulary, fluency, comprehension,
spelling, and writing.
We have invested in teacher training, decodable books,
and classroom resources to make these changes
work. Structured mathematics gives children a clear
sequence of learning and the practice they need to
master essential skills.
For children who need additional help, we have expanded
structured literacy intervention and mathematics support.
Intensive small-group teaching in reading and maths,
together with writing and mathematics acceleration
programmes give students the opportunity to catch
up before gaps become entrenched.

• Twice-yearly assessment in reading, writing, and
mathematics for Years 3–8 provides a consistent
picture of progress.
• The free SMART assessment tool supports teachers
with marking and reporting, alongside the option of
using PAT.
• Early mathematics checks, including the Year 2
numeracy check and Year 5 multiplication and division
check, strengthen our focus on essential skills.
• The Parent Portal gives families practical guidance
and resources to support learning at home.
The Curriculum Insights and Progress Study now
measures reading, writing and mathematics annually
at Years 3, 6 and 8. This gives New Zealand a clearer
national picture and helps direct support to the
students that need it most.

Backing teachers and school leaders
National has refocused professional development on
literacy, mathematics and assessment, strengthened
expectations for initial teacher education, and invested
in support for aspiring and new principals.
We have expanded opportunities to train in schools
so more New Zealanders can learn on the job. From
2027, the School On-site Teacher Training programme
will offer up to 660 places, rising to 704 each year from
2028, with a focus on secondary teaching where the
need is greatest. Participants will receive a $20,000
grant from next year.

We are also helping with the cost of teacher registration
and practicing certificates through to 2028. Stronger
subject knowledge, an understanding of the science of
learning and practical classroom preparation will help
ensure beginning teachers are ready to succeed.

The largest expansion of learning
support in a generation
National has invested nearly three-quarters of a billion
dollars in learning support. High expectations must be
matched by the help children need to meet them.
• Learning Support Coordinators for all schools with
Year 1–8 students from 2028.
• More than 560 additional early-intervention teachers
and specialists, and learning support extended from
ECE through to the end of Year 1.
• Guaranteed, uncapped funding for every child
verified for the Ongoing Resourcing Scheme.
• 1000 specialist places at any one time provided by
the private sector to ease pressure on waitlists.
• Additional teacher-aide hours and training to
support children with behavioural, emotional and
neurodiverse needs – building to two million extra
hours every year from 2028.
• Funding for three new special schools and 142
specialist classrooms, alongside accessibility
improvements.
This expansion will help identify needs sooner, support
families earlier and give teachers greater access to
specialist expertise.

Warm, dry classrooms and better
value for school property
Doing the basics brilliantly also means providing safe,
warm, and dry classrooms. National inherited 352
school property projects without the funding to deliver
them and classrooms costing around $1.2 million each.
We commissioned an inquiry and changed how school
property is planned, funded, and delivered.
Across three Budgets, this Government has invested
around $2.7 billion in school property. Nearly 1,600
additional classrooms and learning spaces have been
fully funded, providing more than 23,000 additional
student places. In 2024, 583 classrooms were
delivered, 31 per cent more than in 2023.
Repeatable designs and offsite construction have
brought the cost of most classrooms below $650,000,
close to half the inherited cost. More than $330 million
saved through this approach has been reinvested in
school property.
Maintenance funding has increased by $880 million
over our first two Budgets, more than twice the
previous 6 years combined. We have raised the base
rate for five-year property funding by 50 per cent,
from $30 to $45 per square metre, and doubled the
minimum allocation for small schools from $45,000 to
$90,000 as schools enter their new funding cycles.
The New Zealand School Property Agency began
operating on 1 October 2026. Its dedicated board brings
commercial and construction expertise to building,
managing and maintaining school property, supporting
a long-term approach that gives school communities
greater certainty and drives better value for money.

Credible qualifications and pathways
for young people
National is replacing NCEA with qualifications that
parents, employers and tertiary providers can trust.
A clear senior secondary curriculum and stronger
subject-based assessment will give students the
knowledge and skills they need for their next step.
The new Foundational Award in literacy and numeracy
will be introduced at Year 11 in 2028, followed by the
New Zealand Certificate of Education at Year 12 in
2029 and the New Zealand Advanced Certificate of
Education at Year 13 in 2030.
Nine new industry-led subjects will strengthen
vocational pathways and connect school learning
with the world of work. We are also expanding Trades
Academies, with places set to nearly double to 20,000
by 2030. Young people should have rigorous, credible
options that match their strengths and aspirations.

Results: raising achievement
There is still a very long way to go, but the evidence is
beginning to show an encouraging change in direction.
Teachers and school leaders are putting the reforms
into practice, and children are starting to benefit.

Curriculum Insights: improvements in primary
mathematics and writing
The 2025 Curriculum Insights and Progress Study
found statistically significant improvements at Year 6.
The proportion meeting or exceeding expectations rose
from 30 to 36 per cent in mathematics and from 33 to
38 per cent in writing: gains of six and five percentage

points respectively. Reading results were broadly stable
but early phonices results in year one and two were
encouraging. These findings show both the progress
being made and why our work must continue.

Phonics: stronger foundations from the start
In the first year of national phonics checks, the
proportion of children at or above expectations after
20 weeks at school rose from 36 per cent in Term 1 to
58 per cent in Term 3 of 2025.
These checks help teachers identify children who need
support early and show encouraging progress in the
foundations of reading.

PISA: a narrowing equity gap
PISA 2025 shows New Zealand remains above the
OECD average in reading, mathematics and science, with
overall achievement stabilising after years of decline.
Most encouragingly, the gap between advantaged
and disadvantaged students narrowed in all three
subjects, driven by gains among students from the
most disadvantaged backgrounds.
Those students made gains equivalent to around a year
of learning in science, three terms in mathematics and
six months in reading.
These are the first positive results across the education
system we have seen in a very long time. While they
are only early indicators, we cannot risk losing this
progress. The reforms need time to embed, and
teachers need continued support to deliver them.
National will stay the course, build on what is working
and keep raising achievement for every child.

2. Our 2026 policy: Set Up for Success
Every parent wants their child to start school ready
to learn, ready to participate and confident about
the next step. The early years lay the foundations
for everything that follows. National’s Set Up for
Success policy will give parents and educators a
clearer, shared picture of the knowledge, skills and
developmental milestones children should be building
before school.

Clearer milestones and earlier support
National will establish clearer developmental
milestones throughout early childhood education and
require progress to be reported to parents. Families
should know what their child is learning, how they are
progressing, and how they can help at home.

Clearer expectations will make it easier to recognise
when a child needs additional help, provide that
support earlier and strengthen the transition from
early childhood education to school.

Where additional support is needed, children will
be connected with the expanded Early Intervention
Service. Earlier identification and a stronger handover
to school will help more children get the right support
at the right time.

A School Start Check for every child

More interaction, fewer screens

National will introduce a School Start Check, completed
in early childhood education before a child starts
school, or on school entry for children who have not
had one. It will help parents, early childhood educators,
and schools understand each child’s strengths and
where additional support is needed.

Technology has a place, but young children need rich
language, conversation, books, movement, and direct
interaction with adults and other children to build
strong foundations for learning.

The check will cover foundations such as oral
language, following instructions, recognising
their name, pencil grip and early letter formation,
interacting with others and taking turns, and growing
independence in self-care, including toileting. Children
with additional needs will be supported according to
their circumstances.

A re-elected National government will introduce:
• No screens or individual devices use in early
childhood education.
• Tight limits on screen and individual-device use
in Years 1–3, with age-appropriate limits for older
primary students.
• Make the writing component of twice-yearly primary
assessments completed on paper.
Set Up for Success will give parents greater clarity,
educators shared expectations and children a stronger
start to their school journey.

3. Our 2026 policy: More Time Learning
National will give more young people time to build
the knowledge and skills they need for a successful
future, and make the school calendar more consistent
for families.

Lifting school leaving requirements
National will require students to stay at school until they
complete Year 11 or turn 16, whichever is later.
Currently, students can leave school when they turn 16.
Many reach that birthday during Year 11, allowing them
to leave before completing Year 11.
For these students, this will mean staying until the end
of Year 11. Those who turn 16 in Year 12 will still need to
stay until they turn 16.
We are replacing NCEA, beginning in 2028, with new
qualifications backed by a clear senior secondary
curriculum and stronger subject-based assessment.
Our expectation is clear. Students should complete a
full Year 11 of compulsory science, English and maths,
and have the opportunity to gain the new Foundational
Award in literacy and numeracy.

The existing early leaving exemption will remain for
a very small number of students each year.
Strong literacy and numeracy skills support employment
and further learning. Completing Year 11 gives students
the opportunity to build those skills and gain the
Foundational Award.

Consistent school dates and more
time learning
National will set consistent school start and finish dates.
Parents have told us how frustrating different dates can
be, leaving families juggling childcare and taking extra
leave from work.

Building on our progress
Set Up for Success will help children arrive at school
ready to learn. More Time Learning will build on that
start, raising achievement, closing the equity gap and
giving every young person the opportunity to succeed
at school and go on to live the life they want.
