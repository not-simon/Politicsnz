## Full text (reading order)

Building the Future:

Fishing

The health of our ocean matters to every New Zealander.
It is part of who we are, provides us food, recreation,
well-being, and a place for families and communities
to connect.
For a family in Auckland, it might mean taking the tinny
out from Half Moon Bay on a summer morning to try
for a snapper. For a group of friends in Whakatāne
or Gisborne, it might mean heading out to get a few
kingis. For a fishing crew in Nelson, it’s their job and
their income.
Recreational fishing generates around $1.7 billion of
economic activity per year, the seafood industry’s
economic output is around $6.4 billion per year, and
the annual worth of the boating and marine industry is
around $3 billion.
We must strike the balance between environmental,
recreational, customary and commercial interests and
look after the long-term health of the whole ocean. We
need to understand the asset, manage it well, and hand
it on to future generations in better condition than we
found it.
In the last three years we have passed the Hauraki
Gulf / Tīkapa Moana Marine Protection Act 2025,
the most significant expansion of marine protection
in more than a decade. And we have delivered
five new marine reserves as part of the South-East
Marine Protection Network, Te Au Roa o Te Rakihouia
protecting 308 square kilometres of unique marine
habitat and sea life.

Authorised by J de Joux, ps@national.org.nz

We want fish stocks managed at healthy and abundant
levels, not simply kept above minimum sustainability
limits. Our decisions will be guided by the best available
science, data and evidence, while upholding property
rights and the Crown’s Treaty settlement obligations.

A re-elected National Government will:
1.

Protect the Hauraki Gulf – including a new
Marine Reserve off Waiheke Island, further
expand the Goat Island Marine Reserve,
and banning commercial fishing in High
Protection Areas.

2. Support recreational fishers – including
ensuring fishers have a seat at the table
in fishing reforms, and preventing the
commercial sale of Marlin.
3. Preserve our seamounts – including
reviewing bottom trawling with an aim to
increase protections from 32% to 50% of our
EEZ by 2040.
4. Invest in innovation – with a $20 million Fish
Tech Innovation Fund in partnership with the
seafood sector.
5. Take an inclusive and principled approach to
fisheries management reform.
6. Ratify the High Seas Treaty in the next term
of Government.

1. Protecting the Hauraki Gulf
The Hauraki Gulf is loved by many Kiwis. It is
a place to swim, boat and fish. It is also a vital
hub for tourism, transport and commercial activity.
It is central to Auckland’s identity and way of life,
and National understands the strength of
that connection.
Since coming into Government, we have passed
the Hauraki Gulf / Tīkapa Moana Marine Protection
Act 2025, delivering the most significant expansion
of marine protection in more than a decade by
tripling marine protection. The Act recognises the
Gulf’s importance to mana whenua, recreational
and commercial fishers, tourism operators and
communities across the Auckland region. It
expands two existing marine reserves, creates
five new Seafloor Protection Areas and 12 new
High Protection Areas.
The decision to allow bottom trawling in some
designated trawl corridors in the Gulf has been
controversial. While this has provided limited access
to the commercial sector, the widespread concern
of environmental groups and the recreational fishing
population has not gone unheard. So, we will review
that also. We believe a consistent approach to
protection of the Gulf is best for all users and that
removing exceptions to the original intent of the
protections is needed.
We have invested in removing the kina barrens to
help restore the kelp forests in the Hauraki Gulf, which
are critical to a healthy ocean. National will build on
these protections.

National believe the waters of the Gulf are best utilised
by those who fish, dive, swim, or collect kaimoana
recreationally. It is Auckland’s playground and the
centrepiece of some of New Zealand’s most iconic
aquatic events.

A re-elected National Party will:
•

Establish the Hākaimangō-Matiatia Marine
Reserve off Waiheke Island, a new 23.5km2
marine protected area in the Hauraki Gulf
Marine Park.

•

Further expand the size of the Goat Island
/ Te Hāwere-a-Maki Marine Reserve, home
to one of the most diverse marine habitats in
the country and an educational, scientific and
tourism hub with 350,000 visitors per year.

•

Ban commercial fishing in the Hauraki Gulf’s
High Protection Areas by removing the
exemptions for ringnet fishers.

•

Keep the ban on harvesting shellfish
and marine species from rockpools and
continue to impose higher penalties and
strict enforcement. National will keep this
ban in place as long as necessary for these
ecosystems to recover and won’t support it
being removed before then.

•

Develop a sustainable fisheries plan in
the wider Hauraki Gulf Marine Park in
partnership with industry and interested
stakeholders, review bottom trawling
corridors, incorporating new innovation and
methodologies and updated environmental
biomass data.

2. Supporting recreational fishers
Enjoying time on the water and catching a feed
is part of what makes us Kiwis. At least 600,000
New Zealanders fish recreationally, now and then.
We are fortunate to have a charter fishing industry
that drives local coastal economies, provides expert
access to deep-water fishing grounds, and makes
recreational boating accessible to everyday kiwis
and our tourists.
Too often we only identify serious problems once a
stock has already declined to a level where drastic
restrictions are required. National will work with
the wider fishing sector to investigate how we can
improve fisheries data in New Zealand. A more
proactive management system would mean that
emerging stock pressures should be identified
earlier, and management responses occur sooner.
As a result, fish stocks will remain healthy and
productive for future generations. Better information
would mean less risk of stock collapse, and more
certainty for recreational fishers, commercial
operators, iwi and our coastal communities.
National will protect the marine environment so
it keeps providing for recreational sport and
customary fishers.

A re-elected National Party will:
•

Rule out a licence for recreational sea fishing.
Fishing for a feed is a Kiwi right. We will not
introduce or require a licence or permit for
recreational fishing in the sea. Existing licences
for freshwater sports fish are not affected.

•

Support the Billfish Accord. National will
prevent the commercial sale of Marlin, so it
stays as a world-class sport fishery.

•

Increase penalties and enforcement,
including instant fines for those exceeding
catch limits. If we want our fish to be there for
our children and grandchildren, we all need
to take only what we need and stick to the
limits. Those who don’t follow the rules – no
matter whether customary, recreational or
commercial – should face consequences.

•

National will increase Honorary Fisheries
Officers to 200 to crack down on illegal
fishing and strengthen patrols and targeted
enforcement to catch offenders and protect
our fisheries. Higher fines reinvested into their
monitoring and enforcement training programs.

Honorary Fishery Officers have similar powers to paid fishery officers,
including the authority to search, question and seize. They also help educate
recreational fishers about the rules. Most spend more than 100 hours a year
in the role, and together they complete between 11,000 to 14,000 inspections
each year, around 40 percent of recreational compliance work.

3.	Protecting our Exclusive Economic Zone (EEZ) and seamounts
There are over 1,900 seamounts and seamount-like
features in New Zealand’s EEZ waters. They support
diverse ecosystems and rare species such as stony
corals, black corals, gorgonian corals and hydrocorals.
Over 70,000 people signed the Live Ocean petition
calling for greater protection for seamounts. National
will act to protect more of these biodiversity hotspots
in our EEZ through a targeted, balanced and scienceled approach.
Spatial management measures will seek to protect
habitats and ecological values that are vulnerable,
while avoiding unnecessary impacts on the value
of the existing fishing catch, and the Crown’s Treaty
settlement obligations with regard to the Fisheries
Settlement. Our proposed approach will retain the
flexibility to review settings as scientific understanding,
environmental conditions and fisheries change over
time rather than applying a blanket ban.
Transparent, reliable data is essential to public
confidence in how much fish is harvested and how our
fisheries are managed. We must balance commercial,

recreational and customary interests while protecting
this shared resource for future generations. Commercial
fishing is a significant contributor to New Zealand’s
economy, earning close to $2 billion in exports each
year and supporting 9000 jobs and incomes in
regional communities.

A re-elected National Party will:
•

Increase protection of the EEZ and preserve
of our seamounts with an aim to increase
protected areas from 32% to 50% by 2040.

•

Prioritise vulnerable corals and seamounts
for protection based on a balanced and
science-led approach.

•

Uphold the Crown’s Treaty fisheries
settlement obligations.

Seamounts and seamount-like features include seamounts defined as
submarine features greater than 1000m in height from base to peak, as well
as other submarine features, such as underwater mounts of lesser elevations,
including hills, knolls, banks, rises and peaks along oceanic ridge systems
greater than 100m in height.

4.	$20 million Fish Tech Innovation Fund
Commercial fishing is an important part of
New Zealand’s economy. It supports thousands of
jobs in coastal communities and provides high-quality,
locally caught seafood for New Zealanders and export
markets alike, with close to $2 billion in annual export
earnings. While some people catch their own fish,
most New Zealanders enjoy seafood from their local
supermarket or fish and chip shop. Ensuring the longterm sustainability of our fisheries is critical for both
coastal communities and consumers.

The Fund will be targeted towards better fishing gear
and practices, including reducing the impact of bottom
trawling. It will also target science and research into
biomass data to improve the responsiveness of the
Quota Management System.

Mitigation technology, properly designed and properly
adopted, can change outcomes. There is good
evidence this approach works. Since 1 October 2024,
New Zealand’s surface longline vessels have been
required to use hook-shielding devices or the “three
out of three” seabird mitigation measures: tori lines,
setting lines at night, and weighted lines. Seabird
captures across all fishing methods fell to 505 in the
third quarter of 2025–26, down from 767 in the same
period a year earlier.

Fish Tech Innovation Fund

National will establish the Fish Tech Innovation Fund
in partnership with the seafood sector, matching
dollar-for-dollar any investment from the sector,
up to $10 million.

By backing science and innovation and working with
the sector, we can improve environmental outcomes
while supporting a sustainable and productive
fishing industry.

Total fund

$20 million

Government

$10 million over four years (from the
Primary Sector Growth Fund)

Seafood sector

$10 million, matched dollar for dollar

Focus

Better fishing gear and methods, aiming
for less seabed contact and less bycatch

A re-elected National Party will:
•

Establish a $20 million Fish Tech Innovation
Fund with the seafood sector to support new
innovation that reduces seabed contact and
protects sensitive habitats.

5.	Sensible fisheries
management reforms

6.	High Seas Treaty and
International Agreements

National will deliver better ecosystem outcomes
through fisheries law reform by making sure our
legislation has the confidence of New Zealanders.
Fisheries management and the Quota Management
System must be fit for purpose and effective in a
changing climate.

Many of the fish stocks we depend on move across
national boundaries and into the high seas. That gives
New Zealand a strong interest in international fisheries
agreements that work.

National will convene a multi-stakeholder forum
to properly examine and test all aspects of the
proposed Fisheries Amendment Bill in order to
achieve a balanced and acceptable package of
fisheries reform legislation.

National support the following principles
for any fisheries reform:
•

Transparent data is critical to building community
trust in how much we harvest and how we manage
our fisheries.

•

Improving the effectiveness of the Quota
Management System should be central to the
reforms.

•

Commercial and recreational interests must be
balanced in a way that protects this asset for those
who come after us.

The High Seas Treaty, formally the Agreement on
Biodiversity Beyond National Jurisdiction (BBNJ),
came into force on 17 January 2026. It makes it
possible to set up marine protected areas in the high
seas, regulate the use of marine genetic resources, and
assess the environmental impact of current and future
human activity.
So far 103 Parties have ratified the Agreement and
145 countries have signed it. New Zealand has signed
but not yet ratified. A re-elected National Party would
ratify it.

A re-elected National Party will:
•

Ratify the High Seas Treaty in the next term
of Government.
