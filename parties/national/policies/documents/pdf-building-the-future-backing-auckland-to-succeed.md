Building the Future:

Backing Auckland to succeed
National is excited for Auckland’s future and, if
re-elected, will spend the next three years continuing
to back our largest city to grow and prosper.

Crime is also falling in Auckland. Between 2023 and
2025, burglaries fell 16 per cent, theft fell 11 per cent,
and Police foot patrols have increased significantly.

We share Aucklanders’ vision for a city where
families can get ahead, businesses can succeed,
and communities can be proud of where they live.

Fewer Auckland families are living in emergency
housing too, with the number of households falling
from 885 in November 2023 to 81 in August 2026.

Auckland is New Zealand’s largest economic
centre and home to more than 1.8 million Kiwis,
and contributes nearly 40% to our national GDP.

And household pressures are easing. Lower mortgage
rates mean an Auckland household with an average
sized mortgage could be paying more than $9000
less a year than under mid-2023 rates, while rents are
increasing at their slowest rate in around 25 years.

When Auckland succeeds, New Zealand succeeds.
Under National the economy is growing faster than
the US, the UK, Canada, and Australia. Exports are up
$20 billion, inflation is down from a 32-year high, and
220,000 new jobs are expected with wages growing
faster than prices.
We want to help Auckland capitalise on that opportunity.
National has a strong record of backing Auckland, from
the Waterview Tunnel and City Rail Link to the new
infrastructure and services being delivered today.
This term, we have opened the City Rail Link, funded
727 new classrooms, expanded cancer treatment in
West Auckland, restored 24/7 urgent care in Counties
Manukau, secured land in Drury for a future South
Auckland hospital, signed the Auckland Regional Deal,
and funded two Roads of National Significance that will
benefit Aucklanders.
And the results are starting to show.
In Auckland, regular school attendance has risen to
66.7 per cent, while 85.3 per cent of school leavers
now achieve at least NCEA Level 2 or above.
More patients are being seen sooner. The proportion
of Auckland emergency department patients being
dealt with within six hours has risen from 67.1 per cent
in 2023/24 to 77.3 per cent in 2025/26.

Authorised by J de Joux, ps@national.org.nz

There is more to do. Auckland is still growing, and
National’s focus is on building the infrastructure,
services and opportunities the city needs for the future.

A re-elected National Government will:
• Plan ahead for Auckland’s future, working
with Auckland Council through the Auckland
Deal to align housing, infrastructure and
economic growth.
• Build the infrastructure Auckland needs to
grow, with better transport, more classrooms
and health services that keep pace with
new communities.
• Back Auckland families with the cost of
living, by growing the economy, supporting
more housing and keeping pressure off
household costs.
• Keep driving crime down, with more visible
Police, tougher consequences for serious and
repeat offenders, and safer neighbourhoods
and public spaces.
• Keep lifting health and education
performance, so patients are seen and treated
sooner, more children are in school, and more
young Aucklanders leave with the skills and
qualifications they need.

Growth and opportunity: unlocking Auckland’s potential
Auckland’s future is not just about building more roads,
classrooms and hospitals. It is also about making better
use of the infrastructure we already have, removing
rules that hold the city back, supporting more homes
and jobs, and backing the places and institutions that
make Auckland attractive to live in, work in and visit.
National is making it easier for Auckland to grow in
the right places and take advantage of the investment
already going into the city.

Already delivering
• Unlocking more homes around key City Rail Link
stations, enabling greater density in the places that
make sense.
• Unlocked Auckland’s City Centre, progressing the
removal of planning restrictions that are holding the
CBD back, potentially allowing for 17,000 additional
dwellings and 37,200 more jobs.
• Opening Eden Park up to more major events, with
planning restrictions eased for more concerts, sport
and cultural events.
• Keeping water infrastructure affordable, saving
Auckland households an estimated $899 million
over four years while enabling record Watercare
investment.

• Delivering more social housing, with more than
3,000 net new social homes added across Auckland
since November 2023.
• Bringing more major events and visitors to Auckland,
with the New Zealand International Convention Centre
now open. Next year’s State of Origin match, alone, is
expected to bring in more than 50,000 international
visitor nights and $17.4 million in economic activity.
• Backing Auckland as a technology and innovation
hub, with the New Zealand Institute for Advanced
Technology launched at GridAKL.
• Scrapped the Auckland Regional Fuel Tax, taking
11.5 cents a litre off the price of petrol and diesel for
Auckland motorists.
• Reformed the planning system, ensuring that
Aucklanders can do more with their property without
having to battle red tape, which could remove the
need for 45% of resource consents.
• 13 Fast-track projects approved, including the
Auckland Port Expansion – creating thousands of
jobs and billions in investment.
• Invested $400m nationwide to support councils
with the costs of housing growth.

• Saving Auckland building owners around $4.5 billion
through earthquake-prone building reforms, removing
more than 1,300 Auckland buildings from costly
strengthening requirements that were disproportionate
to the city’s relatively low seismic risk.
• Securing the future of the ASB classic, contributing
$5 million towards a new centre court roof that will
protect play from the weather and open the venue to
more major events year-round.

Aucklanders know all about the access and safety
issues this policy has created. Some people are having
to walk 10 minutes or more to get home from where
they’ve parked. We’re committing to fixed it.

A re-elected National Government will:
• Abolish the blanket ban on minimum car
parking requirements.

• Brought the Michelin Guide to New Zealand for
the first time, putting Auckland’s food scene on
the global stage, with 46 Auckland restaurants
recognised, including five awarded a Michelin star.

• Allow councils to use minimum car parking
requirements in suburban areas outside those
identified for intensification (e.g. in city centres
or around rapid transit stops).

Next, National will…

This means councils will be able to set minimum
car parking requirements in many suburban
neighbourhoods, greenfield subdivisions, and
business and industrial parks.

National backs growth and wants Auckland to grow
further. But we know that some Kiwis are concerned
about what growth means for them and their streets,
especially in suburban Auckland.
The blanket ban on minimum car parking requirements
has not worked and does not account for the reality
of New Zealand’s suburban streets. Many streets,
especially in Auckland, are clogged and hard to
get through, and people are now parking on berms
and footpaths.

New homes should come with the parking
residents need – in the right places. National
will unclog our streets and make them safer and
more manageable, enabling our suburbs to be
more liveable.

Transport: connecting a growing Auckland
Getting around Auckland should not be one of the
hardest parts of the day. But too many Aucklanders
are still spending too much time sitting in traffic. For
families, that is time lost at home. For tradies and small
businesses, it means fewer jobs in a day. For exporters
and freight operators, congestion adds cost every time
goods have to move across the city.
Under this National-led Government, Auckland has
started moving again. But there is more work to do.
We have to keep investing in the state highway network
that our growing city depends on.
A re-elected National government will allocate $1.75
billion in Budget 2028 to start building Mill Road
Stage One, connecting Manukau and Alfriston, with
construction beginning in late 2028.
We will also invest $100 million to protect the route
for Stages Two and Three towards Drury, making sure
future governments can continue the connection south
as demand grows.

Already delivering
National has already made good progress on some
of Auckland’s biggest transport priorities, with major
projects completed, funded or now underway.
• City Rail Link is open, giving Aucklanders a faster
and more connected rail network.
• The Eastern Busway is underway, with funding
approved and construction progressing on the final
connection to Botany.
• Auckland is receiving a record $8.4 billion in
transport investment through the 2024–27 National
Land Transport Programme.
• The Auckland Deal has been signed, creating a
long-term framework for Government and Auckland
Council to plan infrastructure, housing and transport
growth together.
• Transport decision-making has been reformed,
restoring democracy and accountability to transport
in Auckland and laying the foundations for a joint
Crown/Council 30-year transport plan.

• Invested in critical new Roads of National
Significance in Northland and Waikato, to improve
regional connectivity for people and freight heading
to and from Auckland.
• Improved the rail network, with the Third Main Line,
electrification to Pukekohe, and new stations at
Drury and Paerātā now open, with a third to open
at Ngākōroa next year.
• Accelerated level crossing removals in Takanini
and Glen Innes, improving safety for pedestrians and
efficiency for motorists in South and East Auckland.

Next, National will
• Fund $1.75 billion to construct Mill Road Stage
One and $100 million to route protect Stages Two
and Three, progressing this critical new transport
connection for South Auckland.
• Take the next steps on Auckland’s future harbour
crossing, progressing the Detailed Business Case
and the work needed to determine how this project
can be funded and delivered.
• Complete the Eastern Busway through to Botany,
giving East Auckland a faster and more reliable
public transport connection.
• Progress North-West Rapid Transit, improving
public transport along one of Auckland’s busiest and
fastest-growing corridors.
• Progress CRL level-crossing removals, improving
connections and safety across South and East
Auckland and making the rail network safer and
more reliable.
• Deliver the Auckland Deal, including a coordinated
30-year transport strategy so Auckland can plan
ahead rather than constantly play catch-up.
• Explore options to speed up rapid transit projects
like the Botany to Airport Busway, through rescoping
and cost saving measures.

Law and order: keeping crime
coming down

What we’re doing next

Aucklanders should feel safe in their homes, walking
through their neighbourhoods, running a business,
heading into the city, or catching public transport.

The focus has been on getting Police back into
communities, strengthening consequences for
serious and repeat offending, and targeting the
places Aucklanders should be able to use without
fear of crime or intimidation.

For too long, too many Aucklanders saw crime
becoming more common and more brazen. National
has been focused on restoring consequences for
offenders, putting Police back into communities and
making public spaces safer.

National has also announced a dedicated Auckland
Transport Police Team within New Zealand Police,
adding a visible Police presence across buses, trains,
ferries, stations and interchanges and supporting
Auckland Transport’s existing transport officers.

The early results are encouraging, but there is much
more to do.

National will

Between 2023 and 2025, burglaries across Auckland
fell by 16 per cent, theft fell by 11.0 per cent, and
robbery, blackmail and extortion fell by 5 per cent.
At the same time, there have been an increase in
police foot patrols across Auckland, with a 203%
increase in Waitemata, 156% in Counties Manukau,
and 11% in Auckland City.
Ram raids have also collapsed from their 2022 peak.
Across Auckland’s three Police districts, recorded ram
raids fell from 348 in 2022 to just 14 in 2025, a 96 per
cent reduction.

• Deliver the Auckland Transport Police Team and
make public transport safer, putting dedicated
officers across buses, trains, ferries, stations and
interchanges to protect passengers and staff.
• Keep Police visible and focused on the places and
offenders causing the most harm, with a strong
presence in neighbourhoods, town centres and other
public spaces.
• Keep driving down burglary, theft and violent
offending, with tougher consequences for serious
and repeat offenders and a continued focus on
reducing the number of victims.

Frontline services: healthcare closer
to home
When you are sick, you should be able to get the care
you need quickly and close to home.
That means knowing urgent care is available when
your child gets sick at night. It means cancer patients
spending less time travelling across the city for regular
treatment. And it means making sure Auckland has
the hospital capacity, specialist services and health
workforce it will need as the city grows.
National inherited a health system from Labour where
too many Aucklanders were waiting too long.
In Auckland district, the proportion of emergency
department patients dealt with within six hours fell from
89.9 per cent in 2017/18 to 67.1 per cent in 2023/24,
elective treatment within four months fell from 92.5 per
cent to 65.6 per cent, and immunisation coverage for
two-year-olds fell from 94.0% to 76.3%.
But under National, that decline is starting to turn around.
By 2025/26, 77.3 per cent of Auckland emergency
department patients were dealt with within six hours,
elective treatment to 69.3 per cent, and immunisation
coverage for two-year-olds reached 88.2 per cent.
But there is still more to do.

National is already delivering
Improving the numbers matters, but so does what
patients can see and experience on the ground.
National is investing in more services, more capacity
and better access to care across Auckland.
• Cancer patients in West Auckland can now receive
more treatment closer to home, with infusion
services opened at Waitākere Hospital and Te
Whānau o Waipareira’s Whānau Centre in Henderson.
• Families in South and East Auckland once again have
access to urgent care 24 hours a day, after round-theclock services were restored in Counties Manukau.
• Land has been secured for a future South Auckland
hospital, with the purchase of a 13-hectare site in Drury.
• A new 30-bed spinal rehabilitation centre is being
built at Manukau Health Park, backed by $128.5
million of investment.
• Auckland City Hospital is getting upgraded stroke
and interventional radiology services, with new
investment increasing capacity and replacing ageing
equipment used in some of the most complex
procedures.
• Cardiac services are being upgraded across the
region, with catheter laboratory improvements
planned or underway at Auckland City, Counties
Manukau, North Shore and Starship.
• More care is being shifted closer to patients
through primary care, with a new Auckland and
Northland workforce hub supporting more than 400
GP clinics and training clinicians to provide more
procedures outside hospital.

Next, National will
The next step is to keep lifting performance while
building the health services Auckland will need for the
decades ahead.
• Reduce waits and improve outcomes for Aucklanders,
continuing to deliver National’s elective boost with
more hip, knee and cataract surgeries, alongside
a continued focus on emergency care, specialist
assessments and childhood immunisation.
• Catch cancer earlier and get more medicines
to Aucklanders, with a further $604 million for
Pharmac, free bowel screening from age 50, free
breast screening extended to 74, free cervical
screening for women aged 25 to 69, and new
prostate and lung cancer screening pilots.

• Build more care closer to home across Auckland,

services Auckland’s growing south will need.

Education: classrooms for growing
communities
When families move into a growing community, they
need to know there will be a place for their children
at a good local school.
That means making sure new housing is matched by
new classrooms, and that schools have the space and
facilities they need as rolls grow. It also means investing
in specialist education, so children with high and
complex needs can learn in an environment designed
for them.

Already delivering

• Secure land now for the schools growing
communities will need next, with land acquired for
a future secondary school in Kumeū and planning
underway, alongside funding to secure a 3.2-hectare
site for a future primary school in Drury East.
