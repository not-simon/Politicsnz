Building the Future:

National’s 47-point plan to
support Small Businesses

A.  Making it easier to employ and
train employees
National is making it easier for small businesses to
employ people and grow, with clearer rules and more
practical support for hiring and training.
We restored 90-day trial periods for employers of
every size, removed the 30-day rule so employers and
employees can agree individual terms from day one,
and introduced clearer contractor rules.
The Employment Leave Act has now passed, replacing
the Holidays Act from August 2028 with simpler
leave calculations and a two-year implementation
period. Health and safety reforms are also sharpening
the focus on preventing serious harm and making
obligations clearer for businesses.
Alongside this, skilled-trades residence, Earn as You
Learn manufacturing and industry-led school subjects
will help businesses find the skills they need and give
more New Zealanders practical pathways into work.

What National will do if re-elected…
Helping a sole trader become an employer
Taking on a first employee is one of the biggest steps
a small business can make. Overnight, an owner who
has spent years doing everything themselves becomes
responsible for payroll, employment agreements, leave,
tax, ACC and managing another person.
National wants more successful sole traders to have
the confidence to take that step.
We will introduce a First Employee Support Package
that brings the essentials together in one place.
This will ensure any new employer get the basics right
from day one and have somewhere to turn before a
small uncertainty becomes an expensive mistake.
We will also reduce penalties for genuine first-year
administrative errors where no worker has been
harmed and the employer moves quickly to put things
right. Workers’ pay, entitlements and safety protections
will remain unchanged.

Making repeat seasonal hiring easier
Growers, vineyards and other seasonal businesses often
bring back the same workers year after year. Both sides
may know the job and each other well, but still have to
repeat much of the same paperwork each season.
National will develop a standard recurring seasonal
agreement and a short renewal process so employers
and returning workers can confirm the next season’s
dates, terms and any changes without starting again
from scratch.

Each fixed term will still need a genuine seasonal
reason and existing employee rights will remain.

Making apprenticeship paperwork simpler
Employers training apprentices should be spending
their time teaching skills, not chasing paper records
and signatures.
National will work with training providers to ensure
approved digital competency records and electronic
supervisor sign-off are consistently accepted across
apprenticeships.
Building on systems already in use, this will make it
easier for apprentices, employers and assessors to see
what has been completed and what still needs to be
signed off.

Helping businesses prepare for the new
leave system
The Employment Leave Act is now law, but the new
system does not take effect until August 2028.
Employers must continue using the existing Holidays
Act rules until then.
Payroll systems will need updating, leave balances
converted and employers and employees will need to
understand how the new calculations work.
National will deliver that transition with early guidance,
worked examples and coordinated support for
employers and payroll providers.

National will:

2.	 Make repeat seasonal hiring easier with a
standard recurring seasonal agreement and
shorter renewal process, while retaining the
requirement for a genuine seasonal reason;
3.	 Make apprenticeship record-keeping simpler
with consistent digital competency records
and electronic sign-off; and
4.	 Deliver the transition to the new employment
leave system with early guidance, worked
payroll examples and coordinated
implementation support.

B. Backing businesses to grow
National wants to support small business to invest,
expand, and grow, so they can create more jobs and
contribute more to their communities and the economy.
Investment Boost is one of the biggest changes
National has made to support that investment. It is a
$6.6 billion investment in kiwi businesses. It allows
businesses to immediately deduct 20 per cent of the
cost of eligible new assets.
That could mean better tools or another vehicle for a
builder, new equipment for a café, or machinery that
allows a manufacturer to fill a larger order.
Early evidence suggests businesses are responding.
Inland Revenue found 40 per cent of surveyed
businesses that had invested and understood
Investment Boost said it had increased their
investment spending.

What National will do if re-elected…
Giving small businesses more room to grow
The compulsory GST registration threshold has
remained at $60,000 since 2009. Inflation has steadily
eroded its real value, meaning businesses are reaching
the threshold at a much earlier stage of their growth.

Crossing the threshold brings additional administration,
recordkeeping and pricing decisions, potentially
creating a hurdle for a business that is still getting
established.
National believes there is a strong case to look again at
whether the current threshold remains fit for purpose.
That’s why, in government, we will commission a
review of the GST registration threshold. The review
will focus on reducing unnecessary compliance and
removing barriers to small-business growth, while
carefully considering the fiscal cost and wider impacts
of any change.

National will:

threshold, examining options to reduce
compliance and remove barriers to smallbusiness growth; and
6.	 Keep Investment Boost, retaining the
immediate 20 per cent deduction for eligible
productive assets of any value and businesses
of every size.

C.  Resolving employment disputes
faster and more fairly
Employment disputes are stressful for everyone
involved. For a small-business owner, months spent
dealing with lawyers, paperwork and hearings can
mean thousands of dollars in costs and hours away
from customers and staff. For an employee, the same
delays can leave questions about income, reputation
and their next job hanging over them.
The system should be rigorous where the issues
are serious, but the cost and process should
be proportionate to the scale and complexity of
the dispute.
National will develop a faster pathway for minor matters
and reform the wider system so problems are resolved
earlier, cases keep moving and both sides get a
fair hearing.
This work will build on the Government’s 2026 review
of the employment dispute system, which specifically
sought feedback on the time and cost involved in
disputes, the role of employment advocates and where
the system could work better.

What National will do if re-elected…
A fast track for straightforward disputes
National will develop a simplified fast-track process
within the Employment Relations Authority, for suitable
minor employment disputes.
The new pathway will provide a simpler route to a
binding decision, with straightforward forms, clear
eligibility rules and firm decision timeframes. The aim is
to match the process to the scale of the dispute, rather
than requiring every disagreement to work through the
same pathway.

Serious, complex or precedent-setting cases will remain
on the appropriate existing pathway, and fair-hearing
and challenge rights will be preserved.

Resolve problems earlier
Mediation is already a standard part of the employment
dispute system. We want to make use of the tools
already available to resolve straightforward disputes
earlier, before they become drawn out and expensive.
The law already provides options such as mediator
recommendations and, where the parties agree,
binding decisions. But these options are not routinely
offered or well understood.
National will require the Employment Relations
Authority to clearly explain and offer these resolution
options in suitable minor disputes, while preserving
each party’s choice about whether to use them. We
will also ensure prompt access to mediation, with
exceptions where mediation is inappropriate or urgent
action is required.

Keep Authority cases moving
Where a dispute does reach the Employment Relations
Authority, parties should be able to expect steady
progress and a timely determination.
National will require the Authority to issue
determinations within one month of the investigation
meeting or final submissions, except in genuinely
exceptional circumstances. Public holidays and
recognised court or Authority closedown periods,
including the Christmas and New Year period, will be
excluded when calculating that timeframe.

We will also track cases across the full process, from
filing through case management and investigation to
the final decision. The Authority’s case-management
powers will be strengthened to narrow the issues
genuinely in dispute, direct the production of relevant
information and deal efficiently with repetitive or
unnecessary procedural applications.
National will review the Authority’s ability to enforce its
own decisions, and whether their current powers are fit
for purpose.

Stop people having to give the same
evidence twice
Evidence given in the Authority can currently need to
be heard again if a matter progresses further. This is an
additional layer of cost and duplication that parties and
witnesses just don’t need.
National will require the Authority to keep a reliable
record of oral evidence, with appropriate privacy and
access safeguards. Courts will retain the ability to hear
evidence again where fairness or the circumstances
require it.

Better representation, without pricing
people out
Employees and employers should be able to get
affordable help with an employment dispute, but
paid professional representation should meet basic
standards of competence, conduct and transparency.

National will develop proportionate regulation of paid
employment advocates, including standards of conduct
and competence, clear information about fees and a
proper complaints process.

Ensuring collective bargaining has genuine
workplace support
Under current New Zealand law, a union can initiate
collective bargaining without first demonstrating
support from a minimum number or proportion of the
employees whose work would be covered by the
proposed agreement.
National will change this by requiring a minimum level
of employee support before new collective bargaining
can be initiated. The proposal would require support
from the lesser of 10 employees or 20 per cent of the
employees proposed to be covered, with a minimum of
two employees.
Comparable jurisdictions already use employeesupport tests. In the United Kingdom, a union
generally needs membership of at least 10 per cent
of the proposed bargaining unit to apply for statutory
recognition. In Australia, where an employer has not
agreed to bargain, the Fair Work Commission can
require bargaining where a majority of the employees
proposed to be covered want to bargain.

Earlier certainty over who is an employee
National has already introduced the contractor
gateway, which came into force in February 2026.
Where all of the gateway criteria are met, a worker is
treated as a contractor. Where they are not, the existing
common-law test continues to apply.
But where employment status remains genuinely
disputed, resolving that question early can prevent
the rest of the dispute becoming more complicated
and expensive.
National will create a clearer early process for
determining disputed employment status under section
6 of the Employment Relations Act, consistent with the
contractor gateway. The objective is to give both sides
earlier certainty about which rights and obligations
apply before they incur the cost of litigating the rest of
the dispute.

Employment law people can actually
navigate
New Zealand’s employment framework has
accumulated across multiple statutes over many years.
We currently have a system where obligations are
spread across a range of Acts, making it particularly
difficult for small businesses and employees without
specialist legal support to work out what applies.
National will progress an Employment Relations
Consolidation Bill in the next parliamentary term. The
purpose will be to bring the core employment-law
framework into a clearer and more coherent legislative
structure, reduce unnecessary cross-references and
make rights and obligations easier to find.

Health and safety rules that make sense on
the shop floor
The same principle applies to health and safety. A
workshop, café or construction business should be able
to understand what it needs to do to keep people safe.
National will rewrite priority health and safety codes
and guidance in plain English, with practical examples
developed alongside small businesses, workers and
industry experts. Too often, outdated and difficult-tounderstand guidance adds an unnecessary layer of
compliance difficulty.
This will support the Government’s wider health and
safety reforms by making expectations clearer and
helping businesses focus their effort on the risks that
can actually cause harm.

D.  Giving business owners their time
back
For many small-business owners, the paperwork starts
when the working day ends. After serving customers,
managing staff and paying suppliers, there are still
forms to complete, licences to renew and records
to update.
Each requirement might seem small on its own.
Together, they take time and money away from the
business and time away from family.
National will make everyday compliance simpler.
Businesses should be able to provide information once,
use reliable digital tools and have a strong compliance
record recognised. Requirements should be clear,
practical and proportionate to the risks involved.

What National will do if re-elected…
Tell government once
Changing a business address or contact details
should be straightforward. Owners should not have
to work through a list of agencies updating the same
information in different systems.
National will develop a Tell Government Once service
built around the New Zealand Business Number and
existing government systems. Businesses will be able
to update core details in one place and securely share
those changes with participating agencies, including
Inland Revenue, ACC, the Companies Office and
relevant licensing bodies.
National will also explore this service integrating
with Councils where practical, with any necessary
information-sharing changes supported by clear
privacy rules and business control over the
information shared.

Simpler renewals for reliable businesses
A café can have separate renewal dates for food
registration, alcohol licensing, outdoor dining and
signage. Keeping track of different forms, invoices
and deadlines creates work throughout the year.
National will require councils to offer a coordinated
approach for relevant permits, licences and registrations.
Where possible, we will streamline approvals, aligning
approval lengths, ensuring businesses don’t have to
renew more often than necessary.
We will also introduce automatic or simplified renewals
for suitable low-risk approvals where a business has a
strong compliance record and its circumstances have
not materially changed.
For hospitality businesses, National will also deliver the
alcohol-licensing reforms already announced, including
simpler applications and renewals, longer renewal periods
for suitable licences and changes to the fee structure.

Letting accountants help with statutory
declarations
Many small businesses already rely on their chartered
accountant for trusted advice and official paperwork.
They should also be able to complete a statutory
declaration with that professional rather than arranging
a separate appointment elsewhere.
National will progress Suze Redmayne’s members
bill, first introduced by Carl Bates, to allow eligible
chartered accountants to administer statutory
declarations, with appropriate professional and
identity safeguards.
This is a specific extension of who can take a declaration.
Remote statutory declarations are already permitted
subject to legal requirements, and this reform will build
on those existing options rather than duplicate them.

Making digital the default
Businesses increasingly keep records, sign
documents and serve customers digitally. Government
requirements should make it easy to use reliable
technology rather than forcing businesses back
onto paper.
National will make clear that secure and accessible
electronic records can satisfy document-retention
requirements wherever a physical original is not
genuinely necessary. We will also remove remaining
statutory exceptions and inconsistent agency practices
that unnecessarily require paper.
That will include modernising physical-display rules
for alcohol licences and vehicle consumer information
where accessible digital alternatives can provide
the same information to customers and enforcement
officers. We will also make clear that suitable electronic
proof of delivery, including app confirmations,
photographs and secure tracking records, can meet
relevant requirements, and that accurate electronic
hazardous-substance records can be used where
they remain readily accessible onsite, including in
an emergency.
National will replace unnecessary compulsory
newspaper notices with searchable online publication,
while keeping appropriate ways to inform affected
communities. We will also remove obsolete fax, telex
and other paper-based requirements that create a real
compliance burden.
Where digital options already exist, we will make their
acceptance clearer and more consistent.

Clearer fire-safety guidance
Small-business owners should be able to understand
their fire-safety obligations without needing to become
experts in the system.
National will publish clearer guidance on existing
evacuation requirements, approved schemes and
available templates, helping businesses understand
what applies to them and how to comply.
The objective across these reforms is the same:
less time navigating government processes, fewer
unnecessary forms and more time spent running and
growing the business.

E.  Improving cashflow and lowering
costs for businesses

What National will do if re-elected…

Labour’s Business Payment Practices Act would have
required large businesses to report their payment
practices without requiring them to pay suppliers
any sooner. National repealed it and instead put the
responsibility on government to lead by example, while
committing to work with BusinessNZ on an industry-led
prompt-payment code.

Payment fees take a slice out of every sale. The
Commerce Commission’s implemented interchange-fee
reductions were expected to lower merchant service
fees by around $90 million a year, while proposed
caps on commercial credit cards could deliver another
$40 million in annual savings.

Government agencies are now expected to pay
95 per cent of domestic eInvoices within five business
days and other domestic invoices within 10 working
days. Results reported in July showed agencies paid
95.9 per cent of more than 1.6 million invoices within
10 working days.
National will build on these reforms by continuing to
publish agency payment performance, addressing
persistent delays and ensuring prompt-payment
requirements flow through government supply chains.
We are also making invoicing easier. eInvoicing
sends invoices directly between accounting systems,
reducing manual entry, mistakes and opportunities for
invoice fraud. By July, around 113,000 businesses were
registered to receive eInvoices, up from 52,000 earlier
in the year. Research estimates full adoption could
deliver up to $800 million a year in productivity gains.

Lower-cost, more competitive payments

National will make merchant fees, terminal charges and
other payment-service costs easier to compare, with
the disclosure obligation sitting with providers rather
than small businesses. We will also monitor whether
regulated savings are reaching businesses and support
further evidence-based fee reductions.

Helping independent repairers compete
Modern vehicles increasingly require access to
manufacturers’ information to diagnose and repair
faults. National will progress Dan Bidois’ motorvehicle repair-information reform to give independent
workshops better access to that information.
A regulated market for manufacturers’ repair
information would help independent repairers compete,
give customers more choice and reduce avoidable
delays getting work vehicles back on the road, with
appropriate safety, security and commercial safeguards.

Putting useful technology within reach
Small-business owners can see the potential of AI, but
often lack the time or expertise to work out where to
start. The AI Advisory Pilot helps businesses identify
useful applications and put them into practice, cofunding up to 50 per cent of eligible expert support.
Around 150 businesses have already joined, another
120 places have been announced, and around 650
businesses received co-funded support for AI-related
courses between July 2025 and June 2026.

Giving smaller firms a fair shot at government
work
Government procurement reforms introduced in
December 2025 reduced the main rulebook from 71
rules to 47 and introduced a minimum 10 per cent
weighting for economic benefits to New Zealand in
covered procurement evaluations.
National will monitor the reforms to ensure capable
smaller New Zealand suppliers have genuine
opportunities to compete. Requirements around
insurance, experience and reporting should be
proportionate to the size and risk of the contract
being offered.

F.  Backing the businesses that build
and produce
A builder will not take on another apprentice without
confidence the next job can proceed. A manufacturer
cannot fill a bigger order without the machinery and
space to produce it. For these businesses, delays cost
money and can mean missed opportunities.

National has also opened the door to more trusted
overseas building products. Recognition of Australia’s
WaterMark scheme alone made more than 90,000
plumbing products available under the recognised
pathway, increasing choice and competition for builders
and customers.

What National will do if re-elected…

National is making it easier to invest, build and expand
through clearer rules, faster decisions and better
access to the tools businesses need to grow. Fasttrack approvals are already helping significant housing,
infrastructure and productive projects move through a
more coordinated process.

Faster approvals and less duplicated work

The replacement planning system will bring more
consistent national rules and fewer unnecessary
consents, with an estimated 15,000 to 22,000 fewer
consent and permit applications and $13.3 billion in
administration and compliance savings over 30 years.

National will require regulators to accept and reuse
relevant, appropriately certified reports where they
remain current and fit for purpose. Further information
can still be required where site conditions, the project
or the risks have materially changed.

Building reforms will also consolidate consenting
functions, halve Project Information Memorandum
processing times from 20 to 10 working days and
introduce proportionate liability backed by professional
indemnity insurance and home warranties.

We will continue to recognise trusted overseas
building products and standards where they meet
New Zealand’s requirements. Builders should have
access to a wider range of reliable products, and
customers should benefit from stronger competition.

National will implement the replacement planning system
and carry the building reforms through into practice.
We will also tackle repeated requests for the same
technical information.

Finance for the next stage of growth
National will carry through capital-market reforms that
make it easier for growing firms to raise equity and
debt, with requirements proportionate to the offer and
appropriate protections for investors.
We will continue attracting investment that brings
productive capacity, skills and international connections
into New Zealand. The goal is more businesses able
to buy better equipment, develop new products,
reach new customers and create skilled jobs in
their communities.

National will:
33.	 Deliver the replacement planning system with
consistent national rules, fewer unnecessary
consents and practical support through the
transition;
34.	 Deliver building-consent reforms that enable
greater consolidation of consenting functions
and more consistent decisions;
35.	 Cut Project Information Memorandum
processing times from 20 to 10 working days
as part of the building-system reforms;
36.	 Deliver proportionate building liability, backed

G.  Taking more New Zealand
businesses to the world
A small New Zealand business can have a product the
world wants but lack the contacts, time or expertise to
navigate an unfamiliar market. National will help more
firms make their first export sale and turn promising
overseas demand into regular orders.
National signed the New Zealand - India Free Trade
Agreement in April. It has now been ratified and will
enter into force on 20 October 2026, giving exporters
better access to a market of 1.4 billion people.
The agreement will eliminate or reduce tariffs on 95
per cent of our current exports to India. Fifty-seven per
cent will be tariff free from day one, including sheep
meat, wool and more than 95 per cent of forestry and
wood exports.
National has also expanded access through
agreements with the UAE, brought the NZ-EU FTA into
force ahead of schedule, concluded negotiations with
the Gulf Cooperation Council, and continued removing
non-tariff barriers holding exporters back.

What National will do if re-elected…
Backing the next 200 exporters
National’s Next 200 program will provide intensive
support to 200 high-potential small and mediumsized businesses ready to begin exporting or expand
significantly. Each will receive a dedicated NZTE
relationship manager, tailored market intelligence,
