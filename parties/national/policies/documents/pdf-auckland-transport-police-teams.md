Building the Future:

More Police on Auckland’s
buses and trains
Auckland’s public transport network has just got a
lot bigger. The City Rail Link adds three underground
stations and lifts peak capacity through the city centre by
around half. That is a big win for Auckland, and it means
more passengers, later services, and enclosed platforms
below ground where help needs to be close at hand.

The team will focus on preventing anti-social
behaviour, protecting passengers and transport
staff, responding quickly to incidents, and bolstering
confidence in safety on public transport. The
purpose will be to deter crime before it happens,
and make sure that when a serious incident does
occur, it gets an immediate police response.

Auckland Transport’s transport officers already
play an important role in managing incidents and
supporting passengers, but they do not have the
powers of a sworn police officer. They cannot make
an arrest, they can only observe an incident and
report it. And while New Zealand Police regularly
support transport operations, there is no dedicated
transport policing capability focused on Auckland’s
growing network.

Building on National’s work to
restore law and order

How will the Auckland Transport
Police Team be established?

Since coming into Government, National has worked
hard to restore law and order after the previous Labour
Government oversaw increased levels of crime.

Following the election, the Minister of Police will refresh
the letter of expectations to the Commissioner of Police
to highlight the importance of police visibility on the
public transport network as a critical part of keeping
Auckland safe.

The National-led Government has sent a clear
message that we are tough on crime and we have
pursued policies that crack down on gangs and
criminals, including:
•

Banning gang patches in public.

•

Giving Police stronger powers and put more
officers on the beat.

•

Capping sentence discounts so judges can no
longer slash penalties for offenders.

•

Restoring Three Strikes for the worst repeat
offenders.

•

Introducing tougher tools, like move-on orders,
to reclaim our town centres.

We have also increased police visibility in our
communities. In 2025 alone, Police carried out nearly
double as many foot patrols as they did during Labour’s
final year in government.
The results show that when a Government is tough
on crime and prioritises increased police visibility in
our communities, there is less crime and fewer victims.
Compared to when Labour was in Government:
•

Ram raids are down 85 per cent.

•

Youth offending is down 26 per cent.

•

There are 37,000 fewer victims of serious violent
crime.

A visible police presence helps deter offending before
it occurs. Boosting the visible police presence on trains,
buses and ferries, and at stations and transport hubs,
reassures passengers and transport staff, and ensures
rapid intervention when something does go wrong.

Police will be explicitly resourced to establish this
team, with funding to recruit an additional 50 officers
to fulfil this function. Consistent with their independent
operational role, Police will retain full decision-making
powers on the size, location, and timing of any police
presence on Auckland’s public transport network,
including which stations and routes have more
visible policing.

Fiscal impact
An additional $7 million per year will be made available
to Police to run this pilot, funded through Budget
2027. This cost is based on the “500 Police” initiative
in Budget 2024, updated slightly to reflect the recent
Police pay round.
That funding will support Police to recruit an additional
50 sworn officers to fulfil this function. The exact size of
these teams and their locations will be an operational
matter for New Zealand Police.
