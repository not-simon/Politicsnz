Fixing the Basics. Building the Future.

National’s plan for
Kiwi kids

Fixing the Basics. Building the Future.

National’s plan for Kiwi kids
From Monday 26 October, Kiwis will be able to vote
on New Zealand’s future.
National has a plan to help you secure your future,
whether you’re a young person starting out, a parent
raising your family, or in your retirement.
This election, there is a clear choice between sticking
with National’s plan for a more secure future, no matter
the stage of your life, with a growing economy, less
debt, more jobs and higher wages.
Or going back to Labour – propped up by the Greens,
Te Pati Maori and TOP – who will undo the progress
New Zealand has made over the last three years,
leaving you to pay for it through at least nine new
taxes costing households $2,263 each year.

Authorised by J de Joux, ps@national.org.nz

Under our plan for Kiwi kids, every child will get the
best possible start in life. By sticking to National’s
plan, we will deliver a future where children get the
education they need to set them up for success; and
an economy that has been responsibly managed so
they are not burdened with our debt.
Labour will undo the progress we have made
in education by removing the requirement to
teach one hour a day of reading, writing and
maths, scrapping twice yearly assessments in
Primary Schools, and undoing NCEA changes.

What we’ve delivered to date
Improved childhood immunisation. Lifted immunisation
rates to 92 per cent from 77 per cent.
Strong foundations. An hour a day of reading,
writing and mathematics; structured literacy and
a new mathematics curriculum.
Practical classroom resources. More than
one million mathematics books and resources,
alongside decodable readers, teacher guides
and curriculum support.
Clear information for parents. 20- and 40-week
phonics checks and twice-yearly assessment in
reading, writing and mathematics for Years 3–8.
Support where it’s needed. Nearly $750 million
for the largest expansion of learning support in
a generation, alongside targeted literacy and
mathematics intervention.
Better places to learn. Around $2.7 billion across
three Budgets for school property, with nearly
1,600 additional classrooms and learning spaces
fully funded.
Focused classrooms. Cell phones banned
during the school day. An end to open plan
classrooms and a renewed focus on attendance
and learning.

Better vocational education. National has restructured
the vocational education system to give regions
and industry a greater role in training decisions,
so young people can train for real jobs relevant to
their community.
A focus on improving child safety by implementing
all of the recommendations in the Dame Karen
Poutasi Review.

The results so far
The latest Progressive Achievement Test (PAT) data,
shows a significant improvement in mathematics,
and improvements in reading comprehension
and vocabulary.
The results show primary and intermediate students
made the equivalent of four months’ additional
progress in mathematics between 2024 and 2026,
signalling a turnaround in achievement.
The results also show statistically significant
improvements in reading comprehension and
vocabulary, equivalent to approximately one month’s
additional progress in each area over the last year.
After a prolonged period of declining reading
results, we are finally seeing a statistically
significant improvement.

Our 2026 election policies for Kiwi kids
• $1,500 Baby Boost and automatic enrolment in
KiwiSaver. From 1 July 2027, National will enrol
every child born into KiwiSaver at birth and give
them a $1,500 Baby Boost payment to kickstart their
retirement savings, which will see them benefit from
decades of compounding returns.
• End screen use in ECE and tightly limit it in primary
schools (years 1-3). Technology still has a place
in education, but it should enhance learning, not
become the default way children learn. We will also
place a renewed emphasis on handwriting because
it is an important part of learning to read and write.
• Require ECEs to report on children’s progress
against developmental milestones so you can see
how your child is progressing.
• Introduce a School Start Check at ECE before
children transition to primary school.
• Clearer developmental milestones throughout ECE
and report this progress to parents.

• Replace NCEA with a new national qualification
system. We are replacing NCEA, beginning in
2028, with new qualifications backed by a clear
senior secondary curriculum and stronger
subject-based assessment.
• Lifting the school leaving age. National will
require students to stay at school until they
complete Year 11 or turn 16, whichever is later.
• Consistent school dates. National will set consistent
school start and finish dates. Parents have told us
how frustrating different dates can be, leaving families
juggling childcare and taking extra leave from work.
• Ban under-16s from social media. To protect
children from online harm.
• More warm dry classrooms. Installing acoustic
panels, ceiling insulation bales, and carpet with
built-in underlay across schools nationwide to
improve warmth and noise reduction.

• Keep teaching the basics brilliantly. One hour
of reading, writing and maths every day; the core
learning areas to help raise achievement and
give every young New Zealander the opportunity
to succeed.

• Learning support coordinators in all schools and
more teacher aides. Delivering the most substantial
boost to learning support in a generation with
substantial increases to teacher aide hours and
providing Learning Support Co-ordinators for all
schools with Year 1-8 students.

• Accelerated Maths. National will expand the Year
7-10 Maths Acceleration Programme by an additional
40,000 students to reach 128,000 students across
three years.

• Expand trades training. To help young people
move into apprenticeships, employment and
other structured pathways from education
into employment.

Fixing the Basics. Building the Future. Party Vote National.
