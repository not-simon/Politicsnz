## Full text (reading order)

Building the Future:

Making Trans-Tasman travel
seamless to grow tourism
Introduction
Growing New Zealand’s tourism industry is a critical
part of National’s plan to fix the basics and build the
future. Tourism is our second-largest export earner and
supports around one in nine jobs.
Australia and New Zealand share two of the most
closely integrated economies in the world, including
each other’s largest tourism markets. New Zealand
recorded 1.52 million visitor arrivals from Australia in the
year ended December 2025, the highest annual result
since early 2020. In July 2026, New Zealand recorded
134,900 Australian visitor arrivals, the highest July
month on record. Australian visitors spent $4.2 billion
in New Zealand in the year ended March 2026, with
holiday visitors spending an average of $4,397 per trip.
To support our shared tourism industry to keep
growing, a re-elected National Government will work
with Australia to trial seamless travel for Australian
and New Zealand citizens travelling between the
two countries.

In practice, this means a fully digital trans-Tasman
travel experience, where eligible travellers can rely
on a smartphone, digital credentials and biometric
technology through more of the airport journey. This
would mean no paper passport required at the gate and
faster movement through airports, while maintaining
the security and integrity of New Zealand’s border and
preserving the ability of border agencies to intervene
where risk is identified.
With the ICC Men’s T20 World Cup 2028 to be jointly
hosted by Australia and New Zealand from 21 October
to 19 November 2028, we are expecting a surge in
cross-border travel from Kiwis and Australians. National’s
objective is to introduce a trial for seamless digital travel
prior to that tournament.

How it would work
Eligible travellers (Australian and New Zealand passportholders) will:
•

Provide their passport and travel details digitally
before departure, building on information already
provided to airlines and through the New Zealand
Traveller Declaration.

•

National’s policy

Complete their New Zealand Traveller Declaration
before flying, allowing immigration, customs and
biosecurity information to be assessed earlier.

•

National has already put the foundations in place for
seamless travel. In 2009, the National-led Government
officially launched SmartGate for trans-Tasman
passengers. This was the first step in streamlining
travel between the two countries.

Have their eligibility and identity checked digitally,
with travellers requiring further examination
removed from the streamlined pathway.

•

Use a dedicated trans-Tasman arrival pathway
at Auckland, Wellington, Christchurch and
Queenstown airports, which are supported by
existing eGate and facial-recognition technology.

•

Continue to meet all New Zealand biosecurity
and customs requirements, including baggage
inspection where required. Seamless immigration
processing does not mean bypassing biosecurity.
Travellers would still have to make the same
declarations and could still have baggage
inspected or be referred to an officer.

Making it easier for Australians to visit New Zealand
means more visitors exploring our cities and regions
which results in more incomes for local businesses,
more jobs opportunities for New Zealanders, and a
stronger economy.

In 2024, Prime Ministers Luxon and Albanese endorsed
the work of the Trans-Tasman Seamless Travel Group
and committed to exploring ways to move closer to
seamless travel between the two countries.
A re-elected National Government will deliver on that
commitment and work with Australia to trial seamless
travel for Australian and New Zealand citizens travelling
between the two countries.

Authorised by J de Joux, ps@national.org.nz

New Zealand already has much of the infrastructure
required. The New Zealand Traveller Declaration
is linked to a traveller’s passport, while eGates use
biometric technology to match a traveller’s face with
their ePassport.
To enable this policy, legislative changes to the
Immigration Act 2009 and Customs and Excise
Act 2018 will be required, as they currently impose
obligations to present a travel document and be
presented to a border official on arrival.

International comparisons
•

Singapore has implemented passport-less
clearance in Changi Airport. Arriving and departing
Singapore passport-holders can clear immigration
simply using facial and iris biometrics, without the
need to present their passport. All foreign visitors
can also enjoy the convenience of passport-less
clearance when they depart Singapore.
»

»

•

•

With the full roll-out of passport-less clearance
at Changi Airport, the average clearance time
for each traveller has been reduced by 60%,
from 25 seconds to 10 seconds.
International reporting identifies Singapore’s
passport-free biometric clearance as a leading
operational example.

Common Travel Area – Ireland and the UK – there
are no routine passport controls in operation for
Irish and British citizens traveling between the two
countries. However, you must show identification
to board a ferry or an airplane, and some airlines
and sea carriers only accept a passport as valid
identification. You may also be asked by an
immigration officer to prove that you are a citizen
of Ireland or the UK, so people are encouraged to
still carry a passport on them.
Indonesia is the first country in the world to
introduce an AI-powered biometric ‘seamless
corridor’ system. This system uses AI-enabled
biometrics to validate passenger identities “in
motion”. AI cameras scan and match your face in
real-time as you walk through a wide corridor, so
people do not need to stop, pull out their passport,
or talk to a border official. This is part of the “All
Indonesia” initiative, a major government-led
digital transformation aiming at streamlining the
entry process for international travellers. Testing
shows the system processes over 30 passengers
per minute, which is ten times faster than standard
e-gates. The initial phase focuses on serving
elderly and disabled travellers. The government
plans to expand the technology to all international
passengers across the country’s airport network.

Managing immigration risk
No immigration, customs, biosecurity or lawenforcement powers will be removed. Travellers who
trigger a border alert, do not meet the trial criteria,
cannot be reliably matched to their passport, make a
declaration requiring further examination, or otherwise
require additional checks will be diverted into normal
border processing.

Historical background
•

From 1973-1981, Australia and New Zealand
enjoyed a passport-free understanding where,
under the Trans-Tasman Agreement, Kiwis and
Aussies could live and work in either country with
relatively few restrictions.

•

In 1981, the Australian government announced that
all people entering Australia would be required
to carry a passport. It was prompted in part by
concerns that international terrorists and drug
traffickers could pose as New Zealanders and
enter Australia.

•

In 1987, New Zealand returned the favour and it
became official that Australians needed a passport
to visit New Zealand.

•

In 2009, Prime Minister John Key officially launched
SmartGate for trans-Tasman passengers. This was
the first step in streamlining travel between the
two countries.

•

In 2023, the Trans-Tasman Seamless Travel
Group was established between Albanese and
Hipkins to deliver on Australian and New Zealand’s
commitment to explore ways to move closer
to seamless travel between the two countries.
It is chaired by Australian Border Force (ABF)
Commissioner and New Zealand Customs Service
Chief Executive.

•

In August 2024, Prime Ministers Luxon and
Albanese endorsed the work of the Trans-Tasman
Seamless Travel Group and its vision for easier
travel between Australia and New Zealand while
ensuring the highest levels of security at our
borders. They announced that governments
and industry would implement this vision via
coordinated activities to enhance traveller
experience. This would include Australia trialling
a digital incoming passenger card for the first
time, and New Zealand upgrading eGates.
Prime Ministers noted these were steps towards
‘contactless travel’ when passengers would be
able to rely on digital credentials throughout the
airport journey.
