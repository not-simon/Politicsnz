## Current status

Minister of Health and Minister for State Owned Enterprises since January 2025. He regained the Energy portfolio in the 2 April 2026 reshuffle and replaced Chris Bishop as National's 2026 campaign chair. He is MP for Pakuranga (since 2017) and #4 on National's 2026 list. ([Beehive](https://www.beehive.govt.nz/release/pm-refreshes-ministerial-team))

## Biography

- Born 8 April 1991 in Rotorua. Grew up in Clendon Park, South Auckland, and attended Manurewa High School.
- Active locally from a young age:
  - secretary and treasurer of the Clendon Residents Group
  - inaugural chair of the Manurewa Youth Council
  - Manurewa Local Board member from 2013 (deputy chair).
- At the University of Auckland (BCom/LLB, 2016) he was president of the student anti-abortion group ProLife Auckland. He made a submission opposing the 2013 marriage-equality bill.
- Worked at the Bank of New Zealand before entering Parliament.
- A Baptist Christian. Married to Rebecca; four children.

## Political career

- **2014:** stood in Manurewa and lost to Louisa Wall; list #64.
- **2017:** selected to succeed Maurice Williamson in safe Pakuranga and won by 14,886 votes. ([Stuff](http://www.stuff.co.nz/auckland/local-news/eastern-courier/90798390/youthful-banker-gets-nod-in-pakuranga))
- **2020:** re-elected by 10,050 votes, National's largest electorate majority that year. Collins made him spokesperson for police, corrections, youth and the SFO.
- **2023:** held Pakuranga by 18,710 votes. Became Minister of Transport, Local Government, Energy and for Auckland, and Deputy Leader of the House.
- **19 January 2025 reshuffle:** took Health (replacing Shane Reti) and State Owned Enterprises, and gave up Transport, Local Government and Energy. ([1News](https://www.1news.co.nz/2025/01/19/shane-reti-out-as-health-minister-as-pm-makes-major-cabinet-reshuffle/))
- **April 2026:** took back Energy and became campaign chair.

## Record & notable actions

- **Transport (2023–25):**
  - Cancelled Auckland light rail and stopped the Transport Choices cycling and walking programme.
  - Reversed Labour's "blanket" speed-limit reductions through a new speed rule.
  - Made "New Zealand Transport Agency" the agency's primary name.
  - Put road-user charges on EVs and plug-in hybrids (April 2024).
  - Eased the Clean Car Standard.
  - Launched the NZ$32.9b 2024–27 National Land Transport Programme, including 17 Roads of National Significance and continued funding for the City Rail Link and Auckland busways. ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/506674/national-led-government-officially-cancels-auckland-light-rail-plans), [1News](https://www.1news.co.nz/2024/03/21/nanny-state-speed-limit-reductions-to-be-reversed-minister/))
- **Local government:**
  - Repealed Three Waters under urgency (Feb 2024) and passed the first "Local Water Done Well" law (Aug 2024).
  - Restored binding polls on Māori wards and constituencies, requiring councils to hold referendums at the 2025 local elections. This passed on 30 July 2024. ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/te-manu-korihi/523622/maori-wards-bill-passes-third-reading))
  - Halted Labour's plan to lower the local-election voting age to 16.
- **Health (2025–):**
  - Confirmed the new Dunedin Hospital at the Cadbury site (NZ$1.9b) with a reduced specification. About 35,000 people protested the cuts.
  - Restored a Health NZ board and pushed decentralisation and private–public partnerships.
  - Released a NZ$20b Health Infrastructure Plan.
  - Expanded urgent and after-hours care (NZ$164m in Budget 2025).
  - Lowered the bowel-screening age to 58, funded by cancelling Labour's plan to lower it to 50 for Māori and Pasifika.
  - Contributed NZ$82.5m to the University of Waikato medical school.
  - Kept water-fluoridation policy unchanged.
  - Introduced AI scribes in emergency departments.
  - Ordered Health NZ to find NZ$510m in efficiencies (Dec 2025).
- **Puberty blockers:** in November 2025 he suspended new prescriptions of puberty blockers for gender dysphoria in minors, pending a UK trial. Medical colleges and PATHA strongly criticised the move. On 17 December 2025 the High Court delayed the ban pending judicial review. ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/579385/new-zealand-pushes-pause-on-puberty-blockers-pending-uk-trial), [RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/national/582090/puberty-blockers-ban-delayed-by-judicial-review))
- **Member's bill:** proposed Class C-equivalent penalties for supplying psychoactive substances such as synthetic cannabis. It was voted down.
- **Conscience votes:** voted against the End of Life Choice and abortion bills, and was one of eight MPs to vote against the 2022 ban on conversion practices. As Health Minister he said abortion law would not change. ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/539608/no-change-to-abortion-laws-new-health-minister-simeon-brown-promises))

## Controversies

- **Roe v Wade (2022):** liked Simon O'Connor's Facebook post calling the overturning of Roe v Wade a "good day", then apologised. ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/national/469788/national-mp-removes-post-following-roe-v-wade-decision))
- **Bilingual road signs (2023):** said road signs "should all be in English". Prime Minister Hipkins accused National of dog-whistling, and National's Māori MPs Tama Potaka, Harete Hipango and Shane Reti publicly disagreed with him. ([ODT](https://www.odt.co.nz/news/national/road-signs-should-all-be-english-national), [Newshub](https://www.newshub.co.nz/home/politics/2023/05/national-s-tama-potaka-takes-simeon-brown-for-korero-over-te-reo-road-signs-as-m-ori-mps-break-party-line.html))
- **Gang threats (2021):** received death threats after criticising Marama Davidson's attendance at a Mongrel Mob hui.
- **Clashes with health workers:**
  - Nurses turned their backs on him at the NZNO conference (Sept 2025). ([1News](https://www.1news.co.nz/2025/09/17/dont-guilt-trip-us-nurses-turn-backs-on-health-minister/))
  - The senior doctors' union called his binding-arbitration proposal "unusual and unhelpful".
  - In June 2026 he declined to reappoint the Medical Council's chair and deputy chair, saying the council had become "distracted by politics". Doctors' unions called this political interference. ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/health/598355/concerns-raised-over-minister-s-replacement-of-medical-council-leadership))
- **Abortion-rights concerns:** the Abortion Law Reform Association objected to his appointment as Health Minister because of his anti-abortion activism.
