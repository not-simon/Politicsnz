## Current status

Former MP. She retired at the 2020 election and has since:
- joined Bayleys Real Estate as Director – Strategic Advisory (October 2020);
- hosted TVNZ's *Give Us a Clue* (2021);
- been appointed chair of Pharmac by Associate Health Minister David Seymour (2024). ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/515385/former-deputy-pm-paula-bennett-appointed-pharmac-board-chair))

## Biography

- **Early life:** born 9 April 1969 in Auckland, with Tainui ancestry through her paternal grandmother. She grew up in Kinloch near Taupō and attended Taupō-nui-a-Tia College.
- **Young mother:** had her daughter at 17 and raised her alone, working in hospitality and at times receiving the Domestic Purposes Benefit.
- **Study and work:** rest-home kitchenhand and nurse aide; then a BA in social policy at Massey Albany, where she was students' association president.
- **Before Parliament:** electorate secretary for Murray McCully, then a recruitment consultant.
- **Personal:** married Alan Philps in 2012.

## Political career

- **2005:** entered Parliament as a list MP after contesting Waitakere.
- **Waitakere:** won the seat in 2008. In 2011 a judicial recount confirmed her win by just 9 votes over Carmel Sepuloni.
- **Upper Harbour:** won the new seat in 2014 (majority 9,692) and held it in 2017.
- **Minister:** a surprise appointment as Minister for Social Development (2008–14), followed by a run of senior portfolios.
- **Deputy Prime Minister:** under Bill English (December 2016 – October 2017).
- **Deputy leader under Bridges:** until both were ousted by Todd Muller and Nikki Kaye on 22 May 2020.
- **Retirement:** announced she would retire in June 2020.

**Roles held:**

- Deputy Prime Minister (2016–2017)
- Deputy Leader of the National Party (2016–2020)
- Minister of Police; Women; Tourism (2016–2017)
- Minister of State Services (2014–2017)
- Minister for Climate Change Issues (2015–2017)
- Minister of Social Housing; Local Government (2014–2016)
- Minister for Social Development (and Employment) (2008–2014)
- Minister of Youth Affairs (2008–2013)
- National campaign manager for 2020 (until May 2020)
- MP for Waitakere (2008–2014)
- MP for Upper Harbour (2014–2020)
- List MP (2005–2008)

## Record & notable actions

- **Welfare reform (2010s):**
  - Work-testing for sole parents once their youngest child turned six.
  - Abolished the Training Incentive Allowance, which she had used herself.
  - An "investment approach" to welfare.
- **Ministerial portfolios:** later State Services, Social Housing, Climate Change, Police and Women.
- **Conscience votes:** voted for marriage equality, End of Life Choice and abortion reform (see table).
- **Drug reform:** as National's drug-reform spokesperson she argued for a health-focused approach.

## Controversies

- **Release of beneficiaries' details (2009):** released the benefit details of two sole mothers who had criticised her policy, claiming "implied consent". A Human Rights Proceedings complaint was resolved in 2012. ([NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/nz/hundreds-get-1000-a-week-in-benefits/WZKR7K7OVVMXEQ3QZWBG3BQWCI/))
- **Christine Rankin (2009):** appointed the controversial Christine Rankin as Families Commissioner.
- **Job listings (2012):** read out job listings in Parliament that the retailers concerned said were exaggerated.
- **Firearms (2017):** as Police Minister, rejected 12 of 20 select committee firearms recommendations, two years before the Christchurch mosque attacks. The Police Association later criticised this.
