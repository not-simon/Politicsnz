## Current status

Minister for Trade and Investment, and Minister of Agriculture and Forestry, since November 2023; also Associate Minister of Foreign Affairs. He is MP for Rotorua (since 2008) and #10 on National's 2026 list. On 27 April 2026 he signed the New Zealand–India Free Trade Agreement in New Delhi. ([1News](https://www.1news.co.nz/2026/04/28/new-zealand-and-india-sign-landmark-free-trade-agreement/))

## Biography

- **Early life:** born on 22 November 1968 in Rotorua, son of former National MP Roger McClay. Educated at Tauhara College, Wesley College and Wellington Polytechnic (politics degree).
- **Europe and the Pacific:**
  - Head of staff to Lord Plumb, President of the European Parliament.
  - Founded a lobbying firm, Political Relationship Management.
  - Represented the Cook Islands in Europe from 2000 and became its Ambassador to the EU (and accredited for Niue) in 2002. He was the youngest-ever head of mission to the EU.
- **Personal:** four adult children; engaged to partner Anna Lillis in 2026.

## Political career

- **Rotorua:** won the seat from Labour's Steve Chadwick in 2008 (majority 5,065) and has held it since. Majorities: 7,357 (2011), 7,418 (2014), 7,901 (2017), just 825 (2020) and 8,923 (2023).
- **Fifth National Government:**
  - 2013: Minister of Revenue and Associate Health, outside Cabinet.
  - 2014: entered Cabinet with Revenue, State Owned Enterprises and Associate Foreign Affairs and Trade.
  - December 2015: Minister of Trade after Tim Groser left.
- **Opposition:** later spokesperson for hunting and fishing and, from 2023, agriculture.
- **Sixth National Government, November 2023:** Agriculture, Forestry, Hunting and Fishing, Trade, and Associate Foreign Affairs.

**Roles held:**

- Minister for Trade and Investment (2023–)
- Minister of Agriculture (2023–)
- Minister of Forestry (2023–)
- Minister for Hunting and Fishing (2023–2025)
- Associate Minister of Foreign Affairs (2023–)
- Minister of Trade (2015–2017)
- Minister for State Owned Enterprises (2014–2017)
- Minister of Revenue (2013–2015)
- Associate Minister of Health; Tourism (2013–2014)
- MP for Rotorua (2008–)
- Cook Islands Ambassador to the European Union (2002–2008)

## Record & notable actions

- **Member's bills:**
  - His Prohibition of Gang Insignia in Government Premises Act 2013 became law. It bans gang patches in government buildings, schools and hospitals.
  - An earlier bill to let councils allow Easter Sunday trading narrowly failed at first reading in 2009. ([Stuff](http://www.stuff.co.nz/national/politics/3145240/Easter-trading-bill-defeated-on-conscience-vote))
- **Trade deals:**
  - Concluded free trade agreements with the UAE (2024; signed January 2025) and with the Gulf Cooperation Council (October 2024). ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/new-zealand-reaches-trade-deal-with-gulf-states-2024-10-31/))
  - Signed the India FTA (April 2026) and agricultural cooperation deals with Indonesia, Chile and Ireland.
  - Vice-chair of the WTO's 13th Ministerial Conference (2024).
- **Agriculture and climate:**
  - Confirmed that agriculture would be removed from the Emissions Trading Scheme (June 2024).
  - Cancelled the annual ETS forestry service charge. ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/519248/coalition-exempts-farmers-from-ets-sets-up-fresh-working-group))
- **Conscience votes:**
  - Voted against marriage equality, End of Life Choice and abortion reform.
  - On the 2022 conversion-practices bill he voted against at first reading, for it at second reading and against it at third reading. He was one of eight MPs opposed at the final vote.

## Controversies

- **"You're not in Mexico now" (July 2024):** made the remark to Green MP Ricardo Menéndez March, who was born in Mexico. He withdrew and apologised at the Speaker's direction, and the Prime Minister called the remark inappropriate. ([NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/nz/politics/trade-minister-todd-mcclay-apologises-to-green-mp-ricardo-menndez-march-over-remarks-allegedly-referencing-mexico/QL24DQECPREDVMDQJMPV2FSL7Y/))
- **China:**
  - In 2016 John Key rebuked him for playing down concerns that China might retaliate over a steel-dumping inquiry.
  - Freedom House reported that in 2017 he called Xinjiang camps "vocational training centres", echoing Chinese Communist Party language. ([Freedom House](https://freedomhouse.org/report/special-report/2020/beijings-global-megaphone))
- **Donation allegation (2019):** Jami-Lee Ross alleged McClay had helped facilitate a NZ$150,000 donation to National in 2016 from a company owned by Chinese businessman Lin Lang. McClay and National denied this. ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/397577/national-denies-former-trade-minister-todd-mcclay-helped-facilitate-150k-donation))
