## Current status

Minister of Education and Minister of Immigration since 27 November 2023. She is also responsible for the government's response to the Royal Commission of Inquiry into Abuse in Care. She is MP for East Coast Bays and #5 on National's 2026 list.

Media named her, with Chris Bishop, as one of the two most likely successors if Luxon were replaced. During the August 2026 leadership crisis she declined to confirm her support for Luxon when questioned at Wellington Airport. On 13 August, after Luxon survived Chris Penk's challenge, she issued a statement of "full support" for him. ([The Conversation](https://theconversation.com/luxons-leadership-on-the-line-again-neither-national-nor-labour-stand-to-benefit-289475), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/August_2026_New_Zealand_National_Party_leadership_election))

## Biography

- **Early life:** born Erica Louise Poppelbaum in 1978, the daughter of a Dutch immigrant father. She went to Rangitoto College, where she met her husband Kane, and holds a BA in politics from the University of Auckland. She lives in Ōkura and has two children.
- **Export sales:** worked in export sales for six years.
- **Television:** produced the reality shows *Noise Control* and *Last Chance Dog*, and worked on *Piha Rescue* for more than six years.
- **Political staff:** from 2013 she worked in the electorate office of Foreign Minister Murray McCully, whom she calls her mentor, rising to senior MP support.

## Political career

- **2017:** chosen to succeed McCully in safe East Coast Bays and won with about 66% of the vote. It was her first time standing for any office.
- **2020:** held the seat by 8,764 votes. She was immigration spokesperson.
- **December 2021:** Luxon promoted her from #25 to #7 and made her education spokesperson.
- **2023:** held East Coast Bays by 20,353 votes. She became Minister of Education and Minister of Immigration, and in January 2024 took on the abuse-in-care response. ([Electoral Commission](https://archive.electionresults.govt.nz/electionresults_2023/electorate-details-10.html))

**Roles held:**

- Minister of Education (2023–)
- Minister of Immigration (2023–)
- Minister Responsible for the Government Response to the Royal Commission of Inquiry into Abuse in Care (2024–)
- National spokesperson for education (2021–2023), immigration (2020–2023)
- MP for East Coast Bays (2017–)

## Record & notable actions

**Education**
- Banned cellphones in schools (from April 2024).
- Mandated structured literacy and ended funding for Reading Recovery. ([Newshub](https://www.newshub.co.nz/home/politics/2024/05/structured-literacy-government-to-roll-out-new-approach-to-reading-in-all-state-schools.html))
- Launched a Maths Action Plan with twice-yearly assessments. It was partly funded by moving NZ$30m from the Te Ahu o te Reo Māori teacher-training programme. ([Beehive](https://www.beehive.govt.nz/release/government-transforms-maths-education), [NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/nz/education/education-minister-erica-stanford-reveals-30m-cut-to-te-reo-maori-funding-to-boost-maths-curriculum/65A27XEF6BBPXDS3GQR7HVKNWI/))
- Ordered an inquiry into school property and set up a new School Property Agency. Ended open-plan classrooms (2025).
- Paid teachers' registration and practising-certificate fees (NZ$53m, 2025).
- Introduced financial literacy into the primary curriculum from 2027.
- Rewrote the curriculum for Years 0–10 (2025–26 drafts).
- Announced the replacement of NCEA by about 2030 (August 2025). In August 2025, 89 principals asked for a halt. ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/national/571277/principals-urge-halt-to-ncea-change-plans))
- Removed schools' statutory Treaty of Waitangi obligation, saying it belongs to the Crown (Nov 2025). Hundreds of school boards objected. ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/577761/teachers-shocked-by-government-decision-to-remove-treaty-of-waitangi-requirement-in-schools))
- Restructured the Teaching Council so a majority of members are ministerial appointees. Unions called it a "power grab". ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/577703/education-minister-s-plans-for-teaching-council-a-power-grab-union-says))
- Announced two new specialist schools for high-needs students, the first since 1977. ([1News](https://www.1news.co.nz/2026/01/29/govt-announces-two-new-specialist-schools-for-complex-high-needs/))
- Introduced a colour-coded ERO school-rating system (March 2026).
- Added last-minute homeschooling checks to an education bill after a Gloriavale report, then withdrew them in May 2026 after objections from NZ First and others. ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/education/596484/government-backs-down-on-last-minute-homeschooling-law-change))

**Immigration**
- Tightened Accredited Employer Work Visa (AEWV) rules: lower-skilled holders can no longer sponsor family.
- Introduced a five- to ten-year "Parent Boost" visa (2025) and new seasonal-worker visas. ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/563450/government-announces-new-parent-boost-visa-to-bring-families-together))
- Ran a visa-waiver trial for Chinese visitors who hold Australian visas.
- Proposed law changes to make it easier to deport non-citizens, including raising the residence threshold for deporting permanent residents from 10 to 20 years. ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/572046/deporting-criminals-set-to-get-easier-under-law-change))
- In 2026 publicly accused MBIE officials of misleading ministers over a failed NZ$33m biometrics IT project. This led to integrity investigations. ([Stuff](https://www.stuff.co.nz/politics/360993437/integrity-probe-launched-failed-33m-immigration-technology-project))

**Abuse in care**
- Corrected a legal-fee parity issue in the 2001 Lake Alice settlements.
- Announced redress of at least NZ$150,000 for each Lake Alice torture survivor (Dec 2024). ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/537027/watch-lake-alice-survivors-to-get-150-000-each-in-redress))

**Positions**
- On the liberal wing of National. Voted for the End of Life Choice and abortion bills (see table).
- With Chris Bishop, pushed for a conscience vote on the 2021–22 conversion-practices bill.
- Has spoken in favour of working with the Greens.

## Controversies

- **Parliamentary language (Nov 2024):** apologised and withdrew a remark directed at Labour's Jan Tinetti in the House. ([1News](https://www.1news.co.nz/2024/11/21/education-minister-accused-of-calling-labour-mp-a-stupid-b/))
- **Personal email (2025):** 1News reported she used a personal Gmail account for some ministerial correspondence, including pre-Budget material. This raised Cabinet Manual concerns. ([1News](https://www.1news.co.nz/2025/05/05/erica-stanford-sent-pre-budget-documents-to-her-personal-email/))
- **"Spam" remark (May 2025):** expressed regret after describing unsolicited immigration emails from India as "spam" in the House. ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/indonz/562563/immigration-minister-erica-stanford-regrets-email-spam-remarks-after-backlash))
- **Gaza visas (2024):** declined Dunedin City Council's request for a special visa pathway for relatives of Palestinian New Zealanders, and declined to meet the mayor about it. ([Stuff](https://www.stuff.co.nz/nz-news/350380743/immigration-minister-brushes-palestinian-special-visa-request))
- **Te reo in phonics readers:** RNZ reported in 2025 that she decided to remove most te reo Māori words from the *Ready to Read Phonics Plus* books. She said only 12 books were affected.
