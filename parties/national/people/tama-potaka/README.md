## Current status

Minister of Conservation, Māori Development, Māori Crown Relations: Te Arawhiti, and Whānau Ora, and Associate Minister of Housing (Social Housing), since November 2023. He is MP for Hamilton West, which he won at the December 2022 by-election, and is #11 on National's 2026 list.

## Biography

- **Early life:** born on 8 January 1976 in Raetihi to two schoolteacher parents. He affiliates to Ngāti Hauiti, Whanganui, Taranaki and Ngāti Toa.
- **Education:**
  - Dux of Te Aute College (1993).
  - BA/LLB from Victoria University of Wellington (1999).
  - LLM from Columbia University on a scholarship.
  - Graduate of Te Panekiretanga o Te Reo Māori.
- **Legal career:**
  - Attorney at Simpson Thacher & Bartlett in New York.
  - Later at Rudd Watts & Stone and Bell Gully, and in public-policy roles.
- **Business and iwi roles:**
  - General manager of corporate services at Tainui Group Holdings (about seven years, Hamilton).
  - Senior adviser at the NZ Superannuation Fund.
  - Chief executive of Ngāi Tai ki Tāmaki Trust from 2020.
  - A lead negotiator for the Mōkai Pātea Treaty claim (2021).
- **Personal:** married to Ariana Paul (2008); three children.

## Political career

- **2022 Hamilton West by-election:** held after Gaurav Sharma resigned. Potaka won with 6,974 votes to Labour's 4,541, a majority of 2,433. ([NZ Gazette](https://gazette.govt.nz/notice/id/2022-au5718))
- **2023:** spokesperson for Māori development. He held Hamilton West at the 2023 election by 6,488 votes. ([Electoral Commission](https://archive.electionresults.govt.nz/electionresults_2023/electorate-details-13.html))

**Roles held:**

- Minister of Conservation (2023–)
- Minister for Māori Development (2023–)
- Minister for Māori Crown Relations: Te Arawhiti (2023–)
- Minister for Whānau Ora (2023–)
- Associate Minister of Housing (Social Housing) (2023–)
- National spokesperson for Māori development (2023)
- MP for Hamilton West (2022–)

## Record & notable actions

**Māori–Crown relations**
- Met Kīngi Tūheitia with Luxon (January 2024) and attended the King's national unity hui.
- Moved Te Arawhiti's Treaty-settlement monitoring functions to Te Puni Kōkiri (August 2024). Opposition parties called it a backward step. ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/te-manu-korihi/525032/government-clarifying-functions-of-te-arawhiti-and-te-puni-kokiri))
- Overhauled the Waitangi Tribunal's membership (January 2025), with eight new members including Ron Mark. Te Pāti Māori called it a "whitewash"; Potaka rejected that. ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/te-manu-korihi/539260/minister-overhauls-waitangi-tribunal-membership))
- Set up a technical advisory group to review the Treaty of Waitangi Act 1975, as required by the coalition agreements. ([NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/nz/politics/waitangi-tribunal-review-to-kick-off-this-year-government-confirms-act-calls-it-activist/26L35ZWBDFCFPNMHQG4ONDTRMA/))
- Member of the ministerial group reviewing Treaty clauses in legislation (see Paul Goldsmith).

**Conservation**
- Raised the International Visitor Levy from NZ$35 to NZ$100 (from October 2024).
- Announced 19 new marine protection areas in the Hauraki Gulf.
- Added feral cats to Predator Free 2050 (November 2025). ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/579503/stone-cold-killer-feral-cats-added-to-predator-free-2050-strategy-conservation-minister-announces))
- Planned charges for foreign visitors at four premium natural sites. ([Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/travel/2025/08/05/new-zealand-to-charge-foreign-tourists-to-visit-four-of-its-most-famous-natural-attraction))
- Sponsored the Conservation Amendment Bill (first reading 13 May 2026, 68–54). After criticism, he withdrew its land disposal and exchange clauses in June 2026. ([NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/nz/politics/minister-tama-potaka-to-remove-controversial-disposal-and-exchange-clauses-from-conservation-act/BGHOZIKFXBCZZNNSM4Q3YYCD5E/))

**Social housing**
- Tightened eligibility for emergency housing. ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/national/526461/what-you-need-to-know-about-the-new-rules-tightening-the-gateway-into-emergency-housing))
- Households in emergency-housing motels fell from 3,141 (December 2023) to 591 (December 2024). The government said it had met its target five years early, but could not account for about 20% of those who left. ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/539858/government-says-it-s-hit-emergency-housing-target-5-years-early))
- Announced a programme of 189 social and affordable homes in Rotorua (2025) and the closure of the city's emergency-housing motels.

**Other**
- In 2023 he publicly disagreed with Simeon Brown's view that road signs should be English-only. ([Newshub](https://www.newshub.co.nz/home/politics/2023/05/national-s-tama-potaka-takes-simeon-brown-for-korero-over-te-reo-road-signs-as-m-ori-mps-break-party-line.html))
- He was not in Parliament for the 2013–2022 conscience votes.

## Controversies

- **Criticism of government Māori policy:** opposition parties and Māori leaders have criticised the government's policies on Māori, which he has defended as minister. At the January 2024 hui he disputed speakers' claims that the government was underpinned by white supremacy.
- No personal scandals are recorded in the sources consulted.
