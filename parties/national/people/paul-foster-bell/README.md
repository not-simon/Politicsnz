## Current status

As of 2025 he is a business development manager at the University of Otago and chairs the Shakespeare Globe Centre NZ trust. After Parliament he was political adviser to US Ambassador Scott Brown.

## Biography

- **Early life:** born in March 1977 in Whangārei and raised on a beef farm. He has whakapapa to Ngāti Kahu ki Whangaroa and Te Aupōuri.
- **Education:** BA in archaeology from Otago (2003); Master of International Studies (2023).
- **Diplomatic career:** an MFAT diplomat for about ten years, posted to Pakistan, Iran and Saudi Arabia.
- **Monarchy New Zealand:** vice-chair (2012–13).

## Political career

- **Early contests:** lost Dunedin North in 2002, standing as Paul Foster, and Wellington Central in 2011.
- **List MP (May 2013):** entered Parliament when Jackie Blue resigned.
- **2014:** lost the Whangārei selection to Shane Reti, then lost Wellington Central again but was returned on the list.
- **Retirement:** withdrew from the 2017 Wellington Central selection when Nicola Willis challenged him, and retired at the 2017 election.

**Roles held:**

- List MP (2013–2017)
- Deputy Chair, Justice and Electoral; Education and Science; Government Administration Committees
- MFAT diplomat (deputy high commissioner to Pakistan; deputy head of mission, Saudi Arabia)

## Record & notable actions

- **Anzac Day bill:** his member's bill let RSAs serve traditional tots at Anzac Day gunfire breakfasts. It became law in April 2016.
- **Arbitration Amendment Bill:** introduced in 2017 and passed in 2019 under Andrew Bayly.
- **Coming out (2016):** came out as gay in response to remarks by Destiny Church leader Brian Tamaki. He was a member of the cross-party LGBTI rights group.
- **Conscience votes:** joined Parliament after the 2013 marriage-equality vote and left before 2019, so cast none of the votes tracked here.

## Controversies

- **Staff turnover and expenses (2016):** reports said 12 staff had left his office in 2013–16 amid bullying claims, which he denied. His travel expenses of more than NZ$61,000 in a year were also reported; John Key defended them.
