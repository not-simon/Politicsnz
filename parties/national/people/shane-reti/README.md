## Current status

MP for Whangārei, retiring at the 2026 election, as announced on 10 March 2026. He is not on National's 2026 list. In the 2 April 2026 reshuffle he gave up his remaining portfolios:
- Science, Innovation and Technology and Universities/Tertiary went to Penny Simmonds.
- Statistics went to Scott Simpson.
- Pacific Peoples went to Paul Goldsmith.

He was granted the title "The Honourable" for life on 7 April 2026. ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/589154/national-s-shane-reti-to-retire-from-politics), [NZ Gazette](https://gazette.govt.nz/notice/id/2026-vr1913))

## Biography

- **Early life:** born on 5 June 1963 in Hamilton. Of Ngāpuhi, Ngāti Hine, Ngāti Wai, Te Kapotai and Ngāti Maniapoto descent. Educated at Hamilton Boys' High School and in Idaho, USA.
- **Education:** University of Auckland (BHB, MB ChB 1987, Diploma in Obstetrics), and a diploma in dermatological science from Cardiff.
- **Medicine:** GP for 17 years and Northland DHB member for seven years.
- **Honours and research:** QSM in 2006; Harkness Fellow at Harvard Medical School from 2007, staying about six years in Boston.
- **Service:** Territorial Force (1983–87). He has continued to practise medicine as an MP, including giving COVID vaccinations in Northland in 2021.

## Political career

- **2014:** beat list MP Paul Foster-Bell for the Whangārei selection and won the seat comfortably.
- **2017:** majority of 10,967.
- **2020:** led by 162 on election night but lost to Labour's Emily Henderson by 431 votes on the final count. He returned on the list. ([Electoral Commission](https://www.electionresults.govt.nz/electionresults_2020/electorate-details-64.html))
- **Health spokesperson:** from July 2020, under Collins.
- **Deputy leader:** elected unopposed on 10 November 2020. He was interim leader for a few days after Collins was ousted in November 2021, then stepped aside for Luxon and Willis. ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/430255/shane-reti-becomes-new-deputy-leader-of-the-national-party))
- **2023:** regained Whangārei by 11,424 votes.
- **Ministerial roles:** Minister of Health and Pacific Peoples until January 2025, when he was moved to Science, Innovation and Technology, Statistics and Universities, and dropped from #4 to #9 in Cabinet. ([1News](https://www.1news.co.nz/2025/01/19/shane-reti-out-as-health-minister-as-pm-makes-major-cabinet-reshuffle/))

**Roles held:**

- Minister of Health (2023–2025)
- Minister for Pacific Peoples (2023–2026)
- Minister of Science, Innovation and Technology (2025–2026)
- Minister of Statistics (2025–2026)
- Minister for Universities (2025–2026)
- Deputy Leader of the National Party and Deputy Leader of the Opposition (Nov 2020–Nov 2021)
- Interim Leader of the National Party (Nov 2021)
- National health spokesperson (2020–2023)
- MP for Whangārei (2014–2020, 2023–2026)
- List MP (2020–2023)

## Record & notable actions

**Health (2023–25)**
- Defended the repeal of Labour's smokefree generation law and the abolition of Te Aka Whai Ora (the Māori Health Authority). Medical colleges and Pacific leaders criticised both. ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/national/programmes/checkpoint/audio/2018917256/health-minister-shane-reti-defends-government-s-plan-to-scrap-smokefree-legislation))
- Appointed a Crown observer, then a commissioner, to Health NZ.
- Funded a NZ$50m Māori immunisation package.
- Announced NZ$604m for Pharmac in June 2024, covering 54 medicines including 26 cancer treatments. ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/520419/cancer-drugs-promise-government-confirms-600m-extra-for-pharmac))
- Told Hawke's Bay services to stop prioritising free GP visits by ethnicity, in line with the "need not race" directive. ([Stuff](https://www.stuff.co.nz/nz-news/350409830/maori-and-pacific-young-people-dropped-list-eligible-free-gp-visits-hawkes-bay))
- Proposed scaling back the new Dunedin Hospital, prompting a protest of about 35,000 people. ([NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/nz/dunedin-march-against-broken-promise-as-new-hospital-project-faces-massive-cuts/S577ICUA5REYTMW23ZFQXD3FKA/))

**Statistics**
- Announced the end of the traditional five-yearly census: there will be no 2028 census, and from 2030 it will be replaced by administrative data and annual surveys. ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/national/564470/five-yearly-census-to-be-scrapped-from-2030-replaced-with-administrative-data-annual-surveys))

**Science**
- Oversaw the merger of Crown Research Institutes.

**Other**
- **Tonga (January 2022):** after the volcanic eruption cut Tonga's undersea cables, he publicly asked Elon Musk to provide Starlink connectivity, and SpaceX did so. ([Newshub](https://www.newshub.co.nz/home/politics/2022/01/dr-shane-reti-makes-urgent-plea-to-elon-musk-to-help-with-tonga-s-telecommunication-issues.html))
- **Member's bills:**
  - Medicinal cannabis (2018).
  - Allowing unfunded cancer medicines to be administered in public hospitals (drawn 2021).
  - A te reo Māori bill speech (2016).
- **Conscience votes:** voted against the End of Life Choice and abortion bills, and against the 2022 conversion-practices ban (see table).

## Controversies

- **Northland by-election (2015):** an advocacy group leader accused him of telling her to stay quiet about forestry roads until after the by-election. He said he was trying to help.
- **NZ Herald column (2022):** the Herald removed an opinion piece of his after its data on self-harm during the pandemic was described as misleading.
- **Ambulance ride-alongs (2023):** he spent nearly three months observing paramedic shifts. This raised privacy and consent questions, and St John and Wellington Free changed their observer policies. ([1News](https://www.1news.co.nz/2023/07/23/nationals-reti-says-he-followed-rules-while-shadowing-medics/))
