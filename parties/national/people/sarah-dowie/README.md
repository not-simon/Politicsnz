## Current status

Former MP. She retired at the 2020 election, and in January 2021 was named chief executive of the mental-health charity Able Charitable Trust.

## Biography

- **Early life:** born in 1974, daughter of two police officers.
- **Career:** studied law and ecology at Otago, then worked for the law firm Macalisters and the Department of Conservation.
- **Party:** associated with National's "Blue Greens".
- **Personal:** two children.

## Political career

- **Invercargill (2014):** succeeded Eric Roy, beating Labour's Lesley Soper. She held the seat in 2017.
- **Committee chair:** Justice and Electoral Committee (2014–17).
- **Conservation spokesperson (2018–20):**
  - Opposed the proposed tahr cull and supported recreational whitebaiting.
  - Promoted a bill to regulate shark-cage diving.
- **Retirement:** announced in February 2020. Penny Simmonds replaced her as the candidate.

**Roles held:**

- MP for Invercargill (2014–2020)
- National spokesperson for conservation (2018–2020)
- Chair, Justice and Electoral Committee (2014–2017)

## Record & notable actions

- **Valedictory (July 2020):** received a standing ovation for a speech criticising media coverage of her and calling Jami-Lee Ross a "predator".
- **Conscience votes:** voted against the End of Life Choice and abortion bills.

## Controversies

- **Jami-Lee Ross affair (2018–19):**
  - In January 2019 she was named as the MP who had had an affair with Ross. Police examined a text she had sent him ("You deserve to die") and took no further action.
  - She later described Ross as abusive and criticised how the media had treated her.
