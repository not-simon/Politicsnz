## Current status

Speaker of the House since 5 December 2023. He was elected unopposed after Adrian Rurawhe declined Te Pāti Māori's nomination. He has been Father of the House since October 2022, having sat continuously since 1996. He is a list MP and #9 on National's 2026 list (list-only).

## Biography

- **Early life:** born on 4 February 1956 in Christchurch, the eldest of five children of a sawmiller. He is Roman Catholic and attended St Bede's College.
- **Building and teaching:** worked in the family timber business and qualified as a builder. He then retrained as a teacher and taught woodwork, technical drawing and te reo Māori at Ellesmere College and St Bede's for about 12 years.
- **Local politics:** elected to the Riccarton–Wigram community board in 1989.

## Political career

**Elections**
- Lost Sydenham to Jim Anderton in 1993.
- Won Ilam in 1996 and held it until 2020, when Labour's Sarah Pallett unexpectedly won it by 3,463 votes. He returned on the list at #2. ([Stuff](https://www.stuff.co.nz/national/politics/123128975/election-2020-sarah-pallett-promises-more-inclusive-style-after-defeating-gerry-brownlee-in-ilam))
- In 2022 he announced he would stand list-only in 2023, which was widely read as a bid for the Speakership. ([NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/nz/politics/gerry-brownlee-retiring-from-ilam-race-next-election-hints-at-speaker/TTH3LXSDUDHWH73SZRLLI7P5CI/))

**In opposition, 1999–2008**
- Junior whip, then Shadow Leader of the House from 2001.
- Deputy leader under Don Brash (2003–06), after Nick Smith stood aside.
- Spokesperson for Māori Affairs after Brash's 2004 Orewa speech.

**Fifth National Government, 2008–17**
- Leader of the House throughout.
- Minister of Economic Development and of Energy and Resources (2008–11).
- Minister for Canterbury Earthquake Recovery after the 2010–11 quakes, leading CERA and taking emergency recovery legislation through the House in one sitting.
- Minister of Transport (2011–14), Defence (2014–17) and Foreign Affairs (May–October 2017).

**Since 2017**
- Shadow Leader of the House (2018–20).
- Deputy leader under Judith Collins (July–November 2020).

## Record & notable actions

- **Canterbury recovery:** the government's lead minister after the earthquakes. He pushed demolitions, saying "old dungers… are going under the hammer", and was criticised by heritage advocates.
- **Mining (2009):** a proposal to open parts of Schedule 4 conservation land, including in Mount Aspiring National Park, to mining was abandoned after public opposition.
- **Speakership:**
  - Ruled in December 2024, against the Clerk's advice and an Assistant Speaker's ruling, that the Fast-track Approvals Bill's project lists were not private legislation. Labour then said it had lost confidence in him. ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/536285/fast-track-projects-speaker-rules-no-private-benefit-in-list), [RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/536361/labour-says-it-has-lost-confidence-in-speaker-of-the-house-gerry-brownlee))
  - Told MPs to stop complaining about the use of "Aotearoa".
  - Ejected Kieran McAnulty (Feb 2026) and Chlöe Swarbrick (Aug 2025 and Aug 2026) over their criticism of his rulings on Winston Peters, and Ginny Andersen and David Seymour (Sept 2026). ([NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/nz/politics/labour-mp-kieran-mcanulty-booted-from-the-house-over-speakers-handling-of-winston-peters-racist-remark/MR2AVPBXJBHCJH7AFODN3MICJA/), [RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/politics/883243/chloe-swarbrick-kicked-out-of-house-after-challenging-speaker-s-double-standards), [Newstalk ZB](https://www.newstalkzb.co.nz/news/politics/speaker-gerry-brownlee-boots-two-mps-from-house-within-minutes/))
  - Initially barred Newsroom journalist Aaron Smale from the November 2024 abuse-in-care apology, then reversed the decision.
- **Conscience votes:** consistently conservative. Voted against marriage equality, End of Life Choice and abortion reform (see table).

## Controversies

- **Neil Abel (1999/2002):** in 2002 a District Court found he had used "excessive and unnecessary force" removing a protester from National's 1999 campaign launch, and he was ordered to pay NZ$8,500 damages.
- **Finland remarks (2012):** said in Parliament that Finland "can hardly feed the people who live there… and has no respect for women", drawing a response from Finland's foreign minister.
- **"Carping and moaning" (2012):** apologised after using the phrase about residents in Christchurch's TC3 zone.
- **Airport security breach (2014):** fined NZ$2,000 by the CAA after he and aides bypassed screening at Christchurch Airport. ([Stuff](http://www.stuff.co.nz/national/politics/63297518/Gerry-Brownlee-fined-for-airport-security-breach))
- **COVID comments (Aug 2020):** accused of promoting misinformation by suggesting the government was withholding information about the Auckland outbreak. He denied being a conspiracy theorist. ([Newshub](https://www.newshub.co.nz/home/politics/2020/08/coronavirus-gerry-brownlee-denies-covid-19-questions-make-him-a-conspiracy-theorist.html))
- **Xinjiang (2022):** criticised for saying China was "dealing with a terrorist problem" in response to the UN Xinjiang report.
