## Current status

MP for Kaipara ki Mahurangi, leaving Parliament at the 2026 election.

- **Leadership challenge:** at the emergency caucus meeting on 12 August 2026 he challenged Christopher Luxon for the leadership and lost a confidence vote; the margin was not released.
- **Sacking:** Luxon then removed all his portfolios, saying it was "untenable" for him to stay a minister. Defence, Veterans, Space, GCSB and NZSIS went to Paul Goldsmith, and Building and Construction to Simon Watts.
- **Withdrawal:** Penk then withdrew as a 2026 candidate. He had been #14 on National's list. Jessica Rowe replaced him as the Kaipara ki Mahurangi candidate.
- **Title:** granted "The Honourable" for life on 17 August 2026.

([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/politics/975747/chris-penk-loses-ministerial-portfolios-after-luxon-leadership-challenge), [NZ Gazette](https://gazette.govt.nz/notice/id/2026-vr4650))

## Biography

- **Early life:** born in 1979 in West Auckland; attended Kelston Boys' High School.
- **Education:** BA (1999) and LLB (Hons, 2010) from the University of Auckland.
- **Military:** Royal New Zealand Navy officer on HMNZS *Te Kaha* and aide-de-camp to Governor-General Dame Silvia Cartwright. He then served four years in the Australian Defence Force.
- **Law:** property lawyer.
- **Personal:** married to journalist Kim Choe.

## Political career

- **2014:** stood in Kelston and lost to Carmel Sepuloni.
- **2017:** succeeded John Key in Helensville, winning by 14,608 votes. He was courts spokesperson.
- **2020:** won the new Kaipara ki Mahurangi seat by 4,435 votes. He was shadow Attorney-General and chaired the Regulations Review Committee.
- **December 2021:** elected Senior Whip under Luxon.
- **2023:** held his seat by 19,459 votes and became a minister outside Cabinet: Building and Construction, Land Information, Veterans, and Associate Defence and Immigration.
- **2025–26:** added Small Business and Manufacturing (January 2025). He entered Cabinet in the April 2026 reshuffle as Minister of Defence, GCSB, NZSIS and Space, after Judith Collins' departure.

## Record & notable actions

- **Building:**
  - Made remote inspections the default for building consents (2024).
  - Proposed easing 2023 insulation standards, which he said added NZ$40–50k per house; the Green Building Council objected. ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/522414/dismay-at-plan-to-revert-to-less-strict-insulation-standards))
  - Announced a building-consent overhaul (August 2025): proportional liability to replace joint-and-several liability, and fewer consent authorities. ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/570308/watch-major-shake-up-of-building-consents-announced))
- **Immigration:**
  - Overturned Immigration NZ's decision and granted a visa to US commentator Candace Owens (December 2024). The Spinoff reported that the Free Speech Union lobbied for this. ([Stuff](https://www.stuff.co.nz/culture/360521199/candace-owens-allowed-new-zealand-after-visa-u-turn), [The Spinoff](https://thespinoff.co.nz/politics/11-03-2025/candace-owens-was-denied-a-visa-to-new-zealand-and-then-a-minister-stepped-in))
  - Granted residency to 18-year-old New Zealand-born Daman Kumar after a public campaign, while his parents' deportation went ahead. ([NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/nz/18-year-old-daman-kumar-who-was-facing-deporation-despite-living-in-new-zealand-all-his-life-granted-residency/5JEUZ55DH5A4VKZ3KHVKY2NJEE/))
- **Land information (place names):** declined to rename Petone as Pito-one, despite support from the Geographic Board, council and iwi, and declined macrons for Rangitīkei and Manawatū districts. He also declined renaming Russell as Kororāreka. ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/national/537014/chris-penk-rejects-bid-to-change-petone-s-name-to-pito-one))
- **Veterans:** received Willie Apiata's Victoria Cross, which Apiata handed over to lobby for a broader legal definition of veteran. Penk then widened the definition and created a national veterans' day, but did not expand entitlements.
- **Defence:**
  - Launched a Defence Industry Strategy with Collins (2025).
  - Signed a statement of intent on defence cooperation with Papua New Guinea (July 2026).
  - Appointed former Supreme Court Justice Mark O'Regan to chair the inquiry into the January 2026 Mount Maunganui and Papamoa landslides.
- **Conscience votes:** voted against the End of Life Choice and abortion bills. On the 2022 conversion-practices ban he voted against at first reading, for it at second reading, and against at third reading; he was one of eight opponents at the final vote.
