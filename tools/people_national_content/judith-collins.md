## Current status

President of the Law Commission (Te Aka Matua o te Ture). She announced her appointment on 28 January 2026, gave her valedictory speech on 12 May 2026, and her resignation from Parliament took effect on 14 May 2026. Because it was close to the general election, no by-election was held for Papakura. ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/585206/national-s-judith-collins-retires-from-politics-appointed-law-commission-president))

In the April 2026 reshuffle her portfolios were redistributed:
- Attorney-General went to Chris Bishop.
- Defence, GCSB, NZSIS and Space went to Chris Penk.
- Public Service and Digitising Government went to Paul Goldsmith.

## Biography

- **Early life:** born on 24 February 1959 in Hamilton, the youngest of six children of Walton dairy farmers. Educated at Matamata College, the University of Canterbury and the University of Auckland (LLB, LLM (Hons), MTaxS).
- **Law career:**
  - Commercial, property, employment and tax lawyer; ran her own firm, Judith Collins & Associates (1990–2000).
  - President of the Auckland District Law Society and Vice-President of the NZ Law Society.
  - Chair of the Casino Control Authority and a director of Housing NZ.
- **Personal:** married to David Wong-Tung, a former police officer of Samoan-Chinese heritage; one son. She describes herself as a liberal Anglican.
- **Memoir:** *Pull No Punches* (2020).

## Political career

**Early years**
- **2002:** elected MP for Clevedon.
- **2005:** became social welfare spokesperson after Katherine Rich was demoted over the Orewa speech.
- **Agent Orange:** campaigned for the inquiry into Vietnam veterans' exposure, which led to the 2004 government apology.
- **2008:** moved to Papakura, which she held until 2026 (majority 13,519 in 2023).

**Fifth National Government**
- 2008–11: Police, Corrections and Veterans' Affairs. Her plan to crush boy racers' cars earned her the nickname "Crusher Collins".
- 2011–14: Justice and ACC.
- Resigned in August 2014 (see Controversies) and returned to Cabinet in December 2015 with Police and Corrections.
- 2016–17: Revenue and Energy under Bill English.

**Leadership bids**
- 2016: stood for the leadership, then withdrew in favour of English.
- 2018: stood and lost to Simon Bridges.
- 14 July 2020: elected leader after Todd Muller resigned. National then lost 23 seats at the 2020 election, falling to 33 seats, its second-worst result.
- 25 November 2021: removed by a caucus no-confidence vote, the day after she suddenly demoted Bridges over a historical remark to Jacqui Dean. ([Stuff](https://www.stuff.co.nz/national/politics/300462440/simon-bridges-demoted-by-judith-collins-over-alleged-historical-comments-to-a-female-colleague))

**2023–26**
- Seven portfolios: Attorney-General, Defence (the first woman to hold it), GCSB, NZSIS, Science, Space, and Digitising Government.
- Appointed King's Counsel in December 2023.
- Added Public Service in January 2025.

## Record & notable actions

- **Defence:**
  - Announced the NZ$12b Defence Capability Plan, aiming for 2% of GDP (April 2025). ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/557432/government-unveils-12-billion-defence-capability-plan))
  - Signed a Visiting Forces Agreement with the Philippines (April 2025). ([Philstar](https://www.philstar.com/headlines/2025/04/30/2439523/philippines-new-zealand-sign-visiting-forces-agreement))
  - Ordered the purchase of new maritime helicopters and A321XLR jets.
  - Re-formed No. 62 Squadron as the NZDF's first space unit.
  - Visited Kyiv (September 2025).
  - Launched the Defence Industry Strategy, which encourages weapons exports.
  - Cancelled NZDF holiday leave to cover striking civilian guards (2024).
- **Science:**
  - Announced the end of the ban on genetic modification outside the lab, via a new gene-technology regulator (August 2024). ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/524990/ban-on-genetic-modification-and-genetic-engineering-outside-lab-to-end-government-announces))
  - Ended Marsden Fund grants for the humanities and social sciences (December 2024). ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/national/535669/government-s-marsden-fund-cuts-all-humanities-social-sciences-research-funding-slashed))
- **Attorney-General:** her Bill of Rights report warned that the government's plan to close enrolment before election day could breach the Bill of Rights.
- **Privileges Committee:** as chair, presided over the case against three Te Pāti Māori MPs for their November 2024 haka during the Treaty Principles Bill vote. They received the harshest suspensions the committee had ever recommended. ([Stuff](https://www.stuff.co.nz/politics/360689225/te-pati-maori-receive-harshest-punishment-privileges-committees-ever-handed-out))
- **Earlier record:**
  - Introduced tasers nationwide and banned smoking in prisons.
  - Steered the Alcohol Reform Act 2012, which critics said watered down the Law Commission's recommendations.
- **Conscience votes:**
  - Voted for marriage equality (2013), End of Life Choice and abortion reform, despite opposing civil unions in 2004.
  - Instructed National MPs to oppose the 2021 conversion-practices bill at first reading.

## Controversies

- **Oravida (2014):** endorsed milk from Oravida, a company her husband directs, during an official trip to China. John Key put her on a "final warning".
- **Dirty Politics (2014):** Nicky Hager's book showed she had passed information about a public servant to blogger Cameron Slater. She resigned from Cabinet in August 2014 after an email suggested she had tried to undermine SFO director Adam Feeley. The Chisholm inquiry later cleared her of that allegation. ([Scoop](https://www.scoop.co.nz/stories/PO2007/S00190/press-advisory-on-judith-collins-and-the-book-dirty-politics.htm))
- **David Bain compensation (2012):** Bain sought judicial review after she commissioned a peer review of Justice Binnie's report recommending compensation, without giving his lawyers a copy. ([NZ Herald](http://www.nzherald.co.nz/david-bain-case/news/article.cfm?c_id=1500919&objectid=10853509))
- **Leader of the Opposition:**
  - Was ridiculed for underestimating the price of cheese (2020). ([Newshub](https://www.newshub.co.nz/home/politics/2020/07/watch-national-leader-judith-collins-shocks-the-project-host-with-claim-1kg-block-of-cheese-costs-5.html))
  - Called Siouxsie Wiles a "big, fat hypocrite" over a beach visit that the Director-General of Health said was within the rules (2021). ([ODT](https://www.odt.co.nz/news/national/collins-calls-dr-siouxsie-wiles-big-fat-hypocrite))
  - Called for a referendum on the use of "Aotearoa".
  - Opposed what she called "separatism", which Te Pāti Māori called racist.
