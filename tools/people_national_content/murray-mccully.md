## Current status

Former MP. He announced in December 2016 that he would not stand in 2017, and stayed Foreign Minister until 2 May 2017, when Gerry Brownlee took over. His activities since then are not detailed in the sources consulted.

## Biography

- **Early life and education:** born 19 February 1953 in Whangārei. He attended Dargaville High School and studied law at the University of Auckland and Victoria University of Wellington.
- **Career:** barrister and solicitor.
- **Personal:** he had a long relationship with journalist Jane Clifton; they have two sons.

## Political career

- **Early contests:** cut Richard Prebble's majority in Auckland Central to 289 in 1975.
- **Electorates:** won East Coast Bays in 1987, moved to Albany in 1996 and returned to East Coast Bays in 2002.
- **Fourth National Government:** ministerial roles from 1991 in Customs, Tourism, Housing, Immigration and Sport.
- **Reputation:** known as a party strategist and "fix-it man". He was a key figure in National's 2001–03 leadership changes, backing Bill English and then Don Brash.
- **Fifth National Government:** Minister of Foreign Affairs (2008–2017), as well as Sport and Recreation and Rugby World Cup 2011.

## Record & notable actions

- **Foreign policy:**
  - Signed the Wellington Declaration with Hillary Clinton (2010).
  - Led the campaign that won New Zealand a UN Security Council seat for 2015–16.
  - Played a central role in co-sponsoring UN Security Council Resolution 2334 (December 2016) on Israeli settlements.
  - Put particular emphasis on Pacific relations.
- **Conscience votes:** voted against civil unions (2004) and marriage equality (2013).

## Controversies

- **Tourism Board payouts (1999):** resigned as Tourism Minister in April 1999 after the Auditor-General found payouts to departing Tourism Board members "unlawful". The report accepted the payouts were made in good faith on advice.
