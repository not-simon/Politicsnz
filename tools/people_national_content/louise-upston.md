## Current status

Leader of the House since 2 April 2026, replacing Chris Bishop. She has been Minister for Social Development and Employment since November 2023, and also holds Disability Issues (since April 2024) and Tourism and Hospitality (since January 2025). She has been MP for Taupō since 2008 and is #7 on National's 2026 list.

In September 2026, MSD released a review and apologised after new "Confirming Your Circumstances" checks under the Mandatory Reviews Act 2025 wrongly suspended payments, including Winter Energy Payments for about 16,000 superannuitants and veterans. Upston apologised on behalf of the government. ([MSD](https://www.msd.govt.nz/about-msd-and-our-work/newsroom/2026/findings-of-review-into-suspension-of-some-payments.html))

## Biography

- **Early life:** born Louise McGill on 14 March 1971 on Auckland's North Shore. She grew up in East Coast Bays and attended Rangitoto College.
- **Business career:** left law school at 19 after her mother died and founded the events and project-management consultancy McGill Manning. She later earned an MBA from Waikato Management School.
- **Personal:** has spoken of the hardship of being a single mother in the 1990s. She has three children. In 2025 she married Hamish Craig. She lives near Karapiro.

## Political career

- **2008:** won Taupō from Labour cabinet minister Mark Burton.
- **Re-elections:** held Taupō with majorities of 14,115 (2011), 15,406 (2014), 14,335 (2017), 5,119 (2020) and 16,505 (2023).
- **Whip:** junior government whip (2011–13), then chief whip (2013–14).
- **Minister outside Cabinet (2014–16):** Women, Land Information, and Associate Local Government and Tertiary Education.
- **Cabinet under Bill English (2016–17):** Minister of Corrections and associate minister for forestry and education.
- **Opposition (2017–23):** social development spokesperson under five leaders, plus employment and child-poverty roles.
- **2023:** became Minister for Social Development and Employment, Child Poverty Reduction, and Community and Voluntary Sector.

## Record & notable actions

- **Member's bill:** her Criminal Procedure (Mentally Impaired Persons) Amendment Act 2021, introduced as the "Rights for Victims of Insane Offenders" bill, passed unanimously in December 2021. It lets courts find that a person found not guilty by reason of insanity "caused" the harm. She developed it after a constituent's brother was killed. ([NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/nz/graeme-moyle-wants-victims-and-families-notified-when-an-insane-offender-is-released/LGMNZYZA7NCCEAMQHNECPTOJNQ/))
- **Welfare:**
  - Brought in a "traffic light" obligations and sanctions system (2024–25) with six-monthly Jobseeker reapplication, payment cards and community-work sanctions.
  - Introduced parental-income testing for 18–19-year-old jobseekers whose parents earn over NZ$65,000, from November 2026. ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/575025/jobseeker-parents-earning-more-than-65k-must-support-18-19yo-children))
  - Despite the government's targets, the number of people on benefits reached a 12-year high (427,236) in December 2025. She attributed this to economic conditions she said were inherited from Labour. ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/589310/beneficiary-numbers-soar-to-12-year-high-despite-government-s-reduction-promise))
- **Disability:**
  - Moved disability support delivery from Whaikaha to MSD (August 2024), criticised by disability advocates. ([1News](https://www.1news.co.nz/2024/08/15/disability-community-shell-shocked-by-ministry-changes/))
  - Raised fines for illegally parking in disabled spaces from NZ$150 to NZ$750.
  - Reversed some of the 2024 flexible-funding restrictions from April 2026. ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/589342/we-re-just-meant-to-say-thank-you-government-u-turns-on-disability-funding))
- **Tourism:** put an extra NZ$13.5m into Tourism NZ marketing (2025) and dropped the requirement for certified translations from overseas visitors.
- **Corrections (2017):** required all corrections officers to carry pepper spray and started prisoner mental-health pilots.
- **Conscience votes:** a conservative record.
  - Voted against marriage equality, End of Life Choice and abortion reform (see table).
  - Was one of seven MPs to vote against the second reading of the conversion-practices bill, but voted for it at third reading.

## Controversies

- **Minister for Women (2015):**
  - Criticised for declining to comment on John Key's ponytail-pulling incident.
  - Rejected removing GST from menstrual products.
  - Said in 2014 she was not a feminist.
- **Bill withdrawn (July 2024):** a government social-security bill had to be withdrawn from the Order Paper when no minister moved it in her absence.
- **Gloriavale visit (Jan 2026):** said she saw nothing that caused concern for children's safety. A lawyer for Gloriavale leavers called for her resignation. ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/national/588869/lawyer-calls-for-louise-upston-to-resign-after-being-duped-by-gloriavale-leaders))
- **Payment suspensions (Aug–Sept 2026):** see Current status.
