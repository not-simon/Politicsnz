## Current status

Former MP. He returned to the bar in January 2019 and practises at Bankside Chambers in Auckland. His recent work has included:
- acting for Louisa Wall in her 2020 challenge over her Labour deselection;
- acting for Ngāi Tahu in its freshwater case against the Crown (2025);
- advising Te Pāti Māori MPs on their 2025 Privileges Committee summons.

## Biography

- **Early life:** born in 1956 and raised in Khandallah, Wellington. He was head prefect at St Patrick's College in 1974, and joined National that year after a conversation with Sir Keith Holyoake.
- **Education:** BA in Latin and French and an LLM from Victoria University of Wellington.
- **Legal career:**
  - Partner at Brandon Brookfield and then Bell Gully (1991–2003).
  - At Bell Gully he spent years litigating Ngāi Tahu's Treaty claims against the Crown, leading up to the 1997 settlement.
  - Co-author of *McGechan on Procedure*.
  - Made QC in 2012.
- **Arts:** chaired Creative New Zealand's Arts Board (1998–2001) and sat on the NZSO board.

## Political career

- **Entry:** entered Parliament in 2005 as a list MP.
- **Electorate contests:** stood in Mana and then Rongotai. He had no wish to be an electorate MP and joked he would ask for a recount if he won.
- **Fifth National Government:** Attorney-General and Minister for Treaty of Waitangi Negotiations (2008–17), as well as Arts, the GCSB/NZSIS (from 2014) and Associate Māori Development.
- **Retirement:** retired from Parliament on 30 January 2019; Agnes Loheni replaced him.

## Record & notable actions

- **Treaty settlements:** completed 59 settlements in nine years, an unprecedented number. Academic Margaret Mutu, who negotiated Ngāti Kahu's settlement with him, called him the "best qualified" settlements minister.
- **International Court of Justice:** represented New Zealand in the case against Japanese whaling.
- **Pukeahu:** legislated for the Pukeahu National War Memorial Park.
- **Privileges Committee:** chaired the committee from 2012 to 2017.
- **Conscience votes:** although openly gay, he voted against the 2013 marriage-equality bill, saying he opposed state involvement in marriage.
