## Current status

Minister of Police, Corrections and Emergency Management and Recovery since November 2023. He has also held Ethnic Communities since January 2025, and Sport and Recreation since 2025, when he took over from Chris Bishop. He is MP for Whangaparāoa and #8 on National's 2026 list.

## Biography

- **Early life:** born on 22 May 1968 on Auckland's North Shore and grew up partly at RNZAF Base Whenuapai. His grandfather, Air Commodore Frank Gill, was later a National cabinet minister. He left Rosmini College at 15 to work as a farmhand.
- **NZ Police:** joined at 21 and served 13 years, including as a dog handler and in the Armed Offenders Squad. He was injured several times on duty, including in a confrontation with Mongrel Mob members in Gisborne.
- **Overseas security career:**
  - From 2003, security contractor in Iraq with Control Risks. In 2004 he and his team were besieged for five days at An Nasiriyah.
  - Then six years in Kuwait as CEO and shareholder of Threat Management Group, a security subsidiary of Agility Logistics.
  - Sold the business in 2010.
- **Personal:** married four times, most recently to Sarah Mattson in 2026. He has two children and three stepchildren.

## Political career

- **2011:** won National's selection for Rodney, replacing Speaker Lockwood Smith, and won the seat by over 12,000 votes over Conservative leader Colin Craig.
- **2014–16:** chaired the Foreign Affairs, Defence and Trade Committee.
- **Under Bill English:**
  - December 2016: Minister for Land Information and Statistics, outside Cabinet.
  - May 2017: entered Cabinet as Minister of Defence.
- **Opposition:** spokesperson for defence, justice and Pike River re-entry, among other roles.
- **July 2020:** stood for the leadership after Todd Muller resigned and lost to Judith Collins.
- **Whangaparāoa:** won the new seat in 2020 by 7,823 votes and in 2023 by 23,376. ([Electoral Commission](https://archive.electionresults.govt.nz/electionresults_2023/electorate-details-63.html))

## Record & notable actions

- **Policing:**
  - Set out the government's expectations of Police (Dec 2023), especially on gangs.
  - Promised 500 extra frontline officers by the end of 2025. The target date was later pushed to mid-2026. ([Beehive](https://www.beehive.govt.nz/release/500-more-police-improve-public-safety), [NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/nz/politics/projected-date-to-reach-500-more-cops-relies-on-incredibly-ambitious-assumption-labour/66FKWIU3C5FDZGQRISUXFYSOZE/))
  - With Paul Goldsmith, passed the Gangs Act 2024: a patch ban that also applies at funerals and tangi, plus dispersal and non-consorting orders. ([1News](https://www.1news.co.nz/2024/09/19/gang-patch-legislation-passed-into-law/))
  - Ended funding for the Mongrel Mob-linked Kahukura drug-rehabilitation programme.
- **Youth justice:** set up the military-style academy (boot camp) pilot for serious young offenders (2024). Psychologists and researchers said the evidence shows boot camps do not reduce reoffending; Mitchell said he "completely disagrees" with the critics. ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/national/510963/minister-completely-disagrees-with-boot-camp-critics))
- **Corrections:**
  - NZ$1.9b in Budget 2024 for 470 new corrections officers and 810 extra beds at Waikeria Prison.
  - Corrections Amendment Act 2024, which opens rehabilitation programmes to remand prisoners. The Treaty clause from Labour's version of the bill was removed.
  - In 2025 floated replacing short prison sentences with longer ones so prisoners can access rehabilitation. Critics warned this would sharply increase prisoner numbers. ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/national/559975/mark-mitchell-wants-short-prison-sentences-scrapped-in-hope-of-reducing-reoffending))
- **Emergency management:** responded to the Cyclone Gabrielle recovery, the 2024 Otago floods and the May 2025 Christchurch/Selwyn flooding (state of emergency). Began work on a national buy-out framework for weather-affected homes.
- **Sport:** ordered Sport NZ to scrap its transgender-inclusion guidelines for community sport (July 2025). Labour and the Greens criticised the decision. ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/567827/government-orders-sport-nz-to-scrap-transgender-inclusive-community-sport-guidelines))
- **Conscience votes:** voted against marriage equality (2013), saying in 2018 he would now vote differently, and for the End of Life Choice and abortion bills (see table). ([Newshub](https://www.newshub.co.nz/home/politics/2018/02/national-leadership-hopeful-mark-mitchell-on-gay-marriage-and-war.html))

## Controversies

- **Dirty Politics (2014):** Nicky Hager's book suggested strategist Simon Lusk had helped Mitchell's 2011 selection campaign, with blogger Cameron Slater attacking a rival. Mitchell denied paying either man but said Lusk had advised him on speeches and brochures.
- **Police complaint (1997):** accused of assaulting a gang member in custody while a police officer; cleared by the Police Complaints Authority.
- **Iraq:** he has declined to say whether he killed anyone while working as a security contractor in Iraq.
