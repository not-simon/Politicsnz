## Current status

Prime Minister of New Zealand since 27 November 2023, leading the National–ACT–NZ First coalition (New Zealand's first formal three-party coalition). He is National's leader (since 30 November 2021) and MP for Botany (since 2020), and heads National's 2026 list at #1 while re-contesting Botany.

2026 has been turbulent for his leadership. After a 1News-Verian poll on 20 April 2026 put National at about 30%, Luxon moved a motion of confidence in himself at caucus on 21 April and won; the margin was not released, but was later reported by National MPs as about 27–22. On 12 August 2026, at an emergency caucus meeting, he again won a confidence vote, this time against a challenge from Defence Minister Chris Penk. Luxon then stripped Penk of his portfolios, and Penk withdrew from the election. ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/592984/prime-minister-christopher-luxon-survives-confidence-vote), [RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/politics/975747/chris-penk-says-he-will-not-seek-re-election-after-losing-ministerial-portfolios), [Stuff](https://www.stuff.co.nz/politics/361019447/how-nationals-secret-vote-works-after-chris-penks-failed-leadership-bid))

## Biography

- Born on 19 July 1970 in Christchurch and raised in a Catholic family. He grew up in Christchurch and in Howick (Auckland), and went to Christchurch Boys' High School. He earned a Master of Commerce from the University of Canterbury (1989–92).
- **Unilever (1993–2011):** he started as a management trainee in Wellington and then worked in Sydney, London and Chicago. In 2008 he became president and CEO of Unilever Canada.
- **Air New Zealand (2011–2019):** he joined as group general manager in 2011 and was named CEO in June 2012, taking over at the end of that year and serving until 2019. The airline posted record profits in that time, helped by a tourism boom, cuts to under-performing regional routes and job cuts. He sat on the board of Virgin Australia from 2014 to 2016, while Air NZ held a stake. In 2015 he was named Deloitte Top 200 CEO of the Year.
- He describes himself as an evangelical/non-denominational Christian and has said "my faith is personal to me. It is not in itself a political agenda". He is married to Amanda, and they have two children. His declared wealth (March 2024) was estimated at NZ$21–30 million, including several properties.

## Political career

- **2019:** selected for the safe National seat of Botany after Jami-Lee Ross left the party. ([Stuff](https://www.stuff.co.nz/national/117151149/national-chooses-christopher-luxon-as-botany-mp))
- **2020 election:** won Botany with about 52% of the vote while National lost many seats. He was then spokesperson for local government, research, science, manufacturing and land information.
- **30 Nov 2021:** became National leader unopposed after Judith Collins was removed and Simon Bridges withdrew, about a year after entering Parliament. ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/456886/christopher-luxon-set-to-be-new-national-party-leader-as-simon-bridges-withdraws))
- **2023 election:** National won 38.1% of the party vote and 48 seats, and Luxon held Botany with about 67%. He signed separate coalition agreements with ACT and NZ First on 24 November 2023 and was sworn in on 27 November. ([DPMC](https://www.dpmc.govt.nz/publications/co-24-2-national-act-and-new-zealand-first-coalition-government-consultation-and-operating-arrangements))
- **Cabinet reshuffles:**
  - April 2024: Melissa Lee was removed from Cabinet, and Penny Simmonds lost the Disability Issues portfolio.
  - 19 January 2025: Simeon Brown took Health from Shane Reti, and Chris Bishop took Transport.
  - 2 April 2026: Penk entered Cabinet in Defence, Simmonds returned to Cabinet, and Bishop became Attorney-General but lost Leader of the House and campaign chair to Louise Upston and Simeon Brown. Media widely read the Bishop changes as linked to leadership tensions, which both men denied. ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/515100/media-minister-melissa-lee-demoted-from-cabinet-penny-simmonds-stripped-of-portfolio), [RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/539370/as-it-happened-luxon-announces-cabinet-reshuffle-shane-reti-loses-health-portfolio-to-simeon-brown), [Beehive](https://www.beehive.govt.nz/release/pm-refreshes-ministerial-team))

## Record & notable actions

As Prime Minister, the government he leads has done the following (see [`../../track-record.md`](../../track-record.md) for the party-level record):

- **Economy and spending:**
  - Delivered the NZ$14.7b tax relief package in Budget 2024.
  - Made public-service savings. RNZ estimated about 9,500 public-sector roles were cut by May 2025. ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/politics/562350/how-many-public-sector-jobs-have-really-been-axed))
  - The economy was in recession through 2024. Unemployment rose from 3.7% (June 2023) to about 5.2% (late 2025).
- **Law and order:**
  - Reinstated "three strikes" sentencing.
  - Banned gang patches in public.
  - Piloted a military-style academy (boot camp) for serious young offenders.
  - Ended funding for section 27 cultural reports.
- **Treaty and Māori policy:**
  - Supported ACT's Treaty Principles Bill to first reading only, as the coalition agreement required. National then voted it down.
  - Scaled back co-governance arrangements.
- **Local government and water:** replaced Three Waters with "Local Water Done Well".
- **Education:** with Erica Stanford, announced a new maths curriculum (2024) and the replacement of NCEA (announced August 2025).
- **Abuse in care:** delivered the state's formal apology to survivors of abuse in state and faith-based care (November 2024).
- **Welfare:**
  - Introduced a "traffic-light" sanctions regime for beneficiaries.
  - Required six-monthly Jobseeker reapplication.
  - Brought in parental-income testing for 18–19-year-olds (from November 2026).
- **Foreign and defence policy:**
  - Moved closer to Five Eyes partners and showed interest in AUKUS Pillar II.
  - Announced NZ$12b of defence investment over four years (April 2025), aiming for about 2% of GDP.
  - Designated all of Hamas as a terrorist entity (2024). He later called Israel's conduct in Gaza "utterly, utterly appalling" (August 2025).
- **Personal positions:**
  - He has said he is personally "pro-life", but that National would not change the Abortion Legislation Act 2020.
  - He voted for the Conversion Practices Prohibition Legislation Bill (2022) and gave National MPs a conscience vote on it.
  - He describes himself as a "soft republican".
  - He was not in Parliament for the 2013, 2019 and 2020 conscience votes.

## Controversies

- **Accommodation allowance (2023–24):** he claimed about NZ$52,000 a year in accommodation support while living in his own Wellington apartment. After public criticism he stopped the claim and repaid it.
- **Te reo lessons:** as Opposition leader he used taxpayer-funded te reo Māori lessons, while his government reviewed incentive payments for public servants to learn te reo. ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/504994/christopher-luxon-s-reo-maori-lessons-paid-for-by-taxpayer))
- **Hawaii social-media post (2022):** a Facebook post implied he was in Te Puke while he was on holiday in Hawaii. He blamed a delay in his social-media team's updates.
- **"Marmite sandwich" (March 2025):** his remark about the school-lunch programme drew criticism from principals and child-poverty advocates.
- **Coalition management:** commentators and polls (e.g. April 2024) questioned how much control he has over his coalition partners, Seymour and Peters.
- **Popularity:** he has had persistently low favourability. For example, an October 2025 poll found 27% viewed him positively and 51% unfavourably. ([The Post](https://www.thepost.co.nz/politics/360853311/big-names-risk-labour-pulls-ahead-and-christopher-luxon-loses-ground))
- **Mid-2026 "gaffes":** before the August 2026 confidence vote, media reported:
  - a "parent-child mentality" remark to Rotorua business leaders, for which he apologised;
  - an unconsulted pledge to hold an MMP referendum;
  - use of an NZDF jet after a party fundraiser.
