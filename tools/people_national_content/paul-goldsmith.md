## Current status

A senior Cabinet minister. Since 27 November 2023 he has held Justice, Treaty of Waitangi Negotiations, and Arts, Culture and Heritage. He added Media and Communications in April 2024, and Public Service, Digitising Government and Pacific Peoples in April 2026. After Chris Penk's failed leadership challenge, he took over Defence, Veterans, Space, the GCSB and the NZSIS on 12–13 August 2026. He is a list MP, #6 on National's 2026 list, and is again contesting Epsom. ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/politics/975747/chris-penk-loses-ministerial-portfolios-after-luxon-leadership-challenge))

## Biography

- **Early life:** born in 1971 in Mount Eden, Auckland, to a maths teacher father and a palliative-care nurse mother. He attended Auckland Grammar School.
- **Education:** MA in history from the University of Auckland (1996), with a thesis on missionary William Colenso supervised by Dame Judith Binney.
- **Early career:**
  - Historian for the Waitangi Tribunal on the Taranaki and Wairarapa claims, from 1995.
  - Press secretary and speechwriter for Ministers John Banks and Simon Upton, then for Labour's Phil Goff.
  - From 2000, worked in public relations for Tranz Rail and the University of Auckland.
- **Author:** wrote biographies of John Banks, Don Brash, William Gallagher, Alan Gibbs and Te Hemara Tauhia, plus histories of tax in New Zealand and of Fletcher Building. Nicky Hager reported that the 2005 Brash biography was commissioned by National, contrary to Goldsmith's statement at the time. A labour historian later called his books "hagiographies" of the reform era.
- **Personal:** married to Melissa; four children. He is a pianist and holds a second-dan black belt in taekwondo.

## Political career

- **2005:** stood in Maungakiekie and lost.
- **Auckland City Council, 2007–10:** councillor for the Hobson Ward (Citizens & Ratepayers), deputy finance chair. He lost a ward race for the new Auckland Council in 2010.
- **2011 and 2014:** stood in Epsom, where National told voters to back the ACT candidate. He lost to John Banks and then David Seymour, entering Parliament on the list (#39 in 2011). He chaired the Finance and Expenditure Committee from 2013.
- **2014–16:** minister outside Cabinet for Commerce and Consumer Affairs, and Associate ACC.
- **2016–17:** in Cabinet as Minister for Science and Innovation; Tertiary Education, Skills and Employment; and Regulatory Reform.
- **Opposition, 2017–23:**
  - Spokesperson in turn for arts, economic development and transport.
  - Finance spokesperson in 2019–20, when Stuff described him as possibly the most libertarian person to hold the role.
  - Then education, and from 2021 justice.
  - He lost Epsom to Seymour again in 2017, 2020 and 2023.

## Record & notable actions

**Justice and sentencing**
- Ended legal-aid funding for section 27 cultural reports, passed under urgency in March 2024. Lawyers and a former judge criticised the change. ([1News](https://www.1news.co.nz/2024/03/06/bill-passes-to-remove-taxpayer-funding-for-cultural-reports/))
- Gang legislation, passed September 2024, which:
  - bans gang insignia in public;
  - allows dispersal notices and non-consorting orders;
  - makes gang membership an aggravating factor in sentencing. ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/528460/coalition-s-gang-legislation-passes-into-law-banning-patches-in-public-places))
- Restored "three strikes" and capped sentencing discounts. He encourages cumulative sentences for offending on bail, parole or in custody.
- Proposed coward-punch offences, tougher shoplifting and trespass penalties, and a ban on protests outside private homes (2025).
- New stalking offence, passed December 2025 and in force from 26 May 2026. ([Stuff](https://www.stuff.co.nz/nz-news/360892644/stalking-law-passes-carries-five-year-jail-term))
- Signed Kim Dotcom's extradition order (July 2024). ([BBC](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/c5y363lw204o))
- Halted work on hate-speech law (April 2024). ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/national/513176/muslim-organisation-questions-why-hate-speech-law-reforms-abandoned))
- Introduced a foreign-interference offence (Nov 2024). ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/533983/bill-to-stop-foreign-interference-undermining-core-new-zealand-interests-introduced-to-parliament))
- Set up a retail-crime advisory group in 2024. It was disbanded early in 2026 after controversy over its costs and resignations. ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/top/586438/retail-crime-advisory-group-disbands-four-months-early))

**Electoral law**
- The Electoral Amendment Act, passed 17 December 2025:
  - reinstates a blanket ban on prisoner voting, against High Court, Electoral Commission and Waitangi Tribunal advice;
  - ends election-day enrolment by closing enrolment about 13 days before the election;
  - bans free food and entertainment near polling places. ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/559446/prisoner-voting-ban-to-be-brought-back-paul-goldsmith), [RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/582069/electoral-amendment-bill-passes-its-third-reading-in-parliament))

**Treaty**
- Said the Crown would not sign a settlement with Te Whānau-ā-Apanui that included an "agree to disagree" sovereignty clause (June 2025). ([NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/nz/politics/almost-completed-te-whanau-a-apanui-settlement-now-increasingly-unlikely-under-this-government-after-ministers-sovereignty-stance/OKTV23XBIBDYNIVOIK6NKCSTRI/))
- Led the coalition review of Treaty-principles clauses in legislation, a National–NZ First coalition commitment. In April–May 2026 he confirmed clauses would be repealed or amended in multiple Acts, despite Ministry of Justice warnings of significant risk and objections from the Iwi Chairs Forum. ([The Spinoff](https://thespinoff.co.nz/atea/21-04-2026/treaty-clauses-to-be-weakened-repealed-despite-officials-warning-of-significant-risks), [RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/593922/as-damaging-as-the-treaty-principles-bill-proposed-changes-to-treaty-clauses-revealed))

**Media and climate law (2026)**
- Announced the abolition of the Broadcasting Standards Authority (May 2026). ([1News](https://www.1news.co.nz/2026/05/06/govt-to-axe-broadcasting-standards-authority-self-regulation-signalled/))
- Announced a law change to block climate-damage lawsuits against emitters, including Mike Smith's case against Fonterra and others. Officials had advised against intervening. ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/594922/government-changes-climate-law-to-prevent-lawsuits), [RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/596461/officials-told-government-not-to-intervene-in-a-climate-court-case))

**Conscience votes**
- Voted for marriage equality (2013) and for the conversion-practices ban (2022).
- Voted against the End of Life Choice Bill.
- Voted for the abortion bill at an earlier stage but against it at third reading (2020).

## Controversies

- **"$4 billion hole" (2020):** Labour found errors in National's 2020 fiscal plan, which Goldsmith had prepared. He admitted the accounting mistake ("fair cop"). ([Stuff](https://www.stuff.co.nz/national/politics/122827807/election-2020-fair-cop--nationals-paul-goldsmith-admits-to-accounting-mistake-as-labour-points-out-4b-hole))
- **Colonisation remarks (June 2021):** said colonisation had been "on balance" good for Māori. Some National colleagues disagreed, and he declined to apologise. ([Newshub](https://www.newshub.co.nz/home/politics/2021/06/colonisation-a-good-thing-for-m-ori-on-balance-national-mp-paul-goldsmith.html), [NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/kahu/national-mps-disagree-with-goldsmith-on-colonisation-comments/4ZYOHTE5THCWIYSZZGYWJDVWQM/))
- **Homelessness (council term):** the City Mission and the Greens criticised him for asking officials to investigate removing homeless people from Auckland's CBD. ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/regional/29050/auckland-mayor-defends-councillor%27s-stand-on-homeless))
- **Brash biography (2005):** questions over whether National commissioned the book (see Biography).
