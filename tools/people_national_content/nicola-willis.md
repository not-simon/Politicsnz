## Current status

Minister of Finance since 27 November 2023, and also Minister for Economic Growth (since January 2025) and Minister for Social Investment. She has been deputy leader of the National Party since 30 November 2021. She is a list MP and is #2 on National's 2026 list without an electorate candidacy. She delivered Budget 2026 in late May 2026, forecasting a return to surplus (OBEGALx) by 2028/29 and ruling out "election-year band-aids and sugar hits". ([Treasury](https://www.treasury.govt.nz/publications/budget-speech/budget-speech-2026), [RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/politics/596610/budget-2026-at-a-glance-the-big-changes-winners-and-losers-what-you-need-to-know))

## Biography

- **Early life and education:**
  - Born on 7 March 1981 in Wellington and grew up in Point Howard, Lower Hutt.
  - Her mother was a Press Gallery journalist and her father a Bell Gully partner. Her great-great-grandfather Archibald Willis was a Liberal MP in the 1890s–1900s.
  - Attended Samuel Marsden Collegiate and King's College (Auckland).
  - Studied English literature at Victoria University of Wellington (first-class honours) and took a postgraduate journalism diploma at Canterbury.
- **Early career:** research and policy adviser to Bill English, then senior adviser to John Key in 2008. During 2008 debate preparation she played Helen Clark.
- **Business career:**
  - From 2012, government-relations manager and later general manager at Fonterra.
  - Board member of ExportNZ.
  - Director of the New Zealand Initiative (2016–17).
- **Personal:** married to Duncan Small; four children. ([NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/nz/nicola-willis-at-home-who-is-nationals-new-deputy-leader-away-from-the-spotlight/C6R64EEY4CBSIOVW7BN5TETTTE/))

## Political career

- **2017 election:** contested Wellington Central and lost to Grant Robertson, taking about 26% of the electorate vote. She was #48 on the list and just missed out on the final count.
- **3 April 2018:** entered Parliament from the list when Steven Joyce resigned. She was spokesperson for early childhood education.
- **2020 leadership change:** worked as a "numbers" organiser, with Chris Bishop, in Todd Muller's successful challenge to Simon Bridges. She became housing spokesperson, and then education spokesperson under Judith Collins.
- **2020 election:** lost Wellington Central again and returned on the list. As housing spokesperson she worked with Labour's Megan Woods on the cross-party Medium Density Residential Standards (2021).
- **30 Nov 2021:** elected deputy leader unopposed as Christopher Luxon's running mate. She became finance spokesperson in March 2022 when Bridges resigned.
- **2023 election:** lost Ōhāriu to Labour's Greg O'Connor by 1,260 votes; returned on the list. ([Electoral Commission](https://archive.electionresults.govt.nz/electionresults_2023/electorate-details-33.html))
- **Ministerial appointments:**
  - 27 Nov 2023: Finance, Public Service, Social Investment, Associate Climate Change.
  - January 2025: became the first Minister for Economic Growth and gave up the Public Service portfolio.

## Record & notable actions

- **Fiscal stance:**
  - Mini-budget (20 Dec 2023): NZ$7.47b of claimed operating savings.
  - Budget 2024: about NZ$14b of personal income-tax relief, partly funded by public-service savings and programme cuts.
  - Budget 2025: reduced the new-spending allowance to NZ$1.3b and dropped the planned Digital Services Tax.
  - Budget 2026: focused on returning to surplus.
  - Treasury forecasts (Dec 2024) showed deficits until at least 2029. She adopted the OBEGALx measure, which excludes ACC, as the headline target. ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/536933/hyefu-revealed-nicola-willis-deploys-convenient-new-measure-amid-bleak-books))
- **Interislander ferries:**
  - In December 2023 she declined KiwiRail's request for NZ$1.47b more for the iReX project, which ended it. Cancellation costs were put at about NZ$300m.
  - A replacement procurement through a new company was announced in December 2024, with new ships expected around 2029. ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/national/504650/interislander-ferry-fleet-project-to-wind-down-after-being-denied-further-government-funding))
- **Cost of living and competition:**
  - Took FamilyBoost and Working for Families changes through Budget 2024.
  - Announced an "express lane" consenting regime (2025) to bring new supermarket competitors in.
  - In 2026 accused Foodstuffs and Woolworths of a "full-court press" lobbying campaign against reforms to break up the duopoly. ([1News](https://www.1news.co.nz/2025/08/27/express-lane-reforms-to-increase-supermarket-competition-willis/))
- **Other decisions:**
  - Smallest minimum-wage rise since the 1990s, to NZ$23.50 (Dec 2024).
  - Guidance that public-service working from home is "not an entitlement" (Sept 2024).
  - Proposed relaxing government procurement rules, including the Living Wage requirement for cleaning and security contracts.
  - Announced a review of the Reserve Bank's COVID-era monetary policy (Feb 2026).
- **Member's bill:** her 2023 bill to let parents share paid parental leave and take it at the same time was blocked by Labour at first reading. ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/495025/labour-blocks-national-s-paid-parental-leave-bill-that-would-have-allowed-parents-to-share-time-off))
- **Positions:**
  - Described as a social liberal and a member of National's BlueGreens caucus.
  - Voted for the End of Life Choice and Abortion Legislation bills (see table).
  - Supported Labour's Equal Pay Amendment in 2018, but in 2025 backed the government bill restricting pay-equity claims, while calling herself a feminist. ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/560142/pay-equity-changes-two-sides-of-the-coalition-brought-out))

## Controversies

- **"$250 a fortnight" tax promise (2023):**
  - She admitted that only about 3,000 households would receive the full NZ$250 a fortnight.
  - She said she would resign if the tax plan was not delivered.
  - The foreign-buyer tax meant to help fund it was dropped in coalition talks. ([1News](https://www.1news.co.nz/2023/09/15/nicola-willis-id-quit-as-finance-minister-if-my-tax-cut-plan-fails/), [Newsroom](https://newsroom.co.nz/2023/11/24/which-policies-survived-the-negotiations-and-which-didnt/))
- **Cancer medicines (Budget 2024):** National's election promise to fund 13 cancer drugs was not met in Budget 2024. The government blamed a NZ$1.77b Pharmac funding "cliff" left by its predecessor, and Willis said the government would seek funding later. ([Newshub](https://www.newshub.co.nz/home/politics/2024/05/budget-2024-finance-minister-nicola-willis-defends-national-u-turn-on-funding-new-cancer-drugs.html))
- **Criticism of economic management:**
  - Open letters from 15 economists (Nov 2024) and 20 economists (Oct 2025) blamed fiscal restraint for prolonging the recession. Willis replied that she did not agree.
  - After a 0.9% GDP fall in the June 2025 quarter, Sir Roger Douglas and economist Robert MacCulloch called for her resignation. ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/573459/former-finance-minister-sir-roger-douglas-calls-for-finance-minister-nicola-willis-to-resign))
  - From the right, the Taxpayers' Union ran a "Nicola Fudge Co." campaign in December 2025. Willis challenged its chair, Ruth Richardson, to a debate, which did not go ahead.
