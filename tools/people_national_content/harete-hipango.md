## Current status

Former National MP, now standing for **New Zealand First**. She joined NZ First after the 2023 election. She is #7 on its 2026 list, listed by the Electoral Commission as Harete Hipango-Brownlie, and is its Whanganui candidate. Her NZ First candidate profile is at [`parties/nz-first/people/harete-hipango-brownlie`](../../../nz-first/people/harete-hipango-brownlie/).

## Biography

- **Early life:** born around 1964–65 in Whanganui and raised at Pūtiki. She is of Te Āti Haunui-a-Pāpārangi, Ngāti Apa, Ngā Rauru Kītahi, Ngāti Tamakōpiri and Ngāti Whitikaupeka descent.
- **Law:** LLB from the University of Auckland (1991). She practised law in Whanganui for over 25 years, including during the 1995 Pākaitore occupation.
- **Other roles:** Whanganui DHB member and Legal Aid Tribunal member.

## Political career

- **Whanganui (2017):** won the seat after Chester Borrows retired. She was the first Māori woman National selected for a seat it already held.
- **2020:** lost to Labour's Steph Lewis by 8,191 votes.
- **Return (June 2021):** re-entered Parliament as a list MP when Nick Smith resigned.
- **2023:** stood for National in Te Tai Hauāuru, the party's first Māori-seat candidate since 2002. She came third, and her list place of #31 was too low to return.

## Record & notable actions

- **Conscience votes:**
  - Strongly opposed the End of Life Choice and abortion bills.
  - Criticised for claiming the abortion law allowed full-term abortions.
- **Te reo:** in 2023 she joined Tama Potaka and Shane Reti in disagreeing with Simeon Brown over English-only road signs.

## Controversies

- **Spending (2021):** reported to have bought furniture and a TV with Parliamentary Service funds and had them delivered to her home. Collins said the amounts were small and had been corrected.
- **Anti-vaccination protests:** appeared at an anti-vaccination protest (November 2021) and a Voices for Freedom rally (January 2022). Luxon distanced National from the group's views.
- **Wikipedia edits (2022):** admitted asking a staffer to remove controversies from her Wikipedia article, and later said she regretted it.
- **Caucus relations (2021):** anonymous National MPs criticised her as a poor team player; Todd Muller later admitted being one of them and apologised.
