## Current status

Since the 2 April 2026 reshuffle she has been in Cabinet as Minister for Tertiary Education and Minister of Science, Innovation and Technology. She handed the Environment portfolio, which she held from 2023, to Nicola Grigg. She is MP for Invercargill and is #14 on National's official 2026 list (shown as #15 in policy.nz's earlier list). ([1News](https://www.1news.co.nz/2026/04/02/christopher-luxon-reveals-cabinet-shake-up/), [Beehive](https://www.beehive.govt.nz/release/pm-refreshes-ministerial-team))

## Biography

- **Early life:** born in September 1959 in Southland and grew up in Riversdale and Te Tipua. She attended Gore High School and has a BSc from the University of Otago. She served several years in the Territorial Force.
- **Southern Institute of Technology:** chief executive from 1997 to 2020. She introduced SIT's Zero Fees Scheme.
- **Other roles:** director of the Southland Museum and Art Gallery, Southland DHB board member and Woolf Fisher Fellow (2000).
- **Honours:** CNZM for services to education, sport and the community (2016).
- **Personal:** married with three daughters.

## Political career

- **2020:** chosen for Invercargill after Sarah Dowie retired. She narrowly held the seat for National, beating Labour's Liz Craig by 224 votes, while Labour won most regional seats. ([Stuff](https://www.stuff.co.nz/national/politics/123320312/penny-simmonds-takes-invercargill-electorate-seat))
- **In opposition:** tertiary education spokesperson and a strong critic of the Te Pūkenga polytechnic merger. She also voted against the 2021 COVID vaccination mandate law and the 2022 abortion "safe areas" law.
- **2023:** held Invercargill by 10,945 votes. She became Minister for Disability Issues, the Environment, and Tertiary Education and Skills, and Associate Social Development and Employment, all outside Cabinet.

## Record & notable actions

- **Te Pūkenga:**
  - Led the break-up of the national polytechnic Te Pūkenga.
  - Ten polytechnics regained regional governance from 1 January 2026. Others are to join a federation or await viability decisions, and Te Pūkenga is to be dissolved by 31 December 2026.
  - Workforce Development Councils are being replaced by Industry Skills Boards. ([NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/nz/politics/te-pukenga-government-restores-regional-governance-for-10-polytechnics-unwinds-merger/PTO27FLB3VBTNJVYWLIGJIU35Q/), [Beehive](https://www.beehive.govt.nz/release/regional-governance-will-return-ten-polytechnics))
- **Free speech:** with David Seymour, proposed strengthening universities' free-speech obligations (December 2024). ([Beehive](https://www.beehive.govt.nz/release/strengthening-free-speech-universities))
- **Environment (2023–26):** Minister for the Environment outside Cabinet until April 2026. Her decisions in that portfolio are not covered in the sources used for this profile.

## Controversies

- **Disability funding (March 2024):** ([RNZ: "we got it wrong"](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/512172/we-got-it-wrong-on-disability-announcement-minister-penny-simmonds))
  - Whaikaha suddenly tightened what families could spend flexible respite funding on.
  - Simmonds said the government's "coffers are not an endless open pit" and suggested some families were spending funding on "massages, overseas travel and pedicures". This caused an outcry, including a petition with more than 10,000 signatures in 24 hours.
  - Finance Minister Willis called officials in, and Cabinet took tighter control of the ministry. ([Stuff](https://www.stuff.co.nz/politics/350218178/parents-worried-their-children-will-suffer-disability-services-suddenly-cut), [Stuff](https://www.stuff.co.nz/politics/350219245/finance-minister-nicola-willis-calls-officials-after-cuts-disability-services))
  - On 24 April 2024 Luxon removed her from Disability Issues. ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/515100/media-minister-melissa-lee-demoted-from-cabinet-penny-simmonds-stripped-of-portfolio))
- **2020 election night:** Mayor Tim Shadbolt was criticised for listing her election-night party as an official mayoral engagement.
