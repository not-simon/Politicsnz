## Current status
Little won the October 2025 Wellington mayoral election as Labour's endorsed candidate after incumbent Tory Whanau withdrew and endorsed him ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/national/559306/andrew-little-secures-labour-mayoral-nomination); [Stuff](https://www.stuff.co.nz/nz-news/360851504/mayor-andrew-little-be-joined-wellington-city-council-ray-chung-no-place-tory-whanau)). As mayor he led a review and, in June 2026, a council vote to discontinue the $139m Golden Mile pedestrianisation project ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/politics/598585/councillors-vote-against-continuing-wellington-s-golden-mile-project)), called for an inquiry into the Moa Point sewage plant failure, and declared a local state of emergency for heavy swells in June 2026. He resigned from Parliament on 5 December 2023 (Camilla Belich took his list seat) and is not a 2026 parliamentary candidate. He has publicly supported New Zealand's engagement with AUKUS, unlike Labour's current position ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/524955/former-defence-minister-andrew-little-disagrees-with-aukus-critics-on-china-threat)).

## Biography
Born 7 May 1965 in New Plymouth, Little studied law, philosophy and public policy at Victoria University of Wellington and was VUWSA president (1987) and NZUSA president (1988–89). He became a lawyer for the Engineers' Union, its general counsel (1997) and national secretary of the EPMU, New Zealand's largest private-sector union, from 2000 to 2011; he was Labour Party president from 2009 to 2011 ([RNZ](http://www.radionz.co.nz/news/political/259657/little-takes-union-path-to-leadership)). He was successfully treated for prostate cancer in 2009 and lives in Island Bay with his wife Leigh and their son.

## Political career
- **List MP, 2011–2023.** Stood unsuccessfully in New Plymouth in 2011 and 2014; list-only thereafter.
- **Labour leader and Leader of the Opposition, 18 Nov 2014 – 1 Aug 2017.** Narrowly beat Grant Robertson in the 2014 leadership election, carried by the union affiliates' vote ([Stuff](http://www.stuff.co.nz/national/politics/63305825/Andrew-Little-new-Labour-Party-leader-by-a-whisker)). Credited with ending caucus infighting but polled poorly; he resigned seven weeks before the 2017 election, handing over to Jacinda Ardern — a move later widely described as selfless ([NZ Herald](http://www.nzherald.co.nz/politics/news/article.cfm?c_id=280&objectid=11935044)).
- **Minister, 2017–2023 (12 portfolios):** Justice and Courts (2017–2020), Treaty of Waitangi Negotiations (2017–2023), GCSB and NZSIS (2017–2023), Pike River Re-entry, Workplace Relations (2020), Health (Nov 2020 – Jan 2023), Defence and Public Service (2023), Immigration (June 2023), and lead coordination minister for the Christchurch mosque attack Royal Commission response ([Parliament profile](https://www.parliament.nz/en/mps-and-electorates/former-members-of-parliament/little-andrew/)).

## Record & notable actions
- **Abortion law reform:** as Justice Minister he introduced the bill that removed abortion from the Crimes Act; it passed as the Abortion Legislation Act 2020 ([Stuff](https://www.stuff.co.nz/national/120389800/new-zealands-fourdecade-old-abortion-laws-have-changed-after-law-passes-final-reading)).
- **Pike River:** established the Pike River Recovery Agency and in 2018 entered the portal with families; the drift was re-entered in 2019, but plans to recover the miners' remains were abandoned in 2021 ([Beehive](https://www.beehive.govt.nz/release/andrew-little-enters-pike-river-portal)).
- **Health:** as Health Minister (2020–2023) he oversaw the health system through the COVID-19 period; in mid-2022 he said the system was under pressure but not in crisis, which health workers disputed ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/469518/health-system-under-pressure-not-in-crisis-minister-andrew-little)).
- **Three strikes:** his 2018 plan to repeal the three-strikes law was blocked by NZ First ([NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/nz/news/article.cfm?c_id=1&objectid=12068425)).
- **Intelligence:** in July 2021 publicly attributed malicious cyber activity to Chinese state-sponsored actor APT40 ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/447239/government-points-finger-at-china-over-cyber-attacks)).
- **Treaty settlements:** delivered Crown apologies including to Ngāti Maru (Taranaki) in 2022.
- **Opposition:** drafted a corporate manslaughter member's bill after Pike River and the CTV collapse (2013).

## Controversies
- **Defamation suits:** ACC Minister Judith Collins sued Little and Trevor Mallard for defamation in 2012 (settled); in 2017 hotel owners Earl and Lani Hagaman sued him over remarks linking their National Party donation to a government contract — the jury cleared him on some claims and could not agree on others, and no damages were awarded ([NZ Herald](http://www.nzherald.co.nz/nz/news/article.cfm?c_id=1&objectid=10847321); [RNZ](http://www.radionz.co.nz/news/national/328552/little-%27very-pleased%27-no-damages-awarded)).
- **Australian deportations (2018):** a public dispute with Australian ministers Peter Dutton and Alex Hawke after he criticised Australia's "501" deportation policy ([NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/nz/news/article.cfm?c_id=1&objectid=12092448)).
