## Current status
Woods is No. 4 in Labour's shadow cabinet and No. 5 on the 2026 list. On 3 August 2025 she announced she would not seek re-selection in Wigram and would stand on the list only ([1News](https://www.1news.co.nz/2025/08/03/senior-labour-mp-wont-contest-electorate-will-stand-on-list-in-2026/)). Since March 2025 her portfolios have been energy and resources, manufacturing and industry, and associate finance (she gave up climate change) ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/544048/chris-hipkins-announces-new-labour-economic-team-tangi-utikere-promoted)).

## Biography
Born 4 November 1973 and raised in Wigram, Christchurch, Woods attended Cathedral College and earned an MA and a PhD in history from the University of Canterbury; her doctorate examined Māori urbanisation and integration policy, 1942–1969 ([UC Research Repository](https://ir.canterbury.ac.nz/handle/10092/4352)). She was a business manager at Crop & Food Research and Plant & Food Research (2005–2008) ([The Press](http://www.stuff.co.nz/the-press/news/christchurch/4144405/Anderton-follower-chosen-for-Wigram)). Politically she began in the Alliance (1999–2002) and Jim Anderton's Progressive Party — she stood for the Progressives in Christchurch Central in 2005 — and joined Labour in 2007, the year she came second for the Christchurch mayoralty against Bob Parker.

## Political career
- **Wigram, 2011–present.** Succeeded Jim Anderton; majorities of 1,500 (2011), 4,594 (2017), 14,770 (2020) and 1,179 (2023) ([2011](http://www.electionresults.govt.nz/electionresults_2011/electorate-63.html), [2017](https://archive.electionresults.govt.nz/electionresults_2017/electorate-details-64.html), [2020](https://www.electionresults.govt.nz/electionresults_2020/electorate-details-65.html), [2023](https://archive.electionresults.govt.nz/electionresults_2023/electorate-details-65.html)).
- **Opposition, 2011–2017:** tertiary education spokesperson, then environment and climate change.
- **Minister, 2017–2023:** Minister of Energy and Resources, Research, Science and Innovation, and Greater Christchurch Regeneration from 2017; Minister of Housing from 27 June 2019 (taking over from Phil Twyford); joint head of managed isolation and quarantine from June 2020; Associate Minister of Finance from 2020; held eleven ministerial portfolios in all, earning a reputation as Labour's "Ms Fix-it" ([NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/nz/megan-woods-the-labour-partys-ms-fix-it-the-woman-called-in-to-clean-up-kiwibuild/EBZIOR3IQNG7ECFL3GUE5GPEEU/); [Parliament profile](https://www.parliament.nz/en/mps-and-electorates/members-of-parliament/woods-megan/)).
- Chaired Labour's 2020 and 2023 election campaigns ([The Post](https://www.thepost.co.nz/a/politics/350015012/professionals-true-believers-and-mad-men-the-teams-behind-the-election-campaigns)). Considered a possible successor to Ardern in January 2023 but did not stand.

## Record & notable actions
- **Oil and gas exploration ban (April 2018):** announced an end to new offshore oil and gas exploration permits, with existing permits continuing ([RNZ](https://www.radionz.co.nz/news/national/354816/oil-gas-exploration-move-a-kick-in-the-guts-for-taranaki-mayor)).
- **Housing:** took over KiwiBuild after Twyford; in October 2021 announced, jointly with National leader Judith Collins and Environment Minister David Parker, the cross-party Medium Density Residential Standards (Resource Management (Enabling Housing Supply) Amendment Act) allowing up to three homes of up to three storeys on most urban sites ([NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/nz/politics/labour-and-national-join-forces-for-housing-crisis-fix-ending-decades-of-standoff/Z27M7UF7QFO4RMLNR7UQBQERZI/)). Opened Kāinga Ora's Te Mātāwai mixed-tenure development in 2023 ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/495077/te-matawai-prime-minister-chris-hipkins-opens-government-s-largest-public-housing-development)). In 2024 she criticised National's decision to let councils opt out of the bipartisan density rules ([OneRoof](https://www.oneroof.co.nz/news/ex-housing-minister-rare-moment-of-political-agreement-torpedoed-by-christopher-luxon-45717)).
- **MIQ (2020–2022):** shared responsibility for managed isolation and quarantine ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/419375/housing-minister-megan-woods-to-oversee-managed-isolation-and-quarantine-facilities)).

## Controversies
- **Progressive Home Ownership scheme (2021):** National's Nicola Willis called it a failure after it had cost $17 million and housed 12 families in seven months; Woods defended it ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/437079/progressive-home-ownership-scheme-houses-just-12-families-in-seven-months)).
- **"Handmaid's Tale" tweet (June 2023):** her post comparing National's plan to reinstate $5 prescription charges to *The Handmaid's Tale* was condemned by National as "appalling" ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/491142/national-brands-labour-s-handmaid-s-tale-tweet-as-appalling)).
