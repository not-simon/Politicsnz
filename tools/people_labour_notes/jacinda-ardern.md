## Current status
Dame Jacinda Ardern left Parliament at the 2023 election and is not involved in the 2026 campaign as a candidate. After office she took up dual fellowships at the Harvard Kennedy School (2023) ([AP](https://apnews.com/article/new-zealand-jacinda-ardern-harvard-university-d9d82f742e79815e91687bd54213bd53)), became a trustee of Prince William's Earthshot Prize ([BBC](https://www.bbc.com/news/uk-65180096)), was appointed Special Envoy for the Christchurch Call by Chris Hipkins in April 2023 ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/487340/former-pm-jacinda-ardern-appointed-as-christchurch-call-envoy)), and published her memoir *A Different Kind of Power* in June 2025, which won the 2026 E. H. McCormick Prize for best first book of general non-fiction ([NZ Book Awards](https://www.nzbookawards.nz/new-zealand-book-awards/2026-awards/winners/)). In February 2026 *The Guardian* reported that she and her family had moved to Sydney ([Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/feb/26/jacinda-ardern-living-in-australia-former-nz-new-zealand-pm)). She was made a Dame Grand Companion of the New Zealand Order of Merit in 2023 ([DPMC](https://www.dpmc.govt.nz/publications/kings-birthday-and-coronation-honours-list-2023)).

## Biography
Born 26 July 1980 in Hamilton and raised in Morrinsville and Murupara (her father was a police officer), Ardern joined Labour at 17 and graduated from the University of Waikato with a Bachelor of Communication Studies in 2001 ([Waikato University profile](https://apps.mngt.waikato.ac.nz/Newsroom/StudentProfile/3724)). She worked as a researcher for Phil Goff and Helen Clark, then in London as a policy adviser in the UK Cabinet Office, and in 2008 was elected president of the International Union of Socialist Youth. She was raised in the LDS Church but left it in 2005, citing her support for gay rights ([NZ Herald](http://www.nzherald.co.nz/lifestyle/news/article.cfm?c_id=6&objectid=11789352)). She married broadcaster Clarke Gayford in January 2024; their daughter Neve was born on 21 June 2018 while Ardern was prime minister ([BBC](https://www.bbc.com/news/world-asia-44568537)).

## Political career
- **List MP, 2008–2017.** Entered Parliament from list position 20 in 2008 (losing the Waikato electorate). Stood in Auckland Central in 2011 and 2014, losing to National's Nikki Kaye by 717 and then 600 votes ([2011 results](http://www.electionresults.org.nz/electionresults_2011/electorate-1.html), [2014 results](http://www.electionresults.org.nz/electionresults_2014/electorate-1.html)). Spokesperson for youth affairs (under Goff), social development (No. 4 in Shearer's shadow cabinet, 2011), then justice, children, small business and arts (under Little, 2014).
- **Mount Albert, 2017–2023.** Won the February 2017 Mount Albert by-election with about 77% of the vote ([NZ Herald](http://www.nzherald.co.nz/politics/news/article.cfm?c_id=280&objectid=11807614)); majorities of 15,264 (2017) and 21,246 (2020) ([2017](https://www.electionresults.govt.nz/electionresults_2017/electorate-details-27.html), [2020](https://www.electionresults.govt.nz/electionresults_2020/electorate-details-24.html)).
- **Deputy leader (7 Mar 2017) and leader (1 Aug 2017).** Became leader seven weeks before the 2017 election after Andrew Little resigned; Labour rose from 24% to the low 40s in polls ("Jacindamania") and won 46 seats ([NZ Herald](http://www.nzherald.co.nz/politics/news/article.cfm?c_id=280&objectid=11922098)).
- **Prime Minister, 26 Oct 2017 – 25 Jan 2023.** Led a Labour–NZ First coalition with Green confidence and supply (2017–2020), then the first single-party majority government under MMP after winning 65 seats and 50% of the party vote in 2020 ([Electoral Commission](https://www.electionresults.govt.nz/electionresults_2020/)). Also Minister for National Security and Intelligence, Arts, Culture and Heritage, and the first Minister for Child Poverty Reduction.
- **Resignation.** Announced on 19 January 2023 that she no longer had "enough in the tank", resigned as leader on 22 January and as PM on 25 January, and left Parliament in April 2023 ([Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2023/jan/19/jacinda-ardern-resigns-as-prime-minister-of-new-zealand)).

## Record & notable actions
- **Christchurch mosque attacks (2019):** led the national response to the 15 March 2019 attacks in which 51 people were killed; within a month Parliament banned most semi-automatic weapons and high-capacity magazines, followed by a buy-back ([NYT](https://www.nytimes.com/2019/04/10/world/asia/new-zealand-guns-jacinda-ardern.html)); co-founded the Christchurch Call with Emmanuel Macron ([NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/nz/news/article.cfm?c_id=1&objectid=12226274)).
- **COVID-19:** border closure (March 2020), alert-level 4 lockdown, elimination strategy until October 2021, vaccine rollout from February 2021 ([NYT](https://www.nytimes.com/2021/10/04/world/australia/new-zealand-covid-zero.html)). The MIQ system left many citizens unable to return; in April 2022 the High Court found aspects of MIQ unjustifiably limited citizens' right to enter New Zealand ([High Court judgment](https://www.courtsofnz.govt.nz/assets/Uploads/Judgments-online/2022-NZHC-832.pdf)).
- **Child poverty and welfare:** Child Poverty Reduction Act targets; the 2018 Families Package (26 weeks' paid parental leave, Best Start payment) ([Beehive](https://www.beehive.govt.nz/feature/supporting-new-zealand-families-0)); free school lunches (Ka Ora, Ka Ako); benefit increases; first year of tertiary study fees-free (2018).
- **Climate:** Zero Carbon Act passed with cross-party support (2019); declared a climate emergency in December 2020 ([Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2020/dec/02/new-zealand-declares-a-climate-change-emergency)).
- **Tax:** ruled out a capital gains tax under her leadership in April 2019 after the Tax Working Group recommended one ([RNZ](https://www.radionz.co.nz/news/on-the-inside/387347/week-in-politics-labour-s-biggest-campaign-burden-scrapped)); bright-line test extended to 10 years (2021).
- **Other:** formal apology for the Dawn Raids (2021); cannabis referendum (2020, narrowly defeated; she disclosed afterwards she voted yes) ([Stuff](https://www.stuff.co.nz/national/politics/123261775/pm-defends-no-comment-stance-on-cannabis-referendum)).
- **Conscience votes:** voted for marriage equality (2013) and for the Abortion Legislation Act 2020 ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/412087/abortion-legislation-bill-passes-third-and-final-reading-in-parliament)).

## Controversies
- **Clare Curran (2018):** criticised for initially only removing Curran from Cabinet over an undisclosed meeting rather than dismissing her; Curran later resigned ([Newshub](https://www.newshub.co.nz/home/politics/2018/09/prime-minister-jacinda-ardern-accepts-clare-curran-s-resignation-as-a-minister.html)).
- **Labour staffer sexual-assault complaint (2019):** her account that she had been told the complaint did not involve sexual assault was questioned by media; party president Nigel Haworth resigned ([The Spinoff timeline](https://thespinoff.co.nz/politics/11-09-2019/timeline-labour-staffer-misconduct-inquiry/)).
- **Hot-mic remark (2022):** called ACT leader David Seymour an "arrogant prick" in the House; she apologised and the pair later auctioned a framed copy for charity ([Stuff](https://www.stuff.co.nz/national/politics/130764372/jacinda-ardern-apologises-for-calling-david-seymour-an-arrogant-prick)).
- **Undelivered targets:** critics, including former coalition partner Winston Peters, said her government under-delivered on housing (e.g. KiwiBuild), child poverty and inequality; her approval fell to 29% by late 2022 ([Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2022/dec/24/jacinda-arderns-popularity-plummeted-this-year-things-could-get-worse-in-2023); [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/new-zealands-ardern-leaves-legacy-kindness-disappointments-2023-01-19/)).
- **Threats and abuse:** she was the target of sustained abuse and threats from anti-mandate protesters; a man was convicted of threatening to kill her ([Stuff](https://www.stuff.co.nz/nz-news/350300416/richard-sivell-run-almost-two-years-appears-court)).
