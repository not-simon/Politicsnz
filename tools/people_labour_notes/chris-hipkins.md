## Current status
Hipkins is Leader of the Opposition and Labour leader heading into the 7 November 2026 general election, in which he is standing again in Remutaka and is first on Labour's list ([policy.nz](https://policy.nz/2026/party-vote/parties/labour-party)). Labour's caucus confirmed him as leader after the 2023 defeat on 7 November 2023 ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/501872/labour-votes-to-keep-chris-hipkins-as-leader-carmel-sepuloni-takes-over-as-deputy)). In October 2025 he announced that Labour would campaign on a narrow 28% capital gains tax on property sales (excluding the family home and farms) to fund three free doctor's visits a year, and ruled out a wealth tax ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/577021/labour-to-campaign-on-narrow-capital-gains-tax-no-wealth-tax)).

## Biography
Christopher John Hipkins was born on 5 September 1978 in the Hutt Valley, the son of Doug and Rosemary Hipkins ([Parliament maiden statement](https://www.parliament.nz/en/pb/hansard-debates/rhr/document/49HansS_20081216_00001004/hipkins-chris-maiden-statements); [RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/national/482831/chris-hipkins-from-head-boy-to-prime-minister)). He was head boy of Hutt Valley Memorial College in 1996 and studied political science and criminology at Victoria University of Wellington (BA), where he was students' association president in 2000 and 2001 ([Beehive biography](https://www.beehive.govt.nz/minister/biography/chris-hipkins)). As a first-year student in 1997 he was among protesters arrested at Parliament over the Tertiary Review green paper; a decade later the protesters received an apology and compensation after a court found the arrests unjustified ([Stuff](https://www.stuff.co.nz/dominion-post/news/local-papers/hutt-news/2595536/Parliament-to-say-sorry-to-protestors)). Before entering Parliament he was a policy adviser at the Industry Training Federation, a training manager at Todd Energy, and an adviser to ministers Trevor Mallard and Prime Minister Helen Clark ([NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/nz/chris-hipkins-from-hutt-valley-boy-to-quiet-coup-for-pm-audrey-young-reports/ET4JZSI5PVCKBDDHRCLYVQ3CHI/)).

He married Jade in 2020; they separated in 2022 and have two children. In 2018 he took extended paternity leave, one of the first senior male cabinet ministers to do so. His relationship with Toni Grace became public on election night 2023 and he announced their engagement in November 2025 ([NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/nz/politics/labour-leader-chris-hipkins-reveals-engagement-to-partner-toni-grace/32RDT6TDINBDBCJ3XR2Z23OIAQ/)).

## Political career
**Electorate.** Hipkins won the Labour-held Rimutaka seat (renamed Remutaka in 2020) in 2008 after Paul Swain retired, beating union official Paul Chalmers for the nomination. His majorities: 753 (2008), 3,286 (2011), 6,664 (2014), 8,609 (2017), 8,859 (2023); in 2020 he polled 20,497 votes (official results via [electionresults.govt.nz 2011](http://www.electionresults.govt.nz/electionresults_2011/electorate-43.html), [2014](http://electionresults.govt.nz/electionresults_2014/electorate-44.html), [2017](https://www.electionresults.govt.nz/electionresults_2017/electorate-details-44.html), [2020](https://www.electionresults.govt.nz/electionresults_2020/electorate-details-42.html), [2023](https://www.electionresults.govt.nz/electionresults_2023/electorate-details-42.html)).

**Opposition, 2008–2017.** Spokesperson for internal affairs in his first term; under David Shearer he entered the shadow cabinet as education and state services spokesperson and became Labour's chief whip; he kept education under David Cunliffe, Andrew Little and Jacinda Ardern and was also shadow Leader of the House from 2014 ([Parliament profile](https://www.parliament.nz/en/mps-and-electorates/members-of-parliament/hipkins-chris/)).

**Minister, 2017–2023.** In the Sixth Labour Government he was Minister of Education and Leader of the House (26 Oct 2017 – 25 Jan 2023), Minister for the Public Service (2017–2023), Minister of Health (2 Jul – 6 Nov 2020, after David Clark resigned), Minister for COVID-19 Response (6 Nov 2020 – 14 Jun 2022) and Minister of Police (14 Jun 2022 – 25 Jan 2023), earning a reputation as the government's "fixer" ([The Spinoff](https://thespinoff.co.nz/politics/21-01-2023/the-guy-just-lives-for-diy-what-to-expect-from-prime-minister-chris-hipkins); [DPMC ministerial list Nov 2020](https://dpmc.govt.nz/sites/default/files/2020-11/ministerial-list-2-nov-2020.pdf)).

**Prime Minister, 2023.** After Jacinda Ardern resigned, Hipkins was the only nominee for the Labour leadership (21–22 January 2023) and was sworn in as the 41st Prime Minister on 25 January 2023, with Carmel Sepuloni as the first Pasifika deputy prime minister ([NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/nz/politics/chris-hipkins-sworn-in-as-prime-minister-carmel-sepuloni-as-deputy-jacinda-ardern-vacates-top-job/IERZGZV2BFAE5AWLNJHMMGHYQ4/)). He led Labour to defeat at the October 2023 election (26.91% of the party vote, 34 seats) and conceded on election night ([Electoral Commission](https://elections.nz/media-and-news/2023/official-results-for-the-2023-general-election/); [RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/500185/election-2023-labour-s-chris-hipkins-concedes-congratulates-national-on-results)). He remained caretaker PM until Christopher Luxon was sworn in on 27 November 2023.

**Opposition leader, 2023–present.** He took the ministerial services and national security & intelligence spokesperson roles in his shadow cabinet ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/503581/labour-party-leader-chris-hipkins-reveals-new-shadow-cabinet)) and in March 2025 reshuffled it and created an economic team led by Barbara Edmonds ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/544048/chris-hipkins-announces-new-labour-economic-team-tangi-utikere-promoted)).

## Record & notable actions
- **Education (2017–2023):** legislated to stop new charter schools; all 12 existing charter schools became state-integrated or special-character schools by September 2018 ([Newshub](https://www.newshub.co.nz/home/politics/2018/09/all-nz-charter-schools-now-approved-to-become-state-integrated.html)). Abolished National Standards and reviewed NCEA. Merged the 16 polytechnics and the industry training organisations into Te Pūkenga (from April 2020), a reform that by 2022 faced low enrolments, deficits and senior resignations ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/national/395722/government-confirms-major-overhaul-of-polytechnics-apprenticeships); [Stuff](https://www.stuff.co.nz/waikato-times/300647698/minister-rules-out-te-pkenga-commissioner-at-this-time)). Declined Victoria University's proposed renaming to "University of Wellington" in 2018 ([Stuff](https://www.stuff.co.nz/national/education/109441495/victoria-university-of-wellington-name-change-rejected-by-minister)).
- **COVID-19 response (2020–2022):** introduced mask mandates on public transport, pre-departure testing, the My Vaccine Pass and the COVID-19 Response (Vaccinations) Legislation Act 2021 (vaccine-mandate framework) ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/national/456455/mandate-legislation-pushed-through-parliament-amid-fierce-opposition)); oversaw MIQ, including the January 2022 pause of the MIQ lottery ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/national/459786/government-postpones-next-miq-lottery-due-to-spike-of-omicron-cases-at-border)); announced in October 2022 the end of most COVID powers and a Royal Commission of Inquiry ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/476919/government-signals-covid-19-inquiry-in-the-works)).
- **As Prime Minister (2023):** a "policy bonfire" scrapping or deferring the TVNZ–RNZ merger, the biofuel mandate, lowering the voting age to 16, the Clean Car Upgrade and much of the speed-reduction programme, and delaying social insurance and hate-speech law, with funds redirected to cost-of-living support ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/483875/watch-tvnz-rnz-merger-scrapped-income-insurance-and-hate-speech-laws-delayed); [1News](https://www.1news.co.nz/2023/03/13/pms-policy-bonfire-voting-age-bill-transport-schemes-reprioritsed/)); led the response to the Auckland Anniversary floods and Cyclone Gabrielle; Budget 2023 removed $5 prescription fees, extended 20 hours ECE to two-year-olds and made public transport free for under-13s ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/490161/budget-2023-at-a-glance-what-you-need-to-know)); signed the NZ–EU free trade agreement on 9 July 2023 ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/493457/new-zealand-signs-free-trade-deal-with-european-union-after-years-of-negotiations)); ruled out a capital gains or wealth tax for the 2023 term ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/493596/hipkins-rules-out-capital-gains-tax-wealth-tax-if-labour-re-elected)).
- **In opposition:** called for a Gaza ceasefire (November 2023) and in September 2025 said New Zealand should recognise the situation in Gaza as genocide ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/573334/nz-govt-will-not-respond-on-latest-accusation-of-genocide-in-gaza)); in April 2026 committed Labour's votes to pass the NZ–India free trade agreement, which NZ First opposed ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/business/593185/chris-hipkins-announces-labour-will-back-india-free-trade-deal)).
- **Conscience votes:** voted for the Marriage (Definition of Marriage) Amendment Bill in 2013 ([NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/nz/gay-marriage-how-mps-voted/WLPKK4734FBZEKJIDHBFO4MWFA/)).
- **Member's bills:** Electricity (Renewable Preference) Amendment Bill (defeated at first reading, 2010); Education (Charter Schools Abolition) Amendment Bill (defeated at first reading, 2016) ([Parliament](https://www.parliament.nz/en/pb/bills-and-laws/bills-proposed-laws/document/00DBHOH_BILL68842_1/education-charter-schools-abolition-amendment-bill)).

## Controversies
- **Charlotte Bellis MIQ case (2022):** as COVID-19 Response Minister he publicly released details about pregnant journalist Charlotte Bellis's emergency MIQ application; she said she had not consented and disputed his account. In June 2022 he formally apologised for releasing personal information without consent and for inaccurate comments ([NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/nz/covid-19-omicron-chris-hipkins-formally-apologises-over-charlotte-bellis-emergency-miq-saga/DX35AVZARU5RPUFTFUKDCNMK5A/)).
- **Remarks about Bill English (2022):** apologised to former PM Bill English after suggesting in the House that English had favoured his brothers with government contracts ([Stuff](https://www.stuff.co.nz/national/politics/129959240/chris-hipkins-apologises-to-bill-english-for-dragging-family-into-debate)).
- **As PM he dealt with several ministerial resignations/dismissals** — Stuart Nash, Meka Whaitiri, Michael Wood, Kiri Allan and Jan Tinetti's referral to the Privileges Committee (see those profiles).
- **COVID vaccine advice for under-18s (2026):** the NZ Herald reported he had been briefed in March 2022 on advice to reduce the second-dose mandate for 12–17-year-olds; he initially denied receiving the Cabinet paper then said he had forgotten, and rejected Winston Peters' claim that he misled the COVID-19 Royal Commission ([NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/nz/politics/the-covid-vaccine-and-the-risk-to-under-18s-chris-hipkins-defends-not-making-advice-public/W3RWZP2RLVGLFJA3EUGNM62GDI/); [RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/590823/labour-leader-chris-hipkins-denies-misleading-public-over-covid-vaccine-risk-to-under-18s)).
