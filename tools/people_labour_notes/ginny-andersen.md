## Current status
Andersen is No. 9 in Labour's shadow cabinet; she took education in the 11 March 2026 reshuffle alongside police, jobs and income and Treaty negotiations ([Stuff](https://www.stuff.co.nz/politics/360949687/hipkins-reshuffles-labour-portfolios-henare-departs-and-plan-hatched-maori-seats-race)). In April 2026 she announced Labour would restore school boards' Treaty of Waitangi obligations and reverse several of the coalition's curriculum and testing changes ([Newsroom](https://newsroom.co.nz/2026/04/28/labour-vows-to-reinstate-school-boards-treaty-obligations/)). She is contesting Hutt South again (list No. 12) ([policy.nz](https://policy.nz/2026/party-vote/parties/labour-party)). On 15 September 2026 the Speaker ordered her out of the chamber for heckling ([Newstalk ZB](https://www.newstalkzb.co.nz/news/politics/speaker-gerry-brownlee-boots-two-mps-from-house-within-minutes/)).

## Biography
Born in 1975 and raised around New Zealand by teacher parents before settling in Christchurch (Avonside Girls' High), Andersen studied political science and te reo Māori at the University of Canterbury (MA 2004, on the Ngāi Tahu settlement). She worked at the Office of Treaty Settlements, as private secretary to Margaret Wilson and adviser to David Cunliffe and Trevor Mallard, then for New Zealand Police (from 2006) as a policy manager on gangs and organised crime, including secondment to DPMC on the Methamphetamine Action Plan ([1News](https://www.1news.co.nz/2023/03/20/minister-of-police-who-is-ginny-andersen/)). She was Labour Party vice-president from 2015. She is married to former police inspector Geoff Gwyn.

## Political career
- Lost Ōhāriu to Peter Dunne by 710 votes in 2014; lost Hutt South to Chris Bishop in 2017 (entering on the list); won Hutt South from Bishop by 3,777 in 2020; lost it back to Bishop by 1,332 in 2023 and returned on the list ([2020](https://www.electionresults.govt.nz/electionresults_2020/electorate-details-14.html); [2023](https://www.electionresults.govt.nz/electionresults_2023/electorate-details-14.html)).
- Chair of the Justice Committee (2020–2023).
- **Minister, 2023:** entered Cabinet on 31 January 2023 (Digital Economy and Communications, Small Business, Seniors); Minister of Police from 20 March 2023 after Stuart Nash's removal; Minister of Justice from July 2023 after Kiri Allan's resignation ([The Spinoff](https://thespinoff.co.nz/live-updates/20-03-2023/junior-minister-ginny-anderson-takes-over-police-role-from-stuart-nash)).

## Record & notable actions
- **Miscarriage bereavement leave:** her member's bill, giving three days' paid bereavement leave after miscarriage or stillbirth, passed unanimously in March 2021 — one of the first such laws in the world ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/439076/miscarriage-bereavement-leave-bill-passes-unanimously-in-parliament)).
- As Police Minister introduced a specific ram-raid offence and oversaw fleeing-driver legislation and fog-cannon subsidies for small retailers ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/496851/bill-targeting-ram-raid-offending-passes-first-reading)).

## Controversies
- **Hutt South office sublease (2020):** a decades-old arrangement inherited from Trevor Mallard saw Parliamentary Service pay Labour more rent than Labour paid the landlord, with the difference used by the party; she ended it once it became public ([Stuff](https://www.stuff.co.nz/national/politics/300098040/taxpayer-foots-bill-for-labour-electorate-funds-in-decadesold-subletting-arrangement)).
- **Volunteer bullying complaints (Nov 2023):** Labour investigated a complaint that she had berated a teenage volunteer on election night; she apologised. A second volunteer's allegations she disputed ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/502320/labour-mp-ginny-andersen-formally-apologises-after-bullying-accusation)).
- **Mark Mitchell remarks (Feb 2024):** asked whether Police Minister Mark Mitchell had been "paid to kill people" as a private security contractor in Iraq; after Hipkins disagreed, she said her comments "went too far" and apologised ([NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/nz/politics/labours-ginny-andersen-claims-police-minister-was-paid-to-kill-people-while-working-in-private-security-overseas/RB62YR5I3BEDZLU7JYOM27OKDU/)).
- A June 2023 trip to Paris for an OECD SME meeting drew scrutiny over its $51,000 cost ([The Post](https://www.thepost.co.nz/politics/350107990/minister-paris-3-days-2-meetings-and-51k-bill)).
