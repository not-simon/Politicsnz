## Current status
Wood is Labour's candidate for Mt Roskill at the 2026 election, standing as an electorate-only candidate with no list place ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/politics/579644/former-labour-minister-michael-wood-to-re-enter-politics); [policy.nz](https://policy.nz/2026/party-vote/parties/labour-party)). After losing his seat in 2023 he worked as a negotiation specialist for the union E tū ([The Post](https://www.thepost.co.nz/politics/350237653/how-michael-wood-came-cropper-and-his-new-jobs)) and was elected to Labour's policy council, arguing the party needed more progressive tax policy ([NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/nz/politics/michael-wood-among-familiar-faces-to-win-labour-election-as-party-mulls-tax-discussion/MB3XPAEYIBFULNDNCMEP7RD6YQ/)).

## Biography
Born 10 May 1980, Wood attended Pakuranga College and graduated BA from the University of Auckland (2005). He worked in retail, then as an organiser and senior negotiator for the Finance and Information Workers Union and as an adviser at Habitat for Humanity ([NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/nz/meet-the-minister-michael-wood-transport-and-workplace-relations/5BEQY3X3BKEVRZ3KPZE4IXZZCE/)). He and his wife, Auckland councillor Julie Fairey, were elected together to the Puketāpapa Local Board in 2010; they have three sons.

## Political career
- Unsuccessful Labour candidate in Pakuranga (2002, 2005), the 2011 Botany by-election and Epsom (2014).
- **Mt Roskill MP, December 2016 – 2023:** won the 2016 by-election after Phil Goff became Auckland mayor; majority of 13,853 in 2020; lost to National's Carlos Cheung in 2023 ([2020](https://www.electionresults.govt.nz/electionresults_2020/electorate-details-25.html); [2023](https://archive.electionresults.govt.nz/electionresults_2023/electorate-details-25.html)).
- Parliamentary Under-Secretary for Ethnic Communities and chair of the Finance and Expenditure Committee (2017); Chief Government Whip (2019–2020).
- **Cabinet 2020–2023:** Minister of Transport and Workplace Relations and Safety, Deputy Leader of the House (2020); Immigration (June 2022); Minister for Auckland and Associate Finance (Feb 2023) ([DPMC 2020](https://dpmc.govt.nz/sites/default/files/2020-11/ministerial-list-2-nov-2020.pdf)).

## Record & notable actions
- **Fair Pay Agreements:** as Workplace Relations Minister he was responsible for the Fair Pay Agreements Act 2022, which created industry-wide collective bargaining; it passed its third reading on 25 October 2022 with Labour, Green and Māori Party support and was repealed by the National-led government on 14 December 2023 ([Wikipedia: Fair Pay Agreements Act 2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fair_Pay_Agreements_Act_2022)).
- **Auckland Light Rail:** announced Cabinet approval in January 2022 of a $14.6 billion, partly tunnelled light-rail line from the CBD to Auckland Airport; the incoming Luxon government cancelled it after the 2023 election ([Wikipedia: Auckland Light Rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auckland_Light_Rail); [Newshub](https://www.newshub.co.nz/home/politics/2022/01/14-billion-auckland-light-rail-bid-gets-green-light-decision-on-second-waitemat-harbour-crossing-in-2023.html)).
- **Immigration (2022–23):** defended excluding nurses from the fast-track residency "green list", which National's Erica Stanford criticised ([NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/nz/new-immigration-minister-michael-wood-grilled-on-nurse-shortage-as-health-nz-ceo-joins-criticism/HDSOQNT5AXGRWBQE7XMWFE6YGY/)).
- **Conscience votes:** opposed the End of Life Choice Act; supported legalising recreational cannabis.

## Controversies
- **Undeclared shareholdings (2023):** stood down as Transport Minister on 6 June 2023 after it emerged he still held Auckland Airport shares despite telling the Cabinet Office they had been sold and repeatedly being asked to divest; he resigned as a minister on 21 June after further undeclared shares (Chorus, Spark, NAB) emerged ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/491423/transport-minister-michael-wood-stood-down-after-airport-shares-controversy); [RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/492378/watch-pm-chris-hipkins-announces-michael-wood-has-resigned)). The Registrar of Pecuniary Interests criticised his failure to correct errors, and in August 2023 the Privileges Committee found he had neglected his obligations over a significant period but not committed contempt, and required him to apologise to the House ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/496413/michael-wood-ordered-to-apologise-to-parliament-over-shareholdings)).
- **"River of filth" (2022):** described the Parliament grounds anti-mandate occupation as having a "river of filth" running through it and urged National and ACT MPs not to engage ([Newshub](https://www.newshub.co.nz/home/politics/2022/02/labour-s-michael-wood-warns-mps-who-want-to-support-anti-mandate-protest-it-has-river-of-filth-running-through-it.html)).
