## Current status
Street was not re-elected in 2014 and declined to return as a list MP in 2017 ([Newshub](https://www.newshub.co.nz/home/politics/2017/02/maryan-street-refuses-a-return-to-parliament.html)). She was KiwiRail's employment relations manager (2015–2022), joined the Victoria University council in 2021 and the KiwiRail board in July 2022 for a three-year term ([Treasury](https://www.treasury.govt.nz/information-and-services/commercial-portfolio-and-advice/commercial-portfolio/kiwirail-holdings-limited)). Appointed MNZM in the 2024 New Year Honours.

## Biography
Born 5 April 1955 in New Plymouth, Street was a teacher, chaired the PPTA (1981–1983), was Labour Party president (1993–1995), and lectured in labour studies at the University of Auckland. She was the first openly lesbian MP elected to Parliament.

## Political career
- **List MP 2005–2014**; contested Taranaki-King Country (2005) and Nelson (2008, 2011, 2014).
- **Minister of Housing and Minister for ACC** (31 October 2007 – 19 November 2008) in the Fifth Labour Government ([NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/nz/ithe-reshufflei-street-picks-up-the-weighty-portfolios/6RHD3CLWB55S43ZIJWGPRNEAMU/)).
- Opposition spokesperson for tertiary education, trade, foreign affairs, then health, environment and state services; chaired the Regulations Review Committee (2013–2014).

## Record & notable actions
- Sponsored the End of Life Choice member's bill (later withdrawn), and bills on tenants' insurance and ethical investment, none enacted ([Stuff](https://www.stuff.co.nz/national/health/euthanasia-debate/86092903/national-portrait-maryan-street-the-campaigner)).
- As party president in 1993 publicly said Mike Moore should no longer lead, preceding Helen Clark's accession.
