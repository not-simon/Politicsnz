## Current status
Sir Trevor Mallard resigned as Speaker on 24 August 2022 and from Parliament on 20 October 2022, then served as New Zealand's Ambassador to Ireland from early 2023 until Foreign Minister Winston Peters recalled him in August 2025 ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/570409/carnival-s-over-peters-celebrates-trevor-mallard-s-early-exit-as-irish-ambassador)). In 2025 he was sued, along with the Attorney-General, on behalf of a child protester over the use of loud music to deter the 2022 Parliament occupation ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/565286/ex-speaker-trevor-mallard-being-sued-by-child-over-parliament-protest-music)). He was made a Knight Companion of the NZ Order of Merit in 2024.

## Biography
Born 17 June 1954 in Wellington, Mallard attended Onslow College, graduated BCA from Victoria University (1974) and trained as a teacher, teaching in Wellington and the King Country, where he was a PPTA branch secretary. A Labour member from 1972, he was arrested in Parliament's chamber during a 1977 anti-SIS bill protest. He married journalist Jane Clifton in 2014; he has three children ([Labour profile](https://www.labour.org.nz/trevormallard)).

## Political career
- **MP for Hamilton West 1984–1990** (government whip); **Pencarrow/Hutt South 1993–2017**; list MP 2017–2022.
- **Fifth Labour Government (1999–2008):** Minister of Education and State Services (1999–2007), Sport and Recreation, America's Cup, Race Relations coordinator, Energy; from 2007 Environment, Labour, Broadcasting, SOEs and Associate Finance.
- **Opposition 2008–2017:** education and labour spokesperson, shadow Leader of the House (2011–2013), Assistant Speaker (2014–2017).
- **Speaker of the House, 7 Nov 2017 – 24 Aug 2022** (re-elected unopposed in 2020) ([Newshub](https://www.newshub.co.nz/home/politics/2020/11/jacinda-ardern-judith-collins-congratulate-labour-s-trevor-mallard-on-his-unopposed-re-election-as-speaker-of-the-house.html)).

## Record & notable actions
- As Speaker made Parliament more family-friendly (2017 onward) — widely reported after he bottle-fed MP Tāmati Coffey's baby in the chair in 2019 ([Guardian](http://www.theguardian.com/world/2019/aug/31/babies-in-the-beehive-the-man-behind-new-zealands-child-friendly-parliament)) — including an atrium for MPs' children, family security clearances and feeding/changing facilities ([Parliament](https://www.parliament.nz/en/get-involved/features/parliament-becoming-more-family-friendly/)).
- Ties became optional in the House in February 2021 after he ejected Rawiri Waititi for wearing a hei-tiki instead of a tie ([NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/nz/ties-now-optional-in-parliament-after-rawiri-waititi-booted-out-for-not-wearing-one/NK3ERX5UY7BKW6IY7C3ZJS4RSQ/)).
- As Education Minister faced a long secondary teachers' pay dispute and a contested school-closures programme that was eventually halted.

## Controversies
- **Assault on Tau Henare (2007):** punched National MP Tau Henare outside the chamber; after a private prosecution he pleaded guilty to fighting in a public place ([NZ Herald](http://www.nzherald.co.nz/nz/news/article.cfm?c_id=1&objectid=10483189)).
- **Don Brash interjection (2006)** and crude 2002 remarks about rugby officials, for which he apologised.
- **Defamation (2012):** sued with Andrew Little by Judith Collins over ACC comments; settled.
- **False rape claim (2019–2021):** said in 2019 that a "rapist" worked at Parliament; the staffer sued, and Mallard apologised in December 2020, with the settlement and legal costs totalling about $333,000 of public money. National's motion of no confidence failed in February 2021; in May 2021 he repeated a sexual-assault allegation under privilege, which Ardern called "totally inappropriate" ([NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/nz/politics/trevor-mallard-defamation-case-settling-speakers-false-rape-claim-against-staffer-costs-taxpayers-333000-national/P2J7YX4WTQM3EZWLBCPAWPZGFM/)).
- **2022 Parliament occupation:** turned on sprinklers and played music and messages to deter protesters, criticised by National and ACT; later issued and then withdrew trespass notices against five former MPs including Winston Peters ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/466425/speaker-trevor-mallard-withdraws-five-trespass-orders-relating-to-parliament-protest)).
- **Ambassadorship:** ACT criticised his appointment to Ireland as rewarding "bad behaviour" ([Newshub](https://www.newshub.co.nz/home/politics/2022/10/trevor-mallard-s-salary-perks-in-ambassador-to-ireland-role-revealed.html)).
