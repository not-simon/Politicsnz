## Current status
Jones has been a New Zealand First MP since 2023 (and 2017–2020), is NZ First's deputy leader (elected 17 September 2025) and a senior minister in the National-led coalition (Oceans and Fisheries, Regional Development, Resources) ([Stuff](https://www.stuff.co.nz/politics/360826543/de-facto-deputy-shane-jones-officially-made-nz-first-deputy-leader); [RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/503156/cabinet-lineup-for-new-government-unveiled-who-gets-what)). He is NZ First's 2026 Northland candidate ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/politics/742372/nz-first-promises-to-invest-in-oil-gas-surveys)). His Labour-era record is summarised here because he served as a Labour MP in the 50th Parliament.

## Biography
Born 3 September 1959 in Awanui near Kaitaia (Te Aupōuri, Ngāi Takoto), Jones attended St Stephen's School, graduated BA from Victoria University and earned an MPA at Harvard as a Harkness Fellow. A public servant on Treaty settlements, he chaired the Treaty of Waitangi Fisheries Commission (2000–2004), completing the allocation of fisheries assets to iwi, and chaired Sealord ([NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/kahu/jones-the-right-man-in-the-wrong-party/MCINTLV2DQ6ZFCT2SHNN5GDSNI/)).

## Political career
- **Labour list MP 2005–2014** (contested Northland and Tāmaki Makaurau); chair of the Finance and Expenditure Committee; **Minister for Building and Construction** (Oct 2007 – Nov 2008) with associate Treaty negotiations, immigration and trade roles.
- Opposition spokesperson for economic development, fisheries, Māori affairs and other portfolios; stood for Labour leader in 2013, winning 7 of 34 caucus votes ([Stuff](http://www.stuff.co.nz/national/politics/9166765/Cunliffe-wins-Labour-leadership)).
- Resigned from Parliament in May 2014 to become Pacific Economic Ambassador under National's Murray McCully; Kelvin Davis took his list seat ([NZ Herald](http://www.nzherald.co.nz/nz/news/article.cfm?c_id=1&objectid=11242350)).
- Rejoined Parliament for NZ First in 2017 (Minister for Regional Economic Development and Forestry, running the $3b Provincial Growth Fund), lost his seat in 2020, returned in 2023.

## Controversies (Labour era)
- **Credit card (2010):** demoted after it emerged he had used a ministerial credit card for personal spending, including hotel pay-per-view adult films, which he had repaid ([NZ Herald](http://www.nzherald.co.nz/politics/news/article.cfm?c_id=280&objectid=10651965)).
- **Bill Liu (Yong Ming Yan) citizenship (2008/2012):** stood down from Labour's front bench in 2012 over his 2008 decision, against officials' advice, to grant citizenship to Labour donor William Yan; the Auditor-General found no improper motive or political interference ([OAG report](https://oag.parliament.nz/2013/citizenship/docs/inquiry-citizenship.pdf/view)).
- **Countdown allegations (2014):** accused Progressive Enterprises of extortion of suppliers; the Commerce Commission found no evidence to support the claims ([NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/nz/no-evidence-of-intimidating-suppliers-countdown-off-the-hook/VPYVTWDGON6QKLFMIEKMZHJ4NI/)).
- Later controversies as an NZ First minister (e.g. his April 2026 "butter chicken tsunami" remark about Indian migration, criticised by the Prime Minister as scaremongering) are covered in the NZ First profile ([1News](https://www.1news.co.nz/2026/04/20/jones-butter-chicken-tsunami-comment-scaremongering-says-pm/)).
