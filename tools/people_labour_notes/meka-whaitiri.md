## Current status
Whaitiri left Parliament after the 2023 election, which she contested as Te Pāti Māori's Ikaroa-Rāwhiti candidate, losing to Labour's Cushla Tangaere-Manuel by 2,843 votes ([2023 results](https://archive.electionresults.govt.nz/electionresults_2023/electorate-details-67.html)). She does not appear among 2026 candidates on policy.nz; Te Pāti Māori's 2026 Ikaroa-Rāwhiti candidate is Haley Maxwell ([policy.nz Ikaroa-Rāwhiti](https://policy.nz/2026/ikaroa-rawhiti-electorate/candidates/cushla-tangaere-manuel)). No reliable source was found for her activities since 2023.

## Biography
Born 11 January 1965 in Manutuke near Gisborne (Rongowhakaata, Ngāti Kahungunu) and raised in Whakatu, Hastings; her mother was the model for Napier's Pania of the Reef statue. She was head girl of Karamu High School, worked in a freezing works, earned a master's in education from Victoria University of Wellington and played softball and netball at national level (a Silver Ferns non-travelling reserve) ([NZ Herald](http://www.nzherald.co.nz/nz/news/article.cfm?c_id=1&objectid=10886303)). She worked for Parekura Horomia at the Department of Labour and in his ministerial office, for the Māori Women's Welfare League, and was chief executive of Ngāti Kahungunu Iwi Inc. (2009–2013). She has two sons.

## Political career
- **Ikaroa-Rāwhiti MP 2013–2023:** won the June 2013 by-election after Horomia's death (majority 1,659 over Mana's Te Hamua Nikora); majorities 4,673 (2014), 4,210 (2017) and 6,045 (2020) ([RNZ](http://www.radionz.co.nz/news/political/214123/majority-drops-slightly-in-final-by-election-result); [2020](https://www.electionresults.govt.nz/electionresults_2020/electorate-details-67.html)).
- **Minister:** Minister of Customs (outside Cabinet) 2017–2018 until stood down; reappointed Customs and Veterans, associate agriculture (animal welfare) and statistics (2020), Food Safety (2022), and lead Hawke's Bay cyclone recovery minister (March 2023). Co-chair of Labour's Māori caucus.
- **Defection (3 May 2023):** announced she was leaving Labour to stand for Te Pāti Māori; Hipkins removed her portfolios. Speaker Adrian Rurawhe treated her as an independent for parliamentary purposes without triggering the waka-jumping law, a decision National and ACT criticised ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/489210/speaker-rules-meka-whaitiri-not-affected-by-waka-jumping-law); [1News](https://www.1news.co.nz/2023/05/03/not-an-easy-one-whaitiri-confirms-defection-to-te-pati-maori/)). Willie Jackson and others linked the move to her not being promoted to Cabinet ([NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/nz/politics/willie-jackson-knows-why-meka-whaitiri-left-labour/E7ADK7WUNZH6JKHVOU777LM7LU/)).

## Controversies
- **Staff assault allegation (2018):** stood down as Customs Minister in August 2018 after a staff member alleged she had been assaulted; an inquiry found it "probable" Whaitiri had caused bruising and she was not reinstated that term ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/368002/official-report-into-meka-whaitiri-incident-released)).
- **Mid-term defection (2023):** her switch while a minister, and the handling of her status, was contentious (see above).
