## Current status
Belich became Labour's justice spokesperson in March 2026 and also holds the public service portfolio (No. 19 in the shadow cabinet) ([Stuff](https://www.stuff.co.nz/politics/360949687/hipkins-reshuffles-labour-portfolios-henare-departs-and-plan-hatched-maori-seats-race)). With National's Greg Fleming she co-sponsors a modern slavery bill requiring large companies to report on supply-chain slavery, which passed its first reading on 29 April 2026 with every party except ACT ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/593743/modern-slavery-bill-passes-first-reading-under-new-parliament-rule)). She is Labour's Epsom candidate, No. 23 on the list ([policy.nz](https://policy.nz/2026/party-vote/parties/labour-party)).

## Biography
Raised in Wellington, the granddaughter of former mayor Sir Jim Belich and daughter of trade unionists, Belich studied law and te reo Māori at Victoria University and was NZUSA co-president in 2005 with her future husband Andrew Kirton; they have three children ([NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/nz/news/article.cfm?c_id=1&objectid=12334288)). An employment lawyer, she worked for UK union Unison (2012–2016), winning a European Court of Justice case on working hours, then at Bartlett Law, the CTU (on equal pay) and the PSA.

## Political career
- List MP since 2020 (contested Epsom in 2020 and 2023); chaired the Education and Workforce Committee and was a junior whip (2023). Lost the 2023 Mt Albert selection to Helen White despite Ardern's support.
- Narrowly missed re-election in 2023 but entered Parliament on 6 December 2023 when Andrew Little declined his list seat ([NZ Gazette](https://gazette.govt.nz/notice/id/2023-au5688)). Junior opposition whip until March 2025.

## Record & notable actions
- **Member's bills passed:** Companies (Directors' Duties) Amendment Act 2023 (inherited from Duncan Webb), clarifying directors may consider wider factors such as ESG and Treaty matters; Crimes (Theft by Employer) Amendment Act 2025 (inherited from Ibrahim Omer), making wage theft a crime, passed in opposition with NZ First support ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/544621/theft-is-theft-bill-making-employers-theft-of-workers-wages-a-crime-passes)); Employment Relations (Employee Remuneration Disclosure) Amendment Act 2025, banning pay-secrecy clauses, passed in August 2025 with National, Green and Te Pāti Māori support ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/national/570603/two-labour-bills-pass-into-law)).
