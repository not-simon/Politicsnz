## Current status
Chen was selected in March 2026 as Labour's candidate for Auckland Central ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/indonz_featured/903375/asian-candidates-eye-larger-representation-in-parliament)) and is ranked 35 on the list.

## Biography
Born in Beijing in February 1994, Chen moved to New Zealand aged five and graduated BA/LLB from the University of Auckland (2017). She was president of the NZ Chinese Students' Association, a Foundation North trustee and an Auckland Philharmonia board member.

## Political career
- Contested East Coast Bays in 2017 and 2023 (losing to National's Erica Stanford) and Botany in 2020 (losing to Christopher Luxon by 3,999 votes).
- **List MP 2020–2023**; deputy chair (2020–2023) and then chair (2023) of the Economic Development, Science and Innovation Committee ([Parliament](https://www.parliament.nz/en/mps-and-electorates/former-members-of-parliament/chen-naisi/)).

## Controversies
- Academic Anne-Marie Brady's 2017 paper cited her student-association leadership as a link to united-front networks; Chen said she was "hurt" and rejected it ([Newshub](https://www.newshub.co.nz/home/politics/2019/05/labour-candidate-naisi-chen-outraged-to-be-cited-in-anne-marie-brady-s-china-paper.html)). In 2020 a group distributed flyers calling her a "CCP agent" ([The Spinoff](https://thespinoff.co.nz/politics/16-10-2020/naisi-chen-a-new-generation-of-chinese-new-zealander-is-parliament-bound/)).
