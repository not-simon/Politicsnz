## Current status
Clark retired at the 2023 election (announced 13 December 2022) and became Registrar of the University of Otago ([ODT](https://www.odt.co.nz/news/dunedin/david-clark-takes-otago-university-role)). Rachel Brooking succeeded him in Dunedin.

## Biography
Born 5 January 1973 and raised in Beachlands, Auckland, Clark studied theology and philosophy at Otago and Tübingen, was ordained a Presbyterian minister in 1997, and completed a PhD at Otago (2004). He worked as a Treasury analyst, adviser to minister David Parker, and warden of Selwyn College (2008–2011) ([Stuff](https://www.stuff.co.nz/national/politics/102696540/national-portrait-david-clark-health-minister)). He is married to Katrina and has three children; he has twice completed an Ironman.

## Political career
- **MP for Dunedin North (2011–2020) and Dunedin (2020–2023):** beat National's Michael Woodhouse four times, with a 15,521 majority in 2020 ([2020](https://electionresults.govt.nz/electionresults_2020/electorate-details-08.html)).
- Opposition spokesperson for revenue, economic development and small business; health spokesperson from March 2017.
- **Minister of Health, 26 Oct 2017 – 2 Jul 2020** (and Associate Finance until April 2020); after re-election, Minister of Commerce and Consumer Affairs, Statistics, Digital Economy and Communications, State-Owned Enterprises and the Earthquake Commission (2020 – Jan 2023) ([DPMC 2020](https://dpmc.govt.nz/sites/default/files/2020-11/ministerial-list-2-nov-2020.pdf)).

## Record & notable actions
- **"Mondayisation" (2013):** his member's bill moving Waitangi Day and Anzac Day holidays to the Monday when they fall on a weekend passed against the National government's opposition ([NZ Herald](http://www.nzherald.co.nz/business/news/article.cfm?c_id=3&objectid=10878134)).
- **Health:** announced the $1.4 billion new Dunedin Hospital (2018) ([Stuff](https://www.stuff.co.nz/national/health/103621156/popular-tourist-attraction-cadbury-world-closing-to-make-way-for-14-billion-dunedin-hospital)); signed a $173.5m pay-equity settlement for mental-health and addiction support workers and a safe-staffing accord with nurses (2018); scrapped the planned Waikato medical school; replaced the Waikato DHB board with a commissioner (2019); conceded the promised GP fee cut would be phased in.
- **Supermarkets:** sponsored the Grocery Industry Competition Bill responding to the Commerce Commission's supermarket study ([1News](https://www.1news.co.nz/2022/12/13/six-labour-mps-including-david-clark-to-retire-at-election/)).
- **Conscience votes:** for marriage equality (2013) and the Abortion Legislation Act (2020); against the End of Life Choice Act (2019).

## Controversies
- **Lockdown breaches (April 2020):** drove to a park to mountain-bike and drove his family 20 km to a beach during the alert-level-4 lockdown; Ardern declined his offer to resign but stripped his associate finance role and ranked him last in Cabinet ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/413617/david-clark-offers-to-resign-after-revealing-he-took-a-trip-to-beach-during-covid-19-lockdown)).
- **Bloomfield remarks and resignation (June–July 2020):** criticised for appearing to blame Director-General Ashley Bloomfield for quarantine failures at a press conference; resigned as Health Minister on 2 July 2020, saying he had become a distraction ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/political/420310/david-clark-resigns-as-health-minister-it-s-best-for-me-to-step-aside)).
- **Middlemore Hospital (2018):** accused by Counties Manukau DHB staff of trying to silence concerns about building problems; he denied it but later apologised to the board chair ([NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/nz/news/article.cfm?c_id=1&objectid=12072583)).
