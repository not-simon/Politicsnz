# 2023 New Zealand general election (14 October 2023)

> Structured data: [`data/elections/2023.json`](../data/elections/2023.json) · Parliament: [`data/parliaments/54.json`](../data/parliaments/54.json).

## At a glance

- **Change of government:** Labour (Chris Hipkins) fell from 65 seats to **34** (26.92%, −23.09 pp) – the worst defeat of a sitting
  government under MMP. National (Christopher Luxon) became the largest party with 38.08% and **48 seats** (49 after Port Waikato).
- **Government:** the first **three-party coalition** under MMP – **National–ACT–NZ First** – with separate coalition agreements
  signed 24 Nov 2023 and ministers sworn in **27 Nov 2023**. 68 of 123 seats.
- **Minor parties grew:** Greens 11.61% (15 seats, their best result, plus three electorates: Auckland Central, Wellington Central,
  Rongotai); ACT 8.64% (11 seats, and Brooke van Velden won Tāmaki); NZ First returned with 6.09% (8 seats).
- **Te Pāti Māori** won **six of seven Māori electorates** on 3.08% of the party vote – two overhang seats.
- **The biggest Parliament under MMP: 123 MPs.** ACT's Port Waikato candidate Neil Christensen died during the campaign, so the
  Port Waikato electorate vote was cancelled and a by-election held on 25 Nov 2023; National's Andrew Bayly (already elected from the
  list) won, Nancy Lu came in from National's list, and the House gained a third overhang seat.
- **Two-party vote** (National + Labour) was 65.0%, the lowest since 2002. **Turnout:** 78.20% (party vote).

## Context

Labour's 2020 majority was eroded by the long tail of COVID-19 (Auckland's 2021 Delta lockdown, vaccine mandates and the 2022
Parliament-grounds occupation), high inflation and interest rates, a cost-of-living squeeze, and controversy over **Three Waters**
reform and **co-governance**. Labour lost the 2022 Hamilton West by-election to National. Jacinda Ardern resigned in January 2023 and
Chris Hipkins became PM on 25 January. National had changed leader in 2021, Christopher Luxon
replacing Judith Collins on 30 November 2021.

## Campaign and issues

A Vote Compass survey found the top issues in general electorates were the cost of living (28%), the economy (17%), healthcare (14%),
crime (9%) and the environment (8%); in the Māori electorates, cost of living (35%), Māori issues (15%), the economy (14%).

**National – "Get our country back on track."** $14.6 billion of income-tax cuts for "the squeezed middle" (to be funded by public-
service cuts and new taxes on foreign home buyers, offshore gambling operators and commercial buildings – the foreign buyer tax was dropped in the coalition deal with NZ First); repeal of Three Waters and the "RMA 2.0" laws; a
100-day plan (scrapping the Auckland regional fuel tax, banning gang patches, restoring 90-day trials, banning cellphones in schools);
a $24 billion transport package with 13 "roads of national significance"; a third medical school at Waikato; ending the Clean Car
Discount; reversing blanket speed-limit reductions; a "traffic light" system for Jobseeker beneficiaries; ending the GM ban.

**Labour – "In It For You."** Removing GST from fresh and frozen fruit and vegetables (criticised by many economists); boosting
Working for Families; free dental care for under-30s from 2025; 300 more frontline police; 6,000 more state houses; keeping the
superannuation age at 65; ruling out a capital gains tax (Hipkins, 12 July); keeping free school lunches; a ram-raid
offence; a $1 billion boost for Pharmac.

**Greens – "The Time Is Now."** A wealth tax on net assets over $2 million, a 45% top rate, a 33% company rate and income-tax cuts for
those under $125,000; a guaranteed minimum income of $385 a week; rent controls and a rental warrant of fitness; 35,000 public homes;
free dental care; Hoki Whenua Mai (returning confiscated land). Ran "two-tick" campaigns in Auckland Central, Mount Albert, Rongotai and
Wellington Central.

**ACT.** A Ministry of Regulation and Regulatory Standards Bill; legislating the **principles of the Treaty of
Waitangi** with a referendum; abolishing Māori wards; repealing the Zero Carbon Act; three-strikes law restored; lowering the youth
justice age to 17; abolishing Fair Pay Agreements; abolishing the Ministry for Pacific Peoples, Human Rights Commission and Ministry
for Women; two-tick campaigns in Epsom and Tāmaki.

**NZ First – "Let's take back our country."** Opposing "racist separatism" and co-governance; English names for government
departments and making English an official language; removing GST on basic foods; breaking the supermarket duopoly and taking on
Australian-owned banks; a COVID-19 inquiry; moving the Ports of Auckland to Northport; a "gang prison"; transgender bathrooms and
sports policy.

**Te Pāti Māori** – no income tax under $30,000, a 48% rate over $300,000, a wealth tax, a 33% company rate; replacing Oranga
Tamariki with a Mokopuna Māori Authority; a Māori parliament and entrenched Māori seats. **TOP** – UBI and land tax, the "Teal Deal"
for under-30s, leader Raf Manji's bid for Ilam.

**The electorate-deal question.** National ended its "teacup" understanding with ACT and actively contested Epsom (28 Aug); Labour
ruled out a deal with the Greens in tight seats; Luxon said (25 Sep) he could work with NZ First after the election, and Sir John Key
urged voters to give National their party vote to avoid a hung Parliament.

**Co-governance and the Treaty** were the most polarising issues; National ruled out working with Te Pāti Māori (May 2023).

### Reading the result

- **Seat arithmetic.** National 48 + ACT 11 = 59 on the official count, short of 62 needed in a 122-seat House – so NZ First (8) was
  needed. After Port Waikato: 49 + 11 + 8 = 68 of 123.
- **Overhang.** Te Pāti Māori's six electorates exceeded its four-seat entitlement (2 overhang); the Port Waikato by-election added a
  third. This 123-member House is the largest under MMP.
- **Ticket-splitting.** National won 43.47% of electorate votes vs 38.08% party votes; the Greens and ACT won more electorates than
  ever (3 and 2); Te Pāti Māori's electorate vote (3.89%) was larger than its party vote (3.08%), consistent with Māori-roll voters
  splitting electorate TPM / party Labour or Green.
- **Recounts.** Judicial recounts changed results slightly: Mount Albert (Helen White, Labour, by 18), Nelson (Rachel Boyack, Labour,
  by 26) and Tāmaki Makaurau (Takutai Tarsh Kemp, TPM, by 42) were the three closest seats in the country.
- **Wasted vote.** 5.58% of valid party votes (TOP 2.22%, NZ Loyal 1.21% and many small parties) won no seats.

## Electorates

72 electorates (65 general, 7 Māori). National took 21 electorates from Labour (see the "changed party" list below); Labour's
electorate tally collapsed from 46 to 17, while Labour held Nelson by 26 and Mount Albert by 18. Highlights:

- **Te Pāti Māori** took Hauraki-Waikato (21-year-old Hana-Rawhiti Maipi-Clarke defeating Foreign Minister Nanaia Mahuta),
  Te Tai Hauāuru (Debbie Ngarewa-Packer), Te Tai Tokerau (Mariameno Kapa-Kingi by 517 over Kelvin Davis), Te Tai Tonga (Tākuta Ferris)
  and Tāmaki Makaurau (Takutai Tarsh Kemp by 42 over Peeni Henare), and held Waiariki (Rawiri Waititi, majority 15,891).
  Labour kept **Ikaroa-Rāwhiti** (Cushla Tangaere-Manuel) against Meka Whaitiri, who had defected to TPM.
- **Greens:** Chlöe Swarbrick held Auckland Central; Tamatha Paul won **Wellington Central** and Julie Anne Genter **Rongotai** –
  the first time the Greens held three electorates.
- **ACT:** Brooke van Velden won **Tāmaki** from National; David Seymour held Epsom (8,142) despite National contesting it.
- **Ōhāriu:** Labour's Greg O'Connor held off Finance-spokesperson Nicola Willis (1,260).
- **Port Waikato:** by-election 25 Nov 2023 (see above).

## Government formation

National began talks with ACT and NZ First after election night; the official results (3 Nov) removed the option of a two-party
National–ACT majority. ACT and NZ First held their first direct talks on 8 Nov. The caretaker Labour government stayed on while talks
continued. On 23 Nov the three leaders finalised the deal in Wellington; the **coalition agreements** (National–ACT and National–NZ
First, plus a shared programme) were published on **24 November 2023**, and Luxon and his ministers were sworn in on
**27 November 2023**. Key elements: ACT's **Treaty Principles Bill** would be introduced, but the government would not back ACT's referendum (National and
NZ First supported the bill only through its first reading); ACT's Regulatory Standards Bill (passed into law in Nov 2025, with NZ First
later pledging to repeal it); repeal of Three Waters (passed Feb 2024), Fair Pay Agreements, Auckland light rail and the Māori Health
Authority; Māori wards to face local referendums; English prioritised in government departments; the COVID-19 Royal Commission's scope
widened (NZ First); National's foreign-buyer tax dropped; and the Deputy Prime Minister role shared – Winston Peters, then David Seymour
from 31 May 2025.

## The 54th Parliament (2023–2026)

A turbulent term with many changes (details in [`data/parliaments/54.json`](../data/parliaments/54.json)):

- **Greens:** Golriz Ghahraman resigned (Jan 2024, shoplifting charges); Efeso Collins died (Feb 2024); James Shaw retired (May 2024);
  **Darleen Tana** left the party (Jul 2024) and became the first MP expelled under the 2018 "waka-jumping" law (22 Oct 2024);
  Benjamin Doyle resigned (Oct 2025).
- **Labour:** Andrew Little, Rino Tirikatene, Kelvin Davis, Grant Robertson, David Parker, Adrian Rurawhe and Peeni Henare all resigned
  and were replaced from the list.
- **Te Pāti Māori:** Takutai Tarsh Kemp died (26 Jun 2025) and **Oriini Kaipara** held **Tāmaki Makaurau** at the 6 Sep 2025
  by-election. In Nov 2025 the party **expelled Tākuta Ferris and Mariameno Kapa-Kingi**; Kapa-Kingi's expulsion was later ruled
  unlawful by the High Court (sources differ on the date of reinstatement: 5 Dec 2025 in Parliament's record per Wikipedia's
  54th-Parliament table vs the 10 Mar 2026 judgment), and on 11 May 2026 she left to found the **Te Tai Tokerau Party**. At dissolution
  TPM had 4 MPs and there were 2 independents.
- **National:** Judith Collins resigned (May 2026) to become President of the Law Commission; no by-election (too close to the
  election). Luxon survived leadership votes in April and August 2026.
- **NZ First:** Tanya Unkovich resigned (Jun 2025), replaced by David Wilson.

## What changed

1. The swing from 2020 was the largest under MMP; Labour's vote nearly halved.
2. MMP's first three-party coalition, and a return to formal written coalition agreements with policy "bottom lines".
3. Te Pāti Māori's Māori-seat sweep produced the largest overhang under MMP and a 123-seat House – reigniting debate about the
   overhang rule (the 2012 MMP review had recommended abolishing it).
4. Minor parties (Greens, ACT, NZ First, TPM) together won 29.4% of the party vote, the most of any election in this archive.
