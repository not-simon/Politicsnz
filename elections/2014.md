# 2014 New Zealand general election (20 September 2014)

## At a glance

- **Winner:** National (John Key) – 47.04% and 60 of 121 seats. On election night National had 61 seats, an outright majority, but it
  lost one seat to the Greens when special votes were counted (official results 4 October 2014).
- **Government:** third-term **National minority government**, again with confidence-and-supply partners ACT (1), United Future (1)
  and the Māori Party (2). Ministry sworn in 8 October 2014.
- **Labour** (David Cunliffe) fell to 25.13% and 32 seats – its lowest party-vote share of the MMP era. Cunliffe resigned and Andrew
  Little became leader in November 2014.
- **NZ First** rose to 8.66% (11 seats); the **Greens** were steady at 10.70% (14).
- **Missed out:** the Conservatives (3.97%) and Internet MANA (1.42%) – together more than 129,000 party votes with no seats. Hone
  Harawira lost Te Tai Tokerau, ending Mana's parliamentary presence.
- **Turnout:** 77.90%, with a record 717,579 advance votes (more than double 2011).

## Context

The 50th Parliament had been turbulent for National's support partners: ACT's only MP John Banks resigned in June 2014 after being
convicted (later overturned) of filing a false electoral return; United Future was deregistered for some weeks in 2013; and NZ First
expelled Brendan Horan. The Electoral Commission's 2012 MMP review had recommended lowering the party-vote threshold from 5% to 4% and
abolishing the "one electorate seat" coat-tail rule, but in May 2014 the government announced there was no cross-party consensus and it
would not legislate. So 2014 was fought under the same rules – and the same Epsom/Ōhāriu arrangements – as 2011.

The government had carried out its partial asset sales (Mighty River Power, Meridian and Genesis floated, Crown share cut from 100% to
51%), despite a 2013 citizens-initiated referendum in which a majority opposed them.

## Campaign and issues

- **Dirty Politics.** On 13 August Nicky Hager published *Dirty Politics*, based on hacked emails of blogger Cameron Slater, alleging
  that National Party figures and ministerial staff fed "attack politics" through right-wing blogs. It led to Justice Minister Judith
  Collins resigning as a minister on 30 August after an email suggested she had tried to undermine the head of the Serious Fraud Office.
- **Kim Dotcom and the "Moment of Truth".** The Internet Party, funded by Kim Dotcom, ran a joint list with Hone Harawira's Mana
  Movement ("Internet MANA"), relying on Harawira holding Te Tai Tokerau to bring list MPs in on his coat-tails. On 15 September it
  held "The Moment of Truth" with Glenn Greenwald, Edward Snowden and Julian Assange, about New Zealand's role in Five Eyes surveillance.
  Late in the campaign National's John Key and NZ First's Winston Peters publicly endorsed Labour's Kelvin Davis in Te Tai Tokerau.
- **Housing.** National promised to let KiwiSaver members withdraw member tax credits for a first home, double the KiwiSaver first-home
  deposit subsidy and raise the house-price caps. Labour campaigned on KiwiBuild-style state building and a **capital gains tax**
  excluding the family home – Cunliffe stumbled in *The Press* debate when he could not say whether family homes held in trusts would
  be exempt.
- **Health.** Labour launched (10 August) with free GP visits and prescriptions for pregnant women, under-13s and over-65s.
- **ACT** (new leader Jamie Whyte) promised to repeal the Resource Management Act and abolish the Overseas Investment Office.
- **Electricity.** Labour and the Greens promised reform of the wholesale electricity market (a single buyer, "NZ Power") to cut power
  prices; the power company share prices jumped after National's win.
- **Coalition signals.** Key urged voters not to split the party vote ("If you want steak for dinner tonight, go and buy steak");
  Cunliffe said he would work with the Greens and NZ First; both leaders ruled out working with Internet MANA.

### Reading the result

- **The "wasted" vote** returned to 6.24% of valid party votes – the Conservatives' 3.97% was the largest single vote ever to miss the
  threshold in this period, and it went entirely unrepresented.
- **Coat-tails both ways.** ACT (0.69%) and United Future (0.22%) won seats only through Epsom and Ōhāriu; Internet MANA (1.42%) won
  nothing because Harawira lost his electorate by 743 votes. United Future's seat was an **overhang** (it was entitled to none on the
  party vote), making the House 121.
- **Māori electorates.** Labour won six of seven – taking Tāmaki Makaurau and Te Tai Hauāuru from the Māori Party after Pita Sharples
  and Tariana Turia retired, and Te Tai Tokerau from Mana. The Māori Party kept only Waiariki (Te Ururoa Flavell) plus one list seat
  (Marama Fox).
- **Labour's split-vote problem.** Labour won 34.13% of electorate votes but only 25.13% of party votes – many voters backed Labour
  candidates locally but gave their party vote elsewhere (largely Greens and NZ First).

## Electorates

71 electorates (64 general, 7 Māori). Boundary changes created **Kelston** and **Upper Harbour** and abolished **Waitakere**. Notable:

- **Napier** – Labour's Stuart Nash won after National's retiring Chris Tremain's seat split between National's Wayne Walford and
  Conservative Garth McVicar.
- **Te Tai Tokerau** – Kelvin Davis (Labour) beat Hone Harawira (Internet MANA) by 743; Harawira's request for a judicial recount
  upheld the result.
- **Auckland Central** – Nikki Kaye (National) held off Jacinda Ardern by 600, the closest seat in the country.
- **Epsom** – ACT's new candidate David Seymour won by 4,250 over National's Paul Goldsmith, who again ran a party-vote-only campaign.

## Government formation

With 60 seats, National needed one more vote. It re-signed confidence-and-supply agreements with United Future (29 Sep 2014), ACT, and the
Māori Party (5 Oct 2014), giving it 64 votes in a 121-seat House, and the ministry was sworn in on 8 October 2014. John Key resigned as
Prime Minister in December 2016; Bill English succeeded him on 12 December 2016.

## The 51st Parliament (2014–2017)

The most consequential change was the **2015 Northland by-election** (28 March 2015): after National MP Mike Sabin resigned,
Winston Peters won the safe National seat for NZ First, cutting the government's working majority and showing the vulnerability of
provincial seats. Phil Goff (Mount Roskill) and David Shearer (Mount Albert) resigned and were replaced at by-elections by Michael
Wood and **Jacinda Ardern**; John Key and David Cunliffe resigned within six months of the 2017 election, so their seats were left vacant.

## What changed

1. Electorate deals proved fragile: the coat-tail rule saved ACT and United Future but failed Internet MANA.
2. The Conservatives' near-miss (3.97%) became the standard example of the 5% threshold's cost in "wasted" votes.
3. Labour's third successive defeat pushed it toward the leadership change (Little, then Ardern in 2017) that transformed 2017.
4. Labour reclaimed the Māori electorates (6 of 7), a dominance that lasted until 2023.
