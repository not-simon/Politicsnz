## What is being elected

- **120 seats** (plus any overhang): **71 electorates** – 64 general (48 North Island, 16 South Island) and 7 Māori – and **49 list
  seats**. After the 2023 Census the Representation Commission cut one North Island general electorate (49 → 48), so there is one
  more list seat than in 2020/2023.
- The 2023 Parliament had 123 seats because of three overhang seats; Te Pāti Māori's Māori-seat strength makes another overhang
  possible.
- MMP threshold unchanged: **5% of the party vote or one electorate seat**.

### Electorate boundary changes (2025 review)

Draft boundaries were published on 25 March 2025 and finalised on 8 August 2025. Nearly half of North Island electorates were outside
the ±5% tolerance.

Bays, Wellington North.

### Rule changes since 2023

- **Electoral Amendment Act 2025** (passed 16 Dec 2025): enrolment closes 13 days before polling day; **prisoners barred from
  voting**; anonymous/"voluntary" donation threshold raised from $5,000 to $6,000; ban on handing out food and most drinks near
  polling places. Opposition parties have described the enrolment changes as voter suppression; the government rejects that.
- The Electoral Commission now emails EasyVote information where it has an email address (post otherwise).
- Enrolment: by 15 October, 65.5% of 18–24-year-olds were enrolled; in July more than half of ethnic-Māori voters (302,096) were on
  the Māori roll.

## The record being judged: the 54th Parliament (2023–26)

The National–ACT–NZ First coalition (68 of 123 seats at the start, 67 of 122 at dissolution) made sweeping changes, many under
urgency. Among them: repeal of Fair Pay Agreements, the Clean Car Discount, Three Waters and Labour's RMA replacement laws; disestablishing
the Māori Health Authority; repealing the smokefree generation law; restoring interest deductibility for landlords and a two-year
bright-line test; road-user charges for EVs; reinstating 90-day trials, three-strikes and Māori-ward referendums; the **Fast-track
Approvals Act 2024**; **Local Water Done Well**; the **Equal Pay Amendment Act 2025** (pay-equity changes, opposed by all opposition
parties); repealing the offshore oil-and-gas exploration ban; and the **Regulatory Standards Act 2025**. ACT's **Treaty Principles
Bill** drew the November 2024 hīkoi to Parliament and was voted down at its second reading on 10 April 2025, National and NZ First
having supported it only to select committee. The rotating deputy prime ministership passed from Winston Peters to David Seymour on
31 May 2025.

Inside Parliament, Te Pāti Māori went from six MPs to four (Kemp's death and the Tāmaki Makaurau by-election win by Oriini Kaipara;
the expulsions of Tākuta Ferris and Mariameno Kapa-Kingi), the Greens lost or replaced five MPs, and Luxon faced two leadership
votes in 2026 (April and August). From March 2026 the Iran war and closure of the Strait of Hormuz drove a fuel-price shock.

## Campaign issues

An Ipsos Issues Monitor (May 2026) found the top concerns were the **cost of living/inflation (61%)**, healthcare (39%), the economy
(33%), petrol prices (26%), housing (21%) and unemployment (19%). Respondents rated Labour most capable on inflation, health, petrol,
housing, jobs, poverty and education; National on the economy and crime; the Greens on climate change. A September Ipsos survey found
64% support for the Greens' proposed 2.5% wealth tax on assets over $10 million (excl. family homes) and 45% for Labour's capital gains
tax.

Other prominent issues: **supermarket duopoly** (five parties have break-up or competition policies); energy prices and the fuel
shock; Treaty/co-governance and the future of the Māori seats; immigration and citizenship; law and order; KiwiSaver and retirement
savings; misinformation; and **strategic voting** (Te Pāti Māori urging electorate-TPM/party-Labour-or-Green; National urging no vote
splitting; a businessman's site urging right-leaning voters to give National the electorate vote and ACT/NZ First the party vote).

## Main parties' key promises (summary)

Detailed policy texts are in each party folder (`parties/<party>/policies/`); the summaries below are from campaign coverage
collected on Wikipedia's 2026 election article.

- **National** (Luxon; "ten commitments", launched 27 Sep): no new taxes and three budget-responsibility rules (delaying further tax
  cuts); **compulsory KiwiSaver** with default contributions rising to 6% by 2032 and automatic enrolment of babies; paid parental
  leave extended from 26 to 30 weeks; replacing NCEA; easier 5% deposits for first-home buyers; lower student-loan repayment rates for
  borrowers at home; splitting Foodstuffs to break the supermarket duopoly; $604m for Pharmac and expanded cancer screening, funded by
  cutting non-Pacific aid; welfare savings ($585m) and drug-testing of beneficiaries; referendums on MMP and a four-year term; ruled out
  working with TOP.
- **Labour** (Hipkins; "Better Starts Now"): a **capital gains tax** on property (excl. family home and farms) to fund **three free
  GP visits a year**; a **NZ Future Fund**; repeal of the Regulatory Standards Act in 100 days; forcing supermarkets to separate
  wholesale and retail; public transport caps ($20/week in the main cities); 10% student-loan write-off; restoring pay equity; a "fair
  go" consumer law; debt to 20% of GDP and a Parliamentary Budget Office; recognising Palestine; an under-16 social media ban to select
  committee. Labour and the Greens announced at Waitangi (Feb 2026) that they would work together in government.
- **Greens** (Davidson & Swarbrick; "For All of Us"): wealth, inheritance and corporate taxes with a tax-free band under $10,000 and
  higher rates over $160,000; 40,000 social homes and 2% rent-rise caps; a state-owned **KiwiMart** supermarket chain; Kiwipower
  renewable energy entity; free/cheap public transport and $4b intercity rail; revoking fast-track mining consents; restoring the
  previous school-lunch programme and pay equity.
- **ACT** (Seymour): cutting government spending and merging ministries; ending "race-based hiring"; entrenching equal rights, rule of
  law and parliamentary sovereignty; abolishing the Climate Change Commission and net-zero target; dissolving the Waitangi Tribunal
  once historical claims are settled; progressively raising the retirement age; a three-year minimum-wage cap; tighter immigration
  and welfare rules for residents; opposes breaking up supermarkets.
- **NZ First** (Peters): breaking up Foodstuffs; splitting the "gentailer" power companies; nationalising BNZ to merge with Kiwibank;
  a Crown-funded KiwiSaver for newborns and a $5,000/year "Kiwi Kids Grant"; voting and super restricted to citizens; a referendum on
  the Māori seats and on capping resident visas at 20,000; mining on conservation land; repealing the Regulatory Standards Act it voted
  for; ruled out working with Labour.
- **Te Pāti Māori** (Ngarewa-Packer & Waititi): a 48% top tax rate, wealth/land-banking/vacant-house taxes; a Te Tiriti Commission and
  binding Waitangi Tribunal recommendations; abolishing prisons by 2040; standing in all seven Māori seats.
- **TOP** (Qiulae Wong): land value tax funding a means-tested "citizen's income"; citizens' assemblies; lobbying and donations reform;
  bottom-trawling ban.

## Polling

129 published party-vote polls since the 2023 election (Roy Morgan 35, Taxpayers' Union–Curia 33, 1 News–Verian 20, Talbot Mills 21,
The Post/Freshwater 8, RNZ–Reid Research 8, Curia 2, Anacta 2). National led through 2024; Labour overtook it in most polls from early
2025 into mid-2026; by October 2026 the two were roughly level in the high 20s/low 30s, with NZ First around 10–12%, the Greens rising
into the mid-teens, ACT around 9–11% and TOP at 5–8% – potentially over the threshold. Te Pāti Māori fell to 0.4–2% in several late
polls, though it could still win electorates.
