# 2020 New Zealand general election (17 October 2020)

## At a glance

- **Landslide:** Labour (Jacinda Ardern) won **50.01%** of the party vote and **65 of 120 seats** – the first single-party majority
  since MMP began in 1996, the first time Labour had won an absolute majority of the vote since 1946, the best popular-vote result for any main party since 1951 and the
  most seats any party had won since 1990.
- **National** (Judith Collins) fell to 25.58% and 33 seats, its second-worst result ever (only 2002 was worse).
- **ACT** jumped from 1 seat to **10** (7.59%); the **Greens** won 10 seats (7.86%) – the first minor party to increase its vote after a
  term supporting a government – and Chlöe Swarbrick won **Auckland Central**, the Greens' first electorate since 1999.
- **NZ First** (2.60%) lost every seat; **Te Pāti Māori** returned with Rawiri Waititi winning Waiariki (+1 list seat).
- **Government:** Labour governed alone but signed a **cooperation agreement** with the Greens (31 Oct 2020). Sworn in 6 Nov 2020.
- **Turnout:** 82.24% – the highest of the six elections in this archive.
- **Referendums:** the End of Life Choice Act was approved (65.1%); recreational cannabis legalisation was narrowly rejected (50.7% no).

## Context

The 2017–20 term was dominated by the 15 March 2019 Christchurch mosque attacks (and the subsequent firearms reforms), the 2019 Whakaari
/ White Island eruption and, from March 2020, the **COVID-19 pandemic**. New Zealand's elimination strategy, a seven-week national
lockdown and a near-closed border were popular, and Ardern's approval soared. National changed leader twice in 2020: Todd Muller
replaced Simon Bridges on 22 May, then resigned on 14 July citing health reasons, and Judith Collins took over.

The election had been set for 19 September. After community transmission re-emerged in Auckland on 11 August (Auckland back to alert
level 3), campaigns were suspended and on 17 August Ardern moved polling day to **17 October**, with Parliament dissolving on 6 September.

## Campaign and issues

- **COVID-19 recovery and border management** framed everything; ACT attacked the government's response as "demonstrably
  unsustainable" and called for a "smart border".
- **Tax.** Labour promised a new **39% top tax rate on income over $180,000** (affecting about 2% of earners, ~$550m a year); the Greens proposed a wealth tax and a **guaranteed minimum income** of $325 a week.
- **Infrastructure.** Labour's $12 billion New Zealand Upgrade Programme (announced January 2020); National promised $31 billion of
  transport projects over a decade and abolishing Auckland's regional fuel tax.
- **Energy and climate.** Labour moved its 100% renewable electricity target forward to 2030; the Greens' Clean Energy Plan aimed to end
  coal use by 2030 and proposed mandatory climate-risk disclosure for large companies.
- **Other pledges.** Labour: Matariki public holiday from 2022 (delivered). National: EVs in bus lanes, child dental care, doubling
  Serious Fraud Office funding. NZ First: keep the Provincial Growth Fund as a bottom line, cap skilled immigration, 1,000 more police.
  ACT: an employment-insurance scheme; the End of Life Choice Act on the referendum ballot was David Seymour's member's bill.
- **Coalition signals.** Under Bridges, National ruled out NZ First ("I can't trust New Zealand First"); Muller reopened the door.

### Reading the result

- **A majority from 50%.** With 7.71% of the party vote wasted on parties under the threshold (mainly NZ First, TOP, New Conservative,
  Advance NZ), Labour's 50.01% translated into 65 seats (54%).
- **Electorate sweep.** Labour won 46 of 72 electorates, taking 19 seats from National (including Ilam, Rangitata, Nelson, Whanganui,
  Northcote, Upper Harbour and Wairarapa) and Northland by just 163 votes.
- **National's lowest point.** National won only 23 electorates; its 25.58% was its worst result since 2002.
- **ACT's breakthrough** came from party votes, not deals: 7.59% gave it 9 list seats on top of Epsom.
- **Māori electorates:** Labour 6, Te Pāti Māori 1 (Waiariki, majority 836) – enough with 1.17% party vote for one extra list seat
  (Debbie Ngarewa-Packer).
- **Disinformation-era parties.** Advance NZ (co-led by former National MP Jami-Lee Ross and Billy Te Kahika), running on COVID-19
  conspiracy themes, won 0.98% and no seats.

## Electorates

72 electorates (65 general, 7 Māori) after the 2019–20 boundary review added one North Island general electorate (new or renamed seats: Banks Peninsula, Dunedin, Kaipara ki Mahurangi, Panmure-Ōtāhuhu, Port Waikato, Southland, Taieri, Takanini, Whangaparāoa). Notable:

- **Auckland Central** – Chlöe Swarbrick (Green) won by 1,068 over Labour's Helen White after National's Nikki Kaye retired.
- **Northland** – Labour's Willow-Jean Prime beat National's Matt King by 163, the closest result.
- **Epsom** – David Seymour won 9,224 votes clear of Labour's Camilla Belich; National's party vote was the only one Labour did not
  top (Labour won the most party votes in 71 of 72 electorates).
- **Waiariki** – Rawiri Waititi (Te Pāti Māori) unseated Labour's Tāmati Coffey by 836.
- **Botany** – Christopher Luxon (National) won the seat vacated by Jami-Lee Ross.

## Government formation

Labour could govern alone. On 31 October Ardern announced a **cooperation agreement** (explicitly not a coalition) with the Greens:
co-leaders James Shaw (Climate Change; Associate Environment – biodiversity) and Marama Davidson (new Minister for the Prevention of
Family and Sexual Violence; Associate Housing – homelessness) became ministers outside Cabinet, and the Greens agreed to abstain on
confidence and supply. The Sixth Labour Government's second ministry was sworn in on 6 November 2020.

## The 53rd Parliament (2020–2023)

Ardern resigned as Prime Minister in January 2023; **Chris Hipkins** was sworn in on 25 January 2023, and Ardern left Parliament in
April. National replaced Collins with **Christopher Luxon** (30 November 2021). By-elections: **Tauranga 2022** (Sam Uffindell,
National hold) and **Hamilton West 2022** (Tama Potaka, National gain, after Labour expelled Gaurav Sharma and he resigned to force
the contest). In 2023 Meka Whaitiri left Labour for Te Pāti Māori and Elizabeth Kerekere left the Greens; both sat as independents.
The term also included the 2022 Parliament-grounds protest/occupation, Three Waters reform, the 2023 Auckland floods and Cyclone
Gabrielle.

## What changed

1. MMP produced its first single-party majority – showing it is possible, but only with ~50% of the vote and a high wasted vote.
2. ACT transformed from a one-seat Epsom party into a 10-seat party – reducing the importance of the Epsom deal.
3. NZ First was eliminated (and returned in 2023); the Greens won an electorate; Te Pāti Māori re-entered Parliament.
4. The scale of 2020 set up the scale of the 2023 swing back.
