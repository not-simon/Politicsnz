# How to vote in the 2026 General Election

*Election day is **Saturday 7 November 2026**, 9am to 7pm. Enrolment closed at **midnight Sunday 25 October**. Last checked against the Electoral Commission's pages on Fri 23 Oct 2026.*

> **The rules changed this year.** In December 2025 Parliament passed the Electoral Amendment Act 2025, which (Public Act 2025 No 82, Royal assent 19 December 2025, most of it in force from 20 December 2025). It closes enrolment 13 days before election day, so there is no enrolling at a voting place during advance voting or on election day; sets a 12-day advance voting period; and bars more sentenced prisoners from voting. ## 1. Am I allowed to vote?

*For: everyone; first-time voters; new residents; people on visas; people in prison or on remand.*

- You can enrol and vote if all three are true: you are 18 or older; you are a New Zealand citizen or a 'resident for electoral purposes'; and you have lived in New Zealand continuously for 12 months or more at some time in your life. [[source]](https://vote.nz/enrolling/get-ready-to-enrol/are-you-eligible-to-enrol-and-vote) <sub>vote.nz, 'Are you eligible to enrol and vote?', retrieved 2026-10-23; Electoral Act 1993 s 74</sub>
- 'Resident for electoral purposes' means you live in New Zealand lawfully and your visa does not make you leave by a set date. Resident-visa holders (including the 2021 Resident Visa) qualify. People on work, student or visitor visas with an end date do not. [[source]](https://vote.nz/enrolling/get-ready-to-enrol/are-you-eligible-to-enrol-and-vote) <sub>vote.nz, retrieved 2026-10-23</sub>
- If you are 17 you can fill in the form now. You are enrolled automatically on your 18th birthday. If you turn 18 on or before 7 November 2026, you can vote, as long as you applied to enrol by midnight 25 October. [[source]](https://vote.nz/2026-general-election/about/overview/communications-from-the-electoral-commission/your-easyvote-card) <sub>vote.nz EasyVote page and eligibility page, retrieved 2026-10-23</sub>
- If you live in New Zealand and are eligible, enrolling is compulsory by law. Voting itself is not compulsory. [[source]](https://vote.nz/2026-general-election/voting-in-the-election/need-help-to-vote) <sub>vote.nz, 'Need help to vote?' (dementia section) and 'Facts about New Zealand elections', retrieved 2026-10-23</sub>
- Prison (changed in 2025): you cannot vote if you are serving a sentence of 3 years or more. You also cannot vote if you are serving a sentence of less than 3 years for any offence committed on or after 20 December 2025. You CAN still vote if you are serving less than 3 years and all your offences happened before 20 December 2025, or if you are on remand, home detention or a community sentence. The Commission works with Corrections to provide voting for eligible prisoners. [[source]](https://vote.nz/enrolling/get-ready-to-enrol/are-you-eligible-to-enrol-and-vote) <sub>vote.nz eligibility page, retrieved 2026-10-23; Electoral Act 1993 s 80(1)(d) as at 1 Jan 2026 (raw/howtovote/electoral-act-1993-2026-01-01.pdf); Ministry of Justice, 'Electoral law changes'</sub>
- You also cannot vote if you are a NZ citizen who has not been in New Zealand in the last 3 years, a resident (non-citizen) who has not been in NZ in the last 12 months, if you have been held in a psychiatric hospital for more than 3 years after a criminal charge, or if you are on the Corrupt Practices List. [[source]](https://vote.nz/enrolling/get-ready-to-enrol/are-you-eligible-to-enrol-and-vote) <sub>vote.nz eligibility page, retrieved 2026-10-23; Electoral Act s 80</sub>
- Homeless? You can enrol at the address where you spend most of your time, or the last address where you lived for a month or more. You need a postal address (a PO Box or a friend, family member, church or support group who will hold mail for you). [[source]](https://vote.nz/enrolling/get-ready-to-enrol/are-you-eligible-to-enrol-and-vote) <sub>vote.nz eligibility page, retrieved 2026-10-23</sub>

**Notes**

- The brief said 'prisoners now barred under the 2025 Act'. Verified with a correction: the ban is not total. Sentenced prisoners serving under 3 years can still vote if all their offences were before 20 December 2025 (Electoral Act s 80(1)(d)(i)-(ii); vote.nz). RNZ (Dec 2025) reported the Act 'bans prisoners from voting'; 1News (16 Oct 2026) called it 'a total ban on prisoner voting for prisoners sentenced after December 2025'. The Act's wording turns on when the offence happened, not the sentencing date.
- Unsure? Call 0800 36 76 56. The law is Electoral Act 1993 ss 74 and 80.

## 2. Enrol or update your details by midnight, Sunday 25 October

*For: everyone who has moved house; first-time voters; people on the unpublished roll; Māori choosing a roll.*

- Deadline: midnight, Sunday 25 October 2026. The law now closes the roll 13 days before election day. You cannot enrol or change your address after this, and you cannot enrol at a voting place during advance voting or on election day. Voting place staff can't make exceptions; the rule is set by law. *(Sun 25 Oct 2026)* [[source]](https://vote.nz/2026-general-election/voting-in-the-election/what-to-do) <sub>vote.nz 'What to do' and eligibility pages, retrieved 2026-10-23; Electoral Act s 3(1) 'close of registration' (inserted by Electoral Amendment Act 2025 s 4(2)); 1News 16 Oct 2026 ('there are no exceptions, the Electoral Commission says')</sub>
- Fastest way: online at vote.nz with your NZ driver licence, NZ passport or RealMe verified identity. The same page lets you check what address you are enrolled at. [[source]](https://vote.nz/enrolling/enrol-or-update/enrol-or-update-online) <sub>vote.nz, retrieved 2026-10-23; Electoral Commission release 22 Oct 2026</sub>
- No ID? Fill in the form online and have it emailed or posted to you to sign ('Enrol without ID' / 'Update without ID'), download the PDF form, call 0800 36 76 56, or text your name and address to 3676. Then return the signed form by upload (vote.nz/upload), email (enrol@vote.nz), or drop it at an enrolment hub. Hub times and places are at vote.nz/enrolling/get-help-to-enrol/enrolment-in-your-community. [[source]](https://vote.nz/enrolling/enrol-or-update/other-ways-to-enrol) <sub>vote.nz 'Other ways to enrol', retrieved 2026-10-23</sub>
- Post is slow: the Commission recommended posting forms by Tuesday 20 October. It will accept forms that arrive by post on Tuesday 27 October but not after. If you still have a paper form, upload or email it, or take it to a hub. *(Tue 20 Oct 2026)* [[source]](https://vote.nz/enrolling/enrol-or-update/other-ways-to-enrol) <sub>vote.nz 'Other ways to enrol', retrieved 2026-10-23</sub>
- Moved house but still enrolled at an old address? You can still vote, but you may need to cast a special vote. If your new home is in a different electorate, only your party vote will count. Update your address by 25 October so both votes count. [[source]](https://vote.nz/2026-general-election/voting-in-the-election/enrol-by-25-october) <sub>vote.nz 'Enrol by 25 October', retrieved 2026-10-23</sub>
- Dormant roll: if mail from the Commission came back from your old address you may be on the 'dormant roll'. You can still vote (as a special vote) but, again, if you have moved electorate only your party vote counts. Updating your address before 25 October fixes this. [[source]](https://vote.nz/2026-general-election/voting-in-the-election/enrol-by-25-october) <sub>vote.nz, retrieved 2026-10-23</sub>
- Not enrolled at any address by midnight 25 October? The Commission says you will not be able to vote in this election. If you turn up believing you are enrolled, you can cast a special vote, but it will be disallowed if you turn out not to be enrolled. [[source]](https://vote.nz/2026-general-election/voting-in-the-election/enrol-by-25-october) <sub>vote.nz 'Enrol by 25 October', retrieved 2026-10-23; 1News 16 Oct 2026</sub>
- Unpublished roll: if you fear for your safety, you can ask to go on the confidential unpublished roll (download the form or call 0800 36 76 56). Your details are kept off printed rolls. You will then cast a special vote, you can't check or update your details online, and you can't download overseas voting papers. [[source]](https://vote.nz/enrolling/get-ready-to-enrol/can-you-go-on-the-unpublished-roll) <sub>vote.nz, retrieved 2026-10-23</sub>
- Māori roll or general roll: if you are of Māori descent you choose a roll when you first enrol. You vote for the same parties on either roll; the difference is which electorate candidates you choose from. Already-enrolled Māori could change rolls until midnight 6 August 2026; the 3-month pre-election freeze now applies. If you are enrolling for the first time you can still choose either roll up to 25 October. *(Thu 6 Aug 2026)* [[source]](https://vote.nz/maori-enrolment/about-the-option/about/maori-roll-or-general-roll) <sub>vote.nz key dates and 'Māori roll or general roll?'; Electoral Commission release '17,000 Māori voters change rolls', 18 Aug 2026</sub>

**Notes**

- Why it changed: the Government said late enrolments put 'too much strain on the system' and slowed the count (Justice Minister Paul Goldsmith, quoted by RNZ, July 2025). The Attorney-General reported the cut-off 'appears to constitute an unjustified limit' on the right to vote under the Bill of Rights Act (reported by RNZ). The Chief Electoral Officer told MPs the change was 'not a change that we recommended' and would not make official results faster than 20 days (RNZ, Sept 2025). Labour has said it would reverse the change (RNZ). Both sides' views are reported here; the guide takes no position.
- Scale: in 2023, about 450,000 people enrolled or updated in the final two weeks, including about 110,000 on election day (1News, 16 Oct 2026, citing Commission data). The Commission expects about 55,000 special votes to be disallowed this year, five times more than usual, because the voter turns out not to be enrolled (1News).
- On 21 October 2026, 3,729,667 people were enrolled, 92.1% of those eligible; only 68.6% of 18 to 24 year olds were enrolled (Electoral Commission, 22 Oct 2026).
- Enrolment packs were posted 17 to 22 August 2026; if you didn't get one, your address was out of date or you were not enrolled (Electoral Commission, 10 Aug 2026). The Commission also used data matching with NZTA and Internal Affairs to contact 170,000 people in September (release 17 Sep 2026).

## 3. When and where to vote

*For: everyone; shift workers; people voting away from home.*

- Advance voting: Monday 26 October to Friday 6 November. You can vote at any voting place in New Zealand. Days and hours differ from place to place, and some places open only before election day or only on election day, so check before you go. *(Mon 26 Oct 2026)* [[source]](https://vote.nz/2026-general-election/voting-in-the-election/find-a-voting-place) <sub>vote.nz 'Find a voting place', retrieved 2026-10-23; Electoral Act s 171B (12-day advance period)</sub>
- Election day: Saturday 7 November, every voting place open 9am to 7pm. If you are inside a voting place at 7pm waiting to vote, you can still vote. *(Sat 7 Nov 2026)* [[source]](https://vote.nz/2026-general-election/about/overview/key-dates) <sub>vote.nz key dates; Electoral Act s 161</sub>
- Find a place: use the official map at map.vote.nz (search your address; filter by day, 'open now', accessibility, Whare Pōti Māori, reo rua), the list by electorate and suburb on vote.nz, posters at your local supermarket or library, or call 0800 36 76 56. [[source]](https://map.vote.nz) <sub>vote.nz 'Find a voting place'; Electoral Commission releases 7 and 22 Oct 2026</sub>
- Vote close to home if you can. It is faster because your name is on the printed roll there. Anywhere else you can still vote, but you fill in a special-vote declaration first. [[source]](https://vote.nz/2026-general-election/voting-in-the-election/what-to-do) <sub>vote.nz 'What to do', retrieved 2026-10-23</sub>
- What to bring: nothing. No ID is needed. Bring your EasyVote card if you have one (paper, phone or screenshot); it helps staff find you on the roll. You can vote without it. [[source]](https://vote.nz/2026-general-election/about/overview/free-and-fair-elections/facts-about-new-zealand-elections) <sub>vote.nz 'Facts about New Zealand elections' ('No ID is required') and EasyVote page, retrieved 2026-10-23</sub>
- EasyVote cards went to everyone enrolled by 4 October, by email (from no-reply@easy.vote.nz; follow-up texts from 5101) or by post, between 14 and 22 October. Check your spam folder. If you enrolled after 4 October you won't get one, but you can still vote. [[source]](https://elections.nz/media-and-news/2026/easyvote-cards-are-on-their-way) <sub>Electoral Commission release, 14 Oct 2026; vote.nz EasyVote page</sub>
- Quieter times: if you prefer less crowding or need assistance, the Commission says 9am to 11am and 2pm to 4pm on weekdays are usually best. [[source]](https://vote.nz/2026-general-election/voting-in-the-election/need-help-to-vote) <sub>vote.nz 'Need help to vote?', retrieved 2026-10-23</sub>
- Time off work on election day: if you haven't had a reasonable chance to vote before starting work, your employer must let you leave by 3pm for the rest of the day, with no pay docked. If you do essential work after 3pm, you get a reasonable time off earlier in the day, with up to 2 hours paid. Employers who refuse can be fined up to $1,000. *(Sat 7 Nov 2026)* [[source]](https://vote.nz/2026-general-election/voting-in-the-election/cant-get-to-a-voting-place) <sub>Electoral Act 1993 s 162 (verified in the consolidated Act as at 1 Jan 2026, raw/howtovote/electoral-act-1993-2026-01-01.pdf); vote.nz 'Can't get to a voting place?' (time away from work section)</sub>

**Notes**

- Staff wear orange vests and will guide you. Many are working their first election; the Commission asks for patience if there are delays (vote.nz, 'What to expect at the voting place').
- Monday 26 October is Labour Day; 207 voting places open that day (Commission release 22 Oct 2026; RNZ explainer 23 Oct 2026).

## 4. Voting away from home, special votes, and voting when you can't get to a voting place

*For: students and workers away from home; people who enrolled after 4 October; people on the unpublished or dormant roll; people in hospital, care homes or prison; people with illness or mobility problems.*

- A special vote is a normal vote plus a short declaration form you sign. You cast one if your name is not on the printed roll where you vote: because you enrolled or updated after Writ Day (4 October), you are voting outside your electorate, you are on the unpublished roll, you are on the dormant roll, or you are voting from overseas or away from a voting place. [[source]](https://vote.nz/2026-general-election/voting-in-the-election/what-to-do) <sub>vote.nz 'What to do'; elections.nz 'How are general election votes counted?'; Electoral Act s 61</sub>
- Voting in another electorate: you get the ballot paper for YOUR home electorate, so your electorate vote still counts, as long as you are enrolled there. If you vote for an electorate you are not enrolled in, the electorate vote is disallowed and only your party vote is counted ('Party Vote Only'). [[source]](https://elections.nz/democracy-in-nz/about-elections/how-are-general-election-votes-counted/) <sub>elections.nz, retrieved 2026-10-23</sub>
- How special votes are counted: they are sent back to your home electorate, every declaration is checked against the roll, and they are added to the official count. They can arrive up to 10 days after election day. That is why they are not in election-night results and why official results take until 27 November. *(Fri 27 Nov 2026)* [[source]](https://elections.nz/democracy-in-nz/about-elections/how-are-general-election-votes-counted/) <sub>elections.nz; vote.nz 'What happens after an election?'</sub>
- Can't get to a voting place (illness, mobility)? Someone can collect your voting papers for you ('takeaway' voting). Sign a written authorisation (form on vote.nz, or handwritten with your name, phone, enrolled address, which roll you are on, and a line authorising the bearer). They bring the papers to you; you complete them with a witnessed special-vote declaration; they must be back at a voting place before 7pm on 7 November. [[source]](https://vote.nz/2026-general-election/voting-in-the-election/cant-get-to-a-voting-place) <sub>vote.nz 'Can't get to a voting place?', retrieved 2026-10-23</sub>
- Nobody to collect them? Contact your electorate manager (list on vote.nz). It may be possible for papers to be delivered to you and collected again. [[source]](https://vote.nz/2026-general-election/voting-in-the-election/contact-your-electorate-manager) <sub>vote.nz, retrieved 2026-10-23</sub>
- In hospital: a friend or family member can bring you your voting papers. If you know you will be in hospital, the Commission suggests voting in advance. In a care home or rest home: you don't need to do anything; the electorate manager arranges voting with your home's manager. Ask your home what has been arranged. [[source]](https://vote.nz/2026-general-election/voting-in-the-election/cant-get-to-a-voting-place) <sub>vote.nz, retrieved 2026-10-23</sub>
- Blind, low vision or a physical disability that stops you marking the paper: use telephone dictation voting (see Accessibility section). Remote locations (DOC islands, offshore installations, vessels in NZ waters): apply early to remote@vote.nz. [[source]](https://vote.nz/2026-general-election/voting-in-the-election/cant-get-to-a-voting-place/remote-voting) <sub>vote.nz 'Remote voting', retrieved 2026-10-23</sub>
- In prison or on remand and eligible? The Commission works with Corrections to provide voting services. [[source]](https://vote.nz/2026-general-election/voting-in-the-election/cant-get-to-a-voting-place) <sub>vote.nz, retrieved 2026-10-23</sub>

**Notes**

- 'Voting from home' in the sense of postal voting for everyone does not exist for general elections in New Zealand; the options above (collected papers, electorate-manager delivery, dictation, remote and overseas services) are what the Commission offers.

## 5. Voting from overseas

*For: New Zealanders living abroad; travellers; students overseas; Defence Force and public servants posted overseas.*

- Who can: you must be enrolled, 18+, have lived in NZ for 12+ continuous months at some time, and be either a NZ citizen who has been in NZ within the last 3 years, or a resident for electoral purposes who has been in NZ within the last 12 months. Public servants, diplomats and Defence Force members posted overseas, and their families, qualify however long they have been away. Enrolling from overseas is optional. [[source]](https://vote.nz/2026-general-election/overseas/vote-from-overseas) <sub>vote.nz 'How to vote from overseas', retrieved 2026-10-23; Electoral Act s 80(1)(a)-(b)</sub>
- Enrol or update by midnight Sunday 25 October, NZ time. You can't download papers until your enrolment is processed, so do it early. Your electorate is where you last lived for a month or more. If you have been away too long to update online, email overseas@vote.nz. *(Sun 25 Oct 2026)* [[source]](https://vote.nz/enrolling/get-ready-to-enrol/enrol-from-overseas) <sub>vote.nz, retrieved 2026-10-23</sub>
- Voting opened Wednesday 21 October (NZ time). The easiest way: download your papers at vote.nz/overseas (a 3-page PDF: instructions, declaration, ballot paper), mark them on screen or print and mark by hand, then upload them. You must be physically outside New Zealand when you download; if you download while still in NZ your vote will not be counted. *(Wed 21 Oct 2026)* [[source]](https://vote.nz/2026-general-election/overseas/downloading-your-voting-papers) <sub>vote.nz 'Downloading your voting papers' and 'Common questions', retrieved 2026-10-23</sub>
- The declaration must be signed by you AND a witness who is physically with you. Typed signatures and handwriting-style fonts are not accepted; draw or insert an image of your real signature. Tick your reason for a special vote (e.g. outside NZ on election day). Your witness can be a relative, flatmate, colleague or associate, any registered NZ elector, or an official such as a notary. [[source]](https://vote.nz/2026-general-election/overseas/how-to-complete-your-voting-papers) <sub>vote.nz 'How to complete your voting papers' and 'Common questions', retrieved 2026-10-23</sub>
- Upload at uvp.elections.govt.nz by 7pm on Saturday 7 November, NZ time. Late papers cannot be accepted. Upload once only: more than one submission may mean your vote doesn't count. Files: JPG, PNG, GIF, PDF or TIFF, 10 KB to 10 MB, whole page visible including the 7-digit number top right. *(Sat 7 Nov 2026)* [[source]](https://vote.nz/2026-general-election/overseas/uploading-your-voting-papers) <sub>vote.nz 'Uploading your voting papers', retrieved 2026-10-23</sub>
- In person: 64 overseas locations offer in-person or postal voting, including 10 in Australia. Hours and closing dates differ, some are appointment-only or postal-only, most are closed at weekends, and ALL overseas voting places are closed on election day, 7 November. Check the lookup before you go. [[source]](https://vote.nz/2026-general-election/overseas/find-an-overseas-voting-place) <sub>vote.nz 'Find an overseas voting place'; Electoral Commission release 21 Oct 2026</sub>
- On the unpublished roll? You can't download papers. Ask your nearest overseas voting place for a postal vote or collect papers in person, or email overseas@vote.nz. [[source]](https://vote.nz/2026-general-election/overseas/common-questions) <sub>vote.nz 'Common questions', retrieved 2026-10-23</sub>
- Help: overseas@vote.nz or +64 9 909 4182. Blind or disabled voters overseas can use telephone dictation voting on +64 4 471 2000 (register first). [[source]](https://vote.nz/2026-general-election/overseas/common-questions) <sub>vote.nz, retrieved 2026-10-23</sub>

**Notes**

- In 2023, 88% of overseas voters used the download service; 77,295 overseas votes were received (62,787 in 2020). About 126,000 enrolled voters have told the Commission they live overseas, 64% of them in Australia (Electoral Commission, 21 Oct 2026).
- Niueans, Tokelauans and Cook Islanders vote on the citizen rule (in NZ within 3 years); Australians on the resident rule (within 12 months) (vote.nz 'How to vote from overseas').
- The brief's 'from 21 Oct?' is confirmed: overseas voting started Wednesday 21 October 2026 (vote.nz key dates; Commission release 21 Oct 2026).

## 6. Help to vote and accessible voting

*For: blind and low-vision voters; Deaf voters; disabled voters; people with dementia or cognitive impairment; anyone who needs a hand.*

- A support person: a friend, family member or voting place worker can go behind the screen with you, read out the paper, and mark it as you ask. They cannot tell you who to vote for. The choice must be yours and you must be present. Nobody can vote for you, not even with power of attorney. [[source]](https://vote.nz/2026-general-election/voting-in-the-election/need-help-to-vote) <sub>vote.nz 'Need help to vote?', retrieved 2026-10-23; Electoral Act s 170</sub>
- Telephone dictation voting is for people who are blind, partially blind or have a physical disability that stops them marking the paper. You must register every election, even if you used it before. Register from Monday 5 October to noon on Saturday 7 November by calling 0800 028 028 (option 1) or +64 4 471 2000 from overseas. Registration hours: weekdays 9am to 7pm; from 26 October also Saturdays 9am to 3pm; election day 9am to noon. *(Mon 5 Oct 2026)* [[source]](https://vote.nz/2026-general-election/voting-in-the-election/cant-get-to-a-voting-place/telephone-dictation-voting) <sub>vote.nz 'Telephone dictation voting', retrieved 2026-10-23</sub>
- Then vote by phone from Wednesday 21 October to 7pm on Saturday 7 November (0800 028 028, option 2). You give your registration number and the answer to a secret question, not your name. One official reads out the candidates and parties and marks your paper; a second official checks it back with you. Dictation voting hours: weekdays 9am to 7pm, Saturdays 9am to 3pm (from 26 Oct), election day 9am to 7pm. *(Wed 21 Oct 2026)* [[source]](https://vote.nz/2026-general-election/voting-in-the-election/cant-get-to-a-voting-place/telephone-dictation-voting) <sub>vote.nz, retrieved 2026-10-23; Electoral Commission release 21 Oct 2026</sub>
- Accessible voting places: 1,679 are fully accessible; NZSL interpreters are at six locations; three places have quiet times. The map at map.vote.nz can be filtered by accessibility. [[source]](https://map.vote.nz) <sub>Electoral Commission release 7 Oct 2026; vote.nz 'Find a voting place'</sub>
- Information in other formats: audio, Braille, large print, Easy Read and New Zealand Sign Language (NZSL) versions of the enrolling and voting guides are at vote.nz. Easy Read: 'Get ready to enrol and vote in the 2026 General Election' (PDF). Most vote.nz pages also have te reo Māori and NZSL versions (language toggle at the top). [[source]](https://vote.nz/2026-general-election/resources/information-in-accessible-formats) <sub>vote.nz 'Information in accessible formats' and 'Information in Easy Read', retrieved 2026-10-23</sub>
- Access 2026 is the Commission's accessibility action plan: 20 accessible services for Deaf and disabled people, tāngata Turi (Deaf Māori) and tāngata whaikaha Māori (disabled Māori), with Easy Read, large print, audio and Braille summaries. [[source]](https://elections.nz/about/about-the-electoral-commission/corporate-publications/access-2026-action-plan) <sub>Electoral Commission release 7 Sep 2026; elections.nz Access 2026 page, retrieved 2026-10-23</sub>
- Dementia or cognitive impairment: there is no capacity test for voting. A person can vote if they want to and can clearly show their choice at the time; a support person must follow their instructions. [[source]](https://vote.nz/2026-general-election/voting-in-the-election/need-help-to-vote) <sub>vote.nz, retrieved 2026-10-23</sub>
- Te reo Māori: 67 bilingual (reo rua) voting places let you vote in te reo Māori or English; more than 220 Whare Pōti Māori places are at marae, kura, wānanga and community spaces. Filter the map for 'Whare Pōti Māori' and 'Reo rua'. [[source]](https://map.vote.nz) <sub>Electoral Commission release 7 Oct 2026</sub>

**Notes**

- Quiet times at voting places are generally 9am to 11am and 2pm to 4pm on weekdays (vote.nz).
- The Access 2026 'Quick guide to accessible voting services' PDF could not be downloaded (bot protection); its contents are summarised from the Access 2026 web page and the telephone dictation page instead.
- Disabled voters told 1News (5 Oct 2026, cited in docs/FEATURE_REVIEW.md) they were still hunting for accessible booths; the map's accessibility filter and 0800 36 76 56 are the Commission's answers.

## 7. First time voting? What happens inside, and the two ticks

*For: first-time voters; students; anyone who wants a refresher.*

- Arrive. Staff in orange vests greet you. Give your EasyVote card, or your name and address, to the person issuing papers. They find you on the roll (electronic roll for dormant-roll voters) and hand you your voting paper. If you are not on the printed roll you fill in a special-vote declaration first. [[source]](https://vote.nz/2026-general-election/voting-in-the-election/what-to-expect-at-the-voting-place) <sub>vote.nz 'What to expect' and 'What to do', retrieved 2026-10-23</sub>
- Go to a voting screen and vote in private, with a pen. You get two votes on one paper. LEFT side: your party vote. Tick ONE circle next to the party you want in government. RIGHT side: your electorate vote. Tick ONE circle next to the candidate you want as the MP for your area. [[source]](https://vote.nz/2026-general-election/voting-in-the-election/what-to-do) <sub>vote.nz 'What to do', retrieved 2026-10-23</sub>
- You can tick both sides, or just one side. Both are valid. The party vote decides how many seats each party gets in Parliament; the electorate vote picks your local MP. Your EasyVote card comes with the list of candidates for your electorate; party lists are on vote.nz. [[source]](https://vote.nz/2026-general-election/voting-in-the-election/party-lists) <sub>vote.nz 'What to do' ('vote for only one or the other, it's your choice'); elections.nz 'What happens in a general election?'</sub>
- Made a mistake? Take the 'spoilt' paper back and ask for a new one. Spoilt papers are not counted. An 'informal' vote is one where the counters cannot tell what you intended; it is recorded but does not count. If your intention is clear, your vote counts even if the paper has been drawn on. [[source]](https://vote.nz/2026-general-election/about/overview/free-and-fair-elections/facts-about-new-zealand-elections) <sub>vote.nz 'What to do' (spoilt papers) and 'Facts about New Zealand elections' (informal votes), retrieved 2026-10-23</sub>
- Fold the paper in half and put it in the ballot box. Then leave; the Commission asks voters not to linger so others feel comfortable. [[source]](https://vote.nz/2026-general-election/voting-in-the-election/what-to-do) <sub>vote.nz 'What to do' and 'What to expect', retrieved 2026-10-23</sub>
- Phones and selfies: don't film or photograph inside a voting place. Publishing a photo or video of someone filling in their paper is a potential offence. Candidates who want to be filmed voting must arrange it in advance (returns@elections.govt.nz / the Returning Officer). [[source]](https://vote.nz/2026-general-election/voting-in-the-election/what-to-expect-at-the-voting-place) <sub>vote.nz 'What to expect'; Electoral Commission, 'Rules once voting starts and on election day' (PDF, 2026)</sub>
- Campaigning near voting places: during advance voting, no campaigning, election advertising or influencing voters inside a voting place or within 10 metres of its entrance. On election day those rules apply everywhere until 7pm: no campaign material (a lapel badge or rosette is allowed), no processions, no polling voters. New in 2026: nobody may give out free food, drink (except water) or entertainment within 100 metres of a voting place entrance while voting is open, unless it is their normal business. [[source]](https://elections.nz/assets/2026-General-Election/2026-Rules-once-voting-starts.pdf) <sub>Electoral Commission, 'Rules once voting starts and on election day' (raw/howtovote/rules-once-voting-starts.pdf); Ministry of Justice summary of the 2025 Act</sub>
- Social media on election day: from midnight before election day until 7pm on 7 November, you must not post or share anything likely to influence someone's party or candidate vote. There is no exemption for personal opinions online on election day. Posts made before election day can stay up if not updated. Paid election advertising online is banned on election day. *(Sat 7 Nov 2026)* [[source]](https://elections.nz/guidance-and-rules/advertising-and-campaigning/social-media) <sub>elections.nz 'Social media' and vote.nz 'What to expect', retrieved 2026-10-23</sub>
- Scrutineers: you may see people with party rosettes sitting along one side. They are appointed by candidates and parties to watch, not Commission staff, and they cannot talk to voters. [[source]](https://vote.nz/2026-general-election/about/overview/free-and-fair-elections/facts-about-new-zealand-elections) <sub>vote.nz 'Facts about New Zealand elections', retrieved 2026-10-23</sub>

**Notes**

- Votes are counted by hand; New Zealand does not use voting machines (vote.nz, 'Facts about New Zealand elections').
- Who to vote for is your decision. This guide does not recommend anyone. RNZ's 23 Oct 2026 explainer points first-time voters to non-partisan candidate information at policy.nz and the parties' own websites.
- If you vote for an electorate you are not enrolled in (e.g. you moved and didn't update), only your party vote counts (elections.nz, 'Party Vote Only'). In 2023 more than 42,000 electorate votes were disallowed for this reason; the Commission estimated more than 72,000 could be this year (1News, 16 Oct 2026).

## 8. After you vote: results and forming a government

*For: everyone; election-night watchers.*

- Election night: advance votes are counted from 9am on election day at electorate headquarters; election-day votes are counted at each voting place after 7pm. Preliminary results go up progressively from 7pm on electionresults.govt.nz. The Commission's targets: 50% of results by 10pm and 95% by 11.30pm. *(Sat 7 Nov 2026)* [[source]](https://electionresults.govt.nz) <sub>elections.nz 'How are general election votes counted?'; vote.nz 'What happens after an election?', retrieved 2026-10-23</sub>
- Election-night results are NOT final. They leave out all special votes (including overseas votes) and the rolls have not yet been checked. The Commission says they 'are likely to change'. [[source]](https://vote.nz/2026-general-election/about/overview/free-and-fair-elections/what-happens-after-the-election) <sub>vote.nz, retrieved 2026-10-23</sub>
- Special votes have changed results before. 2023: after specials, National went from 50 seats to 48, Te Pāti Māori from 4 to 6, the Greens from 14 to 15; Nelson, Te Atatū, Tāmaki Makaurau (by 4 votes) and Te Tai Tokerau changed hands; National and ACT alone no longer had a majority. 603,257 special votes were 20.9% of the total. [[source]](https://elections.nz/media-and-news/2023/official-results-for-the-2023-general-election/) <sub>Electoral Commission, 'Official results for the 2023 General Election', 3 Nov 2023</sub>
- 2020: National fell from 35 seats to 33, Labour rose from 64 to 65 and the Māori Party from 1 to 2; Maungakiekie, Northland and Whangārei switched from National to Labour. 504,625 special votes were 17% of the total. [[source]](https://elections.nz/media-and-news/2020/2020-general-election-official-results/) <sub>Electoral Commission, '2020 General Election official results', 6 Nov 2020</sub>
- Official results: Friday 27 November 2026 (20 days after election day). Every vote is recounted by hand, special votes (which can arrive up to 10 days after election day) are checked and counted, and the rolls are checked for double voting. Results are published in the NZ Gazette and on electionresults.govt.nz. *(Fri 27 Nov 2026)* [[source]](https://vote.nz/2026-general-election/about/overview/key-dates) <sub>vote.nz key dates; elections.nz 'How are general election votes counted?'</sub>
- Recounts and challenges: a candidate or party can apply for a recount within 3 working days of the official results; an election petition to the High Court must be filed within 28 days. [[source]](https://vote.nz/2026-general-election/about/overview/free-and-fair-elections/what-happens-after-the-election) <sub>vote.nz 'What happens after an election?', retrieved 2026-10-23</sub>
- Forming a government: every electorate winner gets a seat; the rest are filled from party lists in proportion to the party vote. Under MMP one party rarely wins alone, so parties negotiate a coalition or support ('confidence and supply') deal. There is no deadline for this. The outgoing government stays on as a caretaker, avoiding major decisions, until a new one is sworn in. [[source]](https://vote.nz/2026-general-election/about/overview/free-and-fair-elections/what-happens-after-the-election) <sub>vote.nz 'What happens after an election?' (Government formation); RNZ The House, 2026</sub>
- The writ is returned by 3 December 2026 with the names of elected MPs; the new Parliament's three-year term runs from that day. Parliament must meet within 6 weeks of that date (Constitution Act 1986 s 19), so by 14 January 2027 even if coalition talks are still going. The first sitting day MPs are sworn in and elect a Speaker (Commission Opening); the next day the Governor-General gives the Speech from the Throne (State Opening). *(Thu 3 Dec 2026)* [[source]](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/politics/590326/the-house-what-to-expect-when-you-re-expecting-an-election) <sub>RNZ The House (Clerk of the House David Wilson), 2026; Constitution Act 1986 ss 17 and 19 (raw/howtovote/constitution-act-1986.pdf); vote.nz key dates</sub>

**Notes**

- Why specials lean a certain way is analysis, not Commission fact: 1News (3 Nov 2023) wrote that special votes 'have historically tended to skew left'. The guide reports the shifts, not the reasons.
- In 2023 the official count took three weeks; the Chief Electoral Officer told MPs in September 2025 the 2025 law change would not make the 20-day official result faster, because people enrolling between Writ Day and the 13-day close still cast special votes (RNZ).
- Voting papers are kept sealed for six months in case of a legal challenge, then destroyed (vote.nz).
- Parliament's and DPMC's own post-election pages (election-2026-timeline, Cabinet Office circular CO (26) 5) could not be fetched (bot protection), so government-formation steps are sourced to vote.nz, RNZ's The House and the Constitution Act.

## 9. Check before you share: official sources and where to report

*For: everyone; people who see election claims on social media.*

- The Electoral Commission's campaign, launched 29 July 2026, asks people to 'take a moment' before sharing information about enrolling and voting: 'slow down and check the information you see or hear. If you see something that doesn't look right on your social media feed, don't share it.' Waatea News reported it under the headline 'Think Before You Share'. *(Wed 29 Jul 2026)* [[source]](https://elections.nz/media-and-news/2026/take-a-moment-before-you-share-information) <sub>Electoral Commission release, 29 Jul 2026; vote.nz 'Free and fair elections'; Waatea News, 30 Jul 2026</sub>
- Official sources for when, where and how to vote: vote.nz and 0800 36 76 56. For results: electionresults.govt.nz. The Commission's 'Facts about New Zealand elections' page answers common myths (no ID needed, no voting machines, hand counts, how specials work). [[source]](https://vote.nz/2026-general-election/about/overview/free-and-fair-elections/facts-about-new-zealand-elections) <sub>vote.nz, retrieved 2026-10-23</sub>
- Don't share misinformation even to correct it; post the correct information instead. Check that a claim really comes from a candidate's or party's real account or website. Election ads must carry a promoter statement (name and address); unpaid posts must show it on the account's About or Profile page. [[source]](https://elections.nz/guidance-and-rules/advertising-and-campaigning/social-media) <sub>elections.nz 'Social media', retrieved 2026-10-23</sub>
- Where to report: misleading information about when, where or how to vote, or an ad with no promoter statement, to the Electoral Commission (elections.nz/contact, enquiries@elections.govt.nz, 0800 36 76 56). Paid or boosted social media ads: Advertising Standards Authority (asa.co.nz). TV and radio: Broadcasting Standards Authority. News content: the publisher, then the Media Council. Posts that break platform rules: report to the platform. The Commission refers suspected offences to Police; it does not regulate the content of political ads. [[source]](https://elections.nz/guidance-and-rules/advertising-and-campaigning/advice-and-complaints) <sub>elections.nz 'Advice and complaints' and 'Social media', retrieved 2026-10-23</sub>

**Notes**

- The Commission's own name for the campaign is not stated beyond the release title 'Take a moment before you share information'; 'Think before you share' is the media's phrasing (Waatea News). Not verified as an official slogan.
- The Commission has referred several 2026 election advertising matters to Police (releases 16 and 23 Oct 2026); it names the rule breached, not the content.

## Finding your nearest voting place

The official finder (map.vote.nz, redirects to map.vote.nz/voting-places) is a client-side map. It loads the whole 2026 voting-place dataset into the browser and filters it there, with an address search box (Mapbox geocoding) and filters for day, 'open now', accessibility, Whare Pōti Māori and reo rua. It takes no documented URL parameters: on 23 Oct 2026 we tested https://map.vote.nz/voting-places?electorate=Wellington%20Central and the page loaded the default view. The vote.nz list page lets you pick an electorate and suburb from drop-downs, also client-side. So the app should deep-link to https://map.vote.nz and let the voter type their address, or call 0800 36 76 56. Posters listing nearby voting places are also displayed at local supermarkets and community libraries during advance voting.

- More than 2,300 voting places open on election day (the finder data held 2,314 on 23 Oct); 207 open on Monday 26 October (finder data: 209) offering 1,437 hours of voting; the number grows towards election day. [[source]](https://elections.nz/media-and-news/2026/voting-places-announced-for-2026-election) [[source]](https://elections.nz/media-and-news/2026/voting-starts-on-monday)
- 1,679 fully accessible voting places; NZ Sign Language interpreters at six locations; three voting places with quiet times; more than 220 Whare Pōti Māori voting places (marae, kura, wānanga and community spaces), including 67 bilingual (reo rua) places where you can vote in te reo Māori or English. [[source]](https://elections.nz/media-and-news/2026/voting-places-announced-for-2026-election)
- Votes will be taken at 2,638 locations across the whole voting period (RNZ, citing the Commission). [[source]](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/politics_election-2026/1781478/where-you-can-vote-in-advance-and-on-election-day)

**Data and licence.** https://map.vote.nz/assets/map-data/locations-<hash>.json (the hash changes when the data is updated; on 23 Oct 2026 it was a 1.05 MB JSON file with 2,641 locations, each with name, address, suburb, WGS84 coordinates, accessibility code (none/partial/full), Whare Pōti Māori and reo rua flags, per-day opening hours, and the electorates it serves as a 'Home' or 'Shared' place). It is served from behind the Commission's bot protection (Incapsula) and is not published on data.govt.nz. No licence statement was found on the data file, on map.vote.nz, or on vote.nz/elections.nz (their footers say only 'Copyright © Electoral Commission New Zealand'). Because there is no licence allowing reuse, the dataset is NOT stored in this repository; only the deep-link above is published. tools/howtovote_voting_places.py can normalise the file if the Commission confirms reuse terms (enquiries@elections.govt.nz). For 2023 the Commission published a 'voting place coordinates' CSV on the results archive (archive.electionresults.govt.nz/electionresults_2023/statistics/) marked 'approximate only', again with no licence stated. The Commission announced the 2026 voting places on 7 October 2026 ('about 3 weeks before election day' is its stated practice). Hours and places can still change, so always check the finder before travelling.
