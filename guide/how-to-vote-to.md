## Ko hai ‘e lava ke lēsisita ke fili? · Who can enrol to vote?

- Kuopau ke ke lēsisita kapau ‘oku ke ta‘u 18 pe motu‘a ange, sitiseni New Zealand pe ‘oku ke nofo ‘i he fonuá ni pea ngofua ke ke kau ‘i he filí, pea kuo ke ‘osi nofo ma‘u hokohoko ‘i New Zealand ‘i he māhina ‘e 12 pe toe lōloa ange lolotonga ho‘o mo‘uí.  
   You must enrol if you are 18 years or older, a New Zealand citizen or resident for electoral purposes, and you’ve lived in New Zealand continuously for 12 months or more at some time in your life. (Electoral Commission wording) · [source](https://vote.nz/enrolling/get-ready-to-enrol/are-you-eligible-to-enrol-and-vote)
- Ko e tokotaha nofo fonua koe pea ‘e ngofua ke ke kau ‘i he filí kapau ‘oku fakalao ho‘o nofo ‘i New Zealand pea ‘oku ‘ikai ha tu‘utu‘uni atu ‘a e taimi ‘oku fiema‘u ai ke ke mavahe ai mei he fonuá ni.  
   You’re a resident for electoral purposes if you’re living in New Zealand lawfully and you’re not required to leave within a specified time. (Electoral Commission wording) · [source](https://vote.nz/enrolling/get-ready-to-enrol/are-you-eligible-to-enrol-and-vote)
- Kapau ko ha tokotaha ‘Australia, Niue, Tokelau pe Cook Island koe, ‘e lava ke ke lēsisita kapau kuo laka hake he māhina ‘e 12 pe toe lahi ange ho‘o nofo ma‘u ‘i New Zealand.  
   If you’re Australian, Niuean, Tokelauan or a Cook Islander, you can enrol if you have lived in New Zealand continuously for 12 months or more. (Electoral Commission wording) · [source](https://vote.nz/enrolling/get-ready-to-enrol/are-you-eligible-to-enrol-and-vote)
- Ke ke kau ‘i he fili Falealea 2026, ‘e fiema‘u ke ke lēsisita kimu‘a he tu‘uapō, Sāpate 25 ‘Okatopá. He ‘ikai lava ke ke lēsisita pe fakatonutonu ‘a e ngaahi fakamatala fekau‘aki mo koé, hili e kamata ‘a e filí he ‘aho Mōnite 26 ‘Okatopa.  
   To vote in the 2026 General Election, you need to be enrolled by midnight, Sunday 25 October. You won’t be able to enrol or update your details once voting starts on Monday 26 October. (Electoral Commission wording) · [source](https://vote.nz/enrolling/enrol-or-update/enrol-or-update-online)
- Ko e lēsisitá ‘oku faingofua, pea pehē mo hono vakai‘i pe fakatonutonu ‘onilaine ‘a e ngaahi fakamatala fekau‘aki mo koé ‘i he uepisaiti vote.nz ‘o lava ke ke hū‘aki ho‘o laiseni faka‘uli New Zealand, paasipooti New Zealand pe ‘akauni RealMe.  
   It’s easy to enrol, check or update your details online at vote.nz using your New Zealand driver licence, New Zealand passport or RealMe verified identity. (Electoral Commission wording) · [source](https://vote.nz/enrolling/enrol-or-update/enrol-or-update-online)

## Ko e taimi mo e feitu‘u ‘oku fai ai e filí · When and where to vote

- ‘Oku ‘ikai fiema‘u ia ke ke tali ki he ‘aho ‘o e filí ke toki fai ai ho‘o filí.  
   You don’t need to wait until election day to vote. (Electoral Commission wording) · [source](https://map.vote.nz)
- Kamata e fili: Mōnite 26 ‘Okatopa  
   Voting opens: Monday 26 October (Electoral Commission wording) · [source](https://vote.nz/2026-general-election/about/overview/key-dates)
- Tāpuni e fili: 7pm ‘aho fili, Tokonaki 7 Nōvema  
   Voting closes: 7pm on election day, Saturday 7 November (Electoral Commission wording) · [source](https://vote.nz/2026-general-election/about/overview/key-dates)
- ‘I he ‘aho filí, Tokonaki 7 Novemá, ‘e ava ‘a e ngaahi fai‘anga filí kotoa mei he taimi 9am ki he 7pm.  
   On election day, Saturday 7 November, all voting places will be open from 9am to 7pm. (Electoral Commission wording) · [source](https://map.vote.nz)
- Te ke lava ‘o ma‘u e ngaahi fakahinohino fekau‘aki mo e ngaahi feitu‘u fai‘anga filí kau ai e ‘aho mo e taimi tenau ava aí ‘i he vote.nz pe telefoni ki he 0800 36 76 56  
   You can find information about voting places including the days and hours that they’re open, on vote.nz or by calling 0800 36 76 56. (Electoral Commission wording) · [source](https://map.vote.nz)
- ‘E lava ke ke fili ‘i ha fai‘anga fili pē ‘i New Zealand.  
   You can vote at any voting place in New Zealand. (our translation, not yet checked) · [source](https://map.vote.nz)

## Ko e founga fili · How to vote

1. Ha‘u mo ho‘o kaati fili EasyVote. ‘E lava pe ia ke ke kei fili kapau ‘e ‘ikai ke ke ‘alu mo ho‘o kaati fili EasyVote ki he fai‘anga fili.  
   Bring your EasyVote card. You can still vote if you don’t have your EasyVote card with you when you go to vote. (Electoral Commission wording) · [source](https://vote.nz/2026-general-election/about/overview/communications-from-the-electoral-commission/your-easyvote-card)
2. ‘Oku ‘ikai fiema‘u ke ke ‘omai ha ID.  
   You don’t need to take ID. (our translation, not yet checked) · [source](https://vote.nz/2026-general-election/voting-in-the-election/what-to-do)
3. ‘E ‘i ai pe taha ‘i he fai‘anga filí ke ne tokoni‘i koe. ‘E fakahinohino atu pē ‘e he kau ngāue ‘i he fai‘anga filí ‘a e me‘a ke faí mo e feitu‘u ke ke ‘alu ki ai.  
   There will be someone at the voting place to help you. Election workers at your voting place will show you what to do and where to go. (Electoral Commission wording) · [source](https://vote.nz/2026-general-election/voting-in-the-election/what-to-do)
4. ‘I ha fili falealea, ‘oku ‘i ai ho‘o fo‘i fili ‘e ua – fili paati mo e fili vāhenga fili.  
   In a general election, you get two votes – a party vote and an electorate vote. (Electoral Commission wording) · [source](https://vote.nz/2026-general-election/voting-in-the-election/what-to-do)
5. ‘I ho‘o pepa filí, tiki‘i ‘a e hingoa ‘o e paati politikale ‘oku ke poupou.  
   On your voting paper, place a tick by the name of the political party of your choice. (Electoral Commission wording) · [source](https://vote.nz/2026-general-election/voting-in-the-election/what-to-do)
6. ‘I ho‘o pepa filí, tiki‘i e hingoa ‘o e kanititeiti ‘oku ke fiema‘u ke ne fakafofonga‘i ho vāhenga. Ko e kanititeiti ‘e tokolahi taha ‘a kinautolu te nau fili ‘iate ia ‘e hoko ia ko e fakafofonga ‘o e vāhenga.  
   On your voting paper, place a tick by the name of the candidate you would most like to represent your local area. The candidate with the most votes will become your electorate MP. (Electoral Commission wording) · [source](https://vote.nz/2026-general-election/voting-in-the-election/what-to-do)
7. Te ke fili paati mei he lisi ‘o e ‘ū paatí, pea ‘oku tatau pe ‘a e lisi ‘e fili mei ai ‘a kinautolu ‘oku lēsisita fili Maori pea mo kinautolu lēsisita fili fakalukufuá.  
   You can choose from the same list of political parties whether you’re on the general roll or Māori roll. (Electoral Commission wording) · [source](https://vote.nz/2026-general-election/voting-in-the-election/what-to-do)
8. Kapau te ke fai ha fehālaaki, kole ha pepa fili fo‘ou.  
   If you make a mistake, ask for a new paper. (our translation, not yet checked) · [source](https://vote.nz/2026-general-election/voting-in-the-election/what-to-do)
9. Fa‘o ho‘o pepa filí ‘i he puha pāloti ‘oku fokotu‘u ai. Pea ko ‘ene lavá ia – kuo lava ho‘o fili!  
   Place your voting paper in the ballot box provided. And that’s it — you’ve voted! (Electoral Commission wording) · [source](https://vote.nz/2026-general-election/voting-in-the-election/what-to-do)

## Pepa pāloti makehe (special vote) · Special votes

- Kapau ‘oku ‘ikai ‘asi ho hingoá ‘i he tohi lēsisita fili kuo paaki ‘i he fai‘anga filí, te ke fakafonu ha foomu ‘special declaration vote’ kimu‘a pea ke fili. ‘Oku ui ‘eni ko e pepa pāloti makehe (special vote).  
   If your name is not on the printed roll at a voting place, you’ll need to fill out a ‘special declaration vote’ form before you can vote. This is known as a ‘special vote’. (our translation, not yet checked) · [source](https://vote.nz/2026-general-election/voting-in-the-election/what-to-do)
- ‘E lava ke hoko ‘eni kapau na‘a ke lēsisita hili ‘a e Sāpate 4 ‘Okatopa, ‘oku ke fili ‘i tu‘a mei ho vāhenga fili, pe ‘oku ‘i he tohi lēsisita fakapulipuli (unpublished roll) ho hingoá.  
   Your name may not be on the printed roll if you enrolled after Sunday 4 October, you’re voting away from your home electorate, or you’re on the unpublished roll. (our translation, not yet checked) · [source](https://vote.nz/2026-general-election/voting-in-the-election/what-to-do)
- Kapau te ke lēsisita hili e ‘aho Sāpate 4 ‘Okatopá pea kimu‘a he tu‘uapō Sāpate 25 ‘Okatopá, he ‘ikai ma‘u atu ha‘o kaati fili EasyVote.  
   If you enrol after Sunday 4 October but before midnight, Sunday 25 October, you won’t get an EasyVote card. (Electoral Commission wording) · [source](https://vote.nz/2026-general-election/about/overview/communications-from-the-electoral-commission/your-easyvote-card)
- ‘E lau ‘a e ngaahi pepa pāloti makehe ‘i he ola faka‘ofisiale ‘o e filí ‘i he Falaite 27 Nōvema.  
   Special votes are counted in the official result, due Friday 27 November. (our translation, not yet checked) · [source](https://vote.nz/2026-general-election/about/overview/key-dates)

## Fili mei tu‘apule‘anga · Voting from overseas

- Kapau ‘oku ke ‘i ha feitu‘u kehe ‘i New Zealand, ‘e lava ke ke fili ‘i ha fai‘anga fili pē ‘i New Zealand.  
   Away from home in New Zealand: you can vote at any voting place in New Zealand. (our translation, not yet checked) · [source](https://map.vote.nz)
- ‘E lava pe ke ke fili mei tu‘apule‘anga mei he ‘aho Pulelulu 21 ‘Okatopa ‘o ngata ‘i he 7pm Tokonaki 7 Nōvema (Taimi ‘a NZ ni).  
   You can vote from overseas from Wednesday 21 October until 7pm, Saturday 7 November (NZ time). (Electoral Commission wording) · [source](https://vote.nz/2026-general-election/overseas/vote-from-overseas)
- Ko e founga vave mo faingofua taha ki he fili mei tu‘apule‘angá ko hono taunilouti (download) mo fakafonu ho‘o pepa filí pea toki ‘apulouti (upload) ‘i he vote.nz  
   The easiest and quickest way to vote from overseas is to download and complete your voting papers and then upload them at vote.nz (Electoral Commission wording) · [source](https://vote.nz/2026-general-election/overseas/vote-from-overseas)

## Fiema‘u tokoni ki hono lau mo fakafonu ho‘o pepa fili? · Need help to read or mark your voting papers?

- ‘E lava pe ke tokoni atu ha taha ‘i he fai‘anga fili, pe ko hao fāmili pe maheni ke lau mo fakafonu ho‘o pepa fili. ‘E ‘ikai ngofua ke ne tala atu kia koe ha taha ke ke fili ai.  
   A friend, whānau member, or someone working at the voting place can help you to read or mark your voting paper. They can’t tell you who you should vote for. (Electoral Commission wording) · [source](https://vote.nz/2026-general-election/voting-in-the-election/need-help-to-vote)
- Kapau ‘oku ke kui, pe ‘oku nenefu ho‘o sió pe mo‘ua ‘i ha faingata‘a‘ia fakasino ‘o ‘ikai lava ke ke fakafonu ho‘o pepa filí, ‘e lava ke fai ‘a ho‘o filí ‘aki e founga telefoni fakaongo (phone dictation service).  
   If you’re blind, partially blind or have a physical disability that means you can’t mark your voting paper without help, you can vote using the telephone dictation service. (Electoral Commission wording) · [source](https://vote.nz/2026-general-election/voting-in-the-election/cant-get-to-a-voting-place/telephone-dictation-voting)
- Lēsisita ki he founga ko ‘ení ‘i he 0800 028 028 (fili e 1) kimu‘a he 12 ho‘atā Tokonaki 7 Nōvema.  
   Register for it on 0800 028 028 (option 1) by noon on Saturday 7 November. (our translation, not yet checked) · [source](https://vote.nz/2026-general-election/voting-in-the-election/cant-get-to-a-voting-place/telephone-dictation-voting)
- Kapau ‘oku ‘ikai te ke lava ki ha fai‘anga fili koe‘uhi ‘oku ke tokoto falemahaki, ‘e lava ke ‘omai ho‘o pepa filí ‘e ha taha ‘i ho fāmili pe maheni, mei he ‘aho Mōnite 26 ‘Okatopa.  
   If you can’t get to a voting place because you’re in hospital, a friend or whānau member can bring you your voting papers from Monday 26 October. (our translation, not yet checked) · [source](https://vote.nz/2026-general-election/voting-in-the-election/cant-get-to-a-voting-place)

## Notes on this translation

- The Commission’s Tongan guide has no line saying ID is not needed, and no Friday dates; those lines are ours (basis mt). Its term for a special vote, “pepa pāloti makehe (special vote)”, comes from the Commission’s Tongan “Get ready to enrol and vote” leaflet.
- The Commission’s Tongan guide says a whānau member can bring hospital voting papers “kae kimu‘a ‘i he ‘aho Mōnite 26 ‘Okatopa”, which reads as “before Monday 26 October”, whereas the English guide says “from Monday 26 October”. We did not reuse that sentence; our line (basis mt) follows the English.
- The Commission’s Tongan “Unable to get to a voting place?” PDF could not be downloaded from this environment (bot protection); it is linked but its wording was not compared.
