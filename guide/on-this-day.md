# On this day in New Zealand politics

One to three events from New Zealand's political and constitutional history for every day of the year, written in plain English. Data: [`data/onthisday.json`](../data/onthisday.json). Each entry links to the Wikipedia article used to check the date (Wikipedia is a finding aid; its articles cite the primary sources). Each day also links to NZHistory's own "Today in history" page (Ministry for Culture and Heritage, text CC BY-NC 3.0 NZ as reported; see licence notes in the JSON). Entries marked *approx.* are filed on a nearby day because no political event was found for that date; the exact date is given.

## January

### 1 January

- **1951 — Parliament becomes a single chamber** The Legislative Council, New Zealand's appointed upper house, ceased to exist on this day. Since then Parliament has had only one chamber, the House of Representatives. [[source]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_Legislative_Council)
- **1987 — Constitution Act comes into force** The Constitution Act 1986 took effect. It brought together the main rules on the Sovereign, the Governor-General, Parliament and the courts, and ended the United Kingdom's power to make laws for New Zealand. [[source]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_Act_1986)
- **2004 — Supreme Court of New Zealand established** New Zealand's own final court of appeal came into being, replacing appeals to the Privy Council in London. It heard its first case on 1 July 2004. [[source]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_New_Zealand)
- NZHistory for this day: <https://nzhistory.govt.nz/timeline/1/1>

### 2 January

- **1877 — Provinces abolished** *(approx. — exact date 1877-01-01)* New Zealand's provincial governments formally ceased to exist, leaving a single central Parliament and local councils. The Abolition of Provinces Act had been passed in 1875. [[source]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_Government)
- NZHistory for this day: <https://nzhistory.govt.nz/timeline/2/1>

### 3 January

- **1840 — Survey ship arrives for Wellington settlement** The New Zealand Company's survey ship Cuba reached Port Nicholson (Wellington) to prepare for organised British settlement, weeks before the Treaty of Waitangi was signed. [[source]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Waitangi)
- NZHistory for this day: <https://nzhistory.govt.nz/timeline/3/1>

### 4 January

- **1876 — Māori electorates vote in the 1875-76 election** Polling in the four Māori electorates was held on 4 and 15 January 1876, as part of a general election spread over several weeks. [[source]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/6th_New_Zealand_Parliament)
- NZHistory for this day: <https://nzhistory.govt.nz/timeline/4/1>

### 5 January

- **1977 — Bastion Point occupation begins** Ngāti Whātua and supporters began occupying Takaparawhau (Bastion Point) in Auckland to stop a housing development on former tribal land. The occupation lasted 506 days. [[source]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bastion_Point)
- NZHistory for this day: <https://nzhistory.govt.nz/timeline/5/1>

### 6 January

- **1876 — George Grey wins the Thames seat** Former governor Sir George Grey topped the poll in Thames during the 1875-76 general election. He became premier in 1877. [[source]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Grey)
- **1923 — Birth of Norman Kirk** Norman Kirk, who led Labour to victory in 1972 and died in office in 1974, was born in Waimate. [[source]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_Kirk) Related: [labour](../parties/labour/README.md).
- NZHistory for this day: <https://nzhistory.govt.nz/timeline/6/1>

### 7 January

- **1963 — Northern Maori MP dies** Tāpihana Paikea, Labour MP for Northern Maori, died. Matiu Rata won the by-election in March and later founded Mana Motuhake. [[source]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1963_Northern_Maori_by-election) Related: [labour](../parties/labour/README.md).
- **2018 — Death of Jim Anderton** Jim Anderton, founder of NewLabour, the Alliance and the Progressive Party and deputy prime minister from 1999 to 2002, died aged 79. [[source]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Anderton) Related: [alliance](../parties/alliance/README.md).
- NZHistory for this day: <https://nzhistory.govt.nz/timeline/7/1>

### 8 January

- **1918 — Southern Maori MP dies in office** Taare Parata, the Liberal MP for Southern Maori, died, leading to a by-election in the seat later that year. [[source]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/19th_New_Zealand_Parliament)

- **1957 — Walter Nash becomes prime minister** Walter Nash was sworn in as prime minister of the Second Labour Government, which lasted a single term. [[source]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Labour_Government_of_New_Zealand) Related: [labour](../parties/labour/README.md).
- **1975 — Muldoon becomes prime minister** Robert Muldoon was sworn in as prime minister after National's 1975 landslide. He held office until 1984. [[source]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_National_Government_of_New_Zealand) Related: [national](../parties/national/README.md).
- **2016 — Bill English becomes prime minister** Bill English was sworn in as prime minister, with Paula Bennett as deputy, following John Key's resignation. [[source]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/51st_New_Zealand_Parliament) Related: [national](../parties/national/README.md), [bill-english](../parties/national/people/bill-english/README.md).

### 13 December

- **1949 — First National government takes office** Sidney Holland was sworn in as prime minister, the first from the National Party. [[source]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_National_Government_of_New_Zealand) Related: [national](../parties/national/README.md).
- **1986 — Constitution Act 1986 passed** Parliament unanimously passed the Constitution Act, which replaced the 1852 Act and tidied up New Zealand's constitutional rules. It took effect on 1 January 1987. [[source]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_Act_1986)
- **2011 — David Shearer becomes Labour leader** David Shearer took over as Labour leader after Phil Goff's resignation, defeating David Cunliffe in a caucus vote. [[source]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/50th_New_Zealand_Parliament) Related: [labour](../parties/labour/README.md), [david-shearer](../parties/labour/people/david-shearer/README.md).

### 15 December

- **2021 — Housing intensification law passed** Parliament passed the Resource Management (Enabling Housing Supply) Amendment Act, agreed between Labour and National, allowing three homes of three storeys on most urban sites. ### 16 December

### 17 December

- **1919 — General election and liquor referendum** Voters re-elected William Massey's Reform government and voted in a referendum that narrowly kept the sale of alcohol legal. [[source]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1919_New_Zealand_general_election)
- **1991 — Muldoon leaves Parliament** Former prime minister Sir Robert Muldoon resigned as MP for Tamaki after 31 years, triggering a by-election. [[source]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/43rd_New_Zealand_Parliament) Related: [national](../parties/national/README.md).
- **2018 — Medicinal cannabis law passed** The Misuse of Drugs (Medicinal Cannabis) Amendment Act received royal assent, allowing wider access to medicinal cannabis products. [[source]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/52nd_New_Zealand_Parliament) Related: [labour](../parties/labour/README.md).
- NZHistory for this day: <https://nzhistory.govt.nz/timeline/17/12>

### 18 December

- **2007 — Electoral Finance Bill passes** Parliament passed the Electoral Finance Bill 63 votes to 57 after months of protest. It was signed into law the next day and repealed in 2009. [[source]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_Finance_Act_2007) Related: [labour](../parties/labour/README.md).
- **2018 — Cannabis referendum announced** The Government announced a referendum on legalising recreational cannabis, held with the 2020 election. Voters narrowly rejected the proposal. [[source]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannabis_in_New_Zealand) Related: [labour](../parties/labour/README.md).
- **2019 — Rua Kēnana pardoned** Parliament passed a law pardoning the Tūhoe prophet Rua Kēnana, who was arrested in a 1916 police raid, as part of a Treaty settlement. [[source]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rua_K%C4%93nana_Hepetipa) Related: [labour](../parties/labour/README.md).
- NZHistory for this day: <https://nzhistory.govt.nz/timeline/18/12>

### 19 December

### 20 December

- **1875 — General election of 1875-76 begins** Polling began in a general election spread over six weeks, the last before the provinces were abolished. [[source]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/6th_New_Zealand_Parliament)
- **1893 — Māori electorates vote in the 1893 election** Polling in the four Māori electorates was held three weeks after the general electorates, with Māori women voting for the first time. [[source]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women%27s_suffrage_in_New_Zealand)
- **2011 — 50th Parliament sworn in** Members of the 50th Parliament were sworn in and Lockwood Smith was re-elected Speaker. [[source]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/50th_New_Zealand_Parliament)
- NZHistory for this day: <https://nzhistory.govt.nz/timeline/20/12>
