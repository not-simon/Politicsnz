In a nutshell

National leads the government now, with ACT and NZ First. It wants to keep taxes where they are, cut government debt and get the books back in surplus. It is promising compulsory KiwiSaver, longer paid parental leave and tougher sentencing for some crimes.

Big ideas
  • No new taxes next term, and the government's books back in surplus by 2028/293 policies · policy.nz
  • Make KiwiSaver compulsory for workers and sign babies up at birth1 policy · policy.nz
  • Split Foodstuffs (New World, Pak'nSave, Four Square) into two companies to boost supermarket competition1 policy · policy.nz

Two sides

What they say

National's slogan is "Fixing the Basics, Building the Future"; its fiscal plan says a re-elected National government will "not impose new taxes" and will "return to surplus in 2028/29 and stay there".

National Party fiscal plan, 12 Oct 2026

What critics say

"National promised to grow the economy and get the Government's finances in order. It has done neither – unemployment is higher, growth is weaker, debt has continued to rise, and frontline services have been cut."

Labour Party fiscal plan, 4 Oct 2026
Who they'd work with

Wants to continue the current government. Luxon ruled out a "grand coalition" with Labour (May 2026) and any deal with Opportunity, including confidence and supply, from 27 July 2026.

Are the numbers costed?
Yes — a fiscal plan was published on 12 Oct 2026. It keeps new spending to $2.4b a year, leaves $7.5b of operating money unallocated over four years, and part-funds promises by cutting foreign aid. Independent review: none disclosed.
