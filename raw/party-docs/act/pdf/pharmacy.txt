02 Managing chronic conditions
People with chronic issues, such
as hypertension, type 2 diabetes or
hypercholesterolaemia, should not need
a GP appointment to simply renew the
same medicines. Pharmacists already see
these patients regularly, understand their
medicines, and are well placed to carry out
routine monitoring.
ACT would allow pharmacists to provide
funded chronic disease management for
stable patients. Accredited pharmacists
could conduct an annual medication review,
order the standard blood tests needed to
monitor treatment, and adjust doses within
clear clinical protocols. Anything outside
those limits would be referred back to the
patient’s GP.

Costing
The up-front cost of extending the Minor Ailments Scheme to these conditions is estimated at between
$50 million and $100 million annually, depending on public uptake. Consultations would be co-funded,
with patient charges capped at $20, or $5 for Community Services Card holders. However, the total
net cost of this policy will be lower, because it shifts care out of more expensive GP, urgent-care and
hospital settings to pharmacies. Ultimately, with clinicians freed to treat more complex patients sooner,
ACT’s policy will deliver savings for the health system.
Reduced revenue from five-year pharmacy renewals will cost Medsafe approximately $0.8 million
annually. This will be partially offset by administrative savings, and by a greater number of pharmacies
renewing as a result of the removal of the five-pharmacy cap and proximity rules.
