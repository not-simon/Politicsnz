Dear Minister,

I am writing to raise my concern about local authorities appointing
unelected representatives to council committees and subcommittees,
with full voting rights.

With voting local elections underway this week, New Zealanders have
the opportunity to appraise their local candidates and hold to account
their elected representatives. However, not all decisionmakers around
council tables will be subject to this democratic test.

Local authorities across the country have increasingly added unelected
members to committees with voting rights. These members are often
appointed by mana whenua, but may also include representatives of
youth, business, or environmental lobby groups.

Regardless, voters expect that decisionmakers can be held accountable
and unelected appointees simply do not meet this standard. This
undermines the democratic process and the integrity of the system.

This issue is especially relevant as rising rates place pressure on
household budgets. The ballot box is the only real accountability
mechanism available to ratepayers – but that accountability disappears
when councils appoint unelected individuals. Appointees who are not
answerable to voters have no democratic incentive to reject wasteful
spending or keep rates affordable.

With ACT’s support, the Government has moved to defend local
democracy by restoring the public’s right to vote on the existence of
Māori wards. However, I am concerned that this move fails to address
the greater problem of unelected members. Councils may move to
counter a public vote against Māori wards by appointing unelected mana
whenua representatives to council committees, along the lines of
Auckland Council’s Independent Māori Statutory Board.
In fact, in some territories we see councils with both Māori ward
councillors and unelected mana whenua representatives, as is currently
the case at Wellington City Council.

To address this, I have drafted the Local Government (Restoring
Democratic Integrity) Amendment Bill. This Bill would ensure that
unelected members of committees or subcommittees do not have voting
rights and do not count towards quorum. This protects a council’s ability
to access outside expertise when appropriate, while making clear that
voting power must rest with those who have the public mandate and who
are accountable to voters.

I urge you to consider adopting this as a Government Bill. This would
reinforce the Government’s commitment to local democracy and help
rebuild public confidence in local decision-making.

Please find attached to this letter a copy of my Bill.

I look forward to your response.


Yours sincerely,
