ACT’S PLAN TO MAKE FARMING FUN AGAIN                         20 . 09 . 26
  I got into politics because confidence in rural New Zealand was at an
  all-time low.

  For years, farmers had been hit with one new rule after another.
  Freshwater regulations, Significant Natural Areas, winter grazing rules,
  emissions pricing proposals, the ute tax, firearms laws, health and
  safety paperwork, and layers of resource management red tape all added
  cost and uncertainty to running a farm.

  I had spent more than 20 years farming and had seen first-hand what
  happens when people in Wellington who don’t understand farming try to
  regulate every part of it.

  Things are much better now, thanks to ACT pushing the Government to go
  much further and faster in delivering real change for Rural New Zealand
  than it would have without ACT.

  The ute tax is gone. He Waka Eke Noa is gone. Agriculture has been kept
  out of the ETS. Freshwater farm plans are being simplified. The methane
  target has been halved. We have stopped the expansion of Significant
  Natural Areas, cut back unworkable winter grazing rules, restored 90-
  day trials, and are replacing the Resource Management Act with new laws
  based on property rights.

  But there is much more to do. I want my daughters, if they choose
  farming, to have the freedom to farm well without the red tape I’ve faced
  for 20 years.

  That means getting the next big decisions right.

  Replacing the RMA is a major step forward. Further decisions about the
  national instruments and detailed standards under the new system will
  also have a significant impact on what farmers can do with their land.
  Freshwater rules still need to be rewritten. Te Mana o te Wai needs to be
  scrapped. Climate policy needs to reflect the science of methane and the
  fact that New Zealand is a world-leader in agricultural efficiency.

  ACT is the only party that has never supported introducing a price on
  agricultural emissions and we opposed the Zero Carbon Act when every
  other party supported it.

  I’m also acutely aware of the challenges facing rural communities in
  relation to rural school buses, access to healthcare, rural crime,
  and illegal rubbish dumping. ACT has a plan to address each of these
  important issues.

  Farmers do not need another politician telling them how to farm. They
  need a Government that respects property rights, sets clear rules around
  genuine environmental harm, and otherwise lets them get on with it.

  That is what this plan is about.

  Let farmers farm.




  Andrew Hoggard MP
  ACT Agriculture Spokesperson
  Dairy farmer

               KEEP WELLINGTON OUT
                        of
                     FARMING
            Replacing the Resource Management Act is one of the biggest
            wins for rural New Zealand in decades. The legislation puts
            property rights at the forefront, but further decisions will still
            matter greatly for how the new system works in practice. Key
            decisions will be made through the new system’s national
            instruments, including National Policy Direction and National
            Standards. ACT needs to be in a strong position in the next
            Government to ensure key decisions put rural New Zealand
            front of mind.
            ACT will:
                • ensure National Policy Direction puts property rights
                  first and focuses on actual environmental harm;
                • make sure rules recognise local conditions instead of
                  imposing one-size-fits-all solutions from Wellington;
                • keep standards clear, measurable and practical;
                • remove Te Mana o te Wai and other vague spiritual
                  concepts from freshwater regulation while backing
                  practical steps to improve water quality;
                • ensure those concepts do not simply reappear under
                  another name in the new system;
                • protect farmers from a return of Labour-style Significant
                  Natural Areas;
                • strengthen property rights through the New Zealand Bill
                  of Rights Act; and
                • expand voluntary and incentive-based conservation,
                  including biodiversity credits and mechanisms such as
                  the QEII National Trust.
            The replacement RMA creates a huge opportunity. ACT will
            make sure the detailed rules and national instruments deliver
            on its promise of a simpler, property-rights-based system.

                         PRACTICAL
                     ENVIRONMENTALISM
            Farmers have every incentive to look after the land and water
            their businesses depend on. ACT believes environmental policy
            should focus on outcomes, work with farmers and recognise
            that New Zealand is not one giant catchment.
            ACT will:
                  • increase penalties for illegal rubbish dumping;
                  • provide an additional $4.5 million a year for rural
                    catchment groups to improve water quality, maintaining
                    total funding at the current level of $13.5 million a year,
                    with scope for further increases where groups can
                    demonstrate strong results;1
                  • keep freshwater farm plans workable, practical and
                    affordable;
                  • continue the war on wilding pines, building on the
                    additional $79 million over three years already secured;
                  • allow targeted grazing on suitable DOC land to control
                    weeds, wilding pines and fire fuel; and
                  • ensure permanent forest owners carry the long-term
                    costs and risks created by their forests, including
                    sufficient pest control

                         BACK FARMERS
                               on
                            CLIMATE
            New Zealand farmers produce food with some of the lowest
            emissions intensity in the world. Taxing production here does
            not stop people eating, it simply shifts production to countries
            that will produce the same food less efficiently.
            ACT is the only party that has never supported introducing a
            price on agricultural emissions. We stood alone against the
            Zero Carbon Act, opposed He Waka Eke Noa and successfully
            fought to keep agriculture out of the ETS.
            We will keep it that way.
            ACT will:
                • keep agriculture permanently out of the Emissions
                  Trading Scheme;
                • oppose any agricultural emissions tax or pricing
                  scheme;
                • scrap the Net Zero target;
                • repeal the Zero Carbon Act;
                • abolish the Climate Change Commission;
                • recognise the different warming effects of methane and
                  carbon dioxide;
                • ensure New Zealand’s international climate
                  commitments do not force our farmers to bear costs
                  their overseas competitors avoid; and
                • scrap the artificial ETS price floor that props up the
                  carbon price and makes blanket carbon farming
                  artificially attractive.
            If New Zealand cannot secure a fair international climate deal,
            ACT is prepared to reconsider our participation in the Paris
            Agreement.

                    GIVE FARMERS THE
                          TOOLS
                            to
                          GROW
            New Zealand farmers have always been early adopters of
            technology, often in spite of, not because of, the government.
            ACT will continue to cut red tape to make agri-innovation
            easier.
            ACT will:
                • speed up access to proven agricultural chemicals and
                  veterinary medicines;
                • recognise trusted overseas approvals instead of forcing
                  New Zealand to reinvent the wheel;
                • cut unnecessary restrictions on agricultural drones;
                • remove barriers to new farming technology;
                • maintain employment settings that make it easier for
                  farmers to hire and give people opportunities; and
                • introduce a Rural Workforce Visa providing a reliable
                  pathway for workers needed by farms and rural
                  businesses.

                           BACK RURAL
                          COMMUNITIES
            Rural New Zealand produces much of the wealth New Zealand
            depends on. It deserves public services that work.
            ACT will:
                • fund 100,000 additional rural Police patrol hours every
                  year;
                • establish a dedicated rural crime unit;
                • strengthen offences and penalties for livestock theft and
                  poaching;
                • extend Text 111 eligibility to rural areas where unreliable
                  mobile coverage makes voice calls difficult;
                • introduce Three Strikes for Burglary, so repeat burglars
                  face a minimum three-year prison sentence without
                  parole on their third conviction;
                • introduce an Open Seat Rule so rural children can use
                  spare seats on existing school buses going to nearby
                  schools wherever practical;
                • allow pharmacists to provide more everyday healthcare
                  and manage more stable long-term conditions removing
                  pressure on hospitals and GPs;
                • enable 24-hour prescription e-lockers, making it easier
                  for rural patients to collect medicines without a long trip
                  during limited pharmacy opening hours; and
                • remove red tape relating to homekill and make it easier
                  for farmers to sell their products direct to consumer.

               A STRONGER ECONOMY
            Farmers make long-term decisions about land, stock,
            machinery and staff. They need stable prices, lower interest
            rates and confidence that Government will live within its means
            and stay out of commercial decisions it has no business
            making.
            ACT will:
                • retain the Reserve Bank’s focus on keeping inflation
                  under control so interest rates stay low;
                • balance the Government’s books without introducing
                  new taxes;
                • return the books to surplus faster;
                • keep finding savings so debt can be paid down rather
                  than passed on to the next generation;
                • maintain ACT’s commitment to No New Taxes; and
                • keep cutting unnecessary regulation that raises the cost
                  of doing business.
                • respect the right of farmer-owned companies to make
                  commercial decisions without politicians treating them
                  as instruments of government policy.
