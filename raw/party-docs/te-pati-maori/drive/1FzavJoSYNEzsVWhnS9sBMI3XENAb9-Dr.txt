Climate
Te Taiao is whakapapa. It holds memory,
sustains life, and carries responsibility
across generations. Māori wellbeing cannot
be separated from the wellbeing of our
environment.
When the land is harmed, the people are harmed.
When the climate is damaged, the future of our
mokopuna is at risk.
Climate change directly threatens whenua, wai,
moana, marae, food systems, housing security, our
livelihoods, and cultural continuity.
Te Pāti Māori advances a climate policy grounded
in whakapapa, regeneration, and justice. This
policy centres Māori authority, protects mokopuna
futures, and commits to a just transition that brings
communities with us.

Aotearoa is already experiencing the
impacts of climate breakdown: intensified
storms, flooding, drought, coastal
erosion, and the collapse of life sustaining
ecosystems.
Māori communities are disproportionately
affected due to historic land loss,
environmental dispossession, and
underinvestment in infrastructure and
community resilience. Marae, papakāinga,
and whenua Māori are often located in
areas most vulnerable to climate impacts
yet remain distant from the structural
decision-making that affects them.
Climate change is not a future threat but a
lived reality that must be navigated through
indigenous solutions grounded in tikanga
and mātauranga Māori. It demands radical
system-wide transformation. We must
restore the balance between people and the
natural world and recognise Māori authority
as essential to meaningful climate action.
Te Pāti Māori affirms that:
• Climate justice is inseparable from social
justice
• Māori authority is critical to effective
climate action
• Mokopuna futures must guide present-day
decisions.
Our climate policy is grounded in
regeneration rather than extraction,
restoration rather than exploitation, and
collective wellbeing rather than private
profit.
We reject climate responses that sacrifice
communities, entrench inequality, or protect
polluters. A just transition must uplift
whānau, protect workers, and restore the
mauri of our lands and waters.

Te Pāti Māori will...
1. Ban Sea-bed Mining in Aotearoa
• End new oil and gas exploration and phase
out existing extraction.
• Withdraw existing oil and gas permits
(on and offshore) within five years and
decommission sites within 10 years.
• Begin immediate planning and resourcing
for decommissioning.
• Support workers and communities through
a just transition.
2. Repeal the Fast-Track Approvals Act
• Retrospectively revoke all fast-tracked
mining consents.
3. End mining on conservation land
• Including reserves and Significant Natural
Areas under any new conservation, local
government and resource management
systems.
4. • Establish a $1 billion Pūngao Auaha
Fund
• Retrofit marae, kura, papakāinga, and
housing with solar energy and insulation.
• Support Māori-led community energy
projects.
• Transition Māori-owned businesses away
from coal and gas.
5. • Invest $300 million in Mātai Ahuwhenua
• Support Māori farmers to transition to
regenerative agriculture.
• Phase out synthetic fertilisers over five
years.
• Protect soil health, waterways, and
biodiversity.
6. • Support climate adaptation and
resilience in Māori communities
• Resource marae and hapū as resilience
hubs.
• Strengthen flood protection, water security,
and accessibility to local food systems.
• Ensure climate responses are locally led and
culturally grounded.
7. • Stand in solidarity with Pasifika
whanaunga
• Advocate internationally for climate justice.
• Support adaptation and relocation
strategies led by Pasifika nations.
• Honour whakapapa connections across
Te Moana-nui-a-Kiwa.
8. • Allocate $100m to fund a Māori National
Service Defence framework
• to give hapū, iwi and urban Māori
organisations the same access, resources,
and infrastructure as local government in
the wake of natural disasters.
9. • Establish a $100m Māori Taiao relief
Fund
• with an immediate focus on rebuilding and
cleaning up marae, urupā and papakāinga
impacted by environmental causes.
Our Commitment
Te Pāti Māori is committed to climate
justice that restores balance, protects life,
and honours our responsibility to future
generations.
We will not trade away whakapapa for profit,
nor sacrifice mokopuna futures for short-
term gain. Climate action must be led with
courage, integrity, and care; grounded in
tikanga, guided by Mātauranga Māori, and
accountable to communities and future
generations.
