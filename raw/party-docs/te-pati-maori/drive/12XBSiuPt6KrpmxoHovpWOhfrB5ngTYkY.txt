Mana
Motuhake
There is a new generation of Tangata Whenua
and Tangata Tiriti who recognise that being
forced to work within colonial systems does
not advance mana motuhake. Those systems
were never designed to uphold Māori
authority, Māori wellbeing, or Māori futures.
The only way this nation can truly function is
when Māori assert tino rangatiratanga across all
domains. Constitutional transformation is required
to rebalance power in Aotearoa, restore Māori
authority, and honour the relationship envisaged by
our tūpuna in 1840.
Te Tiriti o Waitangi cannot be settled. It is a living
constitutional covenant, where te tino rangatiratanga
of Tangata Whenua is inalienable.
Te Pāti Māori does not speak of Land Back as
symbolism; we will implement it.

Mana Motuhake
40
40
Since 1840, the retention and return of land
has been the central political aspiration of
Tangata Whenua, and the unifying struggle of
the tino rangatiratanga movement.
From Dame Whina Cooper’s 1975 land
march to noho whenua across Takaparawhā,
Whāingaroa, Pākaitore, Ngāwha, Te Kuri
a Pāoa, and the four-year occupation of
Ihumātao, Māori resistance has been
constant, principled, and enduring.
The establishment of the Waitangi Tribunal
acknowledged breach but did not restore
power. Settlement processes have too
often been framed as closure rather than
continuity, and reconciliation rather than
justice.
As Moana Jackson reminded us: treaties are
not settled, they are honoured.
The Westminster system continues to
constrain Māori authority through colonial
tools of control. Mana motuhake cannot
be achieved by reform alone. It requires
transformation through the restoration of
power and resources to Tangata Whenua
and the establishment of Māori-governed
systems.
Te Tiriti o Waitangi is the foundation of a
living constitutional relationship between
Tangata Whenua and the Crown. Both parties
carry ongoing obligations which have no
expiry date.
Mana motuhake requires:
• Parallel governance.
• Māori authority over Māori affairs.
• The restoration of land, law, and decision-
making power.
By working alongside Tangata Tiriti and
Tangata Moana, we can decolonise and re-
indigenise Aotearoa, and realise an Aotearoa
Hou grounded in Te Tiriti.

Te Pāti Māori will...
Stage 1
1.• Implement all Matike Mai Aotearoa
recommendations immediately.
2. Entrench the Māori electorates.
3. • Return the foreshore and seabed to
mana whenua.
4. • Establish a Parliamentary Commissioner
for Te Tiriti o Waitangi.
5. • End all foreign freehold land sales,
replacing them with leasehold only.
6. End perpetual leases.
Stage 2
7. • Abolish “full and final” and “large natural
groupings” settlement policies.
8. • Introduce a first right of refusal for mana
whenua on all private land.
9. • Insert retrospective relativity clauses into
all Te Tiriti settlements.
10. • Make Waitangi Tribunal
recommendations binding.
11. • Return all conservation land to mana
whenua.
Stage 3
12. • Require Māori wards and mana whenua
representation in all councils.
13. • Return all central and local government
land to mana whenua.
14. • Remove the British Royal Family as Head
of State.
15. • Recognise Aotearoa as the country’s
official name.
16. Establish a Māori Parliament.
1. Implementing Constitutional
Transformation
Te Pāti Māori commits to implementing all
Mātike Mai Aotearoa recommendations,
grounded in tikanga, He Whakaputanga,
Te Tiriti, and international Indigenous rights.
2. Māori Parliament
A Māori Parliament will hold authority
over Māori programmes, funding streams,
and governance structures accountable to
Māori alone.
3. Land Back
Te Pāti Māori will return:
• Foreshore and seabed
• Crown and council land
• Conservation land
Private land sales will first be offered to mana
whenua & perpetual leases will end.
4. • Overhauling the Te Tiriti Settlement
Process
The settlement framework will be reformed
to ensure durability, justice, and hapū-level
authority.
5. Ensuring Effective Crown Oversight
Māori representation will be entrenched, and
a Parliamentary Commissioner for Te Tiriti
will oversee Crown compliance.
6. Te Tiriti Impact Statements
All legislation, regulations, and Cabinet
papers will be required to include Te Tiriti
impact statements and compliance analysis.
Our Commitment
Mana motuhake is not a moment. It is an
inheritance and a responsibility to our
mokopuna.
The role of Te Pāti Māori is to unlock every
available resource across government for
Te Iwi Māori using every tool, lever and
pathway available.
Te Pāti Māori will continue to defend
and make space for rangatiratanga across
all domains and at every opportunity,
until Māori authority is restored, upheld,
and protected.
