Introduction

smartvote Australia is a so-called Voting Advice Application (VAA). It allows voters to
compare their own policy preferences with those of candidates and political parties based
on a comprehensive questionnaire. After answering the questionnaire voters receive a list of
candidates and/or political parties ranked according to their congruence with the voter’s
answers.


Questionnaire

In Australia, the smartvote questionnaire includes 35 questions on a wide range of policy
issues. It has been drafted by an Australian National University (ANU) team at the School of
Politics and International Relations also responsible for the Australian National Study (which
has been running a representative sample survey after all federal elections since 1987, see
https://australianelectionstudy.org). The questions are primarily focused specific policy
issues, only a few questions relate to more general ideological positions. Our hope with this
is to help shift the focus of voters away from personal characteristics, media presence, and
party affiliation and towards an issue-based discourse.

Since some policy issues may not be widely understood, a short text containing factual
information on the issue is provided next to the question. Candidates and parties have the
option of commenting on each of their answer to provide it more nuance or clarity.

There are two types of questions: Standard questions, which require approval or disapproval
of a specific policy proposal (4 answer categories). Budget questions, which ask if one thinks
a department’s budget should be increased, decreased or kept at the same level (5 answer
categories).

All candidates and parties running for the 2019 Federal election have been invited to
participate to the tool. In order to be present on smartvote Australia, candidates and parties
have to answer all questions. Voters on the other hand can decide to skip some questions.
Those to which they did not respond will not be taken into account in their proximity results
with candidates and parties. The tool thus allows voters to concentrate on some policy areas
for instance but responding to more questions will provide more precise results.

uses the Euclidean distance (geometric distance in a multidimensional space) as a measure.

Matching for parties

Parties are also invited to answer the smartvote Australia questionnaire. This allows to
provide matchings between voters and parties based the on parties’ official answers for the
Senate election. The same steps as for the calculation of the congruence with candidates are
taken to produce the matching scores for parties.

For more information on the methodology, please contact: smartvote@anu.edu.au.
