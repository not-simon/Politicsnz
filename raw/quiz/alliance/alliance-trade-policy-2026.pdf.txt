Alliance Party of Aotearoa New Zealand


Alliance Trade Policy
Trade That Serves Aotearoa New Zealand
The Alliance believes international trade should serve the interests of people,
communities, and the Aotearoa New Zealand economy rather than placing the
interests of multinational corporations above democratic decision-making.
Aotearoa New Zealand must retain the sovereign right to determine its own
economic, social, environmental, and employment policies.
The Alliance does not oppose international trade. Our country is a small country at
the end of a long supply line, and roughly a quarter of our economic activity currently
depends on selling to the world. What we oppose is a trade policy that treats market
access as worth any price, including the right to govern ourselves.

1. Economic Sovereignty
Political independence requires economic independence.
The Alliance will oppose international trade agreements and institutions where they
restrict the ability of democratically elected governments to:
       ●​ regulate the country's economy;
       ●​ protect workers and employment;
       ●​ protect the environment;
       ●​ regulate corporations;
       ●​ maintain public ownership;
       ●​ provide essential public services.
We must retain democratic control over essential services and strategic assets,
including:
       ●​ water;
       ●​ electricity;
       ●​ transport;
       ●​ telecommunications;
       ●​ health and other public services.
Trade agreements must never be allowed to force the privatisation or foreign
ownership of essential public services.

2. Trade That Protects Jobs and Living Standards
Trade policy will be designed to support decent employment and a productive
domestic economy.
The Alliance will use trade policy to:
        ●​ protect decent employment;
        ●​ support local manufacturing;
        ●​ encourage value-added domestic production;
        ●​ support regional economic development;
        ●​ protect health and safety standards;
        ●​ protect environmental standards;
        ●​ prevent exploitation of workers.
We must retain the ability to use tariffs and other trade measures to protect
strategic industries, domestic employment, and economic resilience.
Tariffs are a measure to be used sparingly, due to the impact on the price of goods
and the risk of retaliation against local exporters. They still have a role to play in
protecting local manufacturing and industry from the global race to the bottom.
Particular consideration will be given to industries vulnerable to unfair competition
based on exploitative wages and working conditions overseas.

3. No Race to the Bottom
International competition must not become a mechanism for driving down wages,
working conditions, or environmental standards.
The Alliance will oppose trade arrangements that encourage businesses to move
production to countries where workers can be paid poverty wages or where
environmental protections are inadequate.
Imported products should be required to meet appropriate labour, environmental,
health-and-safety standards comparable to those we require of our own producers.
Aotearoa New Zealand should have the right to determine those standards
democratically and to enforce them effectively.
International trade should raise living standards rather than drive a race to the
bottom in wages, working conditions, and environmental protection.

4. Protecting Democratic Government
Trade agreements must not give multinational corporations rights that override the
decisions of elected governments.
The Alliance will oppose trade arrangements that:
   ●​   undermine New Zealand's ability to regulate corporations;

No trade agreement should place corporate rights above democratic rights. The

will seek to remove it from those we are already party to. Aotearoa New Zealand has
already negotiated side letters curbing compulsory ISDS with five CPTPP partners,
which shows this can be done without leaving an agreement.

5. Supporting New Zealand Production
Trade policy must form part of a broader economic development strategy.
The Alliance will use appropriate trade and economic measures to strengthen New
Zealand's productive capacity, including support for:
   ●​   manufacturing;
   ●​   agriculture and food production;
   ●​   locally owned businesses;
   ●​   cooperatives;
   ●​   regional industries;
   ●​   technology and innovation;
   ●​   value-added exports.
The objective is not simply to export more commodities. It is a productive,
diversified, and resilient economy that creates good jobs and retains more
economic value within the country. Our farmers, growers, and food producers need
reliable access to overseas markets, and securing that access is a core function of
trade policy. What we will not do is trade away the right to regulate in exchange for it.

6. Fair and Managed Trade
The Alliance supports managed and fair international trade, rather than the
ideological pursuit of unrestricted free trade.
Trade policy should be negotiated according to New Zealand's national interests and
democratic priorities.
International agreements must be assessed against their effects on:
   ●​ employment;
   ●​ wages;
   ●​ public services;
   ●​ economic sovereignty;

   ●​ regional development;

New Zealand should be free to withdraw from or renegotiate trade arrangements that
substantially undermine these interests.
In government, the Alliance would commission a public review of New Zealand's
existing agreements, including the CPTPP and RCEP, against the tests set out
above, with the findings published and debated in parliament rather than settled in
cabinet.

7. Te Tiriti o Waitangi and Trade Agreements
Every New Zealand free trade agreement since 2001 has included a Treaty of
Waitangi exception clause, intended to preserve the Crown's ability to meet its
obligations to Māori.
The adequacy of that clause has been contested, including in hearings brought to
the Waitangi Tribunal. It has never been tested in a dispute, it was drafted by the
Crown without Māori at the table, and it protects the Crown's freedom to act rather
than Māori rights themselves.
The Alliance will not negotiate trade agreements affecting Māori interests without
Māori participation in the negotiation itself, and will review any exception clause with
Māori to ensure it upholds the mana of Te Tiriti o Waitangi.

8. Buying Local and Government Procurement
Alliance policy commits the state to preferring local materials and local labour in
public construction, and to rebuilding domestic industrial capability.
New Zealand's procurement commitments under the World Trade Organisation
Agreement on Government Procurement, and under the free trade agreements that
build on it, do not prevent this.
Those commitments apply to a defined schedule of entities: the public service
departments, New Zealand Police, the Defence Force, and ten named Crown
agents. Sub-central government is not covered at all. Procurement by state-owned
enterprises, by KiwiRail, and by regional and local government sits outside the
agreements entirely, as do public health, education, welfare, and research services.
Coverage begins at roughly $260,000 for goods and services and roughly $10 million
for construction.
Kāinga Ora is not a covered entity. Neither is any new public construction agency
established outside the public service. The state can therefore prefer New Zealand
materials and New Zealand labour across the great majority of the building

Domestic preference is already government policy in a limited form. The Government
Procurement Rules require agencies to seek economic benefit to New Zealand
above $100,000 for goods and services and $9 million for new construction and
expect contracts below those thresholds to go to capable New Zealand businesses.
The Alliance will strengthen and extend that requirement rather than invent it.
Where coverage does bite, on major departmental contracts above thresholds, we
will pursue the carve-outs needed to apply domestic preference.

Our Trade Vision
The Alliance believes New Zealand should be open to the world without being
owned by the world.
We will support international trade that creates jobs, raises living standards,
develops productive industries, and strengthens relationships with other countries.
We will reject trade arrangements that require New Zealand to surrender democratic
control over our economy, public services, environment, or working conditions.
Trade should be a means to build a stronger country, not an end in itself.
