Small Business, Local Enterprise, and
Cooperative Development
Our Vision
The Alliance believes that small businesses, family enterprises, farmers,
self-employed people, cooperatives, and locally owned enterprises are an essential
part of a strong and democratic economy. They provide employment, sustain
communities, and keep economic wealth circulating locally.

Small operators should not be expected to compete on unequal terms with large
corporations, banks, and financial institutions. Government has a responsibility to
protect smaller enterprises from excessive market power while providing them with
the finance, skills, technology, and infrastructure needed to succeed.

1. Protecting Small and Family Businesses
The Alliance will ensure that taxation and economic policy do not place unnecessary
burdens on small businesses, family enterprises, small farmers, and other
small-scale operators.

Where taxes on land, commercial property, or other assets are introduced to address
excessive concentration of wealth and ownership, appropriate thresholds and
exemptions will ensure that genuinely small businesses, owner-operators, and family
farms are not unfairly penalised.

The objective will be to ensure that taxation falls principally on accumulated wealth
and large-scale ownership, rather than on the productive activity of small enterprises.

2. Challenging Corporate Concentration and Monopoly Power
The Alliance recognises that small businesses and self-employed people can be
exploited by large corporations, banks, and financial institutions through excessive

The Commerce Commission has estimated that the supermarket duopoly takes

The Alliance will strengthen competition law and act against concentration and
monopoly power so that smaller operators have genuine access to markets and are
not forced into unfair relationships with dominant firms.

Small business owners, farmers, contractors, and wage earners share an interest in
an economy that prevents the concentration of economic power in the hands of a
small number of corporations and financial institutions.

3. A Public Development Finance System
The Alliance will establish and strengthen public development finance to provide
affordable capital to small businesses, farmers, cooperatives, community
enterprises, and new ventures that are unable to obtain appropriate finance from
commercial institutions.

A publicly owned Development Bank will provide concessional, low-interest finance
for productive investment, including for young farmers, agricultural cooperatives,
small businesses, and locally owned enterprises.

Access to public finance will be directed towards productive economic activity,
employment, innovation, and regional development rather than speculative
investment.

Government-backed venture capital will be available for small-scale individual and
group enterprise, particularly where it creates lasting employment or meets local
economic and community needs.

4. Supporting Self-Employment and New Enterprises
The Alliance will actively support people seeking to establish viable small
businesses, cooperatives, and self-employment initiatives.

Employment and enterprise resource centres will provide practical assistance,
including business planning, financial management, training, employment advice,
and access to government development programmes.

The purpose will be to give people the practical means to establish and sustain
enterprises, rather than simply expecting individuals to succeed without access to
capital, knowledge, or support.

5. Building Locally Owned Technology and Innovation
The Alliance will support the development and adoption of appropriate technology by
locally owned businesses.

An Applied Technology Programme will provide advice, technical assistance, and
venture capital for technology-based projects, with particular emphasis on
small-scale and appropriate technology that contributes to the wider national
development strategy.

A publicly supported technology advisory service will be available to locally owned
companies, ensuring that small and medium-sized enterprises can access
technological expertise that would otherwise be beyond their means.

6. Supporting Cooperatives and Local Ownership
Cooperatives provide an alternative to concentrated corporate ownership by allowing
producers, workers, consumers, and communities to share ownership and
decision-making.

Public development finance will give particular weight to cooperative ventures,
including agricultural cooperatives and enterprises that retain economic value within
local communities, with particular guidance for businesses that would otherwise shut
down being bought out and turned into a cooperative by their employees.

7. Linking Small Business Policy to Regional Development
Small business policy will form part of a wider national and regional development
strategy.
Government will work with local communities, councils, workers, farmers,
businesses, and cooperatives to identify opportunities for sustainable regional
economic development.

Investment will support local employment, value-added production, innovation,
infrastructure, and training. Particular attention will be given to regions where
economic restructuring or the decline of major employers has weakened local
economies.

The Alliance's objective is not simply to create more small businesses, but to build
strong local economies in which locally owned enterprises can thrive.
8. Training and Skills for Enterprise
Access to appropriate skills and training is essential to the success of small
enterprises.

The Alliance will expand access to business, technical, and vocational training,
including through Employment Resource Centres and other publicly supported
programmes.

Training will be linked to regional development strategies and emerging industries so
that workers and small businesses have the skills required to take advantage of new
economic opportunities.

9. A Fairer Relationship Between Small Business and the State
Government regulation should protect workers, consumers, and the environment
while avoiding unnecessary administrative burdens on genuinely small enterprises.

The Alliance will work with small businesses and their representative organisations
to ensure that regulatory systems are proportionate and practical.

Small-business status must not be used as a means of avoiding employment rights,
health and safety obligations, environmental standards, or taxation. Where a person
is a worker in substance, they should be a worker in law, whatever the contract says.
Alliance workers’ rights policy extends minimum employment protections to
contractors for that reason, and genuine small enterprise has nothing to fear from it.

The businesses undercut by misclassification are overwhelmingly other small
businesses, competing against operators who are not paying holiday pay.

10. Getting Paid on Time
Late payments are one of the most common causes of small business failure. A firm
can be profitable on paper and still go under waiting for money it has already earned.

The Alliance will:

       ●​ legislate maximum payment terms for large firms and public agencies
           contracting with small businesses;
       ●​ require interest to accrue automatically on overdue commercial invoices,
           without the smaller party having to ask;
       ●​ require large firms to publish their average payment times;
       ●​ ensure public agencies meet their own payment terms and report against
           them.

A small business should not be providing an interest-free loan to a company many
times its size.

11. Small Enterprise as Part of a Democratic Economy
We do not believe that economic policy should simply maximise the number or size
of private businesses. Nor do we believe that small businesses should be left to
compete against increasingly concentrated corporate and financial power without
assistance.

The Alliance will build an economic system in which small businesses, workers,
farmers, cooperatives, communities, and publicly owned enterprises can operate
alongside one another, with the government providing the finance, infrastructure,
skills, and regulatory framework necessary to ensure that economic power is widely
distributed.

The objective is an economy in which enterprise serves people and communities,
wealth is retained within Aotearoa New Zealand, productive investment is
encouraged, and economic development contributes to decent employment, regional
prosperity, and social wellbeing.
