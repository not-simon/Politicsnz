Alliance Party of Aotearoa New Zealand


Women, Gender Equality, and Economic
Security
Our Vision
The Alliance believes that women’s equality is fundamental to a democratic, fair, and
socially just society.

Women cannot achieve genuine equality simply through formal legal rights. Equality
requires action to remove the economic, social, and institutional structures that
continue to disadvantage women. This means addressing discrimination in
employment, unequal pay, poverty, unpaid caring work, violence, inadequate access to
healthcare and reproductive rights, and the barriers that prevent women from
participating fully in public and economic life.

We reject the idea that these inequalities can be left to the market to resolve. The state
has a responsibility to ensure that women have the economic security, freedom, safety,
and opportunities necessary to exercise their rights and participate equally in society.

The Alliance will pursue a comprehensive programme for substantive gender equality,
recognising inequality is experienced differently according to income, class, ethnicity,
disability, age, sexual orientation, family circumstances, and other factors.

1. Equality and Systemic Change
The Alliance will place gender equality at the centre of government policy and decision-
making.

We will:

   ●   require the Government to assess the gender impact of legislation, government
       programmes, public expenditure, and major investments before decisions are
       made;

   ●   establish a Gender Audit framework to identify and address the effects of
       government policies;

   ●   ensure that gender equality is incorporated into economic, employment,
       housing, health, education, and social policy;

   ●   address the concentration of women in low-paid, insecure, and undervalued
       occupations;

   ●   remove institutional and structural discrimination against women;

   ●   give particular attention to women who experience multiple or compounded

The Alliance recognises the particular rights and circumstances of wāhine Māori, and

We will also address the specific barriers faced by Pasifika women, women with
disabilities, lesbian women, older women, trans women, migrant women, and women
experiencing poverty or insecure housing.

2. Bodily Autonomy and Reproductive Rights
Every woman has the right to control her own body and make her own decisions about
reproduction.

Reproductive healthcare is a fundamental health right and must not be treated as a
criminal matter.

The Alliance will:

   ●   ensure free, safe, and accessible abortion services throughout Aotearoa;

   ●   remove unnecessary legal, financial, and geographic barriers to abortion;

   ●   ensure access to high-quality, confidential, and non-coercive reproductive
       healthcare and counselling;

   ●   provide universal access to contraception through the public health system;

   ●   ensure access to appropriate reproductive and sexual health services
       regardless of income, age, disability, or location;

   ●   provide comprehensive, age-appropriate sexuality education in schools,
       including education about consent, relationships, health, and bodily autonomy;

   ●   ensure women have access to the information and healthcare necessary to
       make informed reproductive choices.

   ●   support an informed consent model of gender-affirming care.

No woman's economic circumstances, geographical location, or relationship status
should determine whether she can exercise control over her own reproductive life.

3. Recognising Unpaid Work and the Caring Economy
The Alliance recognises that unpaid caring, domestic, and community work makes an
essential contribution to the wellbeing and wealth of Aotearoa.

Women continue to carry a disproportionate share of this work. This contributes
directly to women's lower incomes, reduced retirement savings, interrupted careers,
and economic insecurity.

The Alliance will recognise caring as economically and socially valuable work.

We will:

   ●   establish a Universal Child Benefit for every child, paid directly to the principal
       caregiver and indexed to movements in average weekly earnings;

   ●   ensure that support for children is universal rather than dependent upon narrow
       means-testing;

   ●   recognise the economic contribution of unpaid caring and domestic work in
       national economic and social measures;

   ●   develop measures of national wellbeing that recognise unpaid social and
       community contributions alongside conventional economic indicators;

   ●   ensure that carers are not forced into poverty because they undertake essential
       unpaid work;

   ●   strengthen public services that support families and carers.

The Universal Child Benefit will recognise that raising children is both a private
responsibility and a contribution to the future of society.

4. Equal Pay and Economic Equality
Women must receive equal pay for work of equal value.

The Alliance rejects the assumption that existing market wages are necessarily fair or
neutral. Historical patterns of discrimination have contributed to the systematic
undervaluing of occupations in which women are concentrated.

We will establish a comprehensive programme of employment and pay equity.

This will include:

   ●   strengthened Employment Equity legislation;

   ●   mechanisms for workers to pursue collective and class-based claims where
       discrimination is systemic;


                                                                                             3
   ●   a Pay Equity Bureau with responsibility for monitoring and enforcing pay equity;

   ●   comparisons between female-dominated and male-dominated occupations to
       determine equal value;

   ●   powers to require employers to provide appropriate pay and employment
       information;

   ●   measures to improve wage transparency;

   ●   effective enforcement mechanisms where employers fail to meet pay equity
       obligations;

   ●   equal access to education, training, and career progression;

   ●   measures to prevent discrimination in recruitment, promotion, and
       employment.

The Alliance will also support policies that increase wages and improve conditions in
traditionally female-dominated occupations, recognising that improving women's
economic position requires action on both discrimination and low pay.

5. Work, Family, and Caring Responsibilities
Having children or caring for family members should never result in economic
disadvantage.

The Alliance will make work and family life compatible by ensuring that responsibility
for caring is shared between individuals, employers, and the state.

Paid Parental Leave
We will provide at least 52 weeks of paid parental leave, available to all eligible
workers, including casual, seasonal and part-time workers.

Parental leave arrangements will recognise that caring responsibilities are not limited to
mothers and will support parents and caregivers to share caring responsibilities.

Employment Protections
We will strengthen the Employment Relations Act to require employers to act in good
faith in accommodating legitimate family and caring responsibilities.

Workers should be able to seek flexible working arrangements without being
disadvantaged because they have children or other caring responsibilities.

Childcare
The Alliance will establish a comprehensive, accessible, and affordable system of high-
quality childcare.

This will include support for non-profit and publicly supported childcare services,
including services capable of meeting the needs of people working outside
conventional hours.

We recognise that many workers are employed in shifts, evenings, nights, and
weekends. Childcare provision must reflect the reality of working life rather than
assume that every family operates from 9 am to 5 pm.

Older Persons’ Care
With an ageing population, unpaid care work includes more and more support for older
family members and friends, including peer support.

The Alliance Party will improve retirement provisions, increase funding for aged care
and support, and establish and fund independent advocacy services for older
people.
We believe the whole of society benefits when older people are able to live with as
much dignity and security as anyone else, meaning that the support they need to
thrive should be undertaken by society as a whole.

A Shorter Working Week
The Alliance supports a progressive reduction in standard working hours, including the
movement towards a 35-hour working week, without loss of income.

A shorter working week would help redistribute unpaid domestic labour, improve work-
life balance and allow women and men to participate more equally in paid
employment, family life, and community life.

6. Ending Violence Against Women
Every woman has the right to live free from violence, intimidation, and abuse.

Violence against women is not simply an individual problem. It is a major social and
public health issue requiring sustained government action.

The Alliance will:

   ●   invest in comprehensive prevention programmes addressing the underlying
       causes of violence;

   ●   provide education about healthy relationships, consent, equality, and non-
       violence;

   ●   ensure adequate long-term public funding for women's refuges and specialist
       support services;



                                                                                         5
   ●   end competitive short-term funding arrangements that undermine the stability of
       essential services;

   ●   provide secure, non-contestable funding for organisations providing essential
       protection and support;

   ●   ensure women fleeing violence have access to safe and affordable housing;

   ●   strengthen the enforcement of protection orders;

   ●   ensure police, courts, and other agencies have the resources necessary to
       respond effectively to family and sexual violence;

   ●   ensure children are properly protected from violence and abuse;

   ●   support and provide funding for kaupapa Māori informed models for addressing
       harm;

   ●   provide appropriate support for survivors and their children.

Protection orders and other legal protections must be backed by the resources
necessary to make them effective. A legal right is of little value if the state cannot
provide the practical protection required to enforce it.

7. Women, Poverty, and Economic Security
Women are disproportionately affected by poverty, low wages, insecure employment,
and inadequate retirement income.

The Alliance will address the economic causes of women's disadvantage through a
combination of decent wages, secure employment, adequate income support,
affordable housing, universal public services, and recognition of unpaid caring work.

We will:

   ●   support full employment and secure, properly paid work;

   ●   ensure social security provides an adequate standard of living;

   ●   improve support for sole parents and families;

   ●   ensure that women are not forced into poverty because of caring
       responsibilities;

   ●   strengthen women's access to affordable housing;

   ●   protect and improve public health, education, childcare, and social services;

   ●   address the long-term retirement income consequences of unpaid caring and
       interrupted employment.

8. Women's Participation and Representation
Women must have an equal voice in the institutions that govern and shape society.

The Alliance will promote women's participation and leadership across government,
Parliament, public services, workplaces, and community organisations.

We will:

   ●   remove barriers to women's participation in public and political life;

   ●   promote equal representation in decision-making;

   ●   support women's leadership and participation in traditionally male-dominated
       sectors;

   ●   address discrimination and harassment that prevent women from participating
       fully;

   ●   ensure government decision-making reflects the diversity of women in Aotearoa.

9. A Commitment to Substantive Equality
The Alliance believes that equality means more than treating everyone identically.

A genuinely equal society must address the different circumstances and barriers
experienced by different groups of women.

Our policies will therefore recognise the particular experiences of:

   ●   wāhine Māori;

   ●   Pasifika women;

   ●   women with disabilities;

   ●   lesbian women;

   ●   trans women;

   ●   older women;

   ●   women experiencing poverty;

   ●   women in insecure or low-paid work;

   ●   sole parents;

   ●   migrant and refugee women;

   ●   women experiencing violence and homelessness.

We will ensure that gender policy is developed in partnership with the women and
communities directly affected.

Conclusion
Women's equality is not an optional addition to social policy. It is fundamental to the
kind of society the Alliance Party seeks to build.

The Alliance will pursue a society in which women have genuine economic
independence, control over their own bodies, equal pay, secure employment, access
to affordable childcare, adequate income, freedom from violence, and an equal voice
in the decisions that affect their lives.

We will recognise the value of paid and unpaid work, strengthen the public services on
which families depend, and challenge the structural inequalities that have historically
limited women's freedom and economic security.

The Alliance is committed to a society in which all women are free, secure,
economically independent and equal participants in the life of Aotearoa New
Zealand.
