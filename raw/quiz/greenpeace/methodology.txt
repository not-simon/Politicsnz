Scoring Evidence

This document explains the methodology used to assign scores in Greenpeace Aotearoa's Election
Scorecard’s for 2026. The scorecards assess each political party's support for policies to address
the climate and biodiversity crises. The scorecards also identify which parties have received political
donations connected to Fast Track applicants and the following industries: Agribusiness, Fishing,
Mining, Oil and Gas.


Scoring methodology
Each political party was asked the same set of 20 policy questions. Each question required a direct
Yes or No answer. Parties were assigned a score for their response using the following categories.


 Colour          Point       Definition

 Green           3           Clear and unambiguous support for the specific policy.

 Yellow          2           Substantial support for the policy, but with a material qualification.

 Orange          1           Supports the general policy objective, but only commits to a weaker
                             version

 Red             0           Opposes the policy, has a clearly contrary position, or has no identifiable
                             policy position

Where parties provided a clear yes or no response, scores were assigned directly. Where a party:

   ●​ qualified its response by expressing support only in principle, attaching conditions, or
      supporting only a weaker version of the policy, or
   ●​ did not participate in the survey at all,

scores were assigned using publicly available information, including:

   ●​     Their response in the questionnaire,
   ●​     official party policy documents,
   ●​     public statements by party leaders or spokespersons,
   ●​     parliamentary voting records for the current parliamentary term,
   ●​     for governing parties a failure to enact the policy or actions taken that are inconsistent with
          the policy, during the current parliamentary term.
The National and ACT parties chose not to respond to the survey at all. Scores for these parties
were assigned using the same methodology described above.


Overall Grade
The party’s overall grade was calculated by adding the points they receieved for policy questions
together and dividing them by the number of questions. This gives an average score between 0 and
3, which was converted to a grade:


 Grade    Average score

 A        2.5–3.0

 B        2.0–2.49

 C        1.5–1.99

 D        1.0–1.49

 E        0.5–0.99

 F        0–0.49




Political donations
The scorecards identify parties that have received donations linked to several industries. To
establish this, Greenpeace reviewed Electoral Commission data on donations made directly to
political parties since 2022. Donations made directly to individual candidates were not reviewed or
included.

Where a party received a donation from a company operating in a relevant industry, or from an
individual with a known connection to that industry, the donation was recorded as linked to that
industry. These are recorded here. Due to the extensive number of donations from individuals that
were made to political parties, these were not comprehensively reviewed. Only large donations from
individuals with known industry connections identified through our research were included, as such
the scorecard may not capture all donations linked to the industries assessed. Greenpeace will
update the figures if further information comes to light.

Donations linked to entities involved in Fast-track applications were identified using RNZ’s analysis
of political donations and Fast-track applicants.

14. Restore Te Mana o te Wai

*The hierarchy states:
Freshwater must be managed in a way that prioritises:
   1.​ first, the health and well-being of water bodies and freshwater ecosystems
   2.​ second, the health needs of people (such as drinking water)
   3.​ third, the ability of people and communities to provide for their social, economic, and cultural
