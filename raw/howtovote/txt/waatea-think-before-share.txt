As the 2026 General Election campaign gathers pace, the Electoral Commission is urging New Zealanders to pause before sharing election-related content online, warning that misinformation and false claims can spread rapidly during election periods.
The Commission has launched a new public awareness campaign encouraging voters to verify information about enrolling and voting before passing it on through social media or messaging platforms.
Chief Electoral Officer Karl Le Quesne says the initiative is designed to help New Zealanders distinguish reliable election information from misleading or inaccurate content.
The Electoral Commission says one of its most important responsibilities is ensuring voters have clear, accurate and trusted information about how New Zealand’s electoral system works.
That includes providing authoritative guidance on enrolment, voting procedures, important election dates and how votes are cast and counted.
With social media playing an increasingly significant role in political discussion, the Commission says there is a greater risk that incorrect information can circulate quickly, potentially confusing voters or discouraging participation.
The new campaign encourages people to slow down, check the source of election-related claims and confirm information before sharing it with others.
The Electoral Commission is reminding voters that vote.nz remains the official source for information about enrolling, updating enrolment details and voting in the 2026 General Election.
Voters can also contact the Commission directly through its dedicated election information line for assistance.
Officials say using trusted sources is one of the simplest ways to prevent misinformation from spreading and to ensure eligible voters receive accurate guidance throughout the election period.
Election misinformation has become an increasing concern internationally, with false or misleading claims about voting processes, eligibility and election administration appearing in many democracies.
Such content can range from simple misunderstandings through to deliberately misleading information intended to confuse voters or undermine confidence in electoral systems.
The Electoral Commission says while it does not regulate political advertising or campaign messaging, it does provide guidance on what people should do if they encounter information they believe may be inaccurate or misleading.
Information is also available through the Commission’s website about where complaints relating to election material should be directed.
The Commission says its campaign is not about discouraging political debate.
Instead, it is encouraging informed participation by ensuring discussions are based on accurate information about the voting process itself.
Officials say a well-informed electorate is fundamental to maintaining public confidence in New Zealand’s democratic institutions.
As political parties intensify campaigning in the lead-up to polling day, voters are being encouraged to verify election information before sharing it and rely on official sources when seeking practical information about enrolling or casting a vote.
For the Electoral Commission, the message is straightforward: pause, check the facts and help ensure the 2026 General Election remains free, fair and accessible to every eligible voter.
More information about enrolling, voting and recognising election misinformation is available at vote.nz .
