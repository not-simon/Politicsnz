### EasyVote letters and emails
EasyVote letters and emails were prepared using electoral roll information from 4 October. If you’ve made changes since then, your new details won’t be included in your letter. You can still take your EasyVote card to the voting place, but make sure you tell voting place staff that your details have changed.
#### Automatic translation of EasyVote emails
The translation tools built into some email applications may cause some of the content in your EasyVote email to appear incorrectly. This could include the name of your electorate, your name and address, and the names of electorate candidates, as well as other information in the email.
We don’t recommend translating your EasyVote email into any other language. When you show your EasyVote card to workers at your voting place, it needs to show your electorate details and your name and address as they were sent to you, without translation.
If you have automatic translation turned on in your email application or browser, you should turn it off before viewing your EasyVote email.

If you can't find your enrolment in our online search , it doesn't necessarily mean you're not enrolled. [online search -> https://vote.nz/enrolling/enrol-or-update/enrol-or-update-online]
Some possibilities include:
- The details you've provided don't exactly match our records If there's a small difference between the information we have on file and the information you’ve entered, the search might not find you. This can sometimes be due to your browser autofilling information in the wrong fields, or may also be due to spelling or formatting.
- You’ve enrolled recently If you’ve recently enrolled or updated your details you won’t be able to see your details online straight away. It takes time for us to manually check and process enrolments. We’ll be in touch once your enrolment has been processed.
- You’re on the dormant roll If you’ve moved addresses since you last updated your enrolment, you may have been placed on the dormant roll. You need to update your address with us to find yourself in the search.
- You’re on the unpublished roll If you’ve chosen to go on the unpublished roll, you won’t be able to search for your details. You can contact our unpublished roll team on unpublished.roll@elections.govt.nz
If you have any concerns about your enrolment please contact us so our enrolment team can investigate for you.

- in the 3 months before a general election
- in the 3 months before local elections which are held every 3 years
- before a parliamentary by-election if the change would move you into the electorate where the by-election is being held.

There are two types of votes at a general election: ordinary votes and special votes.
If your name is on the printed electoral roll at the voting place when you go to vote, you’ll cast an ordinary vote.
If your name is not on the printed electoral roll at the voting place, you’ll cast a special vote.
You might not be on the printed electoral roll at the voting place you go to vote at if:
- you’re voting from outside your electorate (or from overseas)
- you’re on the unpublished roll
- you enrolled after Writ Day (4 October 2026).
Both sorts of votes are worth the same when it comes to the final result!
The only real difference is that if you’re making a special vote, you’ll need to fill out an extra form. This means it may take you a little bit longer to complete your vote at a voting place.
Special votes are counted during the official count. Before special votes are counted, we check each voter is enrolled.

If you would prefer to speak with us in a language other than English, we can arrange a telephone interpreter . Just call our team on 0800 36 76 56 .
What can I use an interpreter for?
You can ask for an interpreter when you need help communicating with us about Electoral Commission information or services.
How does it work?
Let our staff know that you need an interpreter and the language you would like to communicate in . You can also ask for an interpreter of a specific gender . We will connect you with our interpreting service and bring them into the call or can book in a time that suits you.
The interpreter is there to help both you and our staff understand each other. They do not provide advice or make decisions on your behalf. They cannot tell you who to vote for.
Is the service confidential?
I nterpreters are required to maintain confidentiality and follow professional interpreting standards.
Is this free?
Yes, there is no cost when accessing Electoral Commission information or services.
